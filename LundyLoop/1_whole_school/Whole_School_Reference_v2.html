One architecture. Three pathways. Four subject dialects. No marking theatre.
The policy sets the entitlement; this guide translates it into classroom practice. Feedback is complete only when a student can respond, the response reaches its pathway’s close, and it influences the next learning move. BUILD closes when an adult genuinely receives it. GROW and LAUNCH close when the pupil writes the line — the adult is audience, not verifier, and no initial is expected.
Status and purpose
This is an implementation guide, not a replacement policy. Where wording differs, the approved Feedback & Marking Policy 2025/26 (pilot for full rollout in 2026/27), Curriculum Policy 2025/26, safeguarding procedures and qualification requirements take precedence.

1 · The non-negotiables — and the flex

The approved policy treats feedback as participation rather than a one-way transaction. The four Lundy elements are sequential: if Space is absent, Voice is impaired; without genuine Audience, R is not earned; without Influence, the cycle has not informed learning.

Non-negotiableWhat staff doWhat is flexible
Feedback moves learning forwardMake the next learning move clear and usable.Verbal, written, modelled, digital, peer or practical.
Space comes firstAttend to regulation, relationship, time, communication and cognitive load.When, where and through whom feedback is re-offered.
Every student has VoiceProvide an accessible way to respond.Edit, answer, point, sign, demonstrate, re-attempt or short digital capture.
R requires AudienceRead, hear or watch the response and engage with it before adding R.Audience may be immediate or, for extended work, deliberately deferred.
Response has InfluenceAdapt the next question, scaffold, explanation, task, plan or target.Influence may confirm, deepen, redirect or re-teach; it need not mean instant success.
Important refinement: R is a concept first and a written code second.
Live loops may happen many times without being logged. Use the written code where there is a natural record, such as a workbook. In practical or oral learning, the watched and received re-attempt can close the loop without generating paperwork.
Colour rule
The approved policy permits an edit on the same page and in the same colour. A school may encourage a contrasting student colour where this helps visibility, but colour is not the entitlement. The response and the adult's Audience are what matter.

2 · Curriculum alignment: Intent → Implementation → Impact

The loop is not an isolated marking routine. It is the formative mechanism connecting curriculum intent, classroom implementation and impact.

Curriculum expectationWhat the Lundy Loop contributes
Breadth, balance and coherenceUses one feedback architecture across academic, practical, creative and personal-development learning while preserving subject integrity.
Appropriate teaching and learning stylesAllows the response modality to follow the student's communication, SEND, SEMH and curriculum needs.
Active student participationMakes the student an active participant in assessment, IEDP review and learning progression rather than the passive recipient of a judgement.
Assessment informs planningInfluence converts formative evidence into adaptation of the next task, lesson, scheme, APDR target, IEDP or EHCP/PfA outcome.
Emerging → Developing → Secure → ExceedingThe loop diagnoses the next move; formal tracking records the broader pattern. Codes must not be mistaken for attainment grades.

Progress is not simply WS becoming I.
Increasing independence may be valuable, but appropriate support remains an entitlement. Judge progress through knowledge, skill and successful access—not by withdrawing support prematurely.

3 · Four subject families: translate, do not standardise

Every subject closes the same loop, but not every subject should manufacture the same artefact. Assign the dominant family by the learning episode, not permanently by the timetable label: Science may be book-based during explanation and practical during an investigation.

FamilyTypical contextsVoiceNatural trace
Book-basedEnglish, Maths, written Science/Humanities, exam practiceEdit, correction, reworked calculation, extended responsePrompt and response on the page; model or worked example
Practical / physicalPE, Outdoor Education, Food, D&T, fieldwork, experimentsCorrected re-attempt, performance, safer or more precise actionUsually observation; photo/clip only where evidence already has value
CreativeArt, Music, Drama, Arts AwardIteration, redraft, second take, reflective annotationVersions, study sequence, rehearsal or performance capture
DiscursivePSHE/RSHE, RE, Citizenship, tutor time, discussionScenario advice, oral reasoning, re-sort, anonymous class questionUnderstanding artefact only; never personal disclosure content
Hybrid episodes are expected
Use the dialect that matches the current learning. A fieldwork session may move from a practical re-attempt to a book-based explanation.
Anti-workload test
If the artefact exists only to prove feedback happened, do not create it. Use the learning evidence already produced.

A stronger definition of Influence

Influence does not mean the student must immediately get the answer right. A response that exposes a misconception can close the loop if the adult genuinely receives it and changes the teaching, scaffold or next attempt. The evidence is that the response mattered.

The implementation test: Can the teacher explain what the student did with feedback and what changed next—without producing evidence created solely for the visitor?

4 · QA, moderation and rollout without quota creep

This guide does not replace the approved QA cycle. The policy requires weekly visibility of the loop across core subjects and half-termly triangulated sampling across Lundy's four conditions. Leaders coach the quality of the loop; they do not count comments, stickers or pages.

Half-termly QA: sample the quadrants

ElementLeader questionPossible evidence
SpaceWas the student able to receive and respond?Student voice, routines, regulation support, lesson visit, teacher explanation.
VoiceDid the student respond in an appropriate modality?Edit, re-attempt, symbol, spoken response, demonstration, digital clip.
AudienceDid an adult genuinely receive and engage with it?Follow-up, acknowledgement, checked correction, observed re-attempt.
InfluenceWhat changed because of the response?Next task, explanation, scaffold, scheme adaptation, APDR/IEDP/EHCP link.
No additional weekly tally.
A separate “loops closed” count risks becoming a quota and contradicting the workload principle. Use naturally occurring evidence and the approved core-subject/half-termly cycles. Absence caused by attendance, subject frequency or regulation should prompt professional discussion—not automatic non-compliance.

Recommended implementation sequence

  1. Calibrate leaders first: agree examples and non-examples of Audience, Influence and lean capture.
  2. Train through real artefacts: compare a closed page loop, watched practical re-attempt, creative iteration and safe discursive revisit.
  3. Issue one family page per dominant teaching context: retain access to all four for hybrid lessons.
  4. Teach students the language: “How will you respond?” and “What changed next?”
  5. Moderate termly: sample work, digital evidence and accreditation without creating a parallel evidence system.
  6. Review impact: student understanding, usefulness of feedback, progress, accreditation, staff workload and recurring CPD needs.
Coaching prompt: Show or describe one recent loop. Where was Space secured? What was the student's Voice? How did you receive it? What did it influence?

The Lundy Loop in Book-Based Learning

Typical contexts: English, Maths, written Science/Humanities, coursework and exam practice.

SPACERegulated, clear task, time to respond—not automatically homework.
VOICEEdit, correct, rework, explain or extend on the page.
AUDIENCEAdult checks and engages with the response before R.
INFLUENCENext question, scaffold, model, teaching or redraft changes.
Practice featureWhat good looks like
Next stepOne or two precise actions linked to the learning goal, success criteria, accreditation standard or mark scheme.
Response timeLive where possible. Extended writing may use the first 10–15 minutes of the next lesson when re-reading adds value.
R-gateR only after the adult has checked the response—not merely because writing appears below the prompt.
EvidenceThe page, worked example, model, correction or re-attempt is sufficient. A contrasting pupil colour may help but is not mandatory unless locally agreed.
Example
NS+ “Use evidence from line 12 to support your inference.” The student adds evidence; the adult reads it, briefly acknowledges the stronger link, adds R, then offers a more demanding inference.
Do not: write lengthy comments no one answers; defer routine correction unnecessarily; use R as a decorative completion mark; correct every SPaG error regardless of the student's needs or lesson purpose.
Quick check: What did the student do with the feedback, how was it received, and what changed next?

The Lundy Loop in Practical & Physical Learning

Typical contexts: PE, Outdoor Education, D&T, Food, fieldwork, experiments and vocational activity.

SPACERight moment, safe positioning, private where public correction may dysregulate.
VOICEThe next attempt: corrected, safer, more accurate or more controlled.
AUDIENCEAdult watches the re-attempt and responds to what actually happened.
INFLUENCENext attempt, support, equipment, explanation or challenge changes.
Practice featureWhat good looks like
Next stepSpoken, specific and actionable: one performance cue at a time.
ResponseThe re-attempt itself is Voice. It does not need converting into written work.
R-gateThe adult has observed and engaged with the re-attempt. Routine live loops need no separate record.
Lean capturePhoto, short clip or one-line note only when it already supports accreditation, EFL/digital evidence, PfA, progress or safety learning.
Example
“Re-take the bearing from the gate and steady the compass before reading.” The student repeats it; the adult watches, checks the method and changes the next route point. No worksheet is required.
Do not: photograph every routine interaction; turn live coaching into retrospective paperwork; record R when the second attempt was not actually observed; interrupt flow merely to create an audit trace.
Quick check: Can the adult describe the feedback, the watched response and the next adaptation?

The Lundy Loop in Creative Learning

Typical contexts: Art, Music, Drama and Arts Award.

SPACEFeedback on the work and process, sensitively timed so it supports rather than kills flow.
VOICENext study, redraft, take, rehearsal or reflective annotation.
AUDIENCEAdult compares versions and names what changed.
INFLUENCECraft, decision-making, process or next experiment develops.
Practice featureWhat good looks like
Feedback targetCraft, technique, intention, process or criteria—not the student's identity, taste or personal style.
VoiceVisible iteration or a student's own explanation of a deliberate creative choice.
AudienceThe adult engages with the new version in relation to the earlier version, intention or criteria.
EvidenceSketchbook sequence, drafts, takes, rehearsal capture or Arts Award evidence already produced.
Example
“In the next study, increase the tonal range around the focal point.” The pupil experiments; the teacher compares both studies and asks the pupil which change best supports the intended focus. The next study follows that decision.
Do not: grade personal expression as taste; require written annotation when a performance or demonstration is the more valid response; treat any second version as improvement without genuinely reviewing it.
Quick check: Is development visible, received and connected to a purposeful next creative decision?

The Lundy Loop in Discursive Learning

Typical contexts: PSHE/RSHE, RE, Citizenship, tutor time and discussion-rich Humanities.

SPACEDistancing, scenarios, right to pass, anonymous class questions; no forced personal disclosure.
VOICEReasoning, scenario advice, card sort, revisit or anonymous question.
AUDIENCEAdult hears the full answer, reads the revisit or answers the class box.
INFLUENCEMisconception, question or reasoning shapes the next explanation or session.
Practice featureWhat good looks like
DistanceAsk what a character could do, not what the student has personally experienced.
Anonymous VoiceQuestion boxes close at class level and remain deliberately untraceable.
R-gateUse individual R only for a safe learning response genuinely received. Do not attach R to anonymous contributions.
EvidenceTwo-colour concept revisit, improved scenario advice or re-sorted cards. Capture understanding artefacts only.
Disclosure firewall
If Voice becomes a safeguarding disclosure, the feedback process stops. Do not add a next-step prompt, photograph it, upload it as learning evidence or investigate. Listen, do not promise confidentiality, and follow the school's safeguarding/DSL procedure immediately. If unsure, treat it as a disclosure.
Example
A student improves advice to a fictional character by adding a trusted adult and a relevant support route. The teacher receives the reasoning and adapts the next scenario to test when emergency help is needed.
Quick check: Did we loop the learning without eliciting, recording or auditing the student's private life?

Five fast decisions

  1. Is the student able to respond now? If not, repair Space and re-offer appropriately.
  2. What response modality preserves entitlement with the least unnecessary load?
  3. Has an adult actually received it? If not, do not add R.
  4. What changed? Work, question, explanation, scaffold, planning or target may change.
  5. Does this require capture? Capture only if it has curriculum, qualification, PfA, safeguarding-compliant or agreed digital evidence value.
Refusal
Treat refusal as information about regulation, communication, demand, timing or relationship. Re-offer; monitor patterns through appropriate planning processes.
Teaching assistants
TAs can create Space, support or scribe Voice and provide Audience. The student's meaning must remain theirs, and significant learning should inform the teacher's next decision.
Version-control items for leadership decision
Confirm the local name of the digital platform (the policy heading says “Digital App Lean Capture” while the body uses EFL); confirm whether contrasting student colour is encouraged but optional; and publish this as an implementation companion beneath the approved policies.
