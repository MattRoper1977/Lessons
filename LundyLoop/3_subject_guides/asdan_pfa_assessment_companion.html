ASDAN & PfA — Lundy Loop Assessment Companion

Subject-guide companion · 2026/27 · covers BUILD, GROW and LAUNCH ASDAN provision

Dominant dialect: practical, project-based and portfolio-building. An ASDAN lesson usually produces something real — a snack, a plan, a delivered activity — and the evidence question is what that product shows, not whether a worksheet was finished.

SPACEPredictable phases, regulated conditions, pass and private routes always available; the open matter is named before opinions are asked.
VOICESay, point, sort, demonstrate, photograph or write — the response mode is the pupil's within the criterion; scribing is access, not lesser authorship.
AUDIENCEThe adult, the group or a real recipient genuinely attends to the contribution; on GROW and LAUNCH the adult is audience, not verifier.
INFLUENCEThe pupil hears what their contribution changed — the next task, the plan, the group decision — at decision level, never as a copied line.

The three loops, kept separate

ASDAN work runs three processes at once, and collapsing them into one is the failure this companion exists to prevent. The learning loop is the adult's read — what did this response mean and which next step follows. The participation loop is the whole-school Lundy Loop above — did the pupil get a genuine say, and did it change anything. The portfolio loop is the movement of evidence through the estate's controlled states toward an award. A photograph, a pupil response and an assessor judgement can each be useful; they are not interchangeable. A photo may show context without showing understanding; a witness note may establish authenticity without establishing every criterion; a powerful pupil choice may demonstrate participation without becoming qualification evidence. Staff reference: the triple-loop model (proposed toolkit).

Closure in ASDAN is the whole-school closure

BUILD closes when an adult genuinely receives it. GROW and LAUNCH close when the pupil writes the line — the adult is audience, not verifier, and no initial is expected.

ASDAN has no closure of its own. This is the sentence carried by the whole-school guidance and by the calibration game, and it is the sentence that wins. If any resource — a pack, a template, a tutor-time routine — implies an ASDAN-specific close, an adult signature on a GROW or LAUNCH closure line, or a countersigned record, it is wrong. The Assessor Witness Statement on the printed evidence pack is portfolio-loop paperwork about a product's authenticity; it is not the loop's close and it never moves onto the closure line. “Not yet = Voice present, Audience pending” is a real answer on every pathway. So is a lesson whose honest outcome is that nothing needed changing.

Reading the response — one card, whole school

The account of what a pupil's response means and which next step follows lives in one place: Reading the Response — five branches (Secure · Mixed pattern · Shared misconception · Access barrier · Method or data problem), nothing recorded, one next step per pupil per lesson. This companion adds no branch set of its own.

Two ASDAN-specific reads that are not branches. ASDAN evidence work raises two further questions — authorship (“which part was the pupil's decision, and what did the adult supply?”) and criterion sufficiency (“does this item actually evidence the named criterion, or does it need a real later opportunity?”). Both are portfolio-loop questions about an item's evidence state, answered on the assessor and TA surfaces of the proposed ASDAN Lundy toolkit — they are not next-step branches, they are never written down as codes beside a pupil's name, and their vocabulary is queued for the whole-school reconciliation of adult-support language (HANDOVER.md queue 18) before any of it reaches a TA.

The portfolio loop — the six evidence states

Evidence status uses the estate's controlled vocabulary, defined once in quality/toolkits/CLAIMS_REGISTER.md (“The six evidence states”, Pass TK-1): draft → curriculum-aligned → candidate evidence → centre-verified → submitted → certified. No state may be skipped forward; states 4–6 are centre and awarding-body actions, never a repository or classroom action; where a state is unknown the honest value is draft. In tutor time, evidence normally moves no further than candidate evidence queued for authorised review — the assessor owns sufficiency, and finishing a lesson certificates nothing.

What is genuinely open — and what is never a vote

SPACE is only genuine when the open matter is real. Name what is open before asking for opinions, and name the boundaries as boundaries — pupils trust a named boundary far more than a pretend choice.

Making Influence visible next lesson

“Last lesson your evidence showed ___. Today we are starting with ___.”

Said aloud at arrival, in your own words. It is something you say, not a form. For decision-level matters (“you said / we did / we could not, because”), the proposed influence report-back board keeps the report-back at decision level without copying anyone's closure line.

What this guide does not add

  • No new pupil-facing surface. Nothing here changes a lesson file, a print pack or a closure line.
  • No adult receipt. No initial, signature or countersignature is introduced anywhere, on any pathway.
  • No record, register, tally or upload queue. The read is expressed as the next step you give — never written down as a code, counted, or collected.
  • No evidence quota and no compulsory collection slot. An empty day is data; a missed optional slot is not a debt.
  • Disclosures leave this workflow entirely — see the ASDAN data firewall. A disclosure never enters a caption, a handoff note or a portfolio.
