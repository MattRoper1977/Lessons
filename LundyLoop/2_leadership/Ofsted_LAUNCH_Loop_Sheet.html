The 10-minute walk (do this yourself)

Six steps, one real book, no preparation. Built to be done, not read.

1Fetch a real book. any LAUNCH pupil's science book — whichever is nearest. Not a "best book"; the point is that any book works.
2Find a yellow NS+. Flick until you hit a dated next step. Read it. Is it one thing, precise, linked to the learning?
3Find the pupil's answer to it. Look for the own-colour response nearby. Is it genuinely theirs — a correction, an extension, a reworked calculation?
4Check the R. Is R present only where a response exists? If you find an R with no visible Voice, that is your coaching conversation — the gate matters more than the code.
5Trace one chain across three weeks. Same class of error, shrinking. That page-flip is the impact evidence.
6Run the QA question on the teacher — and time it. "What did this pupil do with your feedback, and what did it change next?" A fluent answer inside sixty seconds, from an open book, is the whole system working.
The one-line summary for any visitor: feedback here is complete only when the pupil has responded, an adult has genuinely received it, and it has changed what happens next. Everything you can see in the books is a by-product of that, not a display for you.
