Remembrance

  • Wednesday 11 November
  • A time to pause and remember
Presenter notes: Elapsed 00:00 to 00:45 (45 seconds). Say: Today we will think about why people remember those affected by war. There are no graphic pictures or sudden sounds. You do not need to tell us anything personal. You can reflect in your usual way. We will prepare for a two-minute silence together. Presenter: use the planned 10:50 start for a live 11:00 silence. If this assembly is at another time, explain that clearly now.
Slide 2 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Why we remember

  • We remember lives lost
  • and lives changed by war.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 00:45 to 01:45 (60 seconds). Say: Remembrance means taking time to remember. War can cause death, injury, fear and loss. People remember those who served and the civilians whose lives were affected. We do not need to share the same background or beliefs to recognise that human lives matter. This is a time for care and thought, not a celebration of fighting. Pause for ten seconds. Ask pupils to think quietly about the word care; no spoken answer is needed.
Slide 3 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Why this date

  • 11 November 1918
  • An agreement to stop fighting
  • took effect at 11:00.
  • 11:00
Presenter notes: Elapsed 01:45 to 03:00 (75 seconds). Say: In 1918, after years of war, an agreement to stop the fighting took effect at eleven in the morning on the eleventh of November. An agreement to stop fighting is called an armistice. Peace agreements followed later, so stopping the fighting and making a lasting peace were not the same step. The date and time help explain why people still pause on this day. Read the date slowly together in your head. Invite a quiet point to the time on screen, or allow pupils simply to watch.
Slide 4 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

War affects many lives

  • People who serve.
  • Families. Civilians. Communities.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 03:00 to 04:00 (60 seconds). Say: Some people serve in dangerous situations. Others care for injured people, support families or help communities recover. Civilians are people who are not serving as fighters, and war can affect them deeply too. Remembrance can make room for all these experiences. We will not compare whose loss matters most. Give ten seconds of quiet thinking. You do not have to imagine a frightening event; you may focus on the help people give each other.
Slide 5 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Memories can be different

  • Some memories are personal.
  • You can keep yours private.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 04:00 to 05:00 (60 seconds). Say: A person may remember someone they knew, a story they have heard, or people they will never meet. Another person may think about the wish for peace. All of us may approach this moment differently. You do not have to have a family connection to take part, and you do not have to explain your feelings. Allow fifteen seconds to choose a comfortable focus. Do not invite anyone to describe bereavement, conflict or a family experience in front of the group.
Slide 6 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Remembering near us

  • A memorial, a quiet space,
  • or an act of care can help us reflect.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 05:00 to 06:00 (60 seconds). Say: Communities sometimes remember through a memorial, a garden or a gathering. A memorial is something made to help people remember. We are not naming a particular location or claiming that we have visited one. You might notice how a shared space asks people to behave with care. Think quietly: what makes a place feel respectful? Leave ten seconds. Offer the examples of listening, moving carefully and letting others have time. No journey or visit is being arranged by this assembly.
Slide 7 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

The poppy as a symbol

  • A red poppy can stand for
  • remembrance and hope for peace.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 06:00 to 07:00 (60 seconds). Say: A symbol is something that stands for an idea. Red poppies grew on disturbed ground after fighting, and the flower became a familiar symbol of remembrance. For many people it also carries a hope for peace. The flower on this slide is a simple illustration, not a badge or an official design. Wearing a poppy is a personal choice. Respect does not depend on wearing one, buying one or having a particular object. Pause and let pupils look quietly at the shape.
Slide 8 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Choose how to reflect

  • Sit or stand comfortably.
  • Use your usual support.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 07:00 to 08:00 (60 seconds). Say: During the silence, choose a comfortable position. Your eyes can stay open. You may use your usual communication or regulation aid. If you need to move, do so as quietly as you can with support. You can ask an adult for a quiet supported space. We will not judge a person's respect by stillness, eye contact or an involuntary sound. Presenter: check the agreed support arrangements discreetly. Allow time for pupils to settle without drawing attention to individuals.
Slide 9 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Give others space

  • Listen. Avoid jokes or comments.
  • Let others reflect in their own way.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 08:00 to 08:45 (45 seconds). Say: Respect means thinking about how our choices affect someone else. Avoid jokes, comments or deliberate interruptions. Someone next to you may be remembering something important, or may just need a quiet moment. Different bodies and different support needs can look different. We can give one another space without asking questions. Leave a short pause. Presenter: use calm redirection if needed, rather than public criticism.
Slide 10 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

One quiet question

  • What do I hope
  • peace will make possible?
Presenter notes: Elapsed 08:45 to 09:30 (45 seconds). Say: Here is a question you can carry into the silence: what do I hope peace will make possible? You might think about safety, a home, friendship, learning or time with people who care for you. You can choose a different private focus, or simply rest your attention. Leave twenty seconds of quiet thought. Do not collect answers. Keep the atmosphere calm and avoid telling pupils what they must feel.
Slide 11 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Ready for the silence

  • Two minutes.
  • A quiet pause together.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 09:30 to 10:00 (30 seconds). Say: We are about to begin two minutes of silence. You can remember lives affected by war, think about people you care about, or hold a hope for peace. There is no need to speak afterwards. Presenter: check the real clock and move to the next slide at 11:00 if following the live schedule. Use a silent timer for exactly two minutes. If the assembly is at another time, call this a reflection pause and do not announce that it is eleven.
Slide 12 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Two minutes of silence

  • Remember.
  • Reflect.
  • Hope for peace.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 10:00 to 12:00 (120 seconds). Presenter action: remain on this slide for a full two minutes, measured by a quiet adult-controlled timer. For the live schedule this is 11:00 to 11:02. Do not read this note aloud. No music, countdown animation, applause or sudden ending sound is needed. Allow agreed communication and regulation supports. Respond discreetly to distress and support a quiet exit if needed. At the end, say gently: Thank you for giving others time and space to reflect. Then move to the next slide.
Slide 13 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Care after reflection

  • We can listen, include others
  • and solve disagreements peacefully.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 12:00 to 13:30 (90 seconds). Say: A pause is one way to remember. How we treat people afterwards matters too. We can listen before replying, include someone who is left out, and ask for adult help when a disagreement becomes difficult. Peaceful choices do not mean ignoring harm or staying silent about something unsafe. Think of one small action that is within your control today. Give twenty seconds. Invite a voluntary general example if appropriate, with no names or personal stories. Accept a pass and avoid turning reflection into a test.
Slide 14 of 14 · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · AssemblySMSC · Remembrance

Take one kind action

  • Remember with respect.
  • Carry care into today.
Presenter notes: Elapsed 13:30 to 15:00 (90 seconds). Say: We have remembered lives lost and changed by war, and we have made space for different private reflections. Later in tutor time you can record one thing to remember, one hope for peace and one kind action, in your usual mode. Sharing is optional. If today's topic has brought up a worry or sadness, speak privately to a trusted adult. You do not need to manage that feeling alone. Leave twenty seconds for a final thought. Thank pupils calmly, explain the next ordinary routine, and finish without asking for applause.
