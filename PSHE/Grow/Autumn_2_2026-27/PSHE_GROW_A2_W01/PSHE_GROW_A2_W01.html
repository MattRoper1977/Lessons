Guidance

Identify healthy choices and everyday risks.

Unit: Keeping safe, healthy & well

Source assessment: F: scenario sorting. S: 'my trusted network' + safe/unsafe task (Dec).

This is local formative scenario sorting. The later assessment remains separate. No completion of the unit award is claimed.

Purpose and evidence

Individual scenario judgement with clue, reason, safer next step and response after feedback.

One 40-minute introduce + explore lesson. The show-what-you-know task stays inside You do. A copied model or blank form is not completed learner evidence.

Access and judgement

Supported uses two cases read aloud. Independent uses six and explains one. Aspire uses six and identifies a missing fact that could change a judgement. Accept a different defensible answer when the reason and next step fit the facts.

Practical link

The pause, clue and adult-help routine supports decisions in shared activities and spaces. Communication evidence may include explaining a clue, listening to a reason, asking for clarification or dictating a safer step. Staff decide any portfolio mapping from actual work.

Preparation and safety

Keep all examples fictional. Do not ask pupils to describe home routines, health conditions, private messages or past harm. Use respectful language about food, bodies, disability and family circumstances. A behaviour can be unsafe even when the person is familiar. Avoid judging safety from appearance.

Offer the phrase “Can I speak to you in private?” If a concern is raised, listen calmly, avoid investigating in class and follow the setting’s safeguarding procedure. Never promise secrecy. Explain that a relevant adult may need to help. Keep safeguarding records separate from the lesson evidence. If a learner is not heard, help them reach another trusted adult.

Prepare the materials

Use the six fictional case cards, sort labels, picture prompt, private-help card, word mat and chosen Evidence Sheet. Keep a separate model set. Check the room route is clear. Staff handle cutting.

Keep actions hypothetical

No food tasting, risky action, live messages, log-ins or web searches are required. Do not ask a pupil to move electrical equipment. Adults follow local food arrangements. No individual allergy disclosure is required.

Respectful discussion

Do not assess an individual diet or health need. Avoid calorie targets, weight talk, good or bad food labels and assumptions about family resources. Use movement that suits the person. A check reduces risk without a guarantee.

TA Brief

Use the fictional cases and the usual communication aid. Invite a response, give thinking time and allow a pass. Read or scribe exact words without giving the reason.

Route and task

Supported starts with A and B read aloud. Independent sorts A to F and explains one. Aspire uses all six and names a missing fact. Keep the learner’s choice, clue, reason, safer step and response after feedback.

Prompt then fade

Ask: What is the clue? What could happen? What can the person do next? If the clue is vague, return to the words on the case. If the step adds risk, model a pause and adult check, then offer a fresh case.

Model key

A and D: Safer. B and E: Risk. C and F: Need more information. Wet-floor hinge: B, keep clear and tell an adult. Accept a different view when the reason and next step fit the facts.

Private help

Identify the agreed adult and place for private help before teaching. Listen calmly, do not investigate in class and never promise secrecy. Follow the setting’s procedure and keep those records separate from lesson evidence.

Preparation and safety

Keep all examples fictional. Do not ask pupils to describe home routines, health conditions, private messages or past harm. Use respectful language about food, bodies, disability and family circumstances. A behaviour can be unsafe even when the person is familiar. Avoid judging safety from appearance.

Offer the phrase “Can I speak to you in private?” If a concern is raised, listen calmly, avoid investigating in class and follow the setting’s safeguarding procedure. Never promise secrecy. Explain that a relevant adult may need to help. Keep safeguarding records separate from the lesson evidence. If a learner is not heard, help them reach another trusted adult.

Prepare the materials

Use the six fictional case cards, sort labels, picture prompt, private-help card, word mat and chosen Evidence Sheet. Keep a separate model set. Check the room route is clear. Staff handle cutting.

Keep actions hypothetical

No food tasting, risky action, live messages, log-ins or web searches are required. Do not ask a pupil to move electrical equipment. Adults follow local food arrangements. No individual allergy disclosure is required.

Respectful discussion

Do not assess an individual diet or health need. Avoid calorie targets, weight talk, good or bad food labels and assumptions about family resources. Use movement that suits the person. A check reduces risk without a guarantee.
