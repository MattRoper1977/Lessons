Workshop safety wording — proposed changes

For health and safety review against our own risk assessments. Nothing here has been changed yet. These are proposals only, waiting on this review.

Why What you are being asked

Four lessons in the design and technology unit carry safety wording that pupils and staff read on screen and on the printed sheet. Three changes are proposed to that wording. They came out of a content review, not from an incident.

The question for you is one thing: does the proposed wording match what our risk assessments actually require, and does anything here conflict with them or with how the workshop really runs?

If any of it is wrong for our setting, it does not go in. Wording that contradicts the risk assessment is worse than the wording we have now.

1 Dust — the order of control

Where: the finishing and sanding lesson (week 5), on the staff safety notes.

Now reads
Dust masks for sanding, outdoors where possible. Check the treatment's own PPE guidance before the session.
Proposed
Dust: prevent first (choose the method that makes less dust), extract where you can, and only then a mask — selected for the task and fitted. If visible dust is being raised that extraction is not capturing, work stops and an adult is told. Check the treatment's own PPE guidance before the session.

What changes in practice: the mask stops being the first answer and becomes the last one, after choosing a lower-dust method and using extraction. It also adds a stop point — visible dust that extraction is not catching means work stops.

2 Ventilation — who decides how

Where: the same lesson (week 5), staff safety notes.

Now reads
Ventilation is a safety requirement AND an access one — the door stays open.
Proposed
Ventilation is a safety requirement AND an access one — use the room's ventilation as the centre's arrangements require (door and windows open where that is the arrangement), and if the air is visibly dusty, work stops and an adult is told.

What changes in practice: the lesson stops prescribing one specific arrangement — "the door stays open" — and defers to whatever our arrangements actually say. This is the change most likely to need your correction, because it is the one that hands the decision to a document you own rather than to the lesson.

3 Timber — the stamp is not the whole check

Where: three lessons (weeks 1, 2 and 3), on both the pupil-facing checklist and the before-any-tool line.

Now reads
HT pallet wood only (never painted or MB)
Proposed
Accepted wood only — HT-stamped and passed every acceptance check (never painted or MB).

And on the checklist, a new heading and list: the HT stamp is one check, never the whole decision. Wood is accepted only when every check passes:
  • HT stamp — never MB
  • provenance known
  • no staining or odour
  • no paint or varnish
  • no rot or splitting
  • magnet sweep done
Reject on any one failure, even when HT-stamped.

What changes in practice: at the moment an HT stamp reads as sufficient. The proposal makes it one of six checks, any one of which can reject a board. "MB" is the chemical-treatment marking that must never be used.

Ask What would help most

  • Does the ventilation wording match what our risk assessment actually requires for this room?
  • Is the dust control order right for our workshop and our extraction?
  • Are the six timber checks the right six — anything missing, anything that does not apply here?
  • Does anything above conflict with a risk assessment, a COSHH assessment, or how the room is actually run?

A note on any line, or a "this one is wrong", is enough. It does not need to come back as finished wording.

Status: proposed, not applied. The lessons are unchanged and will stay unchanged until this review comes back. Once it does, a counter-signature may be needed depending on the answer.
