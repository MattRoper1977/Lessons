Tutor time scheme of work

Staff planning copy

Autumn 2 Weeks 2 to 7. Three strands in each week: Monday Behaviour, Wednesday KCSIE, Friday British values. The tutor slot is 09:00 to 09:30; each session runs for 15 to 20 minutes.

Follow the delivery weeks below. A session file code keeps its original week identifier even where the plan moves that session to another delivery week.

Start here

Open the gated session page for its arrival picture and colour checks, reveals, team reasoning, three book routes, pupil voice, private help and exit checks. Allow a pass, pointing, drawing or adult scribing. Check the local safeguarding route before delivery. No personal examples or public disclosures.

Every session opens from this page and runs in the browser with nothing to install. The printable packs for the nine new sessions (slides, Word resources, tagged PDFs and staff guidance) are kept in the staff planning folder.

Week 2

Behaviour

Upstanders: behaviour that includes everyone

Open session

  • Explain what bullying is: repeated, deliberate, with a power imbalance, in person or online.
  • Tell the difference between a one-off fall-out and bullying.
  • Use the upstander moves and respectful language.

Evidence: Bullying is ___, ___ and ___. One upstander move I can use is ___. A respectful thing I can say is ___.

KCSIE

Child-on-child abuse: when banter becomes harm

Open session

  • Name the steps on the ladder from banter to harm.
  • Spot when comments, online or in person, become hurtful or sexual.
  • Choose a safe upstander step and know how to report.

Evidence: Banter becomes harm when ___. One harmful example is ___. An upstander can ___ and tell ___.

British values

Democracy: reasons before a decision

Open session

  • Compare options using shared criteria.
  • Vote after hearing evidence.
  • Record a minority view and a reply route.

Week 3

Behaviour

Ready to learn: starting on my own

Open session

  • Follow an instruction the first time it is given.
  • Use the look, listen, start routine.
  • Use try, check the card, ask when stuck, and start a task within two minutes on my own.

Evidence: When I get an instruction I ___. When I am stuck I try, then ___, then ___.

KCSIE

Healthy relationships: care without control

Open session

  • Recognise care, choice and respect.
  • Spot a pattern of control.
  • Choose a safe support step for a fictional friend.

British values

Tolerance: different beliefs, equal access

Open session

  • Respect faith, different beliefs and no belief.
  • Suggest an inclusive activity choice.
  • Explain why tolerance does not mean accepting harm.

Week 4

Behaviour

Notice, name, return: getting back to calm

Open session

  • Notice body signals that a feeling is growing.
  • Name the feeling using words beside colours.
  • Choose a calm tool and return to learning at the current stage without fuss.

Evidence: One body signal is ___. I name it ___ (colour and word). A calm tool I can use is ___. I return by ___.

KCSIE

Consent: clear, free and changeable

Open session

  • Explain free and clear agreement.
  • Recognise that silence or pressure is not consent.
  • Practise a safe check and a respectful response.

British values

Mutual respect: disagree with care

Open session

  • Separate a person's worth from an idea.
  • Use a respectful disagreement script.
  • Recognise when safe adult support is needed.

Week 5

Behaviour

Phones, screens and focus

Open session

  • Explain why phones away in learning time helps focus.
  • Choose kind and safe actions in group chats, including screenshots and joining in.
  • Know what to do if something online upsets you.

Evidence: In learning time I keep my focus by ___. If a chat turns unkind, I will ___.

KCSIE

Exploitation: gifts do not create a debt

Open session

  • Recognise secrecy, debt and carrying requests.
  • Explain exploitation without blaming a child.
  • Choose a safe help route without confrontation.

British values

The rule of law: hearing both sides fairly

Open session

  • Distinguish a school rule from a law.
  • Explain a fair process using evidence.
  • Draft a fair review request.

Made by Matt

Week 6

Behaviour

Repair and restart: owning a mistake

Open session

  • Use three repair questions: what happened, who was affected, and what I can do to put it right.
  • Tell the difference between a sincere apology and a quick sorry.
  • Plan a fresh restart, with adults helping the repair.

Evidence: I can put a mistake right by ___. A sincere apology says ___.

KCSIE

Serious violence and knives: choosing safety

Open session

  • Spot the myths about carrying knives.
  • Say the law in plain words.
  • Plan how to walk away and tell, including that reporting can be anonymous.

Evidence: A myth about knives is ___. The fact is ___. A safe step is to ___ and tell ___.

British values

Individual liberty: privacy and shared choices

Open session

  • Explain a choice and its boundary.
  • Apply privacy to a shared decision.
  • Write a respectful ask-and-check script.

Made by Matt

Week 7

Behaviour

Calm and kind when routines change

Open session

  • Notice that excitement and worry about changes are both normal.
  • Choose safe, calm actions in busy times.
  • Be kind to people who find celebrations hard, and make my own calm plan.

Evidence: When routines change, I can stay calm by ___. I can be kind to someone who finds it hard by ___.

KCSIE

Extremism online: spotting the hook

Open session

  • Spot the hooks extremist groups use online.
  • Tell the difference between a strong opinion and hate.
  • Choose protective steps and know who can help.

Evidence: One hook is ___. A strong opinion is different from hate because ___. A protective step is ___.

British values

Individual liberty: asking for help is a choice

Open session

  • Connect liberty with asking for support.
  • Respect another person's privacy.
  • Choose a safe way to raise a concern.
