Title and settle

Calm and kind when routines change

Use invented examples only. You can pass or point. Do not share names or personal stories. Ask for a private word if you need support.

We will learn to

  • Notice that excitement and worry about changes are both normal.
  • Choose safe, calm actions in busy times.
  • Be kind to people who find celebrations hard, and make my own calm plan.

Books first. A short line, a drawing, or an adult-scribed answer all count.

Spark 1

Changes are coming

  1. Near the end of a busy stretch, routines can change: different rooms, special events, visitors or a louder building.
  2. Some people feel excited, some feel worried, and many feel both. All of these feelings are normal.
  3. Knowing what will change helps. You can ask an adult to show you the plan.

Made by Matt

Spark 2

Calm, safe and kind

  1. In busy times: walk, keep some space, and stay with your group or adult.
  2. Not everyone enjoys celebrations. Some people find them loud, sad or hard. Nobody should be pushed to join in or to explain why.
  3. Make a calm plan: my early signs, my calm tool, my adult.

Help

Who can I talk to?

  • Talk to a trusted adult at school, such as your tutor or a support adult.
  • Ask for the school's designated safeguarding lead. They lead the work to help keep children safe.
  • If the first adult is not available or you are not heard, try another trusted adult.
  • A safe adult outside school or a child support service can help too. Ask an adult to help you find a route.

Staff guide

18 minutes. Check the local safeguarding process before delivery. All examples are invented.

Keep a calm, non-judging tone. Allow a pass or an alternative task. Never ask for personal experiences, proof, names or a public disclosure. If a concern is raised, listen, thank the pupil, do not promise secrecy, do not investigate, record accurately using the school process, and pass it promptly to the designated safeguarding lead. Act on immediate danger through the school emergency procedure. Do not leave a pupil needing urgent help to wait until the session ends.

Use soft instrumental music for the transition in. Arrival: read the picture prompt aloud; pupils point to or tap a picture. The colour check always has words beside each colour. Follow up Amber or Red privately, never in front of the group.

Return-to-learning route: quiet spot, 2-minute reset, rejoin at the current stage.

At check-out, record E for today's session against each pupil's target on the evidence app.

Topic note: keep this faith-neutral and name no celebrations, festivals or dates. Some pupils find celebrations hard for private reasons, including loss, family change or money worries: never ask why. Share the visual plan for any changed routine in advance where you can, and agree quiet spaces before events.

The header timer counts up against the 18-minute plan; it is a staff pacing aid only.
