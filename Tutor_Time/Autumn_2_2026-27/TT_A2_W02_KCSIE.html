Title and settle

Child-on-child abuse: when banter becomes harm

Use invented examples only. You can pass or point. Do not share names or personal stories. Ask for a private word if you need support.

We will learn to

  • Name the steps on the ladder from banter to harm.
  • Spot when comments, online or in person, become hurtful or sexual.
  • Choose a safe upstander step and know how to report.

Books first. A short line, a drawing, or an adult-scribed answer all count.

Made by Matt

Spark 1

The ladder: from banter to harm

  1. 1 Banter
  2. →
  3. 2 Teasing
  4. →
  5. 3 Bullying
  6. →
  7. 4 Harassment
  8. →
  9. 5 Harm
  1. Banter to teasing: shared fun turns into one person being the joke.
  2. Teasing to bullying: it is repeated, it hurts, and the target cannot make it stop.
  3. Harassment and harm: unwanted sexual comments, put-downs about girls or women, or threats. In person or online, it is never okay.

Made by Matt

Spark 2

It is never the target’s fault

  1. Nobody asks to be bullied or harassed. Clothes, looks, a reply or laughing along do not make it their fault.
  2. Sexual comments, rating bodies and jokes that put girls or women down are harmful, even when someone says “it was only banter”.
  3. Upstander steps: do not join in, check in with the target, and tell a trusted adult. Online, do not forward it; show an adult.

You do

Book first: choose your route

Write or draw in your book first. Choose support that helps you.

Supported

Put the ladder cards in order: banter, teasing, bullying, harassment, harm. Point to the first card where you would tell an adult. You can point instead of speak.

Optional make: after your book work, make a word-and-symbol reminder card: “Not funny? Say stop. Tell an adult.” Use one short sentence.

Independent

Write how banter is different from bullying. Write one example of harassment from this session’s invented scenarios, one upstander step and one way to report it for a fictional pupil.

Optional make: after your book work, make a three-panel guide: notice, support, tell. Use an example from this session.

Aspire

Do the task. Explain why “it was only banter” does not make harm okay, and why the target is never to blame. Add how an upstander can help without putting themselves at risk.

Optional make: after your book work, make an if / then guide that shows a joke going too far, a reason, and a safe help route.

Help

Who can I talk to?

  • Talk to a trusted adult at school, such as your tutor or a support adult.
  • Ask for the school's designated safeguarding lead. They lead the work to help keep children safe.
  • If the first adult is not available or you are not heard, try another trusted adult.
  • A safe adult outside school or a child support service can help too. Ask an adult to help you find a route.
  • You can tell an adult in person, write it on paper, or bring a friend with you.

Staff guide

18 minutes. Check the local safeguarding process before delivery. All examples are invented.

Keep a calm, non-judging tone. Allow a pass or an alternative task. Never ask for personal experiences, proof, names or a public disclosure. If a concern is raised, listen, thank the pupil, do not promise secrecy, do not investigate, record accurately using the school process, and pass it promptly to the designated safeguarding lead. Act on immediate danger through the school emergency procedure. Do not leave a pupil needing urgent help to wait until the session ends.

Use invented scenarios and no pupil role-play of bullying or harassment. Do not repeat slurs or sexual comments aloud; describe them in general terms. Never suggest a target caused or invited harm, and do not dismiss harmful sexual behaviour or misogyny as banter. Plan safe private follow-up.

Return-to-learning route: quiet spot, 2-minute reset, rejoin at the current stage. Use soft instrumental music for the transition in. At check-out, record E for today's session against each pupil's target on the evidence app.
