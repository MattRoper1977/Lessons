Spark 1

Notice: body signals

  1. Body signals can be a hot face, a fast heart, tight fists, a wobbly tummy or a loud voice.
  2. Noticing early gives you more time to choose a calm tool.
  3. Everyone has body signals. They are not bad. They are information.

Spark 2

Name it: words beside colours

  1. Green · calm, ready, okay.
  2. Amber · worried, cross, wobbly or fidgety.
  3. Red · very upset or overwhelmed, need a minute. Naming it helps an adult help you.

You do

Book first: choose your route

Write or draw in your book first. Choose support that helps you.

Supported

Point to a body signal picture, a colour word card and a calm tool card. Practise three slow breaths with an adult: in for 4, out for 6.

Optional make: make a word-and-symbol card for your favourite calm tool.

Independent

In your book, draw or write one body signal, its colour and feeling word, one calm tool, and how you return to learning.

Optional make: make a three-panel guide: notice, name, return.

Aspire

Write an invented calm plan: an early signal, the name for it, two tools (one at your desk, one away from it) and the return step. Explain why returning without fuss helps learning.

Optional make: make an if / then guide: if I notice ___, then I can ___.

Help

Who can I talk to?

  • Talk to a trusted adult at school, such as your tutor or a support adult.
  • Ask for the school's designated safeguarding lead. They lead the work to help keep children safe.
  • If big feelings happen a lot or feel too much, you can ask for a private word. Adults can help you make a plan.
  • If the first adult is not available or you are not heard, try another trusted adult.
  • A safe adult outside school or a child support service can help too. Ask an adult to help you find a route.

Staff guide

15-20 minutes. Check the local safeguarding process before delivery. All examples are invented and told in the third person.

Keep a calm, non-judging tone. Allow a pass or an alternative task. Never ask for personal experiences, proof, names or a public disclosure. If a concern is raised, listen, thank the pupil, do not promise secrecy, do not investigate, record accurately using the school process, and pass it promptly to the designated safeguarding lead. Act on immediate danger through the school emergency procedure. Do not leave a pupil needing urgent help to wait until the session ends.

Focus: regulation without shame. Colours always appear with words. Model the breathing (in for 4, out for 6) slowly; let pupils keep eyes open. The quiet spot is a support, never a sanction. On return, greet briefly and point to the current stage; do not ask for an explanation in front of others.

Routines for this session

  • Play soft instrumental music for the transition in.
  • Arrival: the picture check needs no reading; read the question aloud. The colour check always has words beside colours. A red choice is met with a quiet offer, never a public question.
  • Return-to-learning route: quiet spot, 2-minute reset, rejoin at the current stage. No catch-up demand and no public explanation.
  • At check-out, record E for today's session against each pupil's target on the evidence app.
