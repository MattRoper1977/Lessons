How the trap is set

Tap each stage. It usually starts as a friendly message from a stranger.

01
The Contact
A stranger adds you — often pretending to be someone your age who's into the same stuff.
02

Fast compliments, fast trust, fast "send me something private" — sometimes within the hour.
03
The Flip
The second they have an image: "Pay or I send it to everyone you know." The charm was never real.
04

Don't pay. Stop replying. Screenshot. Tell a trusted adult — it can be stopped and taken down.

In the news · 2026

The UK's National Crime Agency has issued repeated alerts about sextortion targeting teenagers — often boys aged 14–17 — through ordinary social and gaming apps.

What happens

Organised criminal groups overseas run it like a business: friendly contact, a private image, then immediate threats demanding money. It moves fast on purpose — panic is the product.

They rely on victims feeling too ashamed to tell anyone. That's the part we break today.

5 lies the blackmailer tells

Tap each to bust it.

1"Pay and I'll delete it."
They won't. Paying proves you'll pay — the demands get bigger.
2"This is your fault."
No. You were tricked by someone who does this for a living. The crime is theirs.
3"Everyone you know will see it."
Sending it gains them nothing — the threat is the weapon, and it collapses once you tell someone.
4"You're the only one this has happened to."
Thousands of UK teens report this every year. You'd be far from alone — and far from the first to get help.
5"No one can help you now."
Wrong. Report Remove takes images down, police take these gangs seriously, and a trusted adult can act today.

What would you do?

A mate tells you, in a panic, that someone online has a private image of them and is demanding £100 by tonight. What's the right move?
A · Tell them to pay it — it's only £100 and it ends it.
⚠ Paying never ends it. It marks them as someone who pays, and the demands grow.
B · Stay with them, help them screenshot & block, and tell a trusted adult together.
✓ Right call. Evidence kept, contact cut, and an adult who can act today is in the loop. That's real backup.
C · Tell them to delete the app and pretend it never happened.
⚠ Hiding it leaves them alone with the threats. The pressure only breaks when a trusted adult knows.

Why it works — speed

0
minutes — first friendly message to first threat can take less than an hour. Panic is the whole plan.

They rush you so you don't think. So do the one thing they can't plan for: slow down and tell someone. That's resilience — and the law is on your side.

Three patterns. Same trap.

The "girl who's into you"
A fake profile flatters fast and asks for a pic "just between us" — then flips to threats the moment it lands.
The gaming-chat "mate"
Weeks of friendly chat in game lobbies builds trust first — the ask comes later, and the threat right after.
The AI shortcut
Some gangs skip the trick entirely — they fake an image with AI and threaten with that. Same trap, same answer: tell.

Every pattern has one thing in common: the victim did nothing wrong — and telling someone ends it.
