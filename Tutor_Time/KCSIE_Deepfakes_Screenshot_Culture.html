What's really happening

The 'just a joke' edit

You've probably seen edited photos in group chats — someone's face on something, passed around 'for a laugh'.

Free AI tools can now make a fake image from one ordinary clothed photo in under 25 seconds. The Internet Watch Foundation reports UK deepfake crimes are up 550% — and 98% of deepfake content online is non-consensual intimate imagery.

💬 Turn & talk: Why do people forward an edit they KNOW is fake — and what happens to the person in it?
Rumour vs fact

'It's just a joke' — vs what the law says

Here's the same edit, seen two ways:

🗣️ The group chat says…

"It's not real, so it's not serious. It's just banter. Delete it and it's gone."

⚖️ UK law actually says…

Creating a deepfake intimate image is a crime (Online Safety Act 2023) — even if you never share it. Sharing it: up to 2 years. If the person is under 18: it's a child sexual abuse image — up to 10 years.

"It's just a joke." — the judge has read that exact message many times. It is not a defence.

The part that matters

Delete ≠ deleted

One photo lives in more places than you think:

1. Your phone's cloud backup — auto-uploaded the moment you took it.

2. The app's servers — Snapchat, Insta, TikTok keep copies, even 'disappearing' ones.

3. Whoever screenshotted it — the group chat, the forwarded DM, someone's camera roll.

4. AI training data and archive sites — public accounts get scraped and crawled.

Rule of thumb: would your mum, your headteacher and a future employer all be fine seeing it? If any one is "no" — don't send it.
Looking out for each other

If you SEE an edit of someone else

Every view keeps it alive. So:

  • 🚫 Don't laugh, don't share, don't forward — don't even 'just look'.
  • 📸 Screenshot the evidence — who posted, where, username.
  • 🛡️ Report it and delete your copy.
  • 🗣️ Tell an adult — you could genuinely be protecting someone.
The person in the image is the victim. How the room reacts decides how much damage it does.
What to do

If it happens to you — 4 moves

1. Screenshot everything. Who posted it, where, their username — before they delete.

2. Report Remove. Free IWF + NSPCC tool for under-18s — gets images taken down across major platforms. Nobody at school is told.

3. CEOP. The police unit for this exact thing — ceop.police.uk. They deal with it every day.

4. Tell an adult. You are the victim. UK police will not treat you as the criminal — Outcome 21 exists to protect young people.

Remember: none of the four moves involves dealing with it alone.
Where to get help

If you've got questions or worries

It's completely okay to want honest answers without being judged.

  • 🤝 A trusted adult at school — your tutor, a teacher, or the safeguarding team.
  • 🛡️ Report Remove — free image takedown for under-18s: search "Report Remove IWF".
  • 👮 CEOP — ceop.police.uk, the police's online child protection unit.
  • 📞 Childline — 0800 1111, free and confidential, 24/7.
Worried about something already out there? That's exactly what Report Remove is for — and it works.
