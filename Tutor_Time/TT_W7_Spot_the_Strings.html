Spot the Strings — Nothing's Free

Recognising when 'kindness' has a catch

Settle

One word: what makes someone trustworthy?
MODEL zone · the spark
  • Most kindness is real. But if gifts, money or lifts come with a 'now you owe me' — that's a string.
  • Warning signs: being asked to keep secrets from safe adults, carry things, or 'prove yourself'.
  • Feeling trapped is the signal to talk. Telling a trusted adult is strength, not snitching — and you're never in trouble for it.
INDEPENDENT zone · the talk
Standard: Sort the scenario cards: 'kind' vs 'strings attached' — and say which warning sign you spotted.
Supported: Match each picture to the 'kind' heart or the 'strings' flag with an adult.
Check-out: Who would you talk to if something felt off?
Round the room, one line or one word each — passing is allowed. End on tomorrow's one-liner.
Evidence slot — pupil & TA run:
  1. Evidence Captain (rotating) takes ONE photo of today's task — work, not faces (unless consent is on file).
  2. Captain reads the caption frame aloud and the class completes it: “Today we learned to spot the strings: a warning sign is ___.”
  3. TA: file it in the school's evidence system, tag KCSIE + tutor group, tick the tracker. Done.

By Week 3 the Captain runs steps 1–2 without prompting; the TA only uploads. That's the routine.

Safeguarding note: protective, pattern-level only. If anything is disclosed, follow the school safeguarding procedure — do not investigate in the session.
