What's been happening

You might have heard about "Mazz" vapes

Over the last week there's been talk locally — on social media and in the news — about something called a "Mazz" vape.

The name is new around here. It started after a child became unwell in another county, and then rumours spread fast online.

💬 Turn & talk: Where do most rumours like this actually come from — and how quickly do they spread before anyone checks if they're true?
Rumour vs fact

So they tested it — and here's what they found

Cleveland & Durham Police got hold of samples and sent them for proper lab testing.

🗣️ The rumour said…

Mazz vapes were "spiked" with fentanyl, amphetamines and other drugs.

🔬 The test actually found…

A synthetic cannabinoid (a SPICE-type chemical, "MDMB‑4en‑PINACA"). No fentanyl or amphetamines were found in these samples.

"It's definitely full of fentanyl." — a rumour isn't a fact until someone checks. Always wait for the evidence.

The real risk

The rumour was wrong — the danger is still real

Here's the part that matters most:

1. An illegal or unregulated vape isn't checked, labelled or made safely.

2. That means you literally cannot know what's inside it — this batch was SPICE; the next could be something else.

3. SPICE can cause agitation, confusion, memory loss, being sick, and trouble breathing.

Bottom line: the safest vape is no vape — but if it's not a sealed, shop-bought, regulated product, no one can promise what's in it.
Looking out for each other

Spotting when a mate needs help

You don't need to be a doctor. If someone seems really unwell after using something, the signs to watch for are:

  • 😵‍💫 Very confused, agitated or "not themselves"
  • 🤢 Feeling sick or being sick
  • 🫁 Struggling to breathe, or breathing slowly
  • 😴 Drowsy, hard to wake, or passing out
You don't have to figure out what they took. If they seem unwell, that's enough — get help.
What to do

Three steps that keep someone safe

1. Stay with them. Don't leave someone who's unwell on their own.

2. Get an adult. Tell a member of staff straight away — or call 999 if it's serious.

3. Tell the truth. Say what you saw. Telling someone to keep a person safe is never grassing.

Remember: in a moment like that, the grown‑ups around you care about one thing first — that everyone's okay.
