Teaching script 2

I do · 5 min

Each person matters, including when a rule is broken. Fair safety limits can apply while people keep their rights. A proposal is not proof that a real policy changed.

We do · 5 min

We do decision checkpoint. All routes: Someone breaks a rule. Do they lose all rights? Choices: A. Yes. Rights are prizes for good behaviour. B. No. Everyone still has rights. C. Yes, if a group votes against them. Take each learner’s choice and clue before revealing. B. Everyone has rights. Harm can be addressed with fair safety limits while treating each person with dignity. Hinge check for all routes. A person needs a clear picture notice. Is one hard-to-read notice fair for everyone? Explain a useful next step. Ask the person what helps, then offer a clear version or agreed aid. If pupils equate fair with identical support, model two access choices and recheck. Record actual help and adapt before independent work.

Teaching script 1

Arrival · 3 min

Invite a private regulation check. Green · Ready, Amber · Help, Red · Pause. Offer a quiet start, usual aid or pass. Use the cue and accept the pupil’s usual communication mode.

Starter · 3 min

Starter question 1 revisits last week. Take one minute for recall and reveal. Last lesson, someone said no to sharing a photo. What did we do? Cue: Respect the answer · do not post. Expected: We respected the answer and did not post the photo. We could choose a different suitable image with permission. Then ask: A group accepts spoken ideas only. Someone wants to write. What could change? Expected: Offer a written note or another agreed way to give an idea. Ask the person which option helps. Start in the book. Use the reference cards.

Teaching script 2

I do · 5 min

Help people take part with respect. Find the barrier in the made-up case. Ask what would help. Offer a workable choice. Hear a reply and check whether the change helps. Adult example: a club takes ideas only by speech. A member asks to write. The organiser accepts notes as well as speech and checks whether that helps. Each person matters, including when a rule is broken. Fair safety limits can apply while people keep their rights. A proposal is not proof that a real policy changed.

We do · 5 min

We do decision checkpoint. All routes: Someone breaks a rule. Do they lose all rights? Choices: A. Yes. Rights are prizes for good behaviour. B. No. Everyone still has rights. C. Yes, if a group votes against them. Take each learner’s choice and clue before revealing. B. Everyone has rights. Harm can be addressed with fair safety limits while treating each person with dignity. Hinge check for all routes. A person needs a clear picture notice. Is one hard-to-read notice fair for everyone? Explain a useful next step. Ask the person what helps, then offer a clear version or agreed aid. If pupils equate fair with identical support, model two access choices and recheck. Record actual help and adapt before independent work.
