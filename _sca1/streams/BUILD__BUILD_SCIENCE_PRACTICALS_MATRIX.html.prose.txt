Practicals & Equipment Matrix · W3–W7
One page per half-term: what each investigation needs, how it is made safe, and the two routes that keep every pupil in the practical — a digital alternative and a no-equipment fallback. A pupil who cannot handle materials still
directs
the investigation.
Safety authority.
No CLEAPSS approval is claimed for any activity on this page. Each activity requires a local, proportionate risk assessment before use; local COSHH/risk-assessment decisions determine the actual controls, and a headteacher signature alone is not sufficient.

W3A
Creature X-Ray sort
Animal/skeleton picture cards or models; two-column sort mat; optional plastic animals; magnifier; pre-printed X-ray cards.
Reusable card; laminate to extend life.
Print and cut the card set; label the two mats.
~2–5 min
Models and diagrams only, never pupils’ bodies; no touching another person’s spine. Handling animal models is optional.
On-screen Creature scanner in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Pupil points to the group; an adult moves the card exactly as directed.
W3B
Backbone detective challenge
Less-obvious animal cards (snake, worm, snail, crab); two trays or hoops; rule card; removable arrow or marker.
Reusable card.
Print and cut the harder card set; write the rule card.
~2–5 min
Cards only; ordinary classroom handling.
On-screen Creature scanner in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Pupil chooses by pointing or saying; the adult places the card.
W4A
Muscle pair investigation
Pre-cut card upper arm and forearm; secured split pin or paper fastener; two lengths of string or yarn; tape; large angle-state card.
Reusable model; replace string and fasteners as worn.
Adult pre-cuts the card, pre-punches the holes and secures the joint. Offer a ready-built model so construction is never the Science barrier.
~2–5 min
Blunt pre-cut card and pre-secured fasteners only. Keep strings short and away from neck and face. No stretched elastic.
On-screen arm model in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Pupil operates a ready-made model, or directs an adult or peer which string to pull.
W4B
Model arm workshop
Ready-built or pupil-assembled card arm; secured split pin; two strings; labels; state cards EXTENDED / PART-BENT / BENT.
Reusable model; replace damaged card and fasteners.
Adult pre-punches and secures every fastener; prepares spare built models.
~2–5 min
As W4A: short strings, no elastic snap risk, damaged parts replaced by an adult.
On-screen arm model in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Pupil directs a peer or adult; the model moves only on the pupil’s decision.
W5A
Food jobs investigation (fictional cards)
Fictional printed food cards; three function mats: ENERGY / GROWTH & REPAIR / BODY FUNCTIONS; blank evidence arrows.
Reusable card.
Print the fictional card set and the three mats.
~2–5 min
Printed fictional resources only: no tasting, no real food, no allergy exposure, no personal-meal questions. The lesson teaches the jobs food does, never anyone’s own eating.
On-screen food jobs board in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Adult holds the cards; pupil points, says or signs the function.
W5B
Nutrition mission (fictional scenarios)
Fictional evidence envelopes or cards; function mats; before/after claim strips; pointing tokens.
Reusable card.
Print the scenario envelopes; sort one set per group.
~2–5 min
As W5A — paper only; no food, no body measurements, no personal disclosure.
On-screen food jobs board in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Fixed choice cards; an adult scribes the pupil’s exact meaning.
W6A
Plate model builder (fictional cards)
Neutral printed food picture/name cards; group trays or hoops; group labels; a “model, not a personal meal” card.
Reusable card.
Print neutral card set; label the group trays.
~2–5 min
Printed fictional resources only — no tasting, no real food or packaging, no allergy exposure. No good/bad food talk; the plate is a teaching model, never a meal plan.
On-screen plate builder in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Pupil points to a group; the adult moves the card.
W6B
Plate design challenge (fictional cards)
Neutral food cards; group trays; removable labels; before/after frame; one audience-feedback card.
Reusable card.
Print the design-brief cards and the before/after frame.
~2–5 min
As W6A — paper only, no tasting, no allergen handling, no personal meal talk.
On-screen plate builder in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Adult manipulates the model under pupil direction; the pupil keeps the scientific decision.
W7A
Food source link investigation
Organism cards; arrow cards or short string; two supplied evidence strips: leaf→caterpillar→bird and grass→rabbit→fox.
Reusable card and string.
Print organism cards and evidence strips; cut string to short table lengths.
~2–5 min
Cards and short string only; string stays on the table or floor workspace, never around bodies.
On-screen chain builder in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Large arrow cards placed by an adult at the pupil’s direction; no string handling required.
W7B
Food chain test and repair
Organism cards; worded arrow cards (“is eaten by”); supplied evidence strips; one deliberately flawed model card.
Reusable card.
Print the card set including the flawed-model card; keep the flawed card visually distinct.
~2–5 min
Cards only; short table-top string only if chosen.
On-screen chain builder in the lesson’s We Do 2 slide.
Pupil points or directs placement; the adult moves the cards.
The two access routes are part of the practical, not a concession.
Every investigation runs three ways — hands-on, digital, or directed — and all three produce the same conceptual evidence. Choosing the directed route records how the pupil accessed the investigation; it says nothing about ability.
