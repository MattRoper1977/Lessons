Backbones and No Backbones
Sorted mat + pupil proof; photo of sort paired with pupil explanation; adult observation of accurate classification; short voice clip if authorised.
W3B uses the same classification rule with less obvious creatures and asks pupils to defend decisions.
I can sort animals into those with a backbone and those without.
Backbone Detectives
Final classification display + two reasons; annotated photo; adult-observed oral defence; short authorised voice/video explanation.
W4A retrieves the skeleton/backbone as the structure muscles pull on to create movement.
I can classify unfamiliar animals using evidence about their body structure.
Muscles Work in Pairs
Labelled model + demonstration; photo of model paired with explanation; adult observation; scribed or authorised voice explanation.
W4B turns the rule into a hands-on build/test task and asks pupils to explain the swap.
I can explain that muscles work in pairs to move a bone.
Build a Working Muscle Model
Model demonstration received by adult; labelled model photo + explanation; staff observation; brief authorised clip.
W5A moves from movement to what bodies obtain from food; body/food discussion stays on models and fictional examples.
I can build or operate a model that shows how a muscle pair moves a bone.
What a Body Needs
Sorted job mat + explanation; photo + pupil voice; adult observation; scribed response; authorised short clip.
W5B turns the job idea into a practical fictional mission where pupils choose a varied toolkit for body functions.
I can name different jobs food helps a body to do and explain why variety matters.
Nutrition Mission
Pupil-selected toolkit + reasoning received by adult; before/after choice; photo + voice; staff observation.
W6A uses the same neutral card set to organise foods into groups and introduce balance.
I can use food-job information to make and explain a sensible scientific choice.
Building a Balanced Plate
Completed fictional plate + explanation; annotated photo; oral explanation received by adult; staff observation.
W6B turns the groups into a practical design-and-explain challenge with explicit re-attempt after feedback.
I can sort foods into groups and describe what balance means.
Balanced Plate Design Challenge
Before/after model improvement + pupil reason; photo + voice; adult observation; labelled display.
W7A shifts from what food does to where animals get food from; plants/animals are compared using models.
I can design, build and explain a balanced plate model using food groups.
Animals Cannot Make Their Own Food
Matched food-source strip + explanation; arrow model; adult observation; authorised short voice/video.
W7B turns the rule into a hands-on food-source chain and checks that arrows/relationships are understood.
I can explain that animals get food by eating, while plants can make their own food.
Build a Food-Source Chain
Completed chain + adult-received explanation; before/after repaired link; photo + voice; staff observation.
End-of-block read: use responses to decide whether the next Science move is transfer, re-model, access change or further evidence work.
I can build and explain a simple food-source chain showing that animals obtain food by eating.
Choose a teaching read only if it helps the next action. Nothing here is stored.
Secure → reduce support or transfer.
Mixed → contrast examples then recheck.
Misconception → stop, re-model/counterexample, then re-attempt.
Access barrier → keep the goal; change regulation/modality/presentation.
Method/evidence → repair the procedure/evidence and repeat.
 Do not turn this into a pupil label or score.
