# The proof lesson — what to look at

**BUILD Humanities, Autumn, Week 1 — "People Special to Me"**
`Humanities_Teesside/BUILD_W1-W8_2026-27/BUILD_HUM_W1_People_Special_To_Me.html`

One 40-minute lesson, nine stages, built straight from `'BUILD Weekly - Autumn'!C46`:
*"Talk about my family and people special to me."* That sentence is the whole
brief. Nothing in the deck goes beyond it.

---

## What it actually teaches

A pupil can usually name someone special. What they skip is the **reason**. So
the lesson teaches one thing: a three-link chain — **who → what they do → why
they are special** — and it insists on the third link.

The worked example is the lollipop person: *stops the traffic → so I can cross
safely*. Then the deliberately incomplete one: *stands at the crossing* — true,
but it stops at the middle box and never says why it matters. That contrast is
the lesson.

**No pupil is asked to disclose anything.** Every task can be done about the
supplied example person instead of anyone real, and nobody is asked to explain
which they chose. That sentence is in the deck twice and on the worksheet.

## The two diagrams, and why they are there

1. **Three rings** — Me / People at home / People who help me. It gives the
   Supported route somewhere to *put* a person without needing to write a
   sentence.
2. **The three-link chain** — three boxes and two arrows. It exists so the
   third box is visibly the one being missed, rather than a thing an adult
   keeps saying.

Both are labelled inline SVG, no image files, and both are referred to by name
in the prose of the stage they sit in. Nothing decorative was added.

## The "We Do" type: commit-and-reveal

Everyone writes the middle box on a whiteboard, then all reveal at once, then
we agree a reason together. Chosen because the failure mode here is social —
one confident pupil answers and the rest agree with them. Simultaneous reveal
takes that away. It is declared in the deck's config as `commit-and-reveal`,
and the gate now checks the declaration against the stage text rather than just
against the list of allowed words.

## Load

**1,922 pupil-facing words.** Family floor 1,555, family median 1,564 — so
×1.23 of a typical BUILD Humanities lesson, about **21 of the 40 minutes** at
the 90 wpm rate the contract assumes. Under the 1.5× ceiling with room to
spare. It should not feel rushed.

---

## Three things to look at in the room

1. **The reveal.** Does everybody actually commit before anybody reveals, or
   does the room still wait for the confident one? If it does, the whiteboards
   are going up too slowly, not the idea failing.
2. **The third box.** Count how many pupils write an action in the *why*
   column. If most do, the chain diagram is not doing its job and the middle
   and last boxes need to look more different from each other.
3. **The worksheet's blank rows.** There are six, at 44px. Too many is
   disheartening, too few and the fast finishers stall. Tell me which way it
   went and I will set the number for weeks 2–8 from that, not from a guess.

---

## One thing I want your ruling on

**The reading level is deliberately below the family band.**

The pack this chassis came from measures Flesch–Kincaid **10.57–12.50**. This
lesson measures **5.60**. That is not an accident and it is not drift: BUILD is
the Entry Level pathway, the outcome is *"Talk about my family"*, and the
sentences are short on purpose.

I could have hit the band by lengthening sentences and using longer words. I
did not, because that would have made the lesson worse for the pupils it is
for, and the band was measured on a *different pack* (W9–W14, Tees industrial
history) rather than set as a rule.

**So: is FK ~5.6 right for BUILD Autumn 1, or do you want it nearer the
family band?** Weeks 2–8 will follow whatever you say. If you say the band,
say it and I will re-pitch this one too — but I would not do that on my own.
