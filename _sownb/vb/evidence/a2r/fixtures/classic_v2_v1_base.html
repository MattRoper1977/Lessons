Arrival Task

Arrival Task – What we know about then and now

You never have to talk about something that happened to you. The scenes on the cards are made up for this lesson. Nobody is asked about their own week, and nothing you say leaves this room.
You can stop for a bit. You can point instead of speak. You can move the cards instead of writing. You can tell an adult what to write. Each one is a full way to do this lesson.
Order means putting things in a line, first to last.

Some things are old. Some things are new. Some things are in between.

You can often tell how old a thing is from what it is made of.

Wood and iron are old. Plastic is newer.

A thing can be new and still do an old job. A kettle is a kettle.

Order means a line. Oldest at one end, newest at the other.

Fair and not fair can be put in a line too. That is the second half of today.

Arrival decision: Pick an object card from the box, or use the two on the table. Both are full answers. You can change your mind later.

On your table there are four object cards. A washing board. A hand wringer. An early machine with a handle. A machine with buttons. They all do the same job. They were made at very different times. Everyone in this room can work with these cards from start to end.

Look at one card now. What is it made of? Point to the part that tells you. That is all you need to do to begin.

Offer the object cards first, not as a fallback. Keep the fairness cards face down until the second half.

Starter

Starter: Two lines of the same four things

Here are two answers about the same four object cards.

Answer A: board, wringer, handle machine, button machine.

Answer B: button machine, handle machine, wringer, board.

Both lines have all four cards. Only one is in order from oldest to newest. The other is the same line backwards.

The need for this lesson is saying which end is which. An order with no reason is just a line. Today every order comes with a reason.

Try it in your head: which is older, a thing you turn with your hand or a thing with buttons? Now you have started the order already.

A reason can be what it is made of. It can be how it works. Both are good reasons.

Two people can put the middle two cards in a different order and both have a reason. We keep both until we look closer.

Do not accept a line with no reason, and do not call a reversed line wrong. Ask which end is oldest; that is the missing part.

I Do

I Do: How to build an order you can explain

The picture shows the four objects in a line with an arrow underneath. The arrow runs from oldest to newest.

Under each object is the reason. Wood. Wood and iron. Metal. Plastic. The materials go in an order too, and that is what makes the line hold.

Notice what is not the reason. Not which one looks nicest. Not which one you would rather use. Those are true, but they do not put things in time order.

Watch me do it: I take the board and the button machine. What is the board made of? Wood. What is the machine made of? Plastic. Wood is older than plastic. So the board goes first. I say the material, then I place the card.

Now the harder pair: the wringer and the handle machine. Both have metal. So material does not settle it. I look at how they work instead. One is turned by hand only. One has a handle that drives a drum. The simpler one comes first.

So an order has two reasons behind it. What it is made of, and how it works. When one runs out, you use the other.

Compare means looking at two things to see how they are different.
Say the reason before placing each card. A learner who orders by size has used a real reason that does not track time; name that rather than moving the card.

Card 2: the hand wringer. What is it made of, and how does it work?

Order first: put your four cards in order along the table, oldest on the left. Everyone commits at the same time, and then we hold up the first card together.

Evidence check

The middle two cards are the hard ones. If two tables put them in a different order and both gave a reason, both stay. We ask for the reason, not for the line.

After the order, one person from each table says one reason out loud. We are listening for a material or for how it works.

Changing your line after you hear someone else is allowed. Move the card and say what changed it.

Have every table finish the line before any answer is taken. A card moved after the check is a success, not a correction.

I Do

I Do: Ranking two ways of treating a person

The second picture is a line too. It runs from more fair to less fair. Same idea as the objects, different thing being ordered.

The reason sits on the arrow. For fairness, the reason is nearly always the same question: who gets left out?

You do not have to say what is always right. You say which of these two is more fair, and why.

Ranking one together: a card says everybody in the game gets a turn. Another says only the quickest get a turn. Which is more fair? Ask the question: who is left out? That gives you the order.

That question works on every fairness card in the box, and it is the one to write down.

Keep the scenes on the cards. If a learner brings a real event, receive it briefly, thank them, and return to the card without exploring it.

◆ Supported

Point to the card you think is more fair.

▲ Standard

Rank the two cards and say who is left out.

★ Stretch

Rank the two cards, then say what would make the second one fairer.

We rank one pair together first. Nobody writes yet. Everyone points to their more fair card at the same time, and we hold up the choices together.

The pair for this one is the two turn taking cards. Your job is the reason. Who is left out on the card you put second?

When we hold up, we may not all agree, and that is allowed. We keep every answer that names somebody who is left out.

Then we say the whole thing together: this card is more fair, because on the other one somebody never gets a turn.

Take the reason from a learner who has not spoken yet today. Scribe exactly what is said; do not improve the wording.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket — Audience receives; influence changes something

Read one reason to a partner or an adult. The listener says back which card came first and why.

Did the listener put your line the other way round? Then change one word in your reason. That change is your influence. It belongs to you, not to them.

Did the listener follow your order first time? Write that down too. A reason that already works is worth noting.

Keep your record. The object line will come back when we look at how work changed.

Nothing goes outside this room unless you say so. Your record stays in your folder.

Close by naming one learner who changed a reason after their partner read the line the other way. That is the influence record, and it belongs to the learner.
