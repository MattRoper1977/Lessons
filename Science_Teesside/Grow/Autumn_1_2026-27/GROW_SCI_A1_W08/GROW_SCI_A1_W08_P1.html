Check five Moon statements

Choose true or false for each. Repair one false statement in your book.

1. The Moon reflects sunlight.

True: the light reaching us is reflected sunlight.

2. The Moon glows by itself.

False: it reflects sunlight rather than making its own visible light.

False: phases come from our changing view of the lit half; Earth’s shadow matters during a lunar eclipse.

Answers and teaching cues

Hinge

Our changing view of the sunlit half causes ordinary phases. Earth’s shadow matters during a lunar eclipse.

Model steps and limits

  • Torch is the Sun, ball is the Moon, observer is Earth. Keep the sunlight direction fixed.
  • Half the Moon is illuminated by the Sun. As it orbits, our view of the illuminated half changes.
  • When the visible lit half faces us, we see full Moon. When it faces away, we see new Moon.
  • Trace the light path: Sun → Moon → observer on Earth. Name the Sun as light source and the Moon as reflecting object.
  • The Moon orbits Earth in about 27.3 days, roughly a month. The phase cycle is about 29.5 days.
  • Sketch the view beside each physical model position. Mark the sunlight direction.
  • A lunar eclipse occurs when Earth blocks sunlight from reaching the Moon; the tilted orbit means this is not monthly.
  • The model does not show realistic sizes, distances or the tilt of the Moon’s orbit.

Activity answers

  • Crescent: A crescent is our view of part of the sunlit half.
  • Full Moon: Full Moon is an ordinary phase; it does not require Earth’s shadow.
  • New Moon: At new Moon the visible side is largely unlit.
  • Dark red Moon: A lunar eclipse involves Earth’s shadow.
  • Own light: Moonlight is reflected sunlight.
  • Every month: The Moon’s tilted orbit prevents an eclipse every month.

Misconception repair

A scientific claim needs relevant evidence, a reason and a fair limit.

Moon statement answers

  • The Moon reflects sunlight. True: the light reaching us is reflected sunlight.
  • The Moon glows by itself. False: it reflects sunlight rather than making its own visible light.
  • Ordinary phases are Earth’s shadow. False: phases come from our changing view of the lit half; Earth’s shadow matters during a lunar eclipse.
  • The Moon orbits Earth in about a month. True: about 27.3 days for one orbit; the phase cycle is about 29.5 days.
  • The Moon is only visible at night. False: the Moon can also be visible in daylight.

Arrival answer cues

Supported

  • 1. Full Moon: the visible disc is illuminated.
  • 2. Planets orbit the Sun.
  • 3. Sunlight reflected from the Moon.
  • 4. Point to the visible part of the illuminated half.

Independent

  • 1. Useful: motion around a centre. Limit: scale or hand-driven motion.
  • 2. Gravity.
  • 3. Sunlight reaches the Moon and reflects towards the observer.
  • 4. The Moon orbits Earth, changing our view of its illuminated half.

Aspire

  • 1. An adult moving a model supplies the motion; it is not a measurement of gravity.
  • 2. Orbit is travel around another body; rotation is turning on an axis.
  • 3. Sunlight illuminates the Moon; reflected light travels towards the observer.
  • 4. A phase changes our view of the lit half; a lunar eclipse blocks sunlight with Earth.

Answers and teaching cues

Hinge

Our changing view of the sunlit half causes ordinary phases. Earth’s shadow matters during a lunar eclipse.

Model steps and limits

  • Torch is the Sun, ball is the Moon, observer is Earth. Keep the sunlight direction fixed.
  • Half the Moon is illuminated by the Sun. As it orbits, our view of the illuminated half changes.
  • When the visible lit half faces us, we see full Moon. When it faces away, we see new Moon.
  • Trace the light path: Sun → Moon → observer on Earth. Name the Sun as light source and the Moon as reflecting object.
  • The Moon orbits Earth in about 27.3 days, roughly a month. The phase cycle is about 29.5 days.
  • Sketch the view beside each physical model position. Mark the sunlight direction.
  • A lunar eclipse occurs when Earth blocks sunlight from reaching the Moon; the tilted orbit means this is not monthly.
  • The model does not show realistic sizes, distances or the tilt of the Moon’s orbit.

Activity answers

  • Crescent: A crescent is our view of part of the sunlit half.
  • Full Moon: Full Moon is an ordinary phase; it does not require Earth’s shadow.
  • New Moon: At new Moon the visible side is largely unlit.
  • Dark red Moon: A lunar eclipse involves Earth’s shadow.
  • Own light: Moonlight is reflected sunlight.
  • Every month: The Moon’s tilted orbit prevents an eclipse every month.

Misconception repair

A scientific claim needs relevant evidence, a reason and a fair limit.

Moon statement answers

  • The Moon reflects sunlight. True: the light reaching us is reflected sunlight.
  • The Moon glows by itself. False: it reflects sunlight rather than making its own visible light.
  • Ordinary phases are Earth’s shadow. False: phases come from our changing view of the lit half; Earth’s shadow matters during a lunar eclipse.
  • The Moon orbits Earth in about a month. True: about 27.3 days for one orbit; the phase cycle is about 29.5 days.
  • The Moon is only visible at night. False: the Moon can also be visible in daylight.

Supported

Independent
