GROW · Science

Follow the warming chain

Autumn 2 · Week 4 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can link a cause of global warming to an effect and a useful action.

Success criteria

  • Trace sunlight and outgoing infrared.
  • Link extra greenhouse gas to warming.
  • Choose an action and explain how it reduces impact.

Use Next to begin. Ask for word help or your usual support when needed.

Arrival · 2 min

Energy and time

Three checks, then a link.

What gives Earth energy?

Does a warm surface give out energy?

Is one cold day the same as a long climate record?

How could gases change the energy leaving Earth?

Teaching diagram; not to scale.

Starter · 3 min

Cold today, warmer climate?

A coat is needed today. What evidence could show a climate trend?

Compare the time covered by each record.

Choose the one that can show a long pattern.

Keep today’s weather as a real observation.

A

Today’s temperature in one town.

B

Many years of measurements from across the world.

Which record is more useful for a global climate trend?

I do · 5 min

Trace energy, not a lid

Earth’s surface warms in sunlight.

The warm surface sends out infrared.

Greenhouse gases absorb some of it.

They send infrared out in all directions.

Some returns towards the surface. Some still leaves to space.

Simplified energy routes. Arrow width and gas-dot count are not measured amounts.

We do · 6 min

Change one part of the chain

Compare the three scenario cards.

Choose a scenario.

Read what changes and what does not.

Choose the energy explanation.

Choose a likely effect, then check.

1 · Cause

Burning coal, oil and gas adds carbon dioxide.

2 · Energy route

Extra greenhouse gas absorbs and re-emits more outgoing infrared. At first, less energy escapes overall.

3 · Warming

Energy builds up. The global average temperature rises until energy flows balance again.

4 · Effects

Warming can melt land ice and increase the risk of heat extremes.

Paper or spoken route

Use the printed energy diagram and four cause-chain cards. Order the cards, explain each link, then use independent Record A to propose evidence for a reduced-emissions action.

I do · 4 min

More gas changes the balance

Burning fossil fuels adds carbon dioxide.

More greenhouse gas changes outgoing energy flow.

At first, less energy escapes overall.

Earth’s average temperature rises.

It warms until energy in and out balance again.

Extra dots mark increased greenhouse gas in a simplified model. No quantity or timescale is predicted.

We do · 5 min

Action needs a cause link

A centre changes its heating controls.

Avoid heating rooms that are empty.

Check that used rooms stay suitable.

Using less fossil fuel can reduce added carbon dioxide.

That helps limit further warming.

Action with a link

Use less unnecessary fossil-fuel energy → less added carbon dioxide.

Claim to repair

One switched-off light removes all greenhouse gas.

Which claim is careful?

Independent · 12 min

Use three new records

Explain the science behind each record.

A: explain how the plan could reduce impact. Name evidence needed.

B: link warming, land ice and sea level.

C: repair the cold-day claim.

Include one energy link in your explanation.

Record A · Heating plan

A fictional centre leaves heating on in empty rooms. Staff propose a timer and temperature checks. This is a plan, not measured results.

Record B · Land ice

Warming increases melting of glaciers and ice sheets. Water from melting land ice adds to the ocean. Adapted factual explanation from the Met Office.

Record C · Cold Tuesday

One town has a cold Tuesday. A pupil says: “Global warming has stopped.” This is a fictional claim to check.

Supported

Use A and B first. Order cause → more gas → energy change → warming → effect. Speak one action link.

Use one card at a time. Say it, point, or ask an adult to write your exact words.

Standard

Explain A, B and C. Include the energy change, an effect and evidence needed for the action.

Use the evidence first. Try your last answer without a sentence frame.

Stretch

Explain why lower added emissions do not mean instant global cooling. Plan the evidence needed to compare a predicted saving with a later measured outcome.

Explain what the evidence can and cannot show.

Say the chain

Choose one cause, effect and action.

Join them with “because”.

Include the energy change.

State one thing the model cannot predict.

  • Describe causes and effects of global warming and how to reduce our impact.

Current Autumn 2 Week 4; absolute Week 12. The operative workbook cell determines this placement; some source filenames use older week labels.

Prior: Recall that the Sun provides energy to Earth. Retrieve weather versus climate without claiming this specific class has already mastered it.

Next: Use the causal explanation in the paired climate-action lesson. Compare a proposed action with actual before/after evidence if collected later.

Preparation

  • Print energy diagram and independent records. No personal carbon audit or live climate dashboard is needed.

Care and safety

  • Calm, solution-aware discussion. No disaster imagery or compulsory family travel, income or energy disclosure. Real heating settings remain an adult responsibility.

Teacher response guidance

  • A: less wasted fossil-fuel energy can reduce added CO2; check measured energy use with occupancy/weather accounted for. No saving is supplied.
  • B: warming increases land-ice melt; added water contributes to sea-level rise.
  • C: one local cold day cannot overturn a long global trend.
  • Model lacks measured fluxes, feedback detail and timescales.

Evidence of learning

Look for a cause–energy–temperature–effect explanation and a cause-linked action. Do not accept “pollution makes it hot” as the complete mechanism.

Access and independence

Read each short instruction aloud if needed. Let the pupil point, speak or direct a scribe. Fade the prompt before the fresh independent task.

Lundy Loop

Receive the pupil’s actual response. Say back the Science they showed. Ask one useful question and let them revise or explain why their first choice still fits. Do not award completion from button presses alone.

Arrival · Energy and time

Recap Sun; yes; no. Introduce the lead-in as a question, not assumed prior mastery.

Starter · Cold today, warmer climate?

No invented temperature graph. The lesson checks the kind of evidence before mechanism.

I do · Trace energy, not a lid

Do not draw a glass dome or impermeable blanket. Arrows show routes, not amounts; gases do not create energy. The natural greenhouse effect helps keep Earth warm enough for life.

We do · Change one part of the chain

This is a qualitative model with no temperature prediction. The action scenario reduces additional emissions, not existing CO2 instantly.

I do · More gas changes the balance

Hold incoming sunlight unchanged within this simple comparison. Avoid “heat trapped forever”. Natural and enhanced effect are linked, not treated as entirely different mechanisms.

We do · Action needs a cause link

Adult building staff decide suitable settings; pupils do not control real equipment. Discuss shared and systemic action, not family blame. Actual savings depend on the building and energy source.

Independent · Use three new records

All records supplied; school plan and Tuesday claim clearly fictional. Do not infer measured savings. Record B addresses land ice rather than confusing floating sea ice with the direct sea-level contribution.

Exit · Say the chain

Assessment needs the causal energy link, not only an action slogan. Receive response and ask a question that lets pupil repair a missing mechanism.

Misconceptions

Gases make a solid lid. Gases absorb and re-emit infrared. Energy still leaves.

Every place is hot every day. Global average changes coexist with variable weather.

Less emission means instant cooling. An action can reduce additional warming pressure; response takes time and depends on wider emissions.

Sources

Met Office · Greenhouse gases
Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation; the natural effect differs from increased greenhouse gases.

Met Office · Causes of climate change
Burning fossil fuels adds carbon dioxide; reducing emissions addresses the human cause of recent warming.

Met Office · Effects of climate change
Warming affects heat extremes, land ice and sea level; climate risk is not a guarantee for every day or place.
