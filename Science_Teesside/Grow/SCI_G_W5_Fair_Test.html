GROW · SCIENCE · WEEK 5 · LESSON A · 2 MINUTES

Planning a Fair Test

Learning objective

I can plan a fair comparison and use repeated measurements to answer a question.

Success looks like

  • I can identify what to change, keep and measure.
  • I can record stopping distances with a unit.
  • I can use repeats and discuss a method limitation.

Can the surface change a trolley’s stopping distance? Design the comparison before reading the results, then keep unusual evidence rather than hiding it.

Two separate lessons

A · Explore and explain · 40 minutes

B · Plan, Repeat, Check and Conclude · 40 minutes

Stop after Lesson A. Lesson B has its own start and exit.

Point, speak, sign, move cards, draw, write or direct an adult. Choose one route; no extra participation form.

Watch the model, then explain

Predict, model, explain

I will change ____, keep ____ and ____ the same, and measure ____ in ____.

Make a prediction. Change one view, then use a label to explain what you notice.

Changing both the surface and ramp height mixes two possible causes.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

In this controlled comparison deliberately change one factor while holding relevant others constant. Not every scientific enquiry is a fair test. Repeats show variation but do not prove accuracy or repair a confounded method. Stopping distance needs a consistent zero and reference point. Keep unusual results; note known mistakes and label retries. Example data are invented, not measurements made in class.

Full text / no-video alternative

00–08 seconds · Find the confound
Changing both the surface and ramp height mixes two possible causes.

08–16 seconds · Repair the method
Keep the trolley, ramp and release the same. Only the run-out surface changes.

16–24 seconds · Place the zero
Measure in centimetres from the fixed ramp-foot mark to the same trolley point.

24–32 seconds · Keep the repeats
Record all trials. An unusual result is a reason to check the method, not silently delete evidence.

Investigate / workshop

GROW · SCIENCE · WEEK 5 · LESSON B · 17 MINUTES

Investigate, test and explain

Adult secures a low ramp, one trolley, two level run-out surfaces, measuring tape and a padded stop. Agree a clear release signal and keep hands out of the run. Release without pushing. Use the supplied data-analysis route when practical testing is unsuitable.

7 min: spend up to four minutes on arrival; then choose surfaces, complete CHANGE / KEEP / MEASURE and check setup.
17 min: aim for three releases per surface. Keep the same trolley, ramp height and release mark; measure from the same zero in cm.
2 min: compare the repeats. Mark a concern without deleting a value because it differs.
6 min: retry a method issue if possible; keep the original and retry. Write, say or show a conclusion and clear the kit.

Knowledge organiser

GROW Science · Week 5 · Both lessons · Shared resource

Learning objective

I can plan a fair comparison and use repeated measurements to answer a question.

Key words

variable — A factor that can vary in an investigation.

change / independent — The factor deliberately changed.

measure / dependent — The outcome measured.

keep / control — A relevant factor kept the same.

repeat — Another trial using the same planned conditions.

mean — The total of values divided by their number.

Remember

  • Question: does the run-out surface affect the trolley’s stopping distance?
  • Change the run-out surface; keep trolley, ramp height, release point and measuring method the same.
  • Measure stopping distance in centimetres, from the same zero to the same trolley reference point.
  • Repeat results help reveal variation; they do not fix an unfair method.

Success looks like

  • I can identify what to change, keep and measure.
  • I can record stopping distances with a unit.
  • I can use repeats and discuss a method limitation.

I will change ____, keep ____ and ____ the same, and measure ____ in ____.

Staff · feedback and access

GROW Science · Week 5 · Both lessons · Shared resource

Audience and response

The lesson teacher is the named adult audience. Hear or read the pupil’s own science response. A click, downloaded file or completed box is not evidence that a request was heard.

Respond within the existing task

Offer reading help, fewer cards at once, clearer labels, an adult-directed model or additional thinking time. Say what can and cannot change, then check whether the adjustment helped.

No additional pupil artefact

A response can be spoken, pointed, signed, drawn or written. Do not require a separate Lundy record, signature or written conversion of an oral response.

An optional pupil-owned note such as “What I said, and what it changed” may be used in existing work. It is not a requirement. If scribing, keep the pupil’s own words; the adult is an audience, not a verifier.

Keep assessment honest

Use current school recording practice and unit criteria separately. This pack does not certify an award. Do not infer a policy code from automated checking or a download.
