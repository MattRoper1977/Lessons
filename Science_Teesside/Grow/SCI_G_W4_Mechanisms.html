GROW · SCIENCE · WEEK 4 · LESSON A · 2 MINUTES

Levers, Pulleys and Gears

Learning objective

I can explain how mechanisms change a force or movement.

Success looks like

  • I can identify a lever, fixed pulley and pair of gears.
  • I can label effort, pivot and load on a lever.
  • I can describe a benefit and a trade-off using evidence.

Change where you push, or how movement is passed on. Compare three mechanisms and test two lever arrangements without changing the load.

Two separate lessons

A · Explore and explain · 40 minutes

B · Two Lever Positions, One Load · 40 minutes

Stop after Lesson A. Lesson B has its own start and exit.

Point, speak, sign, move cards, draw, write or direct an adult. Choose one route; no extra participation form.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

A longer effort arm can reduce required effort for the same load, but effort moves further for the same load rise. A single fixed pulley mainly changes force direction; it does not ideally halve the force. Two directly meshing gears turn in opposite directions; gears linked by a chain are different. No mechanism creates extra energy. Numerical force ratios, if used, are ideal model predictions rather than classroom measurements.

Read the card clues

A rigid bar turns around a pivot.: This describes a lever.

A seesaw turns around its support.: A seesaw is a lever with a central pivot.

Scissors have handles and blades turning at a pivot.: Scissors combine two levers; the blades also cut.

A flagpole rope passes over a fixed wheel.: Pulling down can raise the flag.

One fixed pulley changes the direction of a pull.: Its ideal force advantage is one; practical friction adds resistance.

Two toothed wheels touch and turn oppositely.: Directly meshing gears transfer opposite turning directions.

A small driver turns a larger meshing gear more slowly.: The larger driven gear turns more slowly and can provide greater turning effect in an ideal setup.

A mechanism creates extra energy.: Mechanisms transfer energy; they do not create it.

Answer guidance

Answer: Yes.

Standard · 4 questions

1. Three labels

Name the lever’s input force, turning point and object moved.

Help beside the question
Effort, ____, ____.

Answer guidance

Answer: Effort, pivot, load.

2. Control

Name two things to keep the same.

Help beside the question
Use the investigation plan.

Answer guidance

Answer: Load, effort point and/or target load rise.

3. Gain

What is the possible force benefit of the nearer-load pivot?

Help beside the question
Use the longer effort arm.

Answer guidance

Answer: Less required effort.

4. Cost

For the same load rise, what can happen to hand travel?

Help beside the question
Think longer arm.

Answer guidance

Answer: It increases.

Stretch · 4 questions

1. Test design

Why keep the load rise the same when comparing travel?

Help beside the question
Otherwise two things change.

Answer guidance

Answer: Different rises would confound the comparison of hand travel.

2. Evidence type

Is “felt easier” a force measurement in newtons?

Help beside the question
A force meter gives numerical values.

Answer guidance

Answer: No; it is a qualitative observation.

3. Model limit

Why might the real result differ from an ideal lever?

Help beside the question
Think friction or flexing.

Answer guidance

Answer: Friction, flexing or unstable contacts may change the effort.

4. Honesty

The lever sticks once. What should the record say?

Help beside the question
Keep the observation and identify the method concern.

Answer guidance

Answer: Record the sticking, check the apparatus and label a retry separately.

Investigate, test and explain

Adult prepares a stable card/ruler lever with a broad secured pivot and light secured load. Use small controlled lifts, never launch objects. Keep fingers clear of pivots and gears. Use diagrams or adult operation where equipment is unsuitable; never lift a person or heavy object.

4 min: identify the two marked pivot positions and the same light load. Choose observer, operator or diagram route.
20 min: compare middle and nearer-load pivots. Keep load, effort point and load rise the same. Record effort as easier/similar/harder and compare hand travel.
2 min: check the controls. A subjective effort judgement is not a measured force value.
6 min: repeat or refine one explanation; identify a gain and a cost. Return the secured kit safely.

Check one piece of evidence

Check that the load, effort point and target load height are unchanged before comparing effort or hand travel.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

With the pivot nearer the load and effort point fixed, the effort arm is longer relative to the load arm. The ideal lever therefore requires less effort but more effort-point travel for the same load rise. Actual apparatus can bind or give unclear sensations; record “similar” or uncertainty honestly.

Knowledge organiser

GROW Science · Week 4 · Both lessons · Shared resource

Learning objective

I can explain how mechanisms change a force or movement.

Key words

lever — A rigid bar turning around a pivot.

pivot — The turning point of a lever.

effort — The force applied to operate a mechanism.

load — The object or resistance moved.

pulley — A grooved wheel used with a rope.

gear — A toothed wheel that can transfer turning movement.

Remember

  • A lever turns around a pivot. Effort and load act at particular points.
  • Putting the pivot nearer the load can give a longer effort arm and reduce effort.
  • For the same load rise, this lever advantage costs more hand travel.
  • A fixed pulley changes the pulling direction; directly meshing gears turn oppositely.

Success looks like

  • I can identify a lever, fixed pulley and pair of gears.
  • I can label effort, pivot and load on a lever.
  • I can describe a benefit and a trade-off using evidence.

With the pivot nearer the load, the effort arm is ___. For the same load rise, the hand moves ___.

Staff · feedback and access

GROW Science · Week 4 · Both lessons · Shared resource

Audience and response

The lesson teacher is the named adult audience. Hear or read the pupil’s own science response. A click, downloaded file or completed box is not evidence that a request was heard.

Respond within the existing task

Offer reading help, fewer cards at once, clearer labels, an adult-directed model or additional thinking time. Say what can and cannot change, then check whether the adjustment helped.

No additional pupil artefact

A response can be spoken, pointed, signed, drawn or written. Do not require a separate Lundy record, signature or written conversion of an oral response.

An optional pupil-owned note such as “What I said, and what it changed” may be used in existing work. It is not a requirement. If scribing, keep the pupil’s own words; the adult is an audience, not a verifier.

Keep assessment honest

Use current school recording practice and unit criteria separately. This pack does not certify an award. Do not infer a policy code from automated checking or a download.
