## If the task demand becomes too much

**Likely flashpoint in this task:** a practical separation step is uncertain. This is a planning cue, not a prediction about a pupil.

**Match, then lead:** Adult, quietly: “This step has a lot in it. We can pause. When you're ready, we will pause and point to the approved step.” Then offer one structured choice: “Would you rather pause and point to the approved step or ask the teacher to check equipment?” Both routes return to the same learning. Give time; do not repeat a long explanation.

**Private correction:** “Stop this step until the teacher checks the method.” Say it out of the group's hearing; show the visual and let the pupil try. A brief neutral boundary still applies: “We need to keep the task and people safe; we can pause at the agreed place.”

**Regulate first, process later:** Notice the change in pace, posture or participation. Name plainly what is visible (“Your hands have stopped” / “Your body looks ready to move”) if emotion words do not help. Validate calmly without an audience (“That feels hard right now”). State the short boundary. Use the agreed signal and supervised place. Problem-solve only once regulated, even if that is the next day; no long talk during escalation.

**Re-entry:** Point to the last completed step. Offer one small choice: “Would you like to point to the next part or make the next mark?” Re-establish the first–then cue, and step back as independence returns. The adult does not create a difficulty to collect evidence.

## Following lesson: rehearse and close the loop

At a calm key-adult check-in, ask: “What was the first body or task signal that told you a pause might help?” “Which of our two routes helped you start again?” “What is the first small step you would try next time?” Accept the pupil's usual response mode and rehearse the signal/route briefly. If an NS+ next step was actually given, listen to the pupil's response and engage with it before marking **R**; a next-step instruction alone is not R. Repair privately if needed, with no forced public account.
