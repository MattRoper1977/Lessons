W1 SCI_GROW_SU1_W01

  1. Q1. What happens when pollen reaches a stigma? ✓ Pollination.↳ Dispersal.Reveals: Confuses pollen transfer with seed movement.
  2. Q2. What must follow successful pollen transfer before a seed can develop? ✓ Fertilisation.↳ A fully grown seed instantly.Reveals: Collapses pollination and fertilisation.
  3. Q3. Which plant structure can become a seed after fertilisation? ✓ The ovule.↳ The root.Reveals: Mislocates seed formation.
  4. Q4. When does seed dispersal occur? ✓ After a seed has formed.↳ Before pollen transfer.Reveals: Reverses reproductive stages.
  5. Q5. A bee carries pollen. Has it made a mature seed already? ✓ No; fertilisation and development may follow.↳ Yes; pollen transfer is the whole process.Reveals: Treats pollination as completed seed formation.
  6. Q6. Does every pollen transfer guarantee a viable seed? ✓ No; later processes and conditions matter.↳ Yes; transfer alone guarantees it.Reveals: Overstates a single stage as a guaranteed outcome.

W2 SCI_GROW_SU1_W02

  1. Q1. A seed forms after gametes join. Which process is this? ✓ Sexual reproduction.↳ Asexual reproduction.Reveals: Ignores gamete fusion.
  2. Q2. A strawberry runner grows a new plant without gamete fusion. Which process? ✓ Asexual reproduction.↳ Sexual reproduction.Reveals: Treats all new plants as seed reproduction.
  3. Q3. A potato tuber sprouts a new plant. Is a seed required for this route? ✓ No.↳ Yes, every new plant needs a seed.Reveals: Misses vegetative reproduction.
  4. Q4. What distinguishes a runner from a fertilised ovule? ✓ A runner forms a plant without fertilisation; the ovule follows fertilisation.↳ Both require pollen.Reveals: Collapses sexual and asexual mechanisms.
  5. Q5. Do all plants produced by gardeners come from seeds? ✓ No; runners and tubers can also form plants.↳ Yes; every plant starts from a new seed.Reveals: Overgeneralises the seed route.
  6. Q6. Why may offspring from runners resemble the parent closely? ✓ The route does not mix gametes from two parents.↳ Because wind always selects identical seeds.Reveals: Misattributes similarity to dispersal.

W3 SCI_GROW_SU1_W03

  1. Q1. Which seed feature suggests wind dispersal? ✓ A light parachute-like structure.↳ Hooks for fur.Reveals: Mismatches form and dispersal mechanism.
  2. Q2. How could a hooked seed travel? ✓ Attach to animal fur.↳ Float only in a stream.Reveals: Mismatches hooks and transport.
  3. Q3. Which clue supports water dispersal? ✓ The seed floats and moves in water.↳ A dry pod bursts.Reveals: Confuses water transport with self dispersal.
  4. Q4. What might a bursting dry pod do? ✓ Throw seeds away from the parent.↳ Pollinate a flower directly.Reveals: Confuses dispersal with pollination.
  5. Q5. A model seed flies farther with a fan. What changed? ✓ Air movement.↳ The seed germinated.Reveals: Confuses dispersal and germination.
  6. Q6. Can one model flight distance predict every real seed journey? ✓ No; repeat fairly and state the model limit.↳ Yes; the distance is universal.Reveals: Overgeneralises from a model.

W4 SCI_GROW_SU1_W04

  1. Q1. Ice becomes liquid water. What change is this? ✓ Melting.↳ Dissolving.Reveals: Confuses state change and mixing.
  2. Q2. Liquid water becomes ice. What change is this? ✓ Freezing.↳ Burning.Reveals: Confuses reversible state change with reaction.
  3. Q3. Sugar becomes invisible in water. Has it vanished? ✓ No; it is dissolved.↳ Yes; it has been destroyed.Reveals: Mistakes dissolving for disappearance.
  4. Q4. Which example can be reversed by a change of state? ✓ Ice ↔ liquid water.↳ Paper burned to ash.Reveals: Treats a chemical change as state change.
  5. Q5. How might dissolved salt be recovered? ✓ Remove the water by teacher-approved evaporation.↳ Filter the solution with ordinary paper only.Reveals: Thinks filtration catches dissolved particles.
  6. Q6. Does every colour change prove a new substance formed? ✓ No; more evidence is needed.↳ Yes; colour alone proves a reaction.Reveals: Overinterprets a single observation.

W5 SCI_GROW_SU1_W05

  1. Q1. How can insoluble sand be separated from water? ✓ Filtration.↳ Cooling into ice.Reveals: Chooses a method unrelated to insoluble solid separation.
  2. Q2. Will ordinary filter paper trap dissolved salt? ✓ No; dissolved salt passes through.↳ Yes; all solids are trapped.Reveals: Confuses dissolved and insoluble solids.
  3. Q3. How could salt be recovered from its solution? ✓ Teacher-controlled evaporation.↳ More identical filtering.Reveals: Repeats an unsuitable method.
  4. Q4. What can reverse the melting of wax? ✓ Cooling it.↳ Filtering the liquid wax.Reveals: Does not match method to state change.
  5. Q5. Why do sand and salt need different recovery methods? ✓ Sand is insoluble; salt is dissolved.↳ Both are invisible in water.Reveals: Does not distinguish mixture states.
  6. Q6. Should pupils heat a solution without the teacher practical controls? ✓ No; use the teacher-approved procedure.↳ Yes; any heating is safe.Reveals: Ignores practical controls.

W6 SCI_GROW_SU1_W06

  1. Q1. What happens when water freezes then melts? ✓ A reversible state change.↳ A new substance forms.Reveals: Mistakes state change for reaction.
  2. Q2. What does rusting form from iron? ✓ A new material: rust.↳ Only liquid iron.Reveals: Confuses reaction with melting.
  3. Q3. What remains after paper burns? ✓ New materials such as ash and gases.↳ The original paper unchanged.Reveals: Ignores evidence of a chemical change.
  4. Q4. Will warming a rusty gate simply turn rust back into iron? ✓ No.↳ Yes, like melting ice.Reveals: Treats rusting as a reversible state change.
  5. Q5. Can brown colour alone prove an object is rusting? ✓ No; consider material and other evidence.↳ Yes; all brown marks are rust.Reveals: Overclaims from colour alone.
  6. Q6. Should a burning example be performed as an unsupervised pupil test? ✓ No; discuss the example only.↳ Yes; flame is needed to understand it.Reveals: Assumes a hazardous practical is necessary.
