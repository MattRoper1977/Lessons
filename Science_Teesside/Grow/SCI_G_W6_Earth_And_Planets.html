Earth and the Planets

Learning objective

I can order the planets and explain an orbit using motion, gravity and evidence.

Success looks like

  • I can order the eight planets from the Sun.
  • I can group rocky planets, gas giants and ice giants.
  • I can distinguish the direction of motion from gravity and compare year lengths.

Eight planets, different journeys. Build the order, compare years and use two different arrows to explain an orbit.

Two separate lessons

A · Explore and explain · 40 minutes

B · Build and Challenge an Orbit Model · 40 minutes

Stop after Lesson A. Lesson B has its own start and exit.

Point, speak, sign, move cards, draw, write or direct an adult. Choose one route; no extra participation form.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

Planets orbit the Sun in elliptical paths; many are close to circular. The motion arrow is not an outward force. Gravity acts inward and changes velocity direction. In the no-gravity thought experiment an otherwise undisturbed planet continues along a tangent, not radially away. All diagrams omit accurate size, distance and speed scale; hand-moving a counter is not an experiment measuring gravity.

Earth is third of eight planets. The Sun is the star they orbit. Sizes and spacing here are not to scale.

Gravity keeps changing the direction of motion. This circular diagram simplifies the real orbit.

With no gravity or other force, motion would continue along a straight tangent, not directly out from the Sun.

Check one piece of evidence

Check one inward gravity arrow and one along-path motion arrow. Then support a year comparison with the supplied numbers.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

Mars takes longer than Earth: about 687 versus 365 Earth days. Neptune’s year is longer than Jupiter’s: about 60,190 versus 4,333 days. Gravity points towards the Sun; instantaneous motion is along the tangent. The counter model shows directions and relationships, not measured gravity or accurate scale.

Knowledge organiser

GROW Science · Week 6 · Both lessons · Shared resource

Learning objective

I can order the planets and explain an orbit using motion, gravity and evidence.

Key words

orbit — A curved path around another object due to gravity and motion.

gravity — An attractive force between masses.

year — Time for one complete orbit around the Sun.

rotate — Spin about an axis.

rocky planet — A planet with a solid rocky surface.

giant planet — A large outer planet, classed as gas or ice giant.

Remember

  • Order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
  • Mercury to Mars are rocky; Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants; Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.
  • The Sun is a star, not one of the eight planets. Gravity curves planetary motion into an orbit.
  • A year is one orbit; a day relates to rotation. Farther-out planets in this system have longer years.

Success looks like

  • I can order the eight planets from the Sun.
  • I can group rocky planets, gas giants and ice giants.
  • I can distinguish the direction of motion from gravity and compare year lengths.

The planet moves ___. Gravity pulls ___. One year means ___.

Staff · feedback and access

GROW Science · Week 6 · Both lessons · Shared resource

Audience and response

The lesson teacher is the named adult audience. Hear or read the pupil’s own science response. A click, downloaded file or completed box is not evidence that a request was heard.

Respond within the existing task

Offer reading help, fewer cards at once, clearer labels, an adult-directed model or additional thinking time. Say what can and cannot change, then check whether the adjustment helped.

No additional pupil artefact

A response can be spoken, pointed, signed, drawn or written. Do not require a separate Lundy record, signature or written conversion of an oral response.

An optional pupil-owned note such as “What I said, and what it changed” may be used in existing work. It is not a requirement. If scribing, keep the pupil’s own words; the adult is an audience, not a verifier.

Keep assessment honest

Use current school recording practice and unit criteria separately. This pack does not certify an award. Do not infer a policy code from automated checking or a download.
