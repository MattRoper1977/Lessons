The Moon’s Journey

Learning objective

I can explain Moon phases using sunlight, orbit and our viewpoint.

Success looks like

  • I can explain that moonlight is reflected sunlight.
  • I can match new, quarter and full Moon views to a model.
  • I can distinguish ordinary phases from a lunar eclipse.

The Moon stays round. The view of its sunlit part changes. Control a model, compare different views and challenge the shadow misconception.

Two separate lessons

A · Explore and explain · 40 minutes

B · Test a Phase-wheel Model · 40 minutes

Stop after Lesson A. Lesson B has its own start and exit.

GROW · SCIENCE · WEEK 7 · LESSON A · 7 MINUTES

Watch the model, then explain

Predict, model, explain

The Moon appears ___ because from Earth we see ___ of its sunlit half.

Make a prediction. Change one view, then use a label to explain what you notice.

The sunlit half faces mostly away from Earth. We see almost none of that bright half.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

Outside eclipses the Sun lights approximately half the Moon. Ordinary phases result from changing viewing geometry as the Moon orbits Earth, not Earth’s shadow. A lunar eclipse involves Earth’s shadow and special alignment; the Moon’s orbit is tilted. An orbit relative to the stars takes about 27.3 days; the phase cycle is about 29.5 days. Disc orientation is schematic, not a prediction of today’s sky or a universal horizon orientation.

Local explainer · 32 seconds · silent with captions

Use instead of part of the demonstration. It never autoplays and needs no internet.

Full text / no-video alternative

00–08 seconds · New Moon
The sunlit half faces mostly away from Earth. We see almost none of that bright half.

08–16 seconds · First quarter
We see about half the disc lit. The Moon is still a sphere.

16–24 seconds · Full Moon
The Earth-facing disc looks lit. This does not mean the entire Moon is lit.

24–32 seconds · Last quarter
The visible lit part decreases after full Moon. The phase cycle returns to new after about 29.5 days.

GROW · SCIENCE · WEEK 7 · LESSON B · 20 MINUTES

Investigate, test and explain

Use paper diagrams or an adult-operated ball and low-power torch. Ask before changing light levels; normal room lighting is acceptable and no blackout is needed. Never shine into eyes, use lasers or look at the Sun. Keep the ball out of the observer’s head-shadow; an adult can operate everything.

4 min: use the arrival and lit-side rule. Choose a pre-cut wheel, drawn strip or directing the model.
20 min: compare new, quarter and full views using a fixed light direction. Keep the observer position consistent and the ball out of head-shadow. The still-diagram route shows the same ideas.
2 min: check one view. Distinguish the ball’s fixed shape from its changing visible lit part.
6 min: repair a label, explain the model and one limit, then restore the lighting and clear the kit.

Key words

orbit — The Moon’s path around Earth.

Answers · A · Standard

GROW Science · Week 7 · Lesson A · Standard

Four-question arrival

1. Reflection: Sunlight reflected from its surface.

2. Phase: The visible portion of the sunlit half from Earth.

3. Quarter: Yes.

4. Shadow: No.

Two-check exit

1. Phase cause: Its orbit changes how much of the sunlit half we see from Earth.

2. Shadow: No; a lunar eclipse involves Earth’s shadow.

Staff · feedback and access

GROW Science · Week 7 · Both lessons · Shared resource

Audience and response

The lesson teacher is the named adult audience. Hear or read the pupil’s own science response. A click, downloaded file or completed box is not evidence that a request was heard.

Respond within the existing task

Offer reading help, fewer cards at once, clearer labels, an adult-directed model or additional thinking time. Say what can and cannot change, then check whether the adjustment helped.

No additional pupil artefact

A response can be spoken, pointed, signed, drawn or written. Do not require a separate Lundy record, signature or written conversion of an oral response.

An optional pupil-owned note such as “What I said, and what it changed” may be used in existing work. It is not a requirement. If scribing, keep the pupil’s own words; the adult is an audience, not a verifier.

Keep assessment honest

Use current school recording practice and unit criteria separately. This pack does not certify an award. Do not infer a policy code from automated checking or a download.
