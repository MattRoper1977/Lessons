The courier has lost the route

Learning intention

Trace blood through the heart, lungs and body and explain the heart’s pumping job.

A blood token must visit lungs and body.

Can you rescue its route without skipping the heart?

Success looks like

  • Trace body→right heart→lungs→left heart→body.
  • Explain that arteries carry blood away and veins return it.
  • Correct a reversed arrow or oxygen-colour misconception.

1 • Shared evidence: the rescue route

The cards describe a simplified circuit. It is a route map, not an anatomical picture.

Route A

Right heart → lungs. Blood is oxygen-poor on the way to the lungs.

Route B

Lungs → left heart. Blood has gained oxygen in the lungs.

Route C

Left heart → body. Blood delivers oxygen to tissues.

Route D

Body → right heart. Returning blood is oxygen-poor, not oxygen-free.

Vessel key

Artery = away from heart. Vein = towards heart. Capillaries allow exchange with tissues.

Pump and colour key

Heart muscle contracts; valves help prevent backflow. Human blood is red. Blue lines in models mean oxygen-poor.

TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Trace blood through the heart, lungs and body and explain the heart’s pumping job.

Preparation

  • Print selected routes.
  • Explain that right/left refer to the person’s heart; the route map is not an anatomical front view.

Method precautions

  • Use desk tokens; no pulse measurements, exercise, bodily samples or physical restraint.
  • Do not infer a pupil’s health from participation or prior knowledge.

Practical alternative

Desk route rescue uses supplied labels and arrows; no practical physiological measurements.

Access and regulation

Supported: Use numbered route words and direction choices.

Standard: Trace both loops and justify artery/vein names.

Stretch: Repair a faulty full route and evaluate model limitations.

Regulation: Offer quiet pointing, private writing, drawing or adult scribing. Pass-and-return for public questions. Use fictional cases; no personal medical disclosure or comparison of bodies. Assign response routes from current evidence, not fixed pupil labels.

Teacher answers

R1–R3

R1/R2 blood. R3 oxygen, nutrients or water are acceptable. Oxygen enters blood in the lungs.

W1–W3

W1 A→B→C→D→A; oxygen enters in lungs. W2 lungs→left heart→body; blood is red, and veins are defined by flow towards the heart. W3 pulmonary artery, because flow is away from the heart; oxygen-poor blue is a diagram convention.

Hinge A–C

A incorrect: left heart must come before body. B correct. C incorrect: right heart receives blood from body and sends it to lungs.

S1–S6

S1 right heart→lungs→left heart. S2 lungs. S3 artery. S4 muscles/blood/backward. S5 blue means oxygen-poor; human blood is red. S6 complete directed loop: right heart→lungs→left heart→body→right heart. Accept any spatial arrangement with correct arrows.

N1–N4

N1 correct loop; oxygen gain at lungs. N2 artery/vein/artery/vein; away versus towards heart. N3 muscle contractions pump blood; valves resist backward flow. N4 blue labels oxygen-poor blood on this diagram; it is not actual blue blood.

T1–T4

T1 right heart→lungs→left heart→body→right heart. T2 pulmonary artery goes away from right heart towards lungs and carries oxygen-poor blood. T3 muscle contraction supplies force; valves help make flow one-way. T4 limits include missing four chambers, capillary exchange detail, vessel branching, actual shapes and relative sizes. A justified useful addition such as capillaries showing oxygen exchange is sufficient.

E1–E4 / reflection

E1 left side of heart. E2 carries blood away from heart. E3 contracting heart muscle. E4 oxygen-poor; blood is red. Reflection needs no particular personal response.

Assessment and re-teach

Secure: complete route with both heart sides, direction-based vessel definition and pumping-muscle explanation. Re-teach skipped-heart errors with one token traced twice through labelled heart sides. Re-teach blue-blood or all-arteries-oxygen-rich errors by comparing the pulmonary artery with the colour key.

Word help

heart
A muscular organ that pumps blood.
artery
A blood vessel carrying blood away from the heart.
vein
A blood vessel carrying blood towards the heart.
capillary
A very small vessel where substances pass between blood and tissues.
oxygen-rich
Containing more oxygen than the returning oxygen-poor blood.
valve
A structure that helps prevent backward flow.
