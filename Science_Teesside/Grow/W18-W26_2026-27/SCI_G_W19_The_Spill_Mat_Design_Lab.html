TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Plan a fair material comparison, record water-retention results and justify a product choice.

Preparation

  • Print the shared page. The brief and product costs are fictional teaching values, not market prices.
  • If using the optional practical, prepare sample sizes, test that each can be handled wet, and practise drying/resetting and taring the weighing tray before every measurement. Weigh wet samples promptly after the fixed drip time.
  • Use enough water to immerse each sample fully. Replace or top up consistently; no sample should be partly dry because the tray runs low.
  • Set out separate wet and dry areas and provide paper towels for spills. If the scale resolution or available time is unsuitable, use the complete supplied evidence route.

Method precautions

  • Use only small amounts of room-temperature tap water and clean prepared samples. No tasting, detergents, heating or unknown liquids.
  • Keep the battery-powered scale on a stable dry area. Carry wet samples over the water tray; wipe spills promptly.
  • Use blunt tongs and prepared squares; teacher handles cutting. Avoid splashing and follow the school’s normal eye-protection arrangements if required.
  • Never place the test mat by electrical equipment or use it as real spill-safety equipment. The product brief is a classroom model.

Practical alternative

The default route audits a test plan and analyses supplied constructed measurements. Optional groups collect a small actual comparison only with pre-set apparatus; record actual results separately.

Access and regulation

Supported: Use page 2 with explicit change/measure/keep choices and a subtraction frame. Read numerical values aloud; use calculator, pointing or dictated explanations.

Standard: Use page 3 to specify a method, calculate three first-trial retained masses, interpret repeat evidence and justify the cheapest product that meets the requirement.

Stretch: Use the Stretch route to calculate means, compare the every-trial requirement with an average, and redesign the test for a different question. Same fair-test and evidence objective.

Regulation: Offer dry investigator, timer, recorder or paper analyst roles. No forced water handling. Keep the sequence visible and allow a short planned pause. Assess science separately from handwriting and group confidence.

Teacher answers

R1–R3

R1 absorbent means takes liquid into its structure. R2 keep soak time the same; change product type. R3 no: the wet mass includes the dry product, so subtract dry mass to find retained water.

W1

Repair equal sample area at 10 cm × 10 cm, one layer, fixed 30 s full immersion, fixed 10 s vertical drip without squeezing, dry mass before and wet mass after, same water conditions and scale. Use fresh dry samples for repeats. Two specific relevant fixes establish initial fair-test understanding.

W2 / We do interpretation

Paper retained 5.2, 5.5, 5.3 g: fails 6 g requirement. Cotton retained 7.0, 6.8, 7.2 g: passes every observed trial. Felt retained 8.0, 7.8, 8.2 g: passes. Cotton is cheapest passing product at 4 credits, versus felt 6 credits. Felt holds more, but maximum absorption is not this brief’s only criterion.

Hinge A–D

A incorrect because area changes the comparison. B correct: equal-area dry samples with a fixed method compare these products. C incorrect because wetness at the start changes; use fresh dry samples. D incorrect because squeezing changes retention and introduces inconsistent treatment.

S1–S4

S1 product type. S2 retained water mass in g. S3 any two: area, soak time, drip time, one layer, water conditions, scale/handling; not the final result. S4 paper 6.0−0.8=5.2 g; cotton 9.0−2.0=7.0 g; felt 10.5−2.5=8.0 g.

S5

Cotton; 6; felt. Stronger explanation mentions its lowest observed result is 6.8 g, all three results meet the threshold, and 4 credits is less than 6 credits. Do not choose paper simply because it is cheapest: it fails the performance requirement.

N1–N2

N1 change product; measure water retained after fixed soak/drip method; control area, layer count, immersion time, drip time, handling, water conditions and scale. Three valid controls sufficient. N2 measure fresh dry mass, fully immerse 30 s, drip vertically 10 s without squeezing, measure wet mass, subtract dry mass, repeat using three fresh dry pieces per product.

N3

Paper T1/T2/T3: 5.2/5.5/5.3 g. Cotton: 7.0/6.8/7.2 g. Felt: 8.0/7.8/8.2 g. Example cotton trial 2: 8.8−2.0=6.8 g. Units and correct subtraction matter more than unaided arithmetic.

N4–N5

N4 cotton: minimum 6.8 g exceeds 6 g in these trials, costs 4 credits versus felt 6. N5 these are three trials of particular products, equal area but differing thickness/structure; other mats or criteria may differ. Next test could compare more repeats, repeated-use integrity, drying time or wet strength using matched procedures. No claims about slip or safety have been tested.

T1

Retained masses as N3. Means: paper (5.2+5.5+5.3)/3=5.333… g, about 5.3 g; cotton 21.0/3=7.0 g; felt 24.0/3=8.0 g. Accept sensible consistent rounding.

T2

The extra set averages (5+7+9)/3=7 g, but the 5 g trial fails the 6 g requirement. Thus an average above 6 g cannot establish every observed trial meets 6 g. Even three passing trials do not guarantee all future trials pass.

T3–T4

T3 cotton is lowest-cost passing product. Uncertainty: small sample, product variation, handling, balance precision. Improve with more matched fresh samples and fixed timing/handling; report variation. T4 the test compares equal-area, one-layer pieces of those products for this insert. Retained water per gram describes mass efficiency: using means, paper about 5.33/0.8≈6.7 g/g; cotton 7/2=3.5 g/g; felt 8/2.5=3.2 g/g. It is a different question and may rank products differently; it does not replace the stated same-area threshold brief.

T5

Accept a safe measurable second property. Example reusability: after equal soak/drip/dry cycles, record whether each same-size sample remains intact using a defined criterion; control cycle count and handling, use repeats. Or drying time: after the same saturation/drip protocol weigh at fixed intervals under the same air conditions until a stated mass criterion is reached. Teacher reviews feasibility before any follow-on practical.

E1–E4 / actual record

E1 product type; mass of water retained in g. E2 any two relevant controls with reason, e.g. equal area because larger pieces may hold more. E3 cotton, all trials at least 6 g, minimum 6.8 g, cheapest passing cost 4 credits. E4 a specific improvement linked to evidence; record tears/drops and justified retests openly. Actual results have no fixed answer: assess accurate recording and valid interpretation. Reflection accepts any evidence-linked choice.

Assessment / re-teach

Secure objective: identifies changed factor and measured outcome; specifies at least two meaningful controls; subtracts dry mass correctly with support if needed; records units; makes a brief-matched evidence decision. If choosing heaviest wet sample, revisit the dry-versus-wet worked example. If choosing felt only because greatest retention, reread lowest cost that meets every trial. If claiming all felt is best, ask which products and sizes were actually tested.
