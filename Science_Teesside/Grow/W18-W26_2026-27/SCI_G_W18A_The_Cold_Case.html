GROW · SCIENCE · Lesson 1 of 11

Can a jacket keep something cold?

Learning intention

Use evidence to explain thermal conduction and select insulation for a cold container.

A cold drink must arrive cool.

Your job: choose a jacket and defend it with evidence.

Today: notice → explain → test the claim.

Success looks like

  • Show the direction of energy transfer between warmer and cooler objects.
  • Explain why a thermal conductor transfers energy more readily than an insulator.
  • Choose an insulation jacket using two numbers and identify one test limitation.

Slide 1

TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Use evidence to explain thermal conduction and select insulation for a cold container.

Preparation

  • Print the common evidence page for every pair and choose response pages from the retrieval check.
  • If using the optional touch comparison, leave both safe samples in the same room well in advance and measure them before teaching.
  • Keep the constructed data visible. These are teaching examples, not results collected by this class.

Method precautions

  • For the optional demonstration, use smooth, clean samples without sharp edges or splinters.
  • Touch is optional; pupils may observe temperature readings instead. Do not touch heated or chilled objects.
  • Use the complete paper route if safe sample preparation or supervision is unavailable.

Practical alternative

The default route uses supplied diagrams and constructed evidence. It teaches the complete objective without handling hot or cold materials.

Access and regulation

Supported: Use page 2 with arrow choices, the two most relevant table rows and a sentence frame. Adult reads the same science aloud. Pointing, drawing or dictated answers are valid.

Standard: Use page 3 to calculate temperature rises and justify a design decision using comparative evidence.

Stretch: Use page 4 to evaluate an unfair comparison and transfer the explanation to a warm container. Same thermal objective, deeper reasoning.

Regulation: Offer silent think time, a seated evidence role, optional partner talk and a short pause. No speed contest or public reading requirement. Pupils may change route after the hinge.

Teacher answers

R1–R3

R1: steel/stainless steel or another suitable metal. R2: 20 °C. R3: a thermometer. Re-teach reading temperature values if needed.

W1 / C1

Reject C1. Both samples measured 20 °C. The warmer hand transfers energy to the metal faster, so feeling alone does not establish lower temperature.

W1 / C2

Keep the bounded claim C2: more ice melted in the metal set-up in the same time (9 g versus 3 g), consistent with faster net energy transfer into the ice in this set-up. It does not isolate conductivity alone; tray masses differed and other energy routes exist.

W1 / C3

Improve C3: the foam jacket slowed warming most in this test. It does not make cold and the test does not prove superiority under all conditions.

W2

Foam: final 10 °C and rise 2 °C, versus no jacket 14 °C and rise 6 °C. Useful checks include repeated trials; matched thickness if comparing material properties; matched jacket fit; a longer journey test.

Hinge A–D

A incorrect: a passive insulator makes no cold. B correct: net transfer is into the colder bottle and is slower with insulation. C incorrect: it does not stop transfer forever. D incorrect: direction is warmer room to cooler bottle. Use the cold-container diagram if A, C or D persist.

S1–S3

S1 room. S2 arrow from ROOM (20 °C, warmer) towards BOTTLE (8 °C, cooler). S3 foam at 10 °C.

S4–S6

S4 foam; 10; 14; slows. S5 the warmer hand loses energy faster to metal although both blocks begin at the same temperature. S6 repeat the test, to check whether the pattern happens again. Colour alone does not establish this performance.

N1–N2

N1 room to bottle. N2 none: 14−8=6 °C; cotton: 12−8=4 °C; foam: 10−8=2 °C. Foam warmed 4 °C less than no jacket over the same interval.

N3–N5

N3 recommend this foam sleeve: it slowed energy transfer in from the warmer room, with only a 2 °C rise compared with 6 °C unwrapped. N4 both are 20 °C; metal conducts energy away from the warmer hand faster. N5 one set of results lacks repeat confidence; repeat each test. Thickness was not matched; match it to compare material properties. Check and report fit carefully too. Accept other specific, relevant control improvements.

T1–T2

T1 rises 6, 4 and 2 °C. Recommend foam for this brief; energy enters from the warmer room, more slowly with the jacket. T2 no: thickness changed as well as material. Compare equal-thickness, equal-coverage jackets with identical bottles, starting values and conditions; repeat. This tests those equal-thickness samples, not every possible design.

T3–T4

T3 arrow from 40 °C contents to 20 °C room; insulation slows energy loss so the contents cool more slowly. No physical hot-water method is requested. T4 the evidence compares full tray set-ups, not conductivity alone. Tray masses differed; use matching dimensions, control initial temperatures and contact, document masses and repeat. Even then, heat capacity and other transfer routes require care before attributing the effect solely to conductivity.

E1–E4

E1 room to bottle. E2 slows energy transfer by conduction; accept a correctly explained broader slows heat transfer. E3 foam 10 °C or 2 °C rise; stronger evidence compares 14 °C or 6 °C unwrapped. E4 a testable suggestion such as same material at two thicknesses, repeated trials, or a longer fixed duration. Reflection has no prescribed opinion; assess connection to evidence.

Assessment / re-teach

Secure objective: correct warmer-to-cooler arrow, explains an insulator slows transfer, and numerical recommendation bounded to this test. A correct choice without an explanation is emerging evidence. For cold moves or jackets make heat, narrate the two-temperature diagram, then ask a new cold-lunchbox example. For overclaiming, underline in this test and ask for one missing control.

Word help

thermal
Related to heating and temperature.
conduction
Energy transfer through a material, or between touching materials, because of a temperature difference.
thermal conductor
A material that transfers energy by conduction readily.
thermal insulator
A material that transfers energy by conduction less readily.
evidence
Observations or measurements that help us judge a claim.
