TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Use a checked low-voltage circuit to compare electrical conductors and insulators.

Preparation

  • Teacher checks complete kit, cell condition, matching lamp rating and all leads before the lesson. Use the school-approved low-voltage kit and local procedure.
  • Prepare a known copper test strip with good contact surfaces. Check it immediately before and after the sample sequence.
  • Prepare samples and pupil copies in advance. Use the full paper route if any practical kit is unavailable. Do not substitute loose LEDs without the correct circuit.

Method precautions

  • Use only the single 1.5 V cell and matching school lamp circuit described; never use mains sockets, plugs or electrical appliances.
  • Keep the lamp in series. Never join cell terminals directly with wire or a metal sample; this can heat the cell and leads.
  • Switch off before changing samples. If any component becomes warm, disconnect safely and tell the teacher.
  • Use dry prepared solid samples only. Do not test bodies, liquids or unknown objects; do not dismantle cables or scrape coatings.
  • Keep components out of mouths; teacher manages damaged cells and sharp or broken materials.

Practical alternative

The complete paper fault-clinic uses supplied constructed observations and circuit diagrams. It assesses interpretation and design without handling electrical equipment.

Access and regulation

Supported: Use page 2: trace the loop, classify three provided observations and complete one cable sentence. Read or scribe as required.

Standard: Use page 3: interpret the whole result table, diagnose a failed control and justify both cable layers.

Stretch: Use the Stretch route: evaluate lamp sensitivity and a graphite counterexample, then qualify the electrical-property claim. Same objective, more demanding evidence.

Regulation: Pairs may choose checker, recorder or observer roles. Provide a seated paper route and quiet independent response. Never require touching or handling for participation; no timed wiring contest.

Teacher answers

R1–R3

R1 cell provides the electrical supply, lamp is the light-emitting component, wires make connections. R2 no: there must be a complete loop through both cell terminals and the lamp. R3 no: thermal and electrical conductivity are distinct properties, investigated differently.

W1 / cases A–E

A and E give conductor evidence: bare copper/aluminium light the lamp with controls and contacts valid. B supports classification as a poor conductor/insulator under this low-voltage test: dry plastic gives no visible glow with passing controls. C is uncertain because the known-conductor control fails. D is uncertain for the metal core because clips touch coating; the test concerns the contacted surface.

W2

C: teacher disconnects, checks cell, lamp, leads and switch, restores passing copper check, then repeats the sample and control. D: teacher supplies a safe prepared sample with sound contact to exposed intended material, or records uncertain. Do not scrape/cut samples, add cells or bypass the lamp.

We do table

Copper and bare aluminium provide conductor evidence; dry card and rigid plastic are poor conductors/insulators in this tested setup. No visible glow does not demonstrate zero current or electrical safety at other voltages. These observations are not a quantitative ranking of metals.

Hinge A–D

A wrong: the failed copper check means the plastic cannot yet be blamed. B correct: check circuit and repeat with passing controls. C wrong: investigate equipment/contact rather than claiming copper became an insulator. D wrong: do not add cells; keep the stated safe kit and refer to teacher.

S1–S2

S1 cell, lamp and sample joined in a loop through both cell terminals; an air gap interrupts it and prevents a visible lamp current in this kit. S2 A lights/conductor; B no glow/insulator evidence in this test; C no glow/uncertain because control failed.

S3–S5

S3 copper control; circuit. S4 copper inner path because charge can move readily; plastic outer cover because it resists charge movement and reduces unwanted contact. S5 only that this lamp did not visibly light; very small current may be below the lamp indication threshold.

N1

Copper A: lit, conductor, valid controls. Plastic B: no visible glow, insulator evidence in this test, valid controls and contacts. Foil C: no visible glow, uncertain, failed control. Coated metal D: no glow, uncertain for metal, clips touch paint. Aluminium E: lit, conductor, valid controls.

N2–N3

N2 with switch off use known copper and then switch on to confirm kit works; switch off, connect the prepared dry sample with sound contact and observe; switch off and restore copper, then verify the control still works. Accept equivalent sequence including no bypass of lamp. N3 diagram labels conducting inner copper/aluminium and insulating outer plastic; explain current path and reduced unwanted contact. No real cable dismantling.

N4–N5

N4 low-voltage no-glow results do not establish no current under all conditions. Higher voltages and different conditions can be hazardous; never test mains equipment or bodies. N5 no: visible lamp observations are qualitative, not a measured conductivity ratio. Quantitative comparison needs suitable measurements and controlled sample dimensions and conditions.

T1–T2

T1 reject only metals: graphite is a non-metal and the meter detects a current. T2 the small current may be below that needed for visible lamp glow. A dark lamp reports no visible light in this circuit, not mathematically zero current. Do not increase class voltage to seek a response.

T3–T4

T3 same cell, lamp, leads, sample length/contact spacing and safe contact quality; change sample, check known copper before and after, switch off for changes, record observations and repeat. Limitation: lamp threshold and sample geometry mean qualitative evidence, not exact intrinsic conductivity. T4 C has failed equipment/control validity; D lacks valid contact with the intended metal core.

T5

Conducting inner copper or aluminium enables the current path; insulating outer material helps isolate it. These class observations cannot certify a product for mains wiring, durability or all voltages, nor prove zero leakage current.

E1–E4 / optional record

E1 a connected conducting path is needed around this circuit for a lamp current. E2 establishes the kit worked immediately before and after sample testing; failures require rechecking. E3 copper/aluminium conducting interior; suitable plastic insulating exterior, with functions. E4 relevant improvements: check contacts, repeat with controls, separate observation and inference. Optional actual-test record has no fixed observations: assess truthful recording and sound inference, including uncertain when controls fail. Reflection accepts any evidence-linked revision.

Assessment / re-teach

Secure objective: can trace complete loop, interprets a dark result only after passing controls and valid contacts, and justifies conductor/insulator functions. A material sort without controls is incomplete. Re-teach with Case C beside A: same known copper, different lamp response. Ask what changed about the evidence before retesting conceptual understanding.

Word help

electrical conductor
A material through which electric charge can move readily.
electrical insulator
A material through which electric charge moves much less readily.
current
The rate of flow of electric charge.
complete circuit
A connected loop through which a current can flow.
control check
A known test used to check that the method or equipment works.
