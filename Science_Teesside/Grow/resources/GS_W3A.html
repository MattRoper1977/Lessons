We Do · think together

Choose the job, then judge the force

Sort these situations into helpful, unhelpful or need more information. Defend one choice.

Use the model to explain or predict. Only record observations and practical steps that actually happened.

  1. A: Brake pad rubbing a bicycle wheel.
  2. B: Machine parts heating and wearing as they rub.
  3. C: A handle gripping a hand.
  4. D: Two unknown materials touching.

Discuss, point, draw, write or dictate a first explanation. Choose one example first; extend only if there is time.

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

A helps braking; B can waste energy and damage parts; C helps grip. D needs the intended job and the surface pair. There is no single good/bad label for friction.

Point to the surfaces, then finish: “Resisting sliding helps this job because …”.

Use the current workshop

Draw a box sliding right. Add a friction arrow. Explain one helpful and one unhelpful example using the two touching surfaces.

Teacher model · compare with the pupil’s evidence

The friction arrow on the sliding box points left. Pad–wheel friction helps braking. Friction between moving machine parts can heat and wear them; reducing this friction can be useful.

I Do · explain and model

Look, explain, check

Friction is a contact force between surfaces that slide, or try to slide, past each other.

The force opposes that relative sliding. The effect depends on the pair of surfaces.

Grip can be helpful; unwanted heating and wear can be a problem.

Connect the ideas

On a moving box, draw friction opposite its sliding along the floor.

On a stationary shoe, friction can prevent slipping.

A smooth-looking surface is not enough evidence to predict every surface pair.

Quick check

A brake pad presses against a turning wheel. Would less friction help it stop?

Check the explanation

No. Friction between the pad and wheel resists their relative sliding and slows the wheel.

The wheel is turning and the brake pad presses against its rim. Which surfaces touch, and what do you predict will happen? Give a reason. This is a described scenario, not an observation of moving video.

Teacher explanation for the video or still scenario

The brake pad touches the wheel rim. Friction resists their relative sliding and the wheel slows. The clip is an observation, not a class measurement.

Use a short extract or the still scenario in place of part of the modelling time. A video or model is not evidence that pupils completed a practical.

For the adult

Preparation and a likely misconception

Print the worksheet. Use a still model or an adult demonstration with a low ramp and a light sliding block. Hand rubbing is optional; no heat or flame demonstration.

Watch for: Avoid “friction always opposes an object’s travel” as a universal rule. It opposes relative slipping at a contact; static friction can help us walk forwards.

Exit question and model answer

What information do you need before saying friction is helpful?

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

The touching surfaces, the relative motion or attempted sliding, and the intended job.
