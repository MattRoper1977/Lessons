We Do · think together

Build the order together

Arrange the planet names from the Sun outwards, then explain one placement.

A model helps explain an idea. It does not measure real planetary motion or prove an observation. Label any given numbers as reference values.

  1. Neptune · Earth · Mercury · Jupiter
  2. Venus · Saturn · Mars · Uranus

Discuss, point, draw, write or dictate a first explanation. Choose one example first; extend only if there is time.

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. The first four are rocky planets. The diagram’s gaps and planet sizes are not to scale.

Give a smaller support cue

Start Sun → Mercury → Venus → Earth → Mars. Use an arrow from Earth towards the Sun.

Apply it

Use the current workshop

Order the eight planets, or the inner four with the remaining names provided. Add a Sun–Earth sketch with a gravity arrow and explain what an orbit is.

Teacher model · compare with the pupil’s evidence

Earth is third from the Sun. An orbit is the curved path an object follows around another. The Sun’s gravity pulls inward while Earth keeps moving along its path; the pull changes the motion’s direction.

I Do · explain and model

Look, explain, check

The eight planets orbit the Sun. Earth is the third planet from it.

Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars are the inner rocky planets.

Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants; Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.

Connect the ideas

Draw a gravity arrow from Earth towards the Sun.

Draw a motion arrow along the path, not directly away from the Sun.

An orbit is a curved path. Classroom drawings simplify its size and shape.

Quick check

Does “there is no gravity in space” explain why Earth stays in its orbit?

Check the explanation

No. Gravity acts in space. The Sun’s gravitational pull continually changes the direction of Earth’s motion.

Optional clip or still

Rotation is not an orbit

Question: The globe turns. Is that rotation, an orbit, or evidence of both?

Optional local clip. Play only when ready; the question and still scenario also work without playback.

The same science without video

Imagine this globe turning about its axis while staying in the same position. Is this rotation, orbit, or both? The still itself cannot show movement.

Teacher explanation for the video or still scenario

It illustrates rotation about an axis. It does not show Earth travelling around the Sun. Use the orbit diagram to distinguish the two motions.

Use a short extract or the still scenario in place of part of the modelling time. A video or model is not evidence that pupils completed a practical.

For the adult

Preparation and a likely misconception

Print the name cards and use counters or pointing. Public walking is optional. Use the still diagram; do not look at the Sun. The missing uploaded Block 2 is not assumed to have been reviewed.

Watch for: Do not draw an outward “motion force” balancing gravity. Motion is along the path; gravity changes its direction.

Exit question and model answer

What does the inward arrow represent?

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

The Sun’s gravitational force on Earth, directed towards the Sun.
