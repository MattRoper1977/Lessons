We Do · think together

Test a year-length claim

Approximate orbital periods in Earth days. Which comparisons support “farther out, longer year” for these planets?

A model helps explain an idea. It does not measure real planetary motion or prove an observation. Label any given numbers as reference values.

  1. Compare Earth with Mars.
  2. Why is a single hand-made orbit not enough evidence to measure a year?

Discuss, point, draw, write or dictate a first explanation. Choose one example first; extend only if there is time.

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

Mars takes about 322 more Earth days than Earth (687 − 365). Mercury’s 88 days is shorter than Earth’s year. The reference values support the stated pattern for these examples; hand speed in a counter model is chosen by us.

Give a smaller support cue

Use “The model shows … . It does not show …”. Compare 687 with 365.

Apply it

Use the current workshop

Draw a two-arrow orbit model and use two values from the table to explain a year-length comparison. Add one limitation of your model.

Teacher model · compare with the pupil’s evidence

Gravity points towards the Sun; motion is along the curved path. Mars’s year is about 687 Earth days, longer than Earth’s 365. A counter model does not show real scale or orbital speed, and our hand is moving it.

I Do · explain and model

Look, explain, check

Move an Earth counter around a Sun counter. Keep an inward gravity arrow.

At one position, show the direction Earth is travelling along the path.

The hand moves this model; it is not an experiment proving gravity.

Connect the ideas

An Earth year is about 365 days; a Mars year is about 687 Earth days.

Compare the stated values before predicting a pattern.

These are approximate reference values, not class measurements.

Quick check

If gravity stopped acting in our simple orbit model, would Earth head directly outwards from the Sun?

Check the explanation

No. In the simplified model it would continue along a straight line tangent to its path, rather than along a radius away from the Sun.

Optional clip or still

Rotation is not an orbit

Question: The globe turns. Is that rotation, an orbit, or evidence of both?

Optional local clip. Play only when ready; the question and still scenario also work without playback.

The same science without video

Imagine this globe turning about its axis while staying in the same position. Is this rotation, orbit, or both? The still itself cannot show movement.

Teacher explanation for the video or still scenario

It illustrates rotation about an axis. It does not show Earth travelling around the Sun. Use the orbit diagram to distinguish the two motions.

Use a short extract or the still scenario in place of part of the modelling time. A video or model is not evidence that pupils completed a practical.

For the adult

Preparation and a likely misconception

Provide two counters, two arrow cards and the year table. Use drawing, directing or speaking. No strings, swinging masses or public movement are needed.

Watch for: Near-circular is a simplification, not a claim that all orbits are perfect circles.

Exit question and model answer

How can a model be useful and still have a limitation?

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

It can make a relationship clear while simplifying other features. Naming the limitation prevents an unsupported claim.
