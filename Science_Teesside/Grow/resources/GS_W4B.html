We Do · think together

Predict a gear pair

Two meshing gears have 20 and 40 teeth. The 40-tooth gear makes one complete turn.

The gear calculation uses an ideal model. Only record a lever observation if the comparison actually happened; do not invent force measurements.

  1. How many turns must the 20-tooth gear make?
  2. Do meshing gears turn in the same or opposite directions?
  3. What does this model leave out?

Discuss, point, draw, write or dictate a first explanation. Choose one example first; extend only if there is time.

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

The 20-tooth gear makes two turns in the opposite direction: 40 teeth pass the contact point. This ideal model leaves out friction and deformation. It does not mean the smaller gear supplies both more speed and more turning force.

Give a smaller support cue

Use: “The pivot is closer to … . I noticed … . For the same lift, the hand moves …”.

Apply it

Use the current workshop

Compare the two prepared lever positions, or explain them from the diagram. Record the same load, the pivot position and the observed effort. Explain the gain and the cost.

Teacher model · compare with the pupil’s evidence

With the pivot nearer the load and a longer effort arm, less effort is needed for the same load, but the effort point travels farther for the same load rise. A diagram explanation is modelling; only record observations if a comparison was actually made.

I Do · explain and model

Look, explain, check

Mark one effort point and two pivot positions. Keep the same light load.

Lift the load through the same small height. Compare how much effort is needed.

Observe how far the effort point moves. Easier can mean farther.

Connect the ideas

A longer effort arm relative to the load arm can reduce the needed effort.

Use easier/similar/harder only as a qualitative observation.

Write a force in newtons only if it was measured with a suitable force meter.

Quick check

The load changes from one trial to the next. Can we judge the effect of moving the pivot?

Check the explanation

Not fairly: the load has also changed. Keep the same load when comparing pivot positions.

Optional clip or still

Watch the gear teeth

Question: Find two meshing wheels. How do their teeth transmit movement?

Optional local clip. Play only when ready; the question and still scenario also work without playback.

The same science without video

Imagine the driver gear turning and one tooth pushing the neighbouring gear tooth. In this directly meshing pair, what turning direction must the neighbour have? Use the model to explain; the still does not measure speed or force.

Teacher explanation for the video or still scenario

A tooth pushes a neighbouring tooth, transmitting turning. Directly meshing wheels turn in opposite directions. The close-up does not by itself measure force or establish a gear ratio.

Use a short extract or the still scenario in place of part of the modelling time. A video or model is not evidence that pupils completed a practical.

For the adult

Preparation and a likely misconception

Prepare a stable ruler lever, non-rolling pivot and small secured load close to the table. Mark the effort point and a small target lift. Use the still comparison when practical work is unavailable.

Watch for: A qualitative comparison is not a measured force. Do not add invented newtons to a result table.

Exit question and model answer

Why must the load rise be the same in both comparisons?

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

It lets us compare the effort movement needed to achieve the same lift, rather than two different jobs.
