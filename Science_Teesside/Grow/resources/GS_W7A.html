We Do · think together

Repair the Moon claims

Choose a claim to repair. Use the still model to explain your correction.

A model helps explain an idea. It does not measure real planetary motion or prove an observation. Label any given numbers as reference values.

  1. “The Moon glows by itself.”
  2. “It becomes a different shape every week.”
  3. “It can only be seen at night.”

Discuss, point, draw, write or dictate a first explanation. Choose one example first; extend only if there is time.

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

The Moon reflects sunlight. It remains round; the amount of its sunlit half visible from Earth changes. It can sometimes be seen during the day. No solar observation is required for this lesson.

Give a smaller support cue

Point to the lit part that the observer can see. Finish “We see … because …”.

Apply it

Use the current workshop

Draw three equal circles showing new, half and full Moon views. Explain why the lit part we can see changes.

Teacher model · compare with the pupil’s evidence

The three circles remain the same shape. Shade almost all for new, half for the half-Moon view and none for full. As the Moon orbits Earth, our viewing angle changes, so we see different amounts of the sunlit half.

I Do · explain and model

Look, explain, check

The Moon is Earth’s natural satellite and reflects sunlight.

In the ordinary phase model, sunlight lights half the Moon.

Our view of that lit half changes as the Moon moves around Earth.

Connect the ideas

Near the Sun’s direction we see the new-Moon view. Do not look at the Sun.

At a side position we see a half-Moon view.

Opposite the Sun’s direction we see the full-Moon view; ordinary phases are not eclipses.

Quick check

Is Earth’s shadow the cause of the ordinary monthly Moon phases?

Check the explanation

No. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of its sunlit half. Earth’s shadow matters in a lunar eclipse.

For the adult

Preparation and a likely misconception

Use the embedded still model and circle cards. Optional adult-held low-power torch and ball, with normal room light and no beam in eyes. Agree any light-level change; no blackout, laser or Sun viewing.

Watch for: Keep phases separate from eclipses. “Half is lit” describes the ordinary sunlight model; an eclipse is a different alignment.

Exit question and model answer

What changes during an ordinary phase cycle: the Moon’s shape or our view?

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

Our view of its sunlit half changes; the Moon stays round.
