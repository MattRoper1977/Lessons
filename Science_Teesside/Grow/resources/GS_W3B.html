Apply it

Use the current workshop

If the adult has prepared the apparatus, compare two surfaces twice and record your actual distances. Otherwise analyse the supplied table. State your route and write a conclusion with a limit.

Teacher model · compare with the pupil’s evidence

Sample-data route: B gave 24 and 26 cm, compared with 46 and 48 cm for A. This supports greater resistance on B for that block and release. Actual practical: accept a conclusion justified by the pupil’s own retained values, including a result that does not fit the prediction.

I Do · explain and model

Look, explain, check

Keep the same block, low ramp, release mark and no-push release. Change the run-out surface.

Measure from the same zero to the same edge of the stopped block, in centimetres.

Repeat each surface. Keep the first results even if they differ.

Connect the ideas

A shorter slide can support greater resistance in this particular setup.

Compare repeated values, not just the run that fits your prediction.

A result with a known release mistake needs a note and a retry; keep its record.

Quick check

We change both the block and the surface. Can stopping distance tell us which surface caused the difference?

Check the explanation

No. Changing the block as well as the surface leaves more than one possible cause.

Optional clip or still

Brake pads slow a wheel

Question: Which two surfaces touch, and what happens as the pad grips?

Optional local clip. Play only when ready; the question and still scenario also work without playback.

The same science without video

The wheel is turning and the brake pad presses against its rim. Which surfaces touch, and what do you predict will happen? Give a reason. This is a described scenario, not an observation of moving video.

Teacher explanation for the video or still scenario

The brake pad touches the wheel rim. Friction resists their relative sliding and the wheel slows. The clip is an observation, not a class measurement.

Use a short extract or the still scenario in place of part of the modelling time. A video or model is not evidence that pupils completed a practical.

For the adult

Preparation and a likely misconception

Adult-secured low ramp, light block, two flat run-out surfaces, tape and padded stop. Keep hands out of the run. Pupils may direct, observe or record. Use the labelled teaching table if equipment or suitable supervision is unavailable.

Watch for: Do not turn a surface nickname into a universal ranking, and do not record sample-data work as a completed practical.

Exit question and model answer

Why should we keep a surprising result?

Reveal a worked explanation after an attempt

It is part of the evidence. We can flag uncertainty, check the method and repeat without silently deleting it.
