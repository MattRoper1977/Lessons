Autumn Science: Explain, Check, Improve

SoW outcome: Review and evidence autumn science (Entry Level Science / UAS).

🎯 Learning objective: I can use real autumn science evidence to explain one linked idea, note a limit, and improve my work after feedback.
✅ Success checks for today's activity — not assessment criteria
  • Choose one linked autumn science idea. Name the real evidence you used.
  • Explain how the evidence supports the idea. Do not claim more than it shows.
  • Use one response to make the work clear, fair or wider. Record what changed.

⏱ 3 min
Starter · predict

What would count?

Read two accounts. One repeats a fact. One names evidence and links it to the claim. Find the sentence that shows the science thinking.

Account A

“The model has arrows.” Decide what is present and what is missing. Does this locate evidence, explain a link or state only a visible feature?

Account B

“The arrows point from the food to the animal, so the model shows the direction of energy transfer.” Identify the evidence words and the reasoning words.

Think → choose → explain or point → keep the first response visible.
Checking question: could another reader find the evidence and follow the reason without needing you to add a hidden detail?
Hold the distinction

A fluent repeated fact is not automatically an evidence link. Ask which words locate the record and which words explain what it supports.

⏱ 4 min
I Do · model

Watch the decision trail

Neutral model: a labelled food-chain card shows grass → rabbit → fox. The claim is narrow: the arrows show the direction of energy transfer in this model.

1 · State a claim that the record can answer. “In this model, energy is transferred from the grass to the rabbit and then to the fox.”
2 · Name the evidence: source, result, note or labelled model. “The labelled arrows on source card FC-2 point grass → rabbit → fox.” A source ID and the visible feature make the record locatable.
3 · Use because or therefore to link them. Then add a fair limit. “The arrows support the transfer direction, but this card does not show the amount of energy transferred.” The limit prevents the evidence carrying a claim it cannot support.
Before feedbackKeep the original claim, locator, reason and limit visible. Do not tidy it away while the model is being checked.
After feedbackChange one precise part, then name what changed and why. The response is guidance, not a score or an assessment decision.
Model sentence frame: My claim is … The evidence is … This supports the claim because … It does not show …
Authorship boundary

Model the reasoning route with neutral content. Do not supply a fictional learner result, reconstruct old work or complete the learner's account for them.

Choose the move that makes the science more true, not just longer.

Select 1, 2 or 3, then explain the evidence clue.
ClearAdd the missing locator, label or science word so another reader can find the record and follow the link.
FairNarrow the claim, restore an uncertainty or state what the record cannot show.
WiderConnect the same reasoning to a second authentic record without inventing a result or changing the first one.
Checked changeKeep the first version. Write the new version beside it and name the response you used.

⏱ 3 min
I Do 2 · test

Accuracy includes limits

Read or hear the response. It is not a score. Choose: make clear, make fair or make wider.

First version: “The food chain proves all foxes eat rabbits.” Keep it visible even though it goes beyond the model.
Response: “Make fair: say what this card shows, not what every fox does.” The response identifies the boundary; it does not write the learner's revision.
Checked revision: “This card shows a possible grass → rabbit → fox feeding link.” Record the exact change. Keep the first draft so the change can be seen.

Keep

The first version, genuine source, learner choice and exact response that prompted the check.

Do not add

A reconstructed result, invented quotation, hidden adult sentence, backdated event or assessment claim.
Boundary test: can each new word be traced either to the genuine source or to the learner's own checked reasoning? Check again.
Do not upgrade

A clearer explanation remains teaching work until an authorised centre process says otherwise. Preserve uncertainty and missing evidence.

⏱ 4 min
We Do 2 · rehearse

Build the route together

1 · Locate

Find the genuine record, source or prior idea. Keep its date, filename, page, lesson title or source-card ID attached.

2 · Connect

Explain what it supports and how. Use a label, result, observation or model feature that another reader can find.

3 · Limit

Keep uncertainty, missing evidence and adult decisions visible. Say what the record cannot show.

4 · ReceiveAsk for one bounded response: make clear, make fair or make wider. The learner chooses whether to use it and may ask for it again.
5 · ReviseChange one precise part without erasing the first version. Name the words, label, locator or limit that changed.
6 · CheckRead from claim to evidence to reason to limit. Remove any invented detail and keep “not found yet” when a source is absent.
7 · Hand forwardName the genuine record, learner contribution, adult support, saved change and next authorised action.
Use the W7A evidence map, local GROW Science W8–W13 lessons and the pack's source cards.
Shared rehearsal: one person operates or directs the cards; the group explains each placement. Shared talk may support the work, but the learner's own decision is recorded separately.
Select access, not an answer

Offer fewer cards, a visual sequence, exact-word scribing or private rehearsal. Keep the same locate → connect → limit → revise decision.

★ Stretch

Judge two accounts or two pieces of evidence. Then use the same thinking for a new linked question.

Explain which evidence is more useful for the claim and why. Transfer the reasoning route, not an invented result, to the new question.
1 · Learner decisionWhich claim, evidence, limit and revision move the learner selected.
2 · Authentic source or actionThe genuine record, local card, labelled model, actual result or visible correction used.
3 · Learner meaningThe link, comparison, limit, question or revision expressed by the learner.
Claim checkMake it narrow enough for this record to answer. Replace “proves,” “always” or “all” when the evidence cannot carry those words.
Locator checkName the page, date, filename, lesson, result row, model label or source-card ID. If it is absent, write “not found yet.”
Link checkPoint to the exact evidence feature, then explain how it supports, refines or challenges the claim. Do not add a hidden detail.
Limit checkName what the record cannot show, what is uncertain or what another genuine record would need to check.
Response checkRecord the bounded response as make clear, make fair or make wider. It is guidance, not a mark or award decision.
Revision checkKeep the first version. Show the new version beside it and name the precise change that was made.

Worked evidence check: Begin with the claim, “This labelled model shows energy moving from grass to rabbit.” Ask: is the claim narrow enough for this model? Next write the locator: “Food-chain source card FC-2, arrows and labels.” Ask: could another person find the same feature? Then connect them: “The arrow from grass to rabbit supports the transfer direction.” Ask: have I explained the link, rather than only naming two things? Add a limit: “The card does not show how much energy is transferred.” Ask: have I kept the missing quantity visible? Receive one response: “Make clear: identify the exact arrow.” Keep those words as feedback, not as a score. Revise beside the first version: “The arrow labelled grass to rabbit shows the direction in this model.” Finally record: “I added the labels and kept ‘in this model’ because one diagram cannot show every feeding relationship.” Do not replace this neutral rehearsal with a learner biography, invented result or completed assessment record.

Checking questions: Can I find every source I named? Does each science claim match what the source can show? Have I used evidence words and reasoning words? Is the limit still visible? Can someone see the first version, the response and my change? Have I marked any shared decision or adult support honestly? If one answer is no, return to that box only. Keep the rest of the genuine trail unchanged. Use the honest route, not a shortcut.

End-of-task read-through: claim → locator → evidence → reason → limit → response → saved change. Remove invented detail. Keep shared work and adult support visible. If the evidence is missing, plan a locating check instead of reconstructing it.
Do not reconstruct

Never backdate, stage an event, invent a result or quotation, convert adult wording into learner voice, or turn this activity into an assessed claim.
