⏱ 3 min
Arrival · retrieve

Start with what is real

Sort your old work cards into REMEMBER, NEED A CLUE or REVISIT. You may point or move a card. You do not need to speak.

Space and EarthLook for a labelled model, observation note, sequence card or explanation that you actually made. Keep the date or lesson name with it.
Living thingsLook for a classification choice, body-system model, food-chain link or recorded observation. A clear correction can be useful evidence too.
Forces and materialsLook for a fair-test plan, results table, force diagram or comparison. Keep surprising results rather than tidying them away.
Environment and climateLook for a cause-and-effect chain, source note, action plan or review. Separate what the record shows from what still needs checking.

◆ Supported

Choose from six prepared cards. Put two under REMEMBER and one under REVISIT. Point to the part that makes each card genuine.

▲ Standard

Choose records from two topics. Name the idea each one supports, then mark whether the link is clear, partial or still uncertain.

Keep the locator: lesson title, page, date, filename or source-card ID. If a record cannot be found, write “not found yet”; do not rebuild it from memory.
Least prompt first

Wait → self-prompt → visual cue → general verbal → specific verbal → model → direct support. Record adult support separately.

⏱ 3 min
Starter · predict

What would count?

Which one is evidence: a claim, a checked result, or an opinion? Choose one. Say what clue helped.

Think → choose → explain or point → keep the first response visible.
Decision clue

Evidence has a trail. You can locate the source or action, describe what was recorded and explain how it helps with the claim. A result can still have a limit. A confident sentence without a trail is not made stronger by confidence.

Evidence means information that can support or challenge an idea. Claim is the idea being checked. Limit says what the evidence cannot settle.

⏱ 4 min
I Do · model

Watch the decision trail

Start with this claim: Earth turns, so we get day and night.
Add a useful record. It may be a labelled model or an old pattern.
Explain the link. Then mark what the record cannot show. Never invent a result.
Locate“W8A day-and-night model, labelled drawing.” The locator is specific enough for another person to find the real record.
Connect“The lit side faced the lamp while the dark side faced away; turning the model changed which side was lit.”
ReasonThe model supports the claim because rotation changes which part of Earth faces the light source.
LimitThe model is not to scale and the lamp is not the Sun. It helps explain the pattern; it does not recreate space.
Model check

Listen for four separate moves: claim, locator, link and limit. The model uses a neutral class example. It does not supply a learner’s missing words, backdate a record or turn rehearsal into assessed evidence.

⏱ 3 min
We Do · choose

Choose, then justify

Choose the item with the best trail. Say why the other two need work.

Select 1, 2 or 3, then explain the evidence clue.
Item 1A source and date make the drawing locatable. Its labels may show the idea, but we still ask who made it and what it was meant to show.
Item 2Memory can start a claim, but it needs a source, checked observation, model or result before it becomes a usable evidence link.
Item 3A full results table keeps the trail honest. The odd result may reveal variation, a method problem or a question for the next check.

⏱ 3 min
I Do 2 · test

Accuracy includes limits

Ask: what does this evidence show?
Ask: how does it link to the claim?
Record one limit. It may need a repeat, a clear source, a full label or a new check.
Record A

A dated, labelled model shows where light falls as Earth turns. It can support an explanation of day and night because the parts and movement are visible.

Record B

A sentence from memory says “Earth goes around.” It may point toward a topic, but it does not yet distinguish rotation from orbit or show where the idea came from.

Boundary test

Keep Record A with its model limit. Keep Record B as “needs checking,” not as a polished answer. Accuracy means preserving uncertainty, source gaps and adult-owned decisions instead of quietly improving them.

⏱ 4 min
We Do 2 · rehearse

Build the route together

1 · Locate

Find the genuine record, source or prior idea.

2 · Connect

Explain what it supports and how.

3 · Limit

Keep uncertainty, missing evidence and adult decisions visible.

Shared claim“A rougher surface changed how far the rover travelled.” This is the idea to check, not a result we invent.
Possible recordA real six-row table from the rover task, with three distances for each named surface and the odd values left in place.
Link to explainCompare the pattern for both surfaces. Say which condition usually gave the greater distance and point to the repeated results.
Limit to keepThe table may show a pattern in that setup only. Ramp height, release point or measuring method may need checking before transfer.
Shared rehearsal: one person locates; one points to the evidence clue; one states the link; one keeps the limit. Swap roles or use pointing, cards, symbols or exact-word scribing. No role is a score.
Check the trail
  • Can another person locate the record?
  • Does the reason say how the record supports the claim?
  • Is one gap or limitation still visible?
  • Has adult prompting been kept separate from learner meaning?

⏱ 16 min
Independent · evidence

Make the evidence trail usable

Make an autumn science evidence map. Use real old work and local cards. For each claim, note where the evidence is, how it links, and one limit. End with one gap to check next time.

PrepareTwo topic folders, authentic prior work, local source cards, evidence-map sheet and a way to point, write, type or dictate exact words.
Keep separateLearner choice and meaning; shared rehearsal; adult preparation, prompts, scribing and filing.
Finish withAt least three evidence links, one honest gap and one next check with a responsible role.

◆ Supported

Sort prepared claim and evidence cards for two topics. Explain one match by pointing, writing or exact-word dictation.

Work one row at a time: choose the claim, point to the record, select a reason card, then choose “clear,” “partial” or “needs checking.”

▲ Standard

Build three claim → evidence → reason links across two topics. Mark one honest gap.

Add the source or locator for every record. Use a full sentence only when it helps the explanation; a precise table, diagram or symbol route is equally valid.

★ Stretch

Compare two evidence items for one claim. Judge which helps more. Explain one limit or transfer.

Change one condition or context in your explanation. Decide whether the evidence would still help and name what extra evidence would be needed.
1 · Learner decisionWhich claim, record and next check the learner selected.
2 · Authentic source or actionReal old work, local source card, labelled model, genuine result or recorded correction.
3 · Learner meaningThe link, reason, comparison, limitation, revision or question expressed by the learner.
Map row 1 · ClaimWrite or select one science idea from the autumn sequence. Keep it narrow enough to check against a real record.
Map row 2 · LocatorName the file, lesson, page, date, model, results table or source-card ID. “I remember” is not a locator.
Map row 3 · LinkExplain what the record shows and how that supports, refines or challenges the claim. Keep the actual result visible.
Map row 4 · LimitName what the record cannot show, what is missing or what should be repeated, labelled, sourced or checked next.
End-of-task check: read each row from left to right. Remove any invented detail. Mark shared work and adult support honestly. If a record is absent, keep “not found yet” and write the next locating action rather than reconstructing it.
Do not reconstruct

Never backdate, stage an event, invent a result or quotation, convert adult wording into learner voice, or turn this activity into an assessed claim.

Independent VOICE creates the map · an adult AUDIENCE receives it · INFLUENCE is the next check

⏱ 4 min
Exit · hand forward

One evidence link, one limit, one next step

Hand forward one claim and evidence link, plus one honest gap. You may write, point, record or tell an adult in private.

My evidence link“My claim is … The record is … It helps because …” Point to the exact source, result, label or model feature.
My honest limit“This does not show …” or “I still need to check …” Keep uncertainty visible rather than finishing the sentence for appearance.
My next stepName one useful check: locate a missing source, repeat one measure, improve one label, compare another record or ask a bounded science question.
Adult responseThe receiving adult names back the learner’s link and limit, records any adult-owned action and gives a realistic report-back point.
VOICE · Learner decision and meaning.→AUDIENCE / INFLUENCE · Receiving role, bounded response and report-back.
Close the loop: “I heard … I will … You will hear back …” If no action is authorised yet, say that clearly. The evidence map feeds W7B; it does not become an award decision here.
Close without upgrading
