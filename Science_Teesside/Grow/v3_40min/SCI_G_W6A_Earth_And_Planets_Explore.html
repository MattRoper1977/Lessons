Earth and the Planets

🧭
Science mission
Mission Control: navigate the eight-planet Solar System.
Visual model: use it to predict and explain — not as decoration.

Aut1·W6 — Describe the movement of the Earth and other planets around the Sun.

🎯 Learning objective: I can describe the order and movement of planets around the Sun and explain gravity’s role.
✅ Success criteria
  • I can name the eight planets in order.
  • I can classify rocky planets and outer giants.
  • I can explain that gravity pulls inward while planets continue moving.

🔍 Bridge into today

Imagine you are sending a probe away from Earth. Put these four in your best guess order from the Sun: Mercury · Earth · Jupiter · Neptune. Guessing is allowed.

🎯 Commit to a prediction

Before we look: what holds the planets on their paths?

Choose one. Say it, point to it, or press it.

Nothing here is right or wrong yet. Nothing is marked, scored or stored. A prediction still counts when it turns out differently. That is what makes the test worth running.

✔ What this model helps us seeIt shows the order of the planets and that each orbit is a closed path around the Sun.
✘ What this model does not showIt leaves out the true sizes and distances, and the fact that a real orbit is a stretched circle rather than a perfect one.
Build the order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
Classify the inner four as rocky; distinguish gas giants and ice giants among the outer planets.
State openly that a classroom diagram may show order well without showing sizes and distances to scale.

If the inward pull vanished, the planet would continue along a much straighter path rather than staying in orbit.

🔁 GROW Lundy close — ONE line only.

1) Pupil answers the Science question. 2) Adult genuinely receives it and gives one next step. 3) Pupil writes: “Next I will ______.”

No adult signature, initial or second receipt mark. If nothing needs changing, the next step can be transfer to a new example or greater independence.

✍ GROW written close — the pupil writes this line

“What I said, and what it changed:”

No adult initial is required. The adult is audience for this line, not verifier. There is no signature, initial, tick or receipt mark. The line closes when it is written and received.

If writing is hard: say it, and an adult writes down your exact words. Or point to what changed and say it aloud. Or draw it and give it one spoken caption. Or record it in the school’s own workflow. The line always stays. The route changes how you answer, not the Science.
