Practicals & Equipment Matrix · W3–W7

One page per half-term: what each investigation needs, how it is made safe, and the two routes that keep every pupil in the practical — a digital alternative and a no-equipment fallback. A pupil who cannot handle materials still directs the investigation, and directing it is the assessed skill.

Safety authority. No CLEAPSS approval is claimed for any activity on this page. Each activity requires a local, proportionate risk assessment before use; local COSHH and risk-assessment decisions determine the actual controls, and a headteacher signature alone is not sufficient. An adult does all cutting, piercing and pin-fitting — pupils never cut card, punch holes or fit split pins. Where a mechanism has pinch or trap points (W4A gear teeth and pulley rope entry, W4B pivot and falling load) name them aloud before the practical begins.
Two routes, not one. Every lesson carries a digital alternative and a no-equipment fallback. Neither is a reduced version of the lesson: the Science goal is identical and only the response route changes. The digital column names only routes that are installed in the live lesson files — where a route does not exist, this page says so rather than advertising one.

W3AGrip and Slide InvestigationTrainer sole or rubber offcut; smooth plastic; fabric; rough card; small light block; shallow tray.Soles, plastic, block and tray reusable. Rough card and fabric abrade with use — consumable, expect to replace termly.Adult cuts and labels the four surface pieces before the lesson and tapes them into the tray. Pupils never cut card.4 minLight block only, worked inside the tray. Keep the floor clear. Do not use sandpaper — its cut edge abrades skin.In-lesson Surface explorer (three buttons: smooth / medium / rough). Button-only, no handling, no drag.The pupil directs: names the two surfaces to compare, states the prediction, and an adult performs the slide on the pupil’s instruction. The prediction and the explanation stay the pupil’s.
W3BFixed-Ramp Surface TestSmall block or trolley; low ramp fixed at one height; three surfaces; tape measure; tray; results table.Ramp, block, tape measure and tray reusable. Surfaces and printed results tables consumable.Adult fixes the ramp at ONE height and marks the release point — the fixed height is the controlled variable and must not be adjustable mid-lesson. Pre-print the three-column results table.5 minLow ramp inside a tray. One release at a time. Hands clear of the run-off. Light loads only.In-lesson results tool: enter three distances, the bar chart redraws. Typed entry, keyboard-operable.The pupil calls each release, reads the measurement with an adult’s finger held on the tape, and decides which surface needs a repeat.
W4AMechanism Station HuntRuler and pivot block; simple pulley or pulley image; gear pair or bicycle-gear image; bottle opener; scissors (adult-held only).All items reusable. Printed station cards consumable.Adult sets out four labelled stations and keeps every sharp tool. No station requires a pupil to cut, pierce or force anything.6 min (four stations)Stable pivots and light loads. Adult controls all sharp tools. Name the pinch and trap points aloud before starting: gear teeth mesh, pulley rope entry, and the hinge of the bottle opener. Fingers stay clear of all three.In-lesson lever model (pivot slider, arrow-key operable, with a written result line). It covers the lever station only — pulleys and gears have no installed digital route in this lesson.The pupil directs the hunt: chooses which station to visit, points to where the force goes in and where it comes out, and an adult operates the mechanism exactly as instructed.
W4BLever Pivot InvestigationRuler or wooden strip; stable pivot block; light load; tape measure; optional spring balance; tray.All items reusable. Recording sheets consumable.Adult marks three pivot positions on the ruler in advance and checks the pivot block cannot roll. Agree the effort scale before the lesson if a spring balance is not available.4 minStable pivot, light load, worked over a tray or low table. Keep fingers clear of the pivot and of the falling load — both are trap points. An adult positions the load for any pupil who cannot judge the drop.In-lesson lever model: pivot slider with a written explanation of the effort/distance trade. Native slider, arrow-key operable.The pupil chooses each pivot position and predicts the effect; an adult moves the pivot and reads the result back. The pupil states the conclusion.
W5AFair-Test Planning Lab (planning only — no equipment handled)CHANGE / MEASURE / KEEP-SAME cards; unit cards; a ramp and block for demonstration only; planning frame.Cards and ramp reusable. Planning frames consumable.Adult prepares one deliberately faulty method for the pupils to repair, and checks the card set is complete. Equipment is demonstrated, not issued.3 minPlanning lesson: pupils handle no equipment. The demonstration uses a low ramp, a light object and a clear run-off area. An adult checks every plan before any equipment is used in W5B.In-lesson method repair tool: three checkboxes and a written verdict on whether the method can answer its question.The pupil makes every variable decision aloud and an adult places the cards as directed. No handling is required at any point in this lesson.
W5BRepeat Evidence InvestigationTrolley or block; ramp fixed at the W5A-agreed height; three surfaces; tape measure; repeat table; calculator optional.Ramp, trolley, tape measure reusable. Surfaces and repeat tables consumable.Adult sets the ramp to the height the class agreed in W5A and pre-prints the nine-cell repeat table. Check the calculator if means are to be worked digitally.5 minLow ramp, light block, clear run-off tray. One person releases at a time. Agree who releases and who measures before starting.In-lesson data tool: nine readings in, three means and a bar chart out, with a written prompt to inspect the individual repeats before trusting the summary.The pupil decides the number of repeats, says which reading looks wrong and why, and directs an adult through the measuring. The judgement is the assessed part, not the handling.
W6APlanet Order and Orbit ModelSun card; eight planet cards; orbit arrows; floor or desk space; optional short string circles.All cards reusable if laminated. Unlaminated cards consumable.Adult clears a floor area or desk run and lays the Sun card. Keep string pre-cut, short and flat — it is a trip hazard at length.5 minFloor markers in a clear area only. No running. String stays flat and short. Use the desk version where floor work is not safe.In-lesson Mission Control: order the eight planets by button, with a reset, and a written prompt at each step. No floor movement needed.The pupil names the next planet and an adult places the card. Ordering the Solar System aloud is the whole task — placement is not.
W6BOrbit Model Build and ReviewSun and planet cards or balls; labels; arrows; large paper; tape; optional short string.Balls, tape dispenser and arrows reusable. Paper, labels and string consumable.Adult pre-cuts every card, label and string length. Nothing in this build requires a pupil to cut or pierce. Decide in advance whether the model will be honest about size or about distance — it cannot be both.6 minBlunt, pre-cut materials only. Keep floor models clear of walkways. String stays short and flat.In-lesson orbit model: four orbital positions by button, each with a written gravity/motion prompt.The pupil directs an adult to place each piece exactly where the pupil chooses, then states what the model gets right and what it deliberately gets wrong. Directing the build is the assessed skill.
W7ATorch-and-Moon InvestigationLamp or torch; foam ball; Earth/observer marker; phase cards; a dimmable area, or the bright-room diagram alternative.Lamp, ball and markers reusable. Phase cards consumable if unlaminated.Adult positions the lamp at pupil head height pointing away from faces, and tests the dimming level in advance. If the room cannot be dimmed safely, print the bright-room diagram instead — it is a full alternative, not a lesser one.5 minNever shine a torch towards anyone’s eyes. Keep the room light enough to move safely. Use the bright-room diagram where dimming would distress a pupil or make movement unsafe.In-lesson phase model: four positions by button, each stating that the effect is viewing geometry and not Earth’s shadow.The pupil directs the lamp and ball positions and names what an observer on Earth would see. An adult holds the equipment throughout.
W7BFour-Phase Lunar StudioLamp or torch; foam ball; Earth marker; four phase cards or a ready-made phase wheel.Lamp, ball, marker and wheel reusable. Phase cards consumable.Adult uses a ready-made or pre-punched phase wheel. No pupil uses a split pin or punches a hole. Test the lamp position before the lesson.5 minDo not point light at faces. Pre-punched or ready-made wheels only. Keep the movement area clear before anyone stands up.In-lesson phase model used as a four-phase route (New, First quarter, Full, Last quarter): buttons are the complete task, with the phase-versus-eclipse distinction stated in text.The pupil calls each of the four positions in order and an adult moves the ball. Producing the phases on command is the assessment, whoever holds the equipment.
