Verbatim SoW objective: Review and evidence autumn science (Entry Level Science / UAS).

Arrival · 5 min

Choose a model used this term. Point to one question it could help answer.

Offer a predictable seat or standing spot and the first visual cue. No public test.

Starter · 3 min

Think back. Say, point or write one answer for each question.

  1. What changed in last week’s fair test?
  2. What model can help with a toy motion question?
  3. Name one force you learned in Autumn 1.

Today I will check:

  • I can choose a model that fits my question.
  • I can give a clue that supports my answer.
  • I can name one next step after feedback.

Ask three low-stakes retrieval questions; prior coverage must be checked.

I do · 5 min

I choose a question, then a matching model and clue. For day and night, the torch lights one side of a turning ball. I explain the place turning into shade. This is practice, not help during formal evidence.

Words: evidence · force · rotation · climate

WordIt meansExample
evidencea result or observation that supports a claimA measured distance is evidence about a toy run.
forcea push or pull on an objectFriction is a contact force.
rotationa turn around an axisEarth’s rotation makes day and night.
climateweather patterns over many yearsClimate is not the weather on one day.

Watch: Question: why does a place get night? → choose torch and turning ball → marked place turns away from light → explain Earth’s rotation.

Show me: Show me the model you would use for day and night.

Model the full reasoning step, pre-teach four words and ask the show-me question.

We do · 7 min

In practice, the question and torch-and-ball model are supplied. Complete the missing clue: what happens to the marked place as the ball turns?

Our turn: Question: what slowed the toy? → choose toy and surface → observe ____ → explain a possible force; check with repeat runs.

Talk, then sort.

  • Question about friction
  • Question about day and night
  • Question about climate

F · help

Point to the torch and the marked place before naming the clue.

M · say why

Use a model and matching clue to explain a science idea.

H · stretch

Explain why a correct model can still leave out size, distance or other details.

Show the part-done example, then allow paired talk before an answer. One-minute reset inside this stage.

We do 2 · 5 min

Show everyone which model best helps explain day and night.

Which clue fits a day-and-night claim?

Choose a step.

All of us: A new question asks why a model sled stops sooner on a mat. Which kit can help test it?

Point. Tell a partner why.

Take one quiet minute to reset.

All pupils answer the four-option hinge; use the decision rule, not a score.

Independent · 8 min

Choose two science questions from forces, space or climate. Show an answer with a model and a matching clue. Use only your agreed support if this is formal evidence.

Talk first. You can plan in any language.

Point, sign, speak, draw, type, write, use a scribe or show a staff-captured photo.

F

Match one question to its model.

Say first: For ___, I choose ___.

M

Use a model and matching clue to explain a science idea.

Say first: My answer is ___; I saw ___ in the model.

H

State one limit of a model and suggest a further check.

Say first: The model suggests ___, but I would check ___ because ___.

Go deeper: For one claim, give two checks and one model limit. Say which extra observation you would seek.

Two ways to check: Compare two model limits. Choose a claim neither can settle alone and name a new observation that would help.

One adult supports agreed pupils, the other holds the group; swap across the week. One-minute reset inside this stage.

Try it with kit · prepared model

Kit: Adult-prepared familiar model kit for the selected question

  1. Use only the prepared model selected for the agreed practice or formal task.

Risk: Loose model parts, a toy launch or a torch beam could cause harm if misused.

Safe: Keep model parts on the tray, launch only with adult permission and aim a torch away from eyes.

Check the school risk assessment and exact prepared kit. This is optional model rehearsal, not a claimed core practical.

Review · 4 min

In practice, hear one comment on claim and clue. Self-edit one mismatch. If this is formal evidence, receive and act on feedback after collection under the agreed conditions.

VF: hear one comment. NS(+): name a next step. //: edit one part. ?: ask for a read-back. Take one quiet minute.

Give a specific verbal comment (VF) and invite one self-edit (//) or read-back (?).

Exit · 3 min

Tell the adult what you learned, which clue you used, your feedback and one next step. They use your answer to plan the next model or support.
