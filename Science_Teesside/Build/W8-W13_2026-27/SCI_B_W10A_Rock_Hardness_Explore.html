Rock Hardness: Mark or Groove?

Sequence outcome: Rocks: test hardness.

🎯 Learning objective: I can explain relative hardness and identify a fair, safe scratch method for comparing rock samples.
✅ Success criteria
  • I can say that hardness means resistance to scratching.
  • I can distinguish a loose tool streak from a groove that remains after wiping.
  • I can change the sample while keeping the tool, passes and guide as consistent as practical.

👩‍🏫 I Do · picture first

Make the first scientific idea visible

HARDNESSresistance to a scratchhow well a material resists being scratched under a stated method
GROOVEa line cut into the surfacean indentation that remains in the sample after loose material is wiped away
CONTROLwhat we keep the samea relevant condition held as consistent as practical so samples can be compared

WHAT THIS MODEL HELPS US SEE

The mark–wipe–groove sequence makes the deciding observation visible and keeps loose tool streaks separate from damage to the sample.

WHAT THIS MODEL DOES NOT SHOW

This is a relative classroom comparison with one tool and method. It does not give a Mohs number, prove construction strength or represent every specimen of a named rock.

Step 1

Place a teacher-pretested dry sample in a stable tray. Use only the secured blunt scratch jig or a teacher demonstration.

Step 2

Use the same tool, guide length, number of passes and safe force proxy. Never press harder to make an expected result appear.

Step 3

Wipe the line with the prepared method, then inspect from the same viewing position for a remaining groove.

Word help keeps the scientific term visible and adds a plain-language bridge.

Connect the model to the second idea

Connection: the model is useful when its visible pattern is translated into a scientific explanation.
Connection 1

Compare outcomes with cautious words: deep groove, shallow groove, faint groove or no groove under this method.

Connection 2

A clearer remaining groove means lower resistance to this particular scratch method; it does not measure every kind of strength.

Connection 3

If a sample flakes, crumbles or makes dust, stop. Preserve the observation and use the representative digital or image route rather than forcing a repeat.
