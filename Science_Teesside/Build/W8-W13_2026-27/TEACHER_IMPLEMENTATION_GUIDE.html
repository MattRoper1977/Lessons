Teacher implementation guide

A concise teaching and access guide for all 12 BUILD Science lessons.

Before teaching

  1. Open the lesson locally and check the title slide, equipment and safety notes.
  2. Prepare the physical route and its offline digital/no-equipment equivalent.
  3. Identify the familiar adult who can genuinely receive pupil Voice at the exit.
  4. Decide whether a selected print tier is needed; printing is always an explicit action.

Read-aloud and accessibility

Device speech is intentionally omitted: no media permission is requested, and the deck remains dependable offline. A familiar adult may read the exact current task while the text stays visible. Word help adds a plain-language bridge while preserving the scientific term. Calm view stops automatic local-film motion; Teacher Freeze pauses lesson motion. Large text increases the interface text.

Truthful Lundy closure

Space is offered by the equivalent participation routes. Voice changes state only after a pupil commits a response. Audience changes only after a familiar adult genuinely receives it. Influence changes only after an adult selects and enacts one next teaching move. Reload begins a fresh session; no static completion claim survives.

Lesson list

  • W8A · Sugar Evidence: Read the Label — I can compare sugar values using the same label basis and order four supplied food labels from least to most sugar.
  • W8B · Body Science Checkpoint — I can use models and evidence to show what I know about skeletons, muscle pairs, nutrition, balance and food sources.
  • W9A · Rock Evidence Lab: Look Before You Label — I can compare rock specimens using observed properties and create a group using a stated rule.
  • W9B · Build a Rock Sorting Key — I can apply a clear rock-grouping rule, check a mystery specimen and defend the placement with evidence.
  • W10A · Rock Hardness: Mark or Groove? — I can explain relative hardness and identify a fair, safe scratch method for comparing rock samples.
  • W10B · Hardness Evidence Lab — I can use the same stated scratch method to compare four coded rock samples and give a cautious evidence-based conclusion.
  • W11A · Permeability: Surface or Through? — I can distinguish water on a surface, water soaking in and water passing through a supplied rock sample.
  • W11B · Water Through Rock Lab — I can follow or direct a fixed water-passage method, record what reaches the collector and make a careful comparison of supplied samples.
  • W12A · Rock Jobs: Which Property Matters? — I can identify which tested or observed rock property matters for a stated everyday job.
  • W12B · Choose Rock for the Job — I can choose a supplied rock sample for a stated model job and justify the decision with relevant property evidence and a limit.
  • W13A · Fair Test Planner: Change One Thing — I can plan a safe fair comparison by naming one changed variable, one measured result, the controls and the repeats.
  • W13B · Method Pilot: Test the Test — I can carry out or direct a small method pilot, identify a procedural problem and revise the plan ready for W14.
