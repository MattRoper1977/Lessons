BUILD · Science · Week 8B · Do

Lesson overview

Learning objective

I can use models and evidence to show what I know about skeletons, muscle pairs, nutrition, balance and food sources.

Success looks like

  • Show an idea about skeletons, movement, nutrition and food sources.
  • Connect two science ideas using because.
  • Use a model or supplied evidence to justify an explanation.

We will observe, work together, then use the evidence ourselves.

Arrival task

Arrival task

Choose a route. Think first, then check the answers together.

1. Bones and jobs

Which job does the skull do: protect the brain or digest food?

Think about what is inside your head.

Answer: The skull protects the brain.

2. A muscle moves

Choose the word for a muscle becoming shorter and pulling: contract or relax.

You can show the idea with your hand or point to a word.

Answer: Contract.

3. Animal evidence

A dog has an internal backbone. A worm does not. Which is a vertebrate?

Vertebrate means an animal with a backbone.

Answer: The dog is a vertebrate.

4. Sugar evidence

J has 5 g sugar per 100 g. K has 10 g per 100 g. Which has less sugar?

Compare the same amount of food.

Answer: J: 5 g sugar per 100 g.

1. Skeleton job

Name one job of a skeleton and a bone that helps do it.

Think about support, protection or movement.

Answer: For example: skull protects the brain; ribs protect heart/lungs; bones support the body and work with muscles at joints.

2. A muscle pair

When the biceps contracts to bend an arm, what does its partner do?

Muscles pull. Think about what the triceps needs to allow.

Answer: The triceps relaxes and lengthens in this simplified bending model.

3. Classification

Explain vertebrate using the word backbone.

Use an internal body feature as evidence.

Answer: A vertebrate is an animal with an internal backbone.

4. A supported comparison

J: 5 g sugar per 100 g. K: 10 g per 100 g. Write one supported comparison.

Keep number, unit and basis together.

Answer: J has less sugar per 100 g than K: 5 g compared with 10 g.

1. Connected structures

How can bones give protection and also help movement?

Use two named examples or describe two different roles.

Answer: The skull or ribs protect organs. Muscles pull bones at joints to create movement.

2. Pulling back

Why does a joint need a pair of muscles for opposite movements?

Explain how the movement can reverse.

Answer: Each muscle actively pulls when it contracts. The partner can pull in the other direction; muscles do not actively push the bone back.

3. Food source

What does the arrow mean in grass → rabbit?

Follow the food and its stored energy.

Answer: Food and energy transfer from the grass to the rabbit when the rabbit eats the grass.

4. Limits of evidence

J has less sugar per 100 g than K. Does this show everything about a healthy diet? Explain.

Separate one measured nutrient from a whole eating pattern.

Answer: No. We also need other nutrient information, amounts eaten and the wider eating pattern.

Open shared evidence and task resources

1 Skeleton and animals

  • The skull surrounds the brain. The ribcage surrounds the heart and lungs.
  • Bones form a supporting framework. Joints allow bones to move when muscles pull.
  • Dog: internal backbone. Earthworm: no internal backbone.

2 A muscle pair

  • Bending the elbow: biceps contracts; triceps relaxes.
  • Straightening the elbow: triceps contracts; biceps relaxes.
  • In this model, contract means shorten and pull. A muscle does not actively push a bone.

3 Nutrition and balance

  • Carbohydrate provides energy. Protein supports growth and repair. Calcium helps build and maintain bones. Foods contain mixtures of nutrients.
  • Labels J and K: sugars 5 g and 10 g respectively, both per 100 g.
  • Invented model test: same height and mass, five similar taps. Narrow feet: stayed upright 2/5 times. Wide feet: 5/5. Only foot spacing was deliberately changed.

4 A food chain

  • Grass makes its own food using light. A rabbit eats grass. A fox can eat a rabbit.
  • A food-chain arrow points from the food to the animal eating it. It shows transfer of food and energy.
  • Animals obtain food by eating. A plant uses light energy to make sugars.
