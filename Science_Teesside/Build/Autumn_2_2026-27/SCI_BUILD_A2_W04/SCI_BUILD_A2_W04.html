Verbatim SoW objective: Identify everyday uses of different rocks (links D&T / Construction).

Arrival · 5 min

Look at a safe sample or roof card.

Offer a safe kit choice and short first–then cue.

Starter · 3 min

Think back. Say, sign or point.

  1. What two checks did we use on rocks?
  2. Name one way to sort rocks.
  3. What helps bones move?

Today I will check:

  • I can name one rock use.
  • I can check one rock feature.
  • I can say why the feature helps.

Ask three low-stakes prior questions; answers in note.

I do · 5 min

Watch one match: feature to job.

Words: rock · hard · slate · property

WordIt meansExample
rocka solid thing from the groundThis is a safe rock sample.
hardnot easy to mark or changeThis hard rock takes little mark.
slatea rock that can split into thin sheetsA slate tile can cover a roof.
propertya thing you can checkThis slate is thin.

Watch: This slate sample is thin. Slate can make a roof tile. I match it to a roof.

Show me: What feature of the slate helped our roof example?

Pre-teach words; show the full model; ask the show-me check.

We do · 7 min

Match each sample to a possible job.

Our turn: This sample leaves a mark on the small board. I match it to ____.

Talk, then sort.

  • Thin slate tile
  • Safe sample that leaves a mark
  • Small firm stone pieces

Ready to sort.

F · help

Point to one job card. Feel one safe sample.

M · say why

Match a sample. Say which feature helps.

H · stretch

Could one rock be good for one job but poor for another?

Hand over the missing step; offer F or H route. Reset once.

We do 2 · 5 min

Choose one answer. All of us show it.

What should come before the job match?

Choose a step.

All of us: A new sample stays firm in a safe pressure test. Which possible job fits that clue?

Say why.

Take one quiet minute to reset.

Take all pupil responses at once; use decision rule and parallel hinge.

Independent · 8 min

Choose a sample and show its use.

Say it first. You can plan in any language.

Point, sign, speak, draw, type, write, use a scribe or a staff-captured photo.

F

Match one sample to one job.

Say: I match ____ with ____.

M

Choose a rock and say what feature helps.

Say: This ____ can do the job as it is ____.

H

Why could a soft rock fail on a busy path?

Say: For a ____, I choose ____ as ____.

Go deeper: Can one rock suit two jobs? Show the need for each job.

Job switch: Choose a sample for a roof and a mark. Test each reason.

Choose one adult for supported pupils, one for group; swap across the week.

Try it with kit · prepared model

Kit: Clean blunt adult-checked rock samples, job cards, tray

  1. Feel one safe face, compare a feature with a job card, then give a reason.

Risk: A rough or sharp sample edge could scratch skin.

Safe: Use clean blunt samples on a tray. Do not taste or make dust; wash hands after.

Check the school risk assessment and exact prepared kit. This is optional model rehearsal, not a claimed core practical.

Review · 4 min

Check your reason. Hear one next step.

Hear feedback (VF) and one next step (NS(+)). Edit one part (//).

Take one quiet minute to reset.

Give VF and one NS(+); invite one self-edit. Reset once.

Exit · 3 min

Tell an adult why your match fits.

Read it back: I am learning to match a rock to a job.

My feedback was… I showed…

Next I need to… try one small fix.

How I know: I can name one rock use. I can check one rock feature. I can say why the feature helps.

Tell your teacher. They will pick our next start.

Take one quiet minute before the next lesson.

Hear feedback and next step, choose next start, then one-minute reset.
