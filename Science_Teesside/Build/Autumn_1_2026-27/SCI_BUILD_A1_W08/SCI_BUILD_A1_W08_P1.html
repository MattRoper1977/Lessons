Animals get food by eating

Learning goal

I can explain that animals get food by eating, and plants can make their own food.

Today I can

  • I can say that plants make their own food.
  • I can say that animals must eat to get theirs.
  • I can put a simple food chain in the right order.

I do · follow the evidence

In your book: Watch the model; point to the evidence. No writing yet.

A green plant is a producer: it makes food using light. An animal is a consumer: it eats to obtain food.

Read grass → rabbit → fox: grass is eaten by the rabbit; the rabbit can be eaten by the fox.

Read leaves → caterpillar → robin in the same way. The arrow points from food to its eater.

    Answers and accepted reasoning

    • The plant makes food using light; the animals get food by eating.
    • A simple model suggests a possible change. Other food sources and links can matter.
    • Food passes from grass to rabbit to fox.
    • Food passes from leaves to caterpillar to robin.
    • Repair to grass → rabbit → fox. Read each arrow as is eaten by.
    • Animals obtain food by eating. Green plants can make their own food using light.
    • Cow → eats grass: the animal obtains food by eating. In a chain, grass points towards the cow.
    • The owl obtains food by eating a small animal; this is an example, not every owl’s complete diet.
    • Exit: grass is the producer. The arrow means is eaten by; food/energy passes to the eater. Both chains begin with a green plant that makes food using light. Animals obtain food by eating. Fewer plants may affect consumers indirectly; other feeding links mean exact numbers cannot be inferred.
    • The leaves are part of the green producer plant, not a separate species. Green plants in these examples make food using light; roots take in water and minerals, not ready-made food. Sunlight supplies energy but is not an edible organism. Producer does not mean every producer must be a plant. Animals obtaining food here is a biological idea, not a claim about human cooking. A paper cover supports a qualified prediction, not ecological measurements. A photograph alone does not establish understanding, and no photography or public sharing is required.
