W1 SCI_BUILD_SU1_W01

  1. Q1. Which part takes water in from the soil? ✓ Roots.↳ The stem.Reveals: Confuses uptake with transport.
  2. Q2. Which part holds the plant upright? ✓ The stem.↳ The flower.Reveals: Confuses support with reproduction.
  3. Q3. Which broad green part catches light? ✓ Leaves.↳ Roots.Reveals: Confuses light capture with soil uptake.
  4. Q4. Which part can lead to seeds after pollination? ✓ Flower.↳ Leaves.Reveals: Confuses reproductive and photosynthetic parts.
  5. Q5. A label arrow points below the soil. Which part is it most likely to show? ✓ Roots; check its position on the diagram.↳ Flower, because every part is a flower.Reveals: Ignores position and structure in a labelled diagram.
  6. Q6. Can colour alone prove which plant part a picture shows? ✓ No; check shape and position too.↳ Yes; all leaves are green.Reveals: Relies on colour alone, excluding varied plant forms.

W2 SCI_BUILD_SU1_W02

  1. Q1. Which part first takes water from soil? ✓ Roots.↳ Leaves.Reveals: Confuses absorption with use of transported water.
  2. Q2. What does a stem do with water? ✓ Carries it through the plant.↳ Makes all the water.Reveals: Confuses transport with production.
  3. Q3. What do leaves take in for photosynthesis? ✓ Light.↳ Soil directly.Reveals: Confuses light capture with root uptake.
  4. Q4. What is one job of a flower? ✓ Helping make seeds.↳ Holding the plant in soil.Reveals: Confuses reproduction with anchoring.
  5. Q5. A stem is cut off from roots. Which job is disrupted? ✓ Carrying root-absorbed water upwards.↳ Taking light in from the air.Reveals: Mislocates water transport.
  6. Q6. Are roots and stems doing the same job? ✓ No; roots absorb and stems transport.↳ Yes; both absorb water from soil.Reveals: Collapses related but distinct functions.

W3 SCI_BUILD_SU1_W03

  1. Q1. Which of these is a plant growth need: light or loud music? ✓ Light.↳ Loud music.Reveals: Mistakes an optional stimulus for a growth requirement.
  2. Q2. Where does a plant get water for growth in the example? ✓ From water supplied to the roots.↳ Only from its flower.Reveals: Mislocates water uptake.
  3. Q3. Which is a suitable growth condition: warmth or permanent freezing? ✓ Suitable warmth.↳ Permanent freezing.Reveals: Ignores temperature requirements.
  4. Q4. What do mineral nutrients help support? ✓ Plant growth.↳ A substitute for all light.Reveals: Treats one requirement as a replacement for another.
  5. Q5. Two similar plants get the same water, but only one gets light. What changed? ✓ Light.↳ Water.Reveals: Does not track the variable changed.
  6. Q6. Can one small comparison prove that all plants need exactly the same conditions? ✓ No; it tests one case under stated conditions.↳ Yes; one comparison proves every plant.Reveals: Overgeneralises from a limited test.

W4 SCI_BUILD_SU1_W04

  1. Q1. If testing water, what should differ between the two pots? ✓ The amount of water.↳ The seed type and the light as well.Reveals: Changes several variables at once.
  2. Q2. Should both pots have the same kind of seed? ✓ Yes, for a fair comparison.↳ No, any different seeds are fine.Reveals: Does not control seed type.
  3. Q3. What would you measure over time? ✓ A stated growth measure such as height.↳ How much you like the plant.Reveals: Confuses measurable outcome with preference.
  4. Q4. What should happen to light during a water test? ✓ Keep it as similar as possible.↳ Change it in only one pot.Reveals: Confounds the water comparison.
  5. Q5. Does a plan to use the allotment prove a plant has grown there? ✓ No; observe and record real results later.↳ Yes; planning is an observed result.Reveals: Treats proposed practical work as evidence.
  6. Q6. One pot is watered and also kept in the dark. Can water alone explain a difference? ✓ No; light changed too.↳ Yes; water is the only cause.Reveals: Claims cause when more than one condition changed.

W5 SCI_BUILD_SU1_W05

  1. Q1. What starts a real plant-growth record? ✓ A dated observation of the actual plant.↳ An invented final height.Reveals: Substitutes made-up results for observation.
  2. Q2. Which pair is easier to compare over time? ✓ The same measure on dated checks.↳ Two unrelated guesses.Reveals: Lacks a consistent measure.
  3. Q3. If a plant does not grow, should you add a made-up number? ✓ No; record what was observed.↳ Yes; make the chart look complete.Reveals: Falsifies an observed result.
  4. Q4. What does an ICT photo record need? ✓ A date and plant or pot label without pupil data.↳ A pupil name and an assumed outcome.Reveals: Conflates evidence with personal data or assumption.
  5. Q5. Can a practice picture prove what happened to your real plant? ✓ No; only actual observations can.↳ Yes; all plants grow the same.Reveals: Transfers simulated evidence to the real investigation.
  6. Q6. Why keep water and light checks consistent? ✓ To interpret changes more fairly.↳ To guarantee an exact height.Reveals: Confuses a fair method with a guaranteed outcome.

W6 SCI_BUILD_SU1_W06

  1. Q1. Where does soil water enter the plant? ✓ Roots.↳ Leaves.Reveals: Reverses water uptake direction.
  2. Q2. Which part carries water up from roots? ✓ Stem.↳ Flower only.Reveals: Misidentifies the transport route.
  3. Q3. What comes next in the main route: soil → roots → ? ✓ Stem.↳ Soil again.Reveals: Cannot sequence the pathway.
  4. Q4. Where can transported water reach? ✓ Leaves.↳ Only the roots.Reveals: Omits movement through the plant.
  5. Q5. Are downward arrows from leaves to soil a good model of uptake? ✓ No; main uptake is soil → roots → stem → leaves.↳ Yes; rain falls down.Reveals: Confuses rain direction with internal transport.
  6. Q6. If a stem is blocked, what part of the route is interrupted? ✓ Movement from roots toward leaves.↳ The making of soil water.Reveals: Misunderstands stem transport.
