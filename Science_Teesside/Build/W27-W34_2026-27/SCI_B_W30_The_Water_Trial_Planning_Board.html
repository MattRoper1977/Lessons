TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Plan a controlled comparison of water amount and plant growth, with repeated plants and consistent records.

Preparation

  • Print the shared evidence, one response route and exit page. Routes are alternatives, not a workload ladder.
  • Read the worked answers. Prepare an oral, pointing or adult-scribed route where useful.
  • The supplied fictional plan compares 5 and 15 mL daily for 10 days. These are teaching values, not universal watering recommendations. For a real investigation, an adult pilots suitable amounts for the species, pots and room, records any changes and checks plant welfare daily.
  • Optional setup can begin today: label plants A1–A3 and B1–B3, record baselines, allocate comparable seedlings across groups, and agree who maintains the record. Future observation time must be scheduled separately.

Method precautions

  • Use non-toxic plants; no tasting or direct handling of fertiliser. Wash hands after handling plants or compost.
  • Keep water in trays away from electrical devices; wipe spills. Use needle-free measuring equipment.
  • An adult checks plants daily. Stop or adjust a real treatment if severe stress develops; record the change honestly. Do not overheat or seal plants in a cupboard.

Practical alternative

All core reasoning uses the supplied evidence and can be completed in 40 minutes without a live practical.

Access and regulation

Supported: Short choices, word bank and pointing or adult-scribed reasons; same core scientific goal.

Standard: Make a short evidence-based explanation or record using the supplied information.

Stretch: Apply the core idea to a changed case, evaluate evidence or identify a limit.

Regulation: Preview the sequence. Offer seated observation, quiet paired rehearsal, private feedback and a pass from public sharing. Routes respond to current access needs, not a diagnosis.

Teacher answers

Retrieval 1–3

1 valid requirement. 2 no: leaf condition/colour also matter. 3 cm.

We do W1–W2

W1 water amount; height change plus leaf condition; relevant matched conditions including species, starting size, pot/compost, light or temperature. W2 prediction is expected; a result comes from an observation or measurement actually made.

Hinge

First option correct. Second has confounds; third invents evidence.

S1–S5

S1 water amount only. S2 two valid controls. S3 three. S4 soil surface to main shoot tip. S5 observed later.

M1–M4

M1 how does daily water amount affect these young bean seedlings over 10 days? M2 5 versus 15 mL daily in the fictional plan; two valid controls. M3 label A1–A3/B1–B3; match and allocate seedlings; record baseline; apply planned water; measure soil-to-tip in cm and leaf condition on days 0/3/7/10 at consistent times; keep all records. M4 any reasoned prediction clearly labelled, plus daily care check and adult action if stress.

T1–T4

T1 species, starting size and light all differ; water is intended change. T2 three comparable seedlings of one species per group, matched pots/compost and growing conditions, different water amounts only. T3 dated baseline and agreed repeat observations with consistent soil-to-shoot-tip cm measurement and leaf notes. T4 prioritise suitable care, stop/adjust treatment as necessary, record exactly what changed; interpret the result with this limitation.

Exit E1–E3

E1 daily water amount. E2 individuals vary; repeats make comparison less dependent on one unusual plant. E3 no.

R1–R2

Responses vary. Credit a scientific revision, evidence-based defence or relevant next question. A private or oral response has equal value.
