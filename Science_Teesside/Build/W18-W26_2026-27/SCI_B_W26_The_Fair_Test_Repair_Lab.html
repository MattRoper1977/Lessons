Slide 1

TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Plan and use a fair test of how card height affects shadow height.

Preparation

  • Fix source-screen distance at 60 cm and card position at 30 cm from source. Keep cards upright, centred and parallel to screen.
  • Pre-cut three cards of the same width and material. Card height is the intended changed factor.
  • Pre-check that the shadow fits the screen and ruler. Provide constructed readings if the practical cannot run.

Method precautions

  • Use a cool low-power LED below face height, never a laser, hot lamp or Sun.
  • Secure stands and card holders, keep walkways lit and clear. Adult checks the practical plan before use.
  • Use pre-cut cards with safe edges. Do not put a ruler or head in the beam near the source.

Practical alternative

Analyse the flawed/repaired plans and supplied constructed repeats. Pupils can complete a fair-test plan and conclusion without taking measurements.

Access and regulation

Supported: Choose one route: short choices, word bank and pointing or adult-scribed explanation; keep the same science goal.

Standard: Use the evidence provided to record a decision and explain the science in short sentences.

Stretch: Apply the same core idea to a changed setup, identify a limitation and justify a better decision.

Regulation: Preview the sequence and equipment. Offer seated observation, a quiet paper route, partner rehearsal and a pass. Never require public performance or a darkened room.

Teacher answers

Retrieval 1–3

1 opaque card. 2 yes, card position can affect size. 3 centimetres (cm).

We do W1–W2

W1 change card height, measure shadow height, control card position (30 cm) and source-screen distance (60 cm), with other relevant properties fixed. W2 retain 9, check method and repeat if useful; overall taller-card pattern remains supported.

Hinge

Second option correct. Also keep material, width, orientation and source the same. First option changes two factors; third tests colour instead of height.

S1–S5

S1 card height. S2 shadow height. S3 30 cm. S4 taller. S5 keep it, check the setup and repeat rather than erasing it.

T1–T5

T1 how does card height affect shadow height? T2 height; shadow height; any two relevant controls. T3 fix source-screen at 60 cm and card at 30 cm, use same material/width/orientation, change height, measure same top-to-bottom dimension, repeat three times and record all readings. T4 e.g. 2 cm card gave 4 cm shadows, 6 cm card gave 12 cm shadows; taller cards made taller shadows here. T5 retain it, check ruler zero/edges/position and repeat if needed.

X1–X4

X1 the 4 cm card includes a 9 cm reading, not exactly double. X2 shadow size also depends on source/card/screen geometry and source size. X3 in this setup, taller cards gave taller shadows, around twice card height. X4 check card position or ruler alignment/edge convention; take another reading for the 4 cm card after the check, retaining the original.

Exit E1–E3

E1 card height. E2 e.g. card-source distance, source-screen distance, card material/width/orientation or source. E3 no; keep honest readings and investigate differences.

Optional practical

Accept actual observations, including imperfect proportionality. Do not replace measurements with constructed sample values. Assess the fair comparison and evidence-based conclusion.

R1–R2

Responses vary. Credit a science-related revision or relevant next question. A pass or private response is acceptable; preferences have no fixed right answer.
