👩‍🏫 I Do · watch me think

Watch me find the backbone

🧠 Watch — do not write yet. Nothing appears until I click. Tell me what you can see.
backbone — a line of small bones down the middle of the back.
vertebrate — an animal that HAS a backbone.
👩‍🏫 I Do · the other kind

Now watch me NOT find one

🧠 Watch me think: Same test, same place. Look down the middle. If there is no line of bones, it is the other kind.
invertebrate — an animal that has NO backbone.
skeleton — the hard parts that hold a body up. Ours is inside. A crab’s is outside.
❌ Common mistake: Big animals have backbones, small ones don’t.
✅ Actually: Size is not the test. A mouse is tiny and has a backbone. A lobster is big and has none.

Knowledge Organiser — Backbones and No Backbones

BUILD · White Rose Science Year 3 · Week 3

Key wordWhat it means
backboneA line of small bones down the middle of the back.
vertebrateAn animal that HAS a backbone.
invertebrateAn animal that has NO backbone.
skeletonAll the bones in a body, working together.
supportHolding something up so it keeps its shape.

Key facts

  • A backbone is not one bone. It is many small bones in a line.
  • Fish, birds, frogs, lizards and mammals are vertebrates.
  • Insects, worms, snails, crabs and spiders are invertebrates.
  • No backbone does not mean no support. A snail has a shell. A crab has a hard case. A worm is held up by the liquid inside it.

Success criteria

  • I can find the backbone on a skeleton picture.
  • I can name two animals with a backbone and two without.
  • I can say one way an animal without a backbone is held up.
