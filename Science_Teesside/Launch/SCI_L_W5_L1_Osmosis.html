Osmosis: water across a membrane

Learning intention

I can predict and explain the net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane.

Success looks like

  • Identify the moving substance as water.
  • Locate the more dilute and more concentrated solutions.
  • Explain net movement through a partially permeable membrane.

Point, speak, sign, arrange cards, draw, write or direct an adult. You may pass or leave an answer blank.

Notice, choose, explain

A membrane lets water through but not sugar. What crosses it during osmosis?

Think quietly, point to evidence or share a first idea. You may pass.

Read the complete explainer transcript
  1. Two solutions are separated by a partially permeable membrane.
  2. The left solution contains less solute: it is more dilute.
  3. The right solution contains more solute: it is more concentrated.
  4. Water moves through the membrane in both directions.
  5. There is net water movement from the more dilute left side to the more concentrated right side.
  6. Solute stays on its side in this model. Use the full water-and-membrane explanation.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. A plant cell is placed in a solution more dilute than its cell contents.
  2. Water has net movement into the cell by osmosis through its membrane.
  3. The vacuole expands and the contents press against the cell wall; the cell becomes more turgid.
  4. The supporting wall limits expansion. Do not describe the wall itself as the selectively controlling membrane.

Check a common mistake

Dilute means low solute concentration. Saying “water moves from high to low concentration” is ambiguous unless you say concentration of what.

Before the reveal, predict the next step or explain why it follows.

Knowledge organiser

Learning intention: I can predict and explain the net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane.

Success looks like

  • Identify the moving substance as water.
  • Locate the more dilute and more concentrated solutions.
  • Explain net movement through a partially permeable membrane.

Remember

  • Osmosis concerns water, not the movement of the dissolved solute.
  • A partially permeable membrane is needed for osmosis.
  • Water moves both ways, with net movement from the more dilute to the more concentrated solution.
  • Water movement into or out of plant cells can change tissue mass.

Words that help

osmosis — Net movement of water through a partially permeable membrane from a more dilute to a more concentrated solution.

solute — A substance dissolved in a solvent.

dilute — Containing relatively little dissolved solute.

partially permeable — Allowing some particles through but not others.

Check this idea: Dilute means low solute concentration. Saying “water moves from high to low concentration” is ambiguous unless you say concentration of what.

Knowledge organiser · Osmosis: water across a membrane

Learning intention: I can predict and explain the net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane.

Success looks like

  • Identify the moving substance as water.
  • Locate the more dilute and more concentrated solutions.
  • Explain net movement through a partially permeable membrane.

Remember

  • Osmosis concerns water, not the movement of the dissolved solute.
  • A partially permeable membrane is needed for osmosis.
  • Water moves both ways, with net movement from the more dilute to the more concentrated solution.
  • Water movement into or out of plant cells can change tissue mass.

Words that help

osmosis — Net movement of water through a partially permeable membrane from a more dilute to a more concentrated solution.

solute — A substance dissolved in a solvent.

dilute — Containing relatively little dissolved solute.

partially permeable — Allowing some particles through but not others.

Check this idea: Dilute means low solute concentration. Saying “water moves from high to low concentration” is ambiguous unless you say concentration of what.

Staff answers · Arrival · Stretch

1. Why must you name the substance when discussing a concentration gradient?

Different substances can have different gradients.

2. How does dilute differ from concentrated?

A dilute solution has less solute per amount of solution than a concentrated one.

3. Are the plant cell wall and membrane the same?

No. They are distinct structures with different functions.

4. Does allowing water through mean every dissolved substance can cross?

No. Partial permeability means passage is selective.

Staff key · Shared work

Needed in an osmosis explanation: Water moves.; A partially permeable membrane is present.; Net water movement: dilute → concentrated..

Not correct for this model: Sugar crosses instead of water.; The membrane allows every particle through.; Water moves only one way at all times..

Worked example

  1. A plant cell is placed in a solution more dilute than its cell contents.
  2. Water has net movement into the cell by osmosis through its membrane.
  3. The vacuole expands and the contents press against the cell wall; the cell becomes more turgid.
  4. The supporting wall limits expansion. Do not describe the wall itself as the selectively controlling membrane.

Evidence choices

The solution outside a cell is more concentrated than its contents. What is the net water movement?
Out of the cell, through its partially permeable membrane.

What passes through the membrane in this osmosis model?
Water; the model solute cannot pass.
