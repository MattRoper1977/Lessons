LAUNCH SCIENCE · KNOWLEDGE ORGANISER

Osmosis: water across a membrane

Learning objective: I can predict net water movement across a partially permeable membrane.

Success looks like

  • Name water as the moving substance.
  • Compare the two solutions before drawing the net arrow.
  • Include the partially permeable membrane in an explanation.

Keep these ideas

  • For these models, water crosses the membrane but the represented solute does not.
  • Net water movement is from the more dilute solution to the more concentrated solution.
  • Water molecules move in both directions; net refers to the overall transfer.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

osmosis — Net water movement through a partially permeable membrane from a more dilute to a more concentrated solution.

solute — A substance dissolved in a solvent.

dilute — Containing less solute per volume than the comparison solution.

partially permeable — Allows some substances through more readily than others.

Worked example

Outside: more dilute. Inside: more concentrated.

Moving substance: water.

Barrier: partially permeable cell membrane.

Prediction: net water enters the cell by osmosis.

A useful correction
Osmosis describes water movement. Check the model membrane and the two solutions.

Use the membrane model and paper arrows. No food soaking is required in this concept lesson.

Models are qualitative and not to scale. Real membrane permeability depends on molecular properties and transport proteins; avoid the rule that all small substances always pass.
