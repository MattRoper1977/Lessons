Opening

Source evidence: trace every claim

Learning objective

Paraphrase and compare source evidence, keeping supported claims separate from inference and uncertainty.

40 minutes · one route · start from what you show today. Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

Four clues to start

Supported · 4 questions

1. Attribution

What should you record beside a source claim?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Who says it and where it can be found.

Answer guidance

The publisher/title or source identifier and the relevant location/link.

2. Paraphrase

Does paraphrase mean changing just one word?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Explain the meaning in your own wording.

Answer guidance

No; it means expressing the meaning accurately in your own words.

3. Model

For two carriers of a recessive condition, what is the model chance of an affected child in each pregnancy?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Use the one-in-four model.

Answer guidance

25%.

4. Limit

Does a 25% inheritance probability predict exact symptom severity?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Inheritance and severity are different questions.

Answer guidance

No.

Full transcript

Find. Start with a bounded question and two approved NHS sources about cystic fibrosis.

Label. Record publisher, title, purpose, date information and the precise claim each source supports.

Map. Connect inheritance, a biological effect and one limitation to the research question.

Keep a gap visible. A source can explain inheritance without predicting an individual’s future. State what remains uncertain.

Worked example

Paraphrase the idea “severity varies” without making a prediction about an individual.

Step 1

Locate the relevant source statement.

Step 2

Keep its meaning: people can have different effects or severity.

Step 3

Write: “The effects are not identical for every person” (Source B).

Step 4

Do not add an unsupported claim about who will be affected most.

Claim to checkSourceLimit
CF involves altered CFTR copies______

A particular person will have severe symptoms______

Source A · NHS public information

Cystic fibrosis is an inherited condition. Altered gene copies inherited from both parents affect the movement of salt and water in cells, causing thick, sticky mucus. This can affect the lungs and digestive system. Care is individualised. This summary does not predict any one person’s symptoms.

Accessed 16 September 2026. Page review date is not asserted in this pack.

Open full source (internet)

Source B · NHS Genomics Education

Cystic fibrosis usually results from pathogenic variants in both copies of CFTR. It follows autosomal recessive inheritance. When both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 25% chance of a child with the condition, a 50% chance of a carrier, and a 25% chance of neither altered copy in this model. Severity varies.

Reviewed 12 March 2025; next review 12 March 2028. Accessed 16 September 2026.

Open full source (internet)
Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

CFTR: Source B, but many variants and individual effects vary. Thick mucus: Source A, but it does not predict each individual. Severe symptoms for one person: not supported; leave this claim out or state the gap.

Knowledge organiser

LAUNCH · W14 · Lesson 2 · Shared resource

Learning objective

Paraphrase and compare source evidence, keeping supported claims separate from inference and uncertainty.

Key words

Source — Where information comes from.

Publisher — The organisation responsible for a source.

Paraphrase — Explain the meaning using your own wording.

Carrier — Has one altered allele for a recessive condition in this model.

Autosomal recessive — A condition usually requires two altered alleles on a non-sex chromosome pair.

Uncertainty — A limit or gap in what the source establishes.

Remember

• Use trusted, relevant sources and check their date and purpose.

• Cystic fibrosis usually involves altered CFTR alleles inherited from both parents.

• Inheritance probability does not predict one person’s exact symptoms.

• Use respectful, person-first wording and fictional examples only.

Success looks like

• Paraphrase accurately and label the source.

• Compare agreement and differences in emphasis.

• Remove or qualify unsupported individual predictions.
