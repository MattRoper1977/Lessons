Accuracy includes limits

Receive the question without requiring public disclosure or immediate performance.
Revise when the evidence supports a clearer or more accurate statement.
Hold the wording when the question asks for diagnosis, advice, personal data or evidence the sources do not provide.
ReviseThe audience notices a missing source credit, unclear term or wording stronger than the matrix supports.
HoldThe audience asks for certainty, diagnosis, treatment advice or personal/family information.
Add a gapThe question is scientific but the two-source set cannot answer it. Record the gap and a future source-check action.
Keep authorshipThe learner chooses the change and explains why. Adult exact-word scribing remains labelled as support.

An audience response becomes influence only when a visible, evidence-justified change or hold is recorded. Praise without a next decision does not complete the loop.

Independent · evidence

Make the evidence trail usable

Create and deliver the presentation through the agreed private or small-audience route. Include the question, inheritance explanation, two credited source findings, variation/uncertainty and the boundary. Record the audience question and your evidence-led revise/hold decision.

A · BuildCreate four clear sections: question, Foundation biology, two-source evidence comparison, and variation/uncertainty with source credits.
B · DeliverUse the agreed written, narrated, recorded or private route. Do not require public performance or a personal connection.
C · ReceiveTake one approved audience question. Answer from evidence, ask for clarification, hold, or redirect safely.
D · Record influenceWrite the question, the decision to revise or hold, the evidence reason and the exact visible change or future check.
1 · Learner decisionPresentation structure, delivery route and response choice.
2 · Observable scienceAn evidence-led explanation with two credits, variation and uncertainty.
3 · Learner meaningWhy the audience response caused a revision, a hold or a named source gap.
End check: every scientific statement has a clear role: Foundation explanation, source evidence, reasoning, variation or limit. Remove decorative claims that have no source trail.
Opening checkState the bounded question, why it matters to the C44 research outcome and which approved sources will be used. Do not open with a dramatic personal story, an unverified statistic or a question that invites the audience to disclose health or family information.
Science checkExplain the Foundation relationship among gene, allele, inherited combination, probability and variation before adding detail. Keep source evidence separate from learner reasoning. A presentation should help the audience follow the biology, not reward technical vocabulary that has not been explained.
Credit checkPlace a short publisher and local-locator credit beside each used finding, then keep the fuller source trail in the evidence matrix. An image, diagram or number needs the same trace as prose. Remove material whose origin or relevance cannot be checked.
Audience checkInvite a question about the science or evidence, not about anyone's body or family. Receive it, classify it as answer, clarify, hold or redirect, and state the reason. A respectful hold is stronger than a confident answer the approved sources do not support.

Final rehearsal: point to the exact place where the presentation shows inherited biology, two-source evidence, variation, uncertainty and the privacy boundary. Rehearse the planned audience route and accessible response mode. Finish with one bounded conclusion and one visible gap, then name who owns any further source check and when its outcome will be reported back.
