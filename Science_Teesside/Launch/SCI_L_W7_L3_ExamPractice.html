1. Name two structures shared by typical plant and animal cells. Use the words membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus to help. [2]

Help with this question
Name structures, not microscope parts.

2. An image is 18 mm long. The actual cell is 60 µm long. Calculate magnification. Show working. [3] Use: 18 mm = 18,000 µm; magnification = image ÷ actual. Work out the answer.

Help with this question
Put both lengths in the same unit.

3. State what higher resolution allows a microscope to do. [1] Choose and explain: bigger image / distinguish closer points.

Help with this question
Resolution concerns detail.

4. Explain diffusion. Include random motion, movement in both directions and the net direction. [3] Use: particles move randomly; overall more move from __ to __ because __.

Help with this question
Include random movement and the overall result.

5. Choose thin walls, large area or blood supply. Explain its effect.

Help with this question
Feature → effect.

6. Potato tissue changes from 2.00 g to 2.30 g. Calculate percentage change in mass. [3] Use (2.30 − 2.00) ÷ 2.00 × 100. Work out the result and sign.

Help with this question
Use initial mass as the denominator.

7. Explain how mineral ions can enter a root cell from lower outside to higher inside concentration. [3] Use active transport; against the gradient; respiration. Build a complete explanation.

Help with this question
Name process, direction and energy source.

8. Give two relevant controls for comparing potato pieces in different sucrose concentrations. [2] Choose two: immersion time, temperature, sucrose concentration.

Help with this question
Keep other causes of change comparable.

9. An unusual repeat conflicts with the other results. Give one appropriate next step. [1] Choose: investigate and repeat / silently delete.

Help with this question
Check evidence before discarding it.

Answers are now visible. Use these for review, not as evidence of an independent first attempt.

1. Award 1 each for membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria or ribosomes; maximum 2.

2. 1: 18 mm = 18,000 µm (or 60 µm = 0.06 mm). 1: image ÷ actual. 1: 300×. Allow correct alternative working; do not penalise the same conversion error twice when method is clear.

3. 1: distinguish closer points/structures as separate or reveal finer distinguishable detail. “Makes bigger” alone does not earn the mark.

4. Award one mark each: particles move randomly; particles move in both directions; overall/net movement is from higher to lower concentration. Do not award the same gradient point twice.

5. 1: valid feature, such as thin walls, large area, ventilation or blood supply. 1: linked effect, such as short path, more exchange surface or maintaining a gradient.

6. 1: change +0.30 g. 1: change ÷ initial × 100. 1: +15%. Accept correct alternative working and sensible error-carried-forward method credit.

7. 1: active transport. 1: movement against the concentration gradient. 1: energy from respiration.

8. 1 each for suitable controls such as immersion time, temperature, solution volume, tissue dimensions/source or consistent blotting; maximum 2. Do not accept concentration, which is deliberately changed.

9. 1: investigate and repeat/check labelling, weighing or blotting while retaining the record; or another justified check. Do not credit simply deleting it because it differs.

method mark — Credit for a valid calculation step, even when a later error occurs.

review — Check a response and improve a specific point.

confidence in evidence — How strongly the response supports a conclusion about learning.

1: 18 mm = 18,000 µm (or 60 µm = 0.06 mm). 1: image ÷ actual. 1: 300×. Allow correct alternative working; do not penalise the same conversion error twice when method is clear.

Before you teach: Print the selected paper route and keep the mark scheme separate. Use the first 16 minutes for settling and a different worked example; reserve 21 minutes for the paper and 3 minutes for an exit/review plan. Offer usual access arrangements, not forced handwriting. Read questions without teaching the answer during the independent window. Supported prompts alter independence and must be recorded by staff. Adapt the time where needed rather than making speed the assessed skill.

Start from what pupils show: use the four arrival checks. Do not assume earlier work was taught or completed. Model first where needed; invite a reason or transfer where it is familiar.
