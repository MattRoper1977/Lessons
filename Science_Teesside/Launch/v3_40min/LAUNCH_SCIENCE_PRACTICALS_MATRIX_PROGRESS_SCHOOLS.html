Practicals & Equipment Matrix · W3–W7

One page per half-term: what each investigation needs, how it is made safe, and the two routes that keep every pupil in the practical — a digital alternative and a no-equipment fallback. A pupil who cannot handle materials still directs the investigation, and directing it is the assessed skill.

Safety authority. No CLEAPSS approval is claimed for any activity on this page. Each activity requires a local, proportionate risk assessment before use, and local COSHH and risk-assessment decisions determine the actual controls — a headteacher signature alone is not sufficient. Cork borers, scalpels and any other cutting or boring tool are adult-performed where the setting's risk assessment says so, and are not placed on the pupil bench. Solutions are adult-prepared and labelled. Nothing in any of these lessons is eaten, tasted or drunk, and no breath, blood or other body sample is collected at any point.
Two routes, not one. Every lesson carries a digital alternative and a no-equipment fallback. Neither is a reduced version: the Biology goal is identical and only the response route changes. The digital column names only routes verified present in the live lesson files, and says where one is still awaiting sign-off rather than advertising it as available.
This is Topic 1 teaching, not exam administration. No mark schemes, mark allocations or grade boundaries appear anywhere in this suite or on this page. W7L3 is independent practice with feedback — it is explicitly not run under exam conditions.

W3L1Prepared-Slide Microscope InvestigationLight microscope; prepared slide; lens tissue; observation sheet; pencil.Microscope, slides and lens tissue holder reusable. Lens tissue and any replaced slide are consumable.Adult sets the microscope on a firm bench, away from the edge, and starts it on the LOWEST power. Check no slide is cracked before it is issued.4 minKeep the microscope back from the bench edge. Hold slides by their sides. A cracked slide is never issued or used. Adults handle any replacement.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons that walk the low-power-first routine and separate magnification from resolution.The pupil directs the observation: says what to look for, what power to use and what has been seen, while an adult operates the microscope. The observation and its explanation stay the pupil's.
W3L2Printed Micrograph Scale InvestigationPrinted cell images; rulers; calculators; unit conversion card; pencil.Rulers, calculators and laminated cards reusable. Unlaminated micrographs consumable.Adult issues one image at a time and displays the conversion 1 mm = 1000 µm before any calculation starts.3 minOrdinary rulers and calculators only. Keep the working area clear so images are not torn or lost.In-lesson Local calculation lab — enter image and real size, get magnification, with the unit-matching rule stated on screen.The pupil chooses which measurement to take and states the calculation; an adult measures and writes to dictation. The decision about which quantity to divide is the assessed part.
W3L3Magnification Question CircuitQuestion cards; rulers; calculators; conversion card; paper.Cards, rulers and calculators reusable. Printed question sheets consumable.Adult sets out three question stations and keeps model answers hidden until a pupil has made a full attempt.4 minKeep the room calm. No public scores, no leaderboards and no speed pressure — this is a reasoning circuit, not a race.In-lesson Local calculation lab — one problem at a time, with the method line shown so the working stays visible.The pupil dictates the method step by step and an adult scribes it. Producing a checkable method is the assessment, not the handwriting.
W4L1Food-Colour Diffusion ObservationClear cups; cool and warm water; food colour; timer; white card backing.Cups, timer and card reusable. Water and food colour consumable.An adult sets up the matched cups and pours all water. Use warm water, never hot. Fill both cups to the same level so the only deliberate difference is temperature.5 minThe adult handles all warm water. Nothing in the lab is drunk or tasted. Wipe spills immediately — a wet floor is the real hazard here.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons for random motion, net movement and the factors that change the rate.The pupil calls the start, watches and describes what spreads and how fast, while an adult handles every cup. Describing the net movement is the assessed skill.
W4L2Alveolus Adaptation ModelLarge and small bubble-wrap sheets or paper models; thin card; arrow cards; feature cards.Model kit reusable. Paper or film consumable if remade.Adult lays out clean model parts. No breath, blood or body samples are collected or used at any point.4 minModels only. Do not collect breath, saliva, blood or any body sample. Pupils with latex or material sensitivities use the card model.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons linking surface area, thinness and the gradient to the rate.The pupil directs which feature to add to the model and explains what it does to the rate; an adult assembles it. The explanation is the assessment.
W4L3Explanation Construction CircuitSentence-frame cards; example answers; annotated diagrams; paper.Cards and diagrams reusable if laminated. Printed frames consumable.Adult prepares one incomplete explanation for pupils to finish, and keeps the full version hidden until an attempt exists.3 minDesk-based lesson with no equipment hazard. Keep the task unranked and give thinking time before any verbal prompt.In-lesson Command-word decoder — separates what the question asks you to DO from the Biology, one command word at a time.The pupil says the explanation aloud in order and an adult scribes the exact words. Linking cause to effect is the assessed skill, not writing it down.
W5L1Membrane DemonstrationVisking tubing or a modelled membrane; two solutions; beaker; stand; timer.Stand, beaker and timer reusable. Tubing and solutions consumable.Adult prepares and fills the tubing and ties both ends. Pupils observe and predict; they do not fill or cut anything.5 minAdult-performed demonstration. Nothing is tasted. Wash hands afterwards. Follow the local risk assessment for the solutions in use.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons for the partially permeable membrane and the direction of water movement.The pupil predicts the direction of water movement and explains it from the model; the demonstration is adult-run for everyone anyway, so no pupil is treated differently.
W5L2Potato Osmosis Core PracticalAdult-prepared matched potato cylinders; pre-soaked samples; labelled solutions; balance; forceps; paper towel; eye protection as the local risk assessment requires.Balances, forceps and vessels reusable. Potato samples and prepared solutions consumable.All cutting and boring is adult-performed before the lesson. Use pre-soaked matched samples so a mass change is measurable within the lesson. Label every solution. Follow the local risk assessment.6 minCork borers and scalpels are adult-only tools and are not on the pupil bench. Nothing is eaten or tasted. Clean spills at once and wash hands at the end. Eye protection per local policy.In-lesson Percentage-change lab (enter two masses, get the change) plus the Osmosis prediction lab — button-only, three solutions held side by side. The prediction lab is a specimen awaiting sign-off.The pupil chooses the solutions to compare, predicts each direction and calculates from the class dataset while an adult handles all samples and the balance. The prediction, the calculation and the evaluation are the assessment.
W5L3Graph and Method ReviewPrepared data; graph paper; ruler; calculator; method review cards.Rulers, calculators and cards reusable. Printed graph and data sheets consumable.Adult gives the same dataset to everyone, and marks the axes and units before any plotting begins.3 minDesk-based. Rulers and calculators used normally. Keep the task calm and unranked — this lesson judges a method, not a person.In-lesson Osmosis data studio — plot the series, read the trend and locate the zero-change crossing.The pupil states each plotted point and where the line crosses zero while an adult draws. Reading the pattern and evaluating the method is the assessed part.
W6L1Against-the-Gradient DemonstrationGradient model cards; counters or beads; two labelled zones; energy tokens.All reusable. Printed zone cards consumable if unlaminated.Adult lays out the two zones and the counters. Keep the energy tokens visibly separate so the cost of the movement stays obvious.3 minDesk-based, but not equipment-free. Small counters and beads are a choking risk — check suitability for the group and supervise throughout.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons for movement against the gradient and the respiration that pays for it.The pupil directs which counter moves where and states what the movement costs; an adult moves the pieces. Naming the energy source is the assessment.

Highlighted copies consumable.Adult prepares the oxygen-effect dataset in advance so pupils meet the evidence, not the experiment. No plant or animal tissue is dissected.3 minDesk-based, evidence only. No dissection, no live material, no body samples.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons linking cell shape, surface area and respiration to the uptake evidence.The pupil says which data support active transport and why; an adult highlights to dictation. Interpreting the evidence is the assessed skill.
W6L3Transport Decision CircuitScenario cards; three process labels; comparison grid; paper.Cards, labels and grid reusable. Printed grids consumable.Adult shuffles the scenario cards so the order is not predictable, and keeps the answer key hidden until a full attempt exists.3 minDesk-based with no equipment hazard. Keep decisions private — no public sorting into right and wrong.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons for the two questions that separate the three processes.The pupil classifies each scenario aloud with a reason and an adult places the card. W7L2Command-Word Decoding CircuitCommand-word cards; question cards; answer-shape frames; paper.Cards and frames reusable if laminated. Printed frames consumable.Adult pairs each command word with a question on the same Biology, so the only thing changing is the instruction.3 minDesk-based with no hazard. No timed conditions and no public marking — this lesson decodes questions, it does not test them.In-lesson Command-word decoder — choose a command word and see what the answer has to do differently.The pupil says what the command word demands and an adult scribes the shaped answer. W7L3Independent Question Practice and ImprovementQuestion set; answer paper; improvement frame; pen.Question sets reusable if laminated. Answer paper consumable.Adult prepares the question set and one improvement frame per pupil. Keep any worked response hidden until an attempt exists.3 minDesk-based. Not run under exam conditions: no timing pressure, no silence rule and no public results. Pupils may pause at any point.In-lesson Scientific reasoning model — reveal-step buttons for building an answer and then improving one.The pupil dictates the answer and then dictates the improvement, with an adult scribing both.
