Gas exchange: built for diffusion

Learning intention

I can explain how alveoli and their blood supply support gas exchange by diffusion.

Success looks like

  • Show oxygen and carbon dioxide moving in opposite net directions.
  • Link thin walls and large area to diffusion.
  • Explain how ventilation and blood flow maintain gradients.

Point, speak, sign, arrange cards, draw, write or direct an adult. You may pass or leave an answer blank.

Starter · 3 min

Notice, choose, explain

Does oxygen move into blood because alveoli push it through their walls?

Think quietly, point to evidence or share a first idea. You may pass.

Teacher reveal · check the first idea
No. Oxygen diffuses down its concentration gradient across the thin exchange surface.
Words that help: alveolus · capillary · ventilation · diffusion distance

  1. Alveolar air and arriving blood are separated by thin exchange surfaces.
  2. Oxygen is higher in alveolar air than in the arriving blood.
  3. Oxygen has net diffusion from the air into the blood.
  4. Carbon dioxide is higher in arriving blood than in alveolar air.
  5. Carbon dioxide has net diffusion from blood into the air.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. Feature: the alveolar wall is thin.
  2. This gives a short diffusion distance.
  3. Therefore oxygen can diffuse into the blood rapidly, when a gradient is present.
  4. “Thin walls are good” names a feature but does not explain its effect.

Check a common mistake

Breathing moves air; diffusion moves gases across the exchange surface. These are related but different processes.

Before the reveal, predict the next step or explain why it follows.

Knowledge organiser

Learning intention: I can explain how alveoli and their blood supply support gas exchange by diffusion.

Success looks like

  • Show oxygen and carbon dioxide moving in opposite net directions.
  • Link thin walls and large area to diffusion.
  • Explain how ventilation and blood flow maintain gradients.

Remember

  • Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into blood when its concentration is higher in the air.
  • Carbon dioxide diffuses from arriving blood into alveolar air when its concentration is higher in the blood.
  • Many alveoli provide a large surface area; thin alveolar and capillary walls provide a short diffusion path.
  • Ventilation refreshes air and blood flow carries gases, helping maintain concentration differences.

Words that help

alveolus — A tiny air sac in the lungs; plural alveoli.

capillary — A very small blood vessel with a thin wall.

ventilation — Movement of air into and out of the lungs.

diffusion distance — The length of the path a substance must cross.

Check this idea: Breathing moves air; diffusion moves gases across the exchange surface. These are related but different processes.

Knowledge organiser · Gas exchange: built for diffusion

Learning intention: I can explain how alveoli and their blood supply support gas exchange by diffusion.

Success looks like

  • Show oxygen and carbon dioxide moving in opposite net directions.
  • Link thin walls and large area to diffusion.
  • Explain how ventilation and blood flow maintain gradients.

Remember

  • Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into blood when its concentration is higher in the air.
  • Carbon dioxide diffuses from arriving blood into alveolar air when its concentration is higher in the blood.
  • Many alveoli provide a large surface area; thin alveolar and capillary walls provide a short diffusion path.
  • Ventilation refreshes air and blood flow carries gases, helping maintain concentration differences.

Words that help

alveolus — A tiny air sac in the lungs; plural alveoli.

capillary — A very small blood vessel with a thin wall.

ventilation — Movement of air into and out of the lungs.

diffusion distance — The length of the path a substance must cross.

Check this idea: Breathing moves air; diffusion moves gases across the exchange surface. These are related but different processes.

Staff key · Shared work

Larger area: Many small alveoli; Folded exchange surface.

Shorter diffusion path: Thin alveolar wall; Thin capillary wall.

Maintains a gradient: Ventilation refreshes air; Blood flow carries oxygen away.

Worked example

  1. Feature: the alveolar wall is thin.
  2. This gives a short diffusion distance.
  3. Therefore oxygen can diffuse into the blood rapidly, when a gradient is present.
  4. “Thin walls are good” names a feature but does not explain its effect.

Evidence choices

Oxygen concentration is higher in alveolar air than arriving blood. Which direction is net movement?
From alveolar air into the blood.

Which best explains a thin exchange surface?
It provides a short diffusion distance.

Staff answers · You do · Standard

1. Complete both net gas directions in the table.

Oxygen: alveolus → blood. Carbon dioxide: blood → alveolus.

2. Explain how a thin exchange surface helps.

It gives a short diffusion distance, allowing rapid gas exchange.

3. Explain the benefit of many alveoli.

They provide a large total surface area for gas exchange.

4. Explain how blood flow helps maintain oxygen diffusion.

Blood carries oxygen away and brings blood with lower oxygen concentration, helping maintain the gradient.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 4

Staff answers · You do · Stretch

1. Explain why opposite arrows are not contradictory.

Each gas has its own concentration gradient, so their net diffusion can be in opposite directions.

2. Predict the effect of a thicker exchange barrier, keeping other factors the same.

It increases diffusion distance and can reduce the rate of gas transfer.

3. Why is “large lungs” less precise than “large exchange surface area”?

The area across which gases diffuse is the relevant feature, not total organ size alone.

4. Compare the roles of ventilation and blood flow.

Ventilation refreshes alveolar air; blood flow refreshes the blood side. Both help maintain gas gradients.
