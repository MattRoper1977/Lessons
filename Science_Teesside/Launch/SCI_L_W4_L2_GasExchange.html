Read the full explainer transcript
  1. Air reaches many tiny alveoli.
  2. Incoming blood has less oxygen than alveolar air.
  3. Oxygen diffuses across the thin barrier into the blood.
  4. Carbon dioxide has the opposite gradient and diffuses into the alveolus.
  5. Many alveoli give a large area; thin walls give a short path.
  6. Ventilation and flowing blood help maintain the gradients.

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. Claim: a thin alveolar wall helps oxygen enter blood quickly.
  2. Feature: the wall is thin.
  3. Link: this shortens the distance oxygen travels.
  4. Outcome: diffusion can be faster for the same gradient and area.

Watch for this mistake

Knowledge organiser

Knowledge organiser

LAUNCH SCIENCE · KNOWLEDGE ORGANISER

Diffusion in the lungs

Learning objective: I can explain gas exchange in alveoli and link an adaptation to faster diffusion.

Success looks like

  • Show the directions of oxygen and carbon dioxide movement.
  • Link a large area or thin wall to diffusion.
  • Keep a conclusion about supplied gas-test evidence within its limits.

Keep these ideas

  • Oxygen normally diffuses from alveolar air into incoming blood.
  • Carbon dioxide normally diffuses from incoming blood into alveolar air.
  • A large exchange area and short diffusion distance support rapid exchange.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

alveolus — A tiny air sac in a lung; plural: alveoli.

capillary — A very small blood vessel.

ventilation — Moving air into and out of the lungs.

adaptation — A feature that helps a structure do its job.

Worked example

Claim: a thin alveolar wall helps oxygen enter blood quickly.

Feature: the wall is thin.

Link: this shortens the distance oxygen travels.

Outcome: diffusion can be faster for the same gradient and area.

A useful correction
Breathing ventilates the lungs; gases cross the exchange surface by diffusion.
