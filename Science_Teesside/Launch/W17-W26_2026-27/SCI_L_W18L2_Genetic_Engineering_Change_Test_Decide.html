This is a paper evaluation. Crop-trial numbers are constructed sample data, not observations from a named commercial product.

Mechanism

Some Bt crops contain added genetic information originating from Bacillus thuringiensis. The crop can produce a protein that harms particular insect pests. It does not become a bacterium and is not protected from every pest.

Possible resistance risk

Pests vary. Inherited variants that survive exposure may reproduce, so resistance can become more common over generations. Monitoring and resistance-management strategies aim to delay this.

Evidence limits

The trial covers one site and one season. It reports damage and yield, not food safety or effects on every non-target species. Neither crop received target-insect spray. All applications need evidence relevant to the crop, trait and setting.

TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Describe genetic engineering and use evidence to weigh a specific benefit, a risk and an unanswered question.

Preparation

  • Explain that the crop trial is a constructed sample, not a claim about a named product or UK approval.
  • Print the evidence page; no online reading is needed for this lesson.
  • Keep mechanism teaching brief and concrete. Optional enzyme detail is for pupils ready for Higher-tier material, outside the Foundation core gate.

Method precautions

  • Paper and screen lesson; no live organisms, biological cultures or chemical work.

Practical alternative

All evidence is supplied for offline use. Discuss, point, annotate or dictate instead of cutting cards or writing extended prose.

Access and regulation

Supported: Same scientific objective, with chunked cards, word bank, read-aloud access and short evidence stems. Accept accurately explained oral or annotated responses.

Standard: Use two evidence sources and a reasoned explanation independently.

Stretch: Evaluate limitations, explain a mechanism and transfer to unfamiliar evidence.

Regulation: Offer solo or partner working and a quiet response route. Preview the sequence; allow a brief reset and re-entry at the next numbered task. No compulsory performance or timed competition.

Teacher answers

R1–R3 retrieval

R1 a gene is a section of DNA containing genetic information; many code for proteins. R2 humans choose which organisms reproduce for desired inherited traits. R3 yes: inherited variants that survive and reproduce can become more common in later generations.

W1 / S2 / M3 data comparisons

Constructed trial: damage falls from30 to8 plants per100, a difference of22 plants per100 (22 percentage points if expressed as percentages). Mean yield rises from6.9 to7.9 t/ha, a difference of1.0 t/ha. These are means in one site/season, not universal outcomes. Optional relative damage reduction22/30×100≈73%; do not confuse this with22 percentage points.

W2 justified claim / S3 benefit

“This trial shows a promising benefit here” is supported: less target-pest damage and higher mean yield. “Success everywhere” or “proof that all GM crops are safe” is not. Neither group received target-insect spray, so this table does not directly measure a reduction in spraying. Future reduced spray use is a possible benefit requiring its own comparison.

W3 next checks

Repeat across sites/seasons tests whether the result generalises; monitor resistance tests whether pest susceptibility/effectiveness changes across generations. Crop colour is not relevant to the stated performance question without a further mechanism. Online likes measure popularity, not biological outcomes. These two checks still leave questions such as non-target effects and crop-specific safety assessment.

S1 / E1 definition

Genetic engineering directly alters an organism’s DNA/genome using laboratory technology. A Bt gene can enable the crop to make a protein that harms particular insect pests. DNA may be added, changed or deleted; not all engineering transfers DNA between species.

S4 resistance mechanism

Pests contain inherited variation; those resistant to the protein may survive exposure and reproduce, passing on relevant alleles. Resistance can therefore become more common across generations. Do not accept that individual pests intentionally change because they need to survive.

S5 / M5 provisional recommendation

Model: continue controlled trials with resistance monitoring before making a wider-use recommendation. The initial reduction in damage is promising, but test matched crop groups over more sites/seasons and monitor pest survival/trait effectiveness. A consistent lack of yield benefit or increasing resistance would weaken the recommendation. Accept a reasoned alternative if it uses supplied evidence and states uncertainty.

M1 distinction

Selective breeding chooses parents with desired inherited traits, then breeds and selects across generations. Genetic engineering directly changes DNA using laboratory technologies. Both can influence inherited characteristics; the method distinguishes them.

M2 mechanism

The added gene provides information for production of a protein in suitable plant cells. That protein affects particular target pests and may reduce damage. The crop remains a plant; not every insect is affected, and the intended trait needs testing.

M4 risk and unknown

Risk: selection can increase inherited pest resistance, reducing future effectiveness. Unknowns: effects across settings, non-target species, food safety, long-term performance or social/economic consequences. Match the claim to what the table actually measures. Do not treat an unanswered question as proof of harm.

T1 two settings

First yield difference Bt−non-GM=+1.0 t/ha; second=−0.1 t/ha. First damage difference=22 fewer plants per100; second=1 fewer. The second setting weakens a blanket benefit claim and suggests context (including pest pressure) matters. It does not prove the first result false or that the crop is always ineffective; means, spread and trial replication matter.

T2 scope and investigation

The trials measure target-pest damage and yield, not composition/toxicity/allergenicity or every ecological interaction. A focused environmental investigation might compare abundance/survival of specified non-target organisms in matched replicated plots over time. A relevant crop-specific safety assessment would need different evidence. Do not infer safety/harm from yield alone.

T3 balanced evaluation

A defensible answer links the initial benefit to reduced target-pest damage, identifies resistance or a relevant possible environmental issue, notes missing repeated/appropriate evidence, and considers growers, consumers or ecosystems. A provisional decision must be conditional, not a universal judgement on the technique.

T4 optional Higher mechanism

Restriction enzyme cuts at a particular recognition sequence; complementary sticky ends can base-pair; ligase seals/joins the DNA backbone; a vector such as a plasmid carries DNA into suitable cells. Successful insertion alone does not guarantee expression, sufficient functional protein or the desired whole-organism effect, so suitable control information/conditions and checks are needed. This detail is optional Higher-tier content, not a Foundation assessment requirement.

Hinge A–D

C is correct. A wrongly says all methods transfer between species. B confuses one useful gene with complete organism identity. D makes an unsupported universal safety/offspring guarantee.

E2–E3 exit

E2 accept lower damage30→8 per100 or greater mean yield6.9→7.9 t/ha, explicitly in the constructed trial. E3 accept resistance with monitoring across pest generations; accept another scientifically plausible specific risk with an appropriate focused test, not a blanket assertion.

L1–L2 reflection and Week4 bridge

Accept a concrete revision prompted by a question, such as naming the crop/trait, correcting a unit or identifying a missing comparison. Good Week4 questions are narrow and answerable, for example “How does this Bt trait affect a named non-target insect in a particular environment?” or “When does lower pest damage lead to a worthwhile yield gain?”

Word help

genome
An organism’s complete set of genetic information.
genetic engineering
Using laboratory technologies to directly alter an organism’s DNA.
gene expression
Using genetic information to make a functional product, such as a protein.
GM organism
An organism whose genetic information has been altered using genetic engineering.
Bt
Bacillus thuringiensis, a bacterium from which some pest-control protein genes originate.
provisional conclusion
A conclusion supported now, which can change when new evidence is added.
