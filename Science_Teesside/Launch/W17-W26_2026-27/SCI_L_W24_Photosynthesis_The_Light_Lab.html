Can light change what a plant makes?

Learning intention

Describe photosynthesis and investigate how light intensity affects a measured proxy for its rate.

  • Explain a fair light investigation with a measured outcome and controls.

TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Describe photosynthesis and investigate how light intensity affects a measured proxy for its rate.

Preparation

  • Print the shared evidence page, one chosen response route and exit page. Routes are alternatives, not a workload ladder.
  • Read the teacher answers before teaching. All numerical sample data in this lesson are constructed for learning.
  • The paper/data route is complete. If demonstrating, technician/teacher prepares an approved pondweed shoot with a fresh cut under water and checks bubbling before the lesson. Do not acquire or release invasive species.
  • Keep the same shoot, solution volume/concentration, counting interval and background light. Let the shoot adjust for at least 2 minutes after a light change; monitor temperature. Record actual results separately from constructed values.
  • For data logging, position the light sensor consistently beside the vessel facing the lamp; log one reading each second for 30 seconds at each position and record the mean. Lux is a practical light indicator, not a direct measurement of photosynthetic photon flux.

Method precautions

  • Use the fully supplied paper route if apparatus is not prepared. No flames, gas tests or hot-lamp practical is required.
  • Teacher handles cutting and fragile glass. Keep electrical equipment away from water; use a stable tray and school-approved lamp setup.
  • Do not taste solution or plant material. Wash hands after handling and dispose of plant material according to school procedures; never release it outdoors.

Practical alternative

Analyse the supplied constructed table and apparatus diagram. Pupils make the same predictions, controls and evidence-based conclusions without using water, plants or electrical equipment.

Access and regulation

Supported: Same core scientific goal, with word bank, short prompts, read-aloud or pointing/dictation. Do not assign a route from diagnosis.

Standard: Use the supplied evidence to explain and justify an independent scientific decision.

Stretch: Optional challenge after secure core: evaluate a limitation and transfer the science. Higher-tier-only material, where present, is labelled explicitly.

Regulation: Preview the sequence. Offer solo or partner work, a pass and quiet response. Allow a brief reset and re-entry at the next numbered task; no compulsory disclosure, performance or speed competition.

Teacher answers

Retrieval

R1 chloroplasts. R2 a factor kept the same to improve fairness. R3 each 60 seconds.

W1–W3

W1 means 5,10,17 bubbles/min at 300,600,1200 lux. W2 use the same shoot/matched replicates and change the intended light level only, controlling temperature and solution. W3 count rises with measured light in this range; no unlimited-growth or exact-volume claim is justified.

Hinge

A false: light is energy, not a material reactant. B true: count indicates a pattern with bubble-size limitations. C false: different bubble sizes/dissolved gas. D false: changing shoots introduces another variable.

S1 / M1

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, with light energy absorbed by chlorophyll. Photosynthesis takes in energy; glucose can contribute to biomass or respiration.

S2 / M2

(4+5+6)/3=5;(9+10+11)/3=10;(16+17+18)/3=17 bubbles/min. From 300 to 1200 lux the mean increases by 12 bubbles/min. This is the observed range, not a universal proportionality.

S3 / M3

Change measured light level using lamp position. Count bubbles for equal one-minute intervals; repeat three times after adjustment. Same shoot, solution volume/concentration, background light and comparable temperature. Sensor orientation/location consistent; optional 30 second logging mean. Controls reduce alternative explanations.

S4 / M4

Higher measured light is associated with higher mean count here, for example 300 lux→5 and 1200 lux→17 bubbles/min. Counts do not equal exact gas volumes because sizes differ and gas may dissolve. Collect gas volume over a fixed time with appropriate apparatus to reduce the bubble-size limitation; maintain other controls.

T1

(16+17+35)/3=68/3≈22.7 bubbles/min only if all counts were truly one minute. If the 35 count lasted two minutes, averaging raw counts as equal rates is invalid. The note is uncertain; check the record before recalculating.

T2

Retain and annotate the original record; verify timing or repeat matched measurements with a clear timer/observer. Do not silently delete an inconvenient value or assume the cause from its size alone.

T3

Carbon dioxide availability or temperature could limit the rate. Example: compare matched carbon dioxide availability at the same light and temperature with repeats. The plateau alone does not identify which factor is limiting.

T4

Lux measures light weighted to human visual sensitivity, not glucose production or exact photosynthetic photon flux. Bubble counts indicate gas release, with volume/dissolution limits; glucose itself is not measured.

Exit and assessment

E1 carbon dioxide and water. E2 15÷3=5 bubbles/min. E3 valid controlled factor plus bubble-size/dissolved-gas limit. L1 accepts a specific evidence-based revision or justified label. Secure core = correct reaction, fair variable logic and proxy-aware conclusion.

Word help

photosynthesis
Using light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen.
chlorophyll
A pigment that absorbs light in photosynthesis.
rate
How much happens in a stated time.
proxy
A measurement used to indicate another quantity, with limitations.
control variable
A factor kept the same to make a comparison fairer.
limiting factor
A factor in short supply that restricts the rate of a process.
