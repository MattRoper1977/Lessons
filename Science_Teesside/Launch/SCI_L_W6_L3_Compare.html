Three transport processes: choose and justify

Learning intention

I can compare diffusion, osmosis and active transport using substance, direction, membrane and energy evidence.

Success looks like

  • Select the process from a scenario.
  • Make an explicit similarity or difference.
  • Use evidence rather than an organ name alone.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. Question: Compare diffusion and active transport.
  2. Both move substances and may occur across cell membranes.
  3. Diffusion has net movement down a concentration gradient without respiration energy.
  4. Active transport moves against a gradient and requires energy from respiration.

Check a common mistake

“They all move things” is a similarity, but it is not enough to distinguish the processes. Compare a named feature on both sides.

Knowledge organiser

Success looks like

  • Select the process from a scenario.
  • Make an explicit similarity or difference.
  • Use evidence rather than an organ name alone.

Remember

  • Diffusion and osmosis are passive: neither requires energy supplied by respiration.
  • Osmosis is specifically water moving through a partially permeable membrane.
  • Active transport uses respiration energy for movement against a concentration gradient.
  • Diffusion can occur without a membrane, for example when gases mix.

Words that help

compare — State relevant similarities and/or differences between things.

net movement — The overall result of movement in both directions.

gradient — A concentration difference.

energy requirement — Whether energy supplied by respiration is needed for the transport.

Knowledge organiser · Three transport processes: choose and justify

Learning intention: I can compare diffusion, osmosis and active transport using substance, direction, membrane and energy evidence.

Success looks like

  • Select the process from a scenario.
  • Make an explicit similarity or difference.
  • Use evidence rather than an organ name alone.

Remember

  • Diffusion and osmosis are passive: neither requires energy supplied by respiration.
  • Osmosis is specifically water moving through a partially permeable membrane.
  • Active transport uses respiration energy for movement against a concentration gradient.
  • Diffusion can occur without a membrane, for example when gases mix.

Words that help

compare — State relevant similarities and/or differences between things.

net movement — The overall result of movement in both directions.

gradient — A concentration difference.

energy requirement — Whether energy supplied by respiration is needed for the transport.

Check this idea: “They all move things” is a similarity, but it is not enough to distinguish the processes. Compare a named feature on both sides.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 6

Staff answers · You do · Standard

1. Classify A–D.

A diffusion; B osmosis; C active transport; D diffusion.

2. Compare diffusion and osmosis using one similarity and one difference.

Both are passive and arise from random molecular motion. Osmosis is specifically water crossing a partially permeable membrane; diffusion is broader and need not involve one.

3. Compare diffusion and active transport.

Diffusion: down a gradient, no respiration energy required. Active transport: against a gradient, respiration energy required.

4. A plant absorbs water and ions. Explain why one process name is insufficient.

Water may enter by osmosis, while ions can enter against their gradients by active transport. The mechanism depends on the substance and conditions.
