Starter

2 minutes

Starter · notice the evidence

Fictional trials reach the same endpoint in 80 s at 15 °C and 40 s at 30 °C. Describe before explaining.

Point to a clue. Give a first idea, then check it against the organiser or model.

Check the first idea
The higher-temperature trial reaches the same endpoint in half the time. Faster particle movement is a plausible explanation when other factors are controlled.

Silent, with visible captions. The video repeats the six teaching steps. Pause or replay; no internet is needed.

Read the full explainer transcript
  1. Read the variables, units and endpoint.
  2. Describe the pattern: higher temperature, shorter time.
  3. Do not call a longer time a faster rate.
  4. Explain using faster random particle movement.
  5. Check that distance, concentration and endpoint match.
  6. A small data set supports a limited conclusion, not every possible temperature.

I do 2

3 minutes

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. Describe: endpoint time fell from 80 s to 40 s.
  2. Explain: particles moved faster at the higher temperature.
  3. Method: the amount, distance and endpoint were kept the same.
  4. Limit: two supplied temperatures cannot establish the pattern at every temperature.

Watch for this mistake

“It is faster” is already a full scientific explanation.

State what changes and connect it to a mechanism such as faster particle movement or a shorter path.

3 minutes

We do 2 · repair the claim

Keep any correct idea. Replace the weak part using a number, a stated condition or a scientific reason. You can explain rather than write.

Reveal a model repair
At 30 °C the stated endpoint is reached sooner. Particles have more kinetic energy and move faster on average; they do not move with a purpose.

Explaining diffusion with evidence

Learning objective: I can build an explanation that links a diffusion factor to an outcome and respects the evidence.

Success looks like

  • Describe a pattern using data.
  • Explain it with particle movement or diffusion distance.
  • Identify a fair comparison and a limit.

Keep these ideas

  • Higher temperature usually makes particles move faster.
  • A steeper concentration gradient can increase net diffusion rate.
  • Larger area or a shorter distance can increase diffusion rate.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

describe — Say what happens or what the evidence shows.

explain — Give a linked scientific reason.

control variable — A condition kept the same in a comparison.

rate — How much change happens per unit time.

Worked example

Describe: endpoint time fell from 80 s to 40 s.

Explain: particles moved faster at the higher temperature.

Method: the amount, distance and endpoint were kept the same.

Limit: two supplied temperatures cannot establish the pattern at every temperature.

A useful correction
State what changes and connect it to a mechanism such as faster particle movement or a shorter path.
