Starter · 3 min

Notice, choose, explain

A 2 cm cube has more surface area than a 1 cm cube. Must it have a higher SA:V ratio?

Think quietly, point to evidence or share a first idea. You may pass.

Teacher reveal · check the first idea
No. Its volume also increases; the ratios are 3:1 and 6:1 respectively.
Words that help: surface area · volume · ratio · justify

Silent video with captions built into each frame. The complete text is below.

Read the complete explainer transcript
  1. A cube has six square faces.
  2. For side 1 cm: area = 6 × 1² = 6 cm² and volume = 1³ = 1 cm³.
  3. The ratio is 6:1.
  4. For side 2 cm: area = 24 cm² and volume = 8 cm³.
  5. 24:8 simplifies to 3:1.
  6. The larger cube has more total area but less area relative to its volume.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. Compare cubes with side lengths 1 cm and 3 cm.
  2. 1 cm: area 6 cm², volume 1 cm³, ratio 6:1.
  3. 3 cm: area 54 cm², volume 27 cm³, ratio 2:1.
  4. The 1 cm cube has more surface area per unit volume; this supports exchange relative to its size.

Check a common mistake

Total surface area and surface-area-to-volume ratio are different quantities. A larger cube can have more area but a smaller ratio.

Before the reveal, predict the next step or explain why it follows.

Answers are now visible. Use these for review, not as evidence of an independent first attempt.

1. Area scales with the square of length, but volume scales with the cube; volume grows faster.

2. The 3 cm cube has 54 cm² compared with 6 cm², but its ratio is 2:1 rather than 6:1.

3. Lungs are complex branching structures; cubes illustrate a principle rather than reproduce their real geometry.

4. A low overall SA:V and long internal distances can prevent surface diffusion alone from meeting all cells’ needs.

Remember

  • For a cube of side a: surface area = 6a² and volume = a³.

surface area — The total area of the outside surfaces.

ratio — A comparison between quantities.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 4

Knowledge organiser · Diffusion evidence: explain and calculate

Learning intention: I can use diffusion data and surface-area calculations to support an exam-style explanation.

Success looks like

  • Use figures from a supplied table.
  • Calculate and compare surface-area-to-volume ratios.
  • Link exchange features to their effects.

Remember

  • For a cube of side a: surface area = 6a² and volume = a³.
  • A small cube has a larger surface-area-to-volume ratio than a larger cube of the same shape.
  • A larger total surface area does not necessarily mean a larger surface-area-to-volume ratio.
  • Multicellular organisms need specialised exchange surfaces and transport systems to meet cell needs.

Words that help

surface area — The total area of the outside surfaces.

volume — The amount of space an object occupies.

ratio — A comparison between quantities.

justify — Support a choice using evidence.

Check this idea: Total surface area and surface-area-to-volume ratio are different quantities. A larger cube can have more area but a smaller ratio.

Staff answers · Arrival · Stretch

1. Link one alveolar adaptation to its effect.

Thin walls shorten the diffusion path, or many alveoli give a large area.

2. Calculate 6 × 3².

54.

3. Distinguish area and volume units.

Area uses square units, such as cm²; volume uses cubic units, such as cm³.

4. Why is “because it is better” not enough?

It gives no specific evidence or scientific reason.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 4

Staff key · Shared work

Area / cm²: 1 cm cube: 6; 2 cm cube: 24.

Volume / cm³: 1 cm cube: 1; 2 cm cube: 8.

SA:V ratio: 1 cm cube: 6:1; 2 cm cube: 3:1.

Worked example

  1. Compare cubes with side lengths 1 cm and 3 cm.
  2. 1 cm: area 6 cm², volume 1 cm³, ratio 6:1.
  3. 3 cm: area 54 cm², volume 27 cm³, ratio 2:1.
  4. The 1 cm cube has more surface area per unit volume; this supports exchange relative to its size.

Evidence choices

A cube has side 2 cm. Which SA:V ratio is correct?
24:8 simplifies to 3:1.

The 3 cm cube has more total surface area than the 1 cm cube. What about area per unit volume?
It has less area per unit volume.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 4

Staff answers · You do · Standard

1. Complete the missing SA:V ratios.

2 cm: 24:8 = 3:1. 3 cm: 54:27 = 2:1.

2. Which cube has the largest total area, and which has the largest SA:V?

Largest area: 3 cm cube. Largest SA:V: 1 cm cube.

3. Explain why many small exchange units can be useful in lungs.

They provide a large total exchange area, supporting rapid gas diffusion.

4. Repair: “Diffusion is faster because the body is bigger.”

Body size alone does not establish faster diffusion. Use a specific factor such as larger exchange area, shorter path or steeper gradient.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 4

Staff answers · You do · Stretch

1. Explain why the ratio falls as side length rises.

Area scales with the square of length, but volume scales with the cube; volume grows faster.

2. Use numerical evidence to explain the difference.

The 3 cm cube has 54 cm² compared with 6 cm², but its ratio is 2:1 rather than 6:1.

3. Why should a cube ratio not be treated as an exact measurement of a lung?

Lungs are complex branching structures; cubes illustrate a principle rather than reproduce their real geometry.

4. Explain why a large organism needs transport as well as exchange surfaces.

A low overall SA:V and long internal distances can prevent surface diffusion alone from meeting all cells’ needs.

Before you teach: Provide calculators and cube nets or blocks if useful. Keep physical handling optional. The numerical model does not need agar, dyes or cutting.

Start from what pupils show: use the four arrival checks. Do not assume earlier work was taught or completed. Model first where needed; invite a reason or transfer where it is familiar.
