1. Enzyme and substrate

Link to today: Amylase is the enzyme; starch is its substrate. Which one speeds up the breakdown reaction?

Help with this question

An enzyme is a biological catalyst: it speeds up a reaction without being used up.

Answer: Amylase is the enzyme and speeds up starch breakdown.

2. Read the colour

Iodine becomes blue-black with starch. A sampled drop turns blue-black: is starch detected?

Help with this question

Blue-black = starch detected. Orange-brown = starch not detected.

Answer: Yes, starch is detected in the sampled drop.

3. Recall binding

Which substrate fits amylase’s active site: starch or an unrelated substrate?

Help with this question

Answer: Starch fits amylase’s active site.

4. Prepare the evidence

Which comes first: prepare the iodine wells / mix and start timing?

Help with this question

The reaction continues after mixing, so the sampling equipment must already be ready.

1. Enzyme and substrate

Identify the enzyme and substrate in amylase acting on starch.

Help with this question

Enzyme = catalyst. Substrate = molecule the enzyme acts on.

Answer: Amylase is the enzyme; starch is the substrate.

2. Read the colour

At 60 s iodine is blue-black; at 90 s it stays orange-brown. Which sampled time first meets the endpoint?

Help with this question

The agreed endpoint is the first sampled time when starch is not detected.

Answer: 90 s. The exact change between the two samples was not observed.

3. Change and measure

State the independent and dependent variables, then name one condition to keep constant.

Help with this question

Change buffer pH; measure endpoint time. Controls include temperature and starting solution amounts.

Answer: Independent: buffer pH. Dependent: endpoint time. A valid control is temperature, solution volume/concentration or sampling interval.

4. Prepare the evidence

Why prepare the iodine wells and time headings before mixing?

Help with this question

Once amylase and starch are mixed, the reaction continues while equipment is being prepared.

Answer: Preparation allows samples and observations to begin at the agreed times, reducing missed early observations.

1. Enzyme and substrate

Why does an iodine colour change provide indirect evidence of amylase activity?

Help with this question

Iodine detects starch in a sample; it does not show the enzyme molecules working.

Answer: The colour indicates whether starch remains; its disappearance under controlled conditions supports breakdown, rather than directly measuring molecular activity.

2. Read the colour

At 60 s starch is detected; at 90 s it is not. Is an exact endpoint of 75 s justified?

Help with this question

No observation was made at 75 s. Distinguish an observed endpoint from its possible timing window.

Answer: No. Record 90 s as the first observed endpoint; the endpoint was reached after 60 s and by 90 s.

3. Change and measure

Distinguish controlled temperature from a comparison with no active amylase.

Help with this question

A controlled variable stays matched. A comparison without active enzyme checks whether the enzyme is needed for the observed change.

Answer: Matched temperature limits an alternative explanation across pH trials; the no-active-amylase comparison checks for change without enzyme activity.

4. Prepare the evidence

If the 60 s sample is missed, can the 90 s endpoint have the original 30 s timing window?

Help with this question

The last actual starch-positive sample might now be 30 s. Use observed times only.

Answer: No. If 30 s was positive and 90 s negative, the observed window is wider: after 30 s and by 90 s. Preserve the missed sample.

Word help

enzyme

A biological catalyst that speeds a reaction without being used up.

substrate

The substance an enzyme acts on.

active site

The region where the substrate binds and the reaction is catalysed.

specificity

An enzyme acts on particular substrates because of its complementary active site.

endpoint

The agreed observable point at which timing is recorded.

control variable

A factor kept the same to make the comparison valid.

denaturation

A change in an enzyme’s three-dimensional structure that disrupts the active site and its function. Unsuitable pH or high temperature can cause this.

  1. Identify the enzyme and substrate in amylase acting on starch.
  2. At 60 s iodine is blue-black; at 90 s it stays orange-brown. Which sampled time first meets the endpoint?

  4. Why prepare the iodine wells and time headings before mixing?

Mark scheme · one point per mark

Independent

1. State the first observed endpoint for pH 7 and the interval in which it was reached, using the supplied observations. [2 marks]

  1. Record 90 s (1 mark)
  2. The crossing was after 60 and by 90 s (1 mark)

2. Order the four method cards. Explain why preparation must happen before mixing. [2 marks]

  1. Prepare/check, start together, sample at fixed times, record raw observations (1 mark)
  2. Reaction starts on mixing, so preparation must be completed first (1 mark)

3. State the independent variable, dependent variable and one matched control, explaining why the control matters. [4 marks]

  1. Change pH (1 mark)
  2. Measure endpoint time (1 mark)
  3. Keep temperature or solution amounts matched (1 mark)
  4. The named control could affect rate independently of pH (1 mark)

4. Repair the proposed exact endpoint of 75 s. Explain what should be recorded if a sample is missed. [2 marks]

  1. 75 s was not observed, so record 90 s (1 mark)
  2. Keep a missed-sample gap and use actual observed bounds (1 mark)

5. Compare two pH values using their times. State one limit of the conclusion from this first series. [3 marks]

  1. For example pH 7 takes 90 s and pH 4 takes 240 s (1 mark)
  2. pH 7 is faster in these matched first runs (1 mark)
  3. Single trials, tested range or subjective endpoint limits the claim (1 mark)

Aspire

1. Distinguish the recorded endpoint from the true endpoint window for pH 7. State the window’s width. [3 marks]

  1. Record 90 s (1 mark)
  2. Threshold crossing is after 60 and by 90 s (1 mark)
  3. The window is 30 s wide (1 mark)

2. Explain how preparing the tile first and using a fixed sampling interval protect different parts of the evidence. [2 marks]

  1. Preparing the tile avoids losing early observations (1 mark)
  2. A fixed sampling interval gives consistent timing gaps (1 mark)

3. Explain the different roles of matched temperature and a comparison containing no active amylase. [2 marks]

  1. Matching temperature limits its effect as an alternative cause (1 mark)
  2. The no-active-amylase comparison tests change without active enzyme (1 mark)

4. Evaluate this claim: The 60 s sample is missing in a second record; 30 s is blue-black and 90 s orange-brown. State what is now known and repair the claim “the same precision”. [3 marks]

  1. The observed crossing is after 30 and by 90 s (1 mark)
  2. The window is 60 s wide (1 mark)
  3. It is wider than the original 30 s window (1 mark)

5. Compare pH 4 and pH 7 using the supplied times, limiting your claim to these trials. Explain why neither a smooth expected trend nor a hard-to-read colour justifies altering a raw observation. [3 marks]

  1. pH 7 has a shorter endpoint time than pH 4: 90 versus 240 s (1 mark)
  2. The conclusion is limited to these matched trials (1 mark)
  3. Changing observations to fit an expected trend would make the record untruthful (1 mark)

Accept scientifically equivalent wording. Award each distinct point once. The mark tariff is for the question, not a participation score. Keep the answer scheme staff-only during independent work.
