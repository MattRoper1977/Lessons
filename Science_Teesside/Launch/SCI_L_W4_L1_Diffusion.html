Starter · 3 min

Notice, choose, explain

At an even distribution, do particles stop moving?

Think quietly, point to evidence or share a first idea. You may pass.

Teacher reveal · check the first idea
No. They continue random motion, with no overall concentration-driven net movement.
Words that help: diffusion · concentration · gradient · net

I do · Model · 6 min

Watch the model build

Predict what the next step will show. Pause, replay or move one step at a time.

Start with more of one kind of particle on the left than on the right, in equal volumes.

1 / 6
Watch the 24-second captioned explainer

Silent video with captions built into each frame. The complete text is below.

Read the complete explainer transcript
  1. Start with more of one kind of particle on the left than on the right, in equal volumes.
  2. Each particle moves randomly, including movement in both directions.
  3. More cross from the crowded side overall, so net movement is left to right.
  4. The concentration difference becomes smaller.
  5. Particles continue moving when the distribution becomes more even.
  6. The model shows an overall pattern, not purposeful journeys or a prediction of exact individual paths.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. In one short interval, 9 particles cross left to right and 4 cross right to left.
  2. Net movement = 9 − 4 = 5 particles left to right.
  3. Both directions occurred; “net” summarises their difference.
  4. The supplied counts illustrate one interval, not a universal diffusion rate.

Check a common mistake

Particles do not decide where to go. They continue moving after concentrations become equal.

Before the reveal, predict the next step or explain why it follows.

Knowledge organiser

Learning intention: I can explain diffusion using random particle motion and a difference in concentration.

Success looks like

  • Identify higher and lower concentration.
  • Describe net movement down a gradient.
  • Explain one factor affecting diffusion rate.

Remember

  • Particles in gases and solutions move randomly.
  • A concentration difference produces net movement from higher to lower concentration.
  • Diffusion does not require energy supplied by cellular respiration.
  • At an even distribution, particles keep moving; net movement is approximately zero.

Words that help

diffusion — Net spreading from higher to lower concentration through random particle motion.

concentration — Amount of a substance in a given volume.

gradient — A difference in concentration between places.

net — The overall result after movement in both directions is considered.

Check this idea: Particles do not decide where to go. They continue moving after concentrations become equal.

Knowledge organiser · Diffusion: moving down a gradient

Learning intention: I can explain diffusion using random particle motion and a difference in concentration.

Success looks like

  • Identify higher and lower concentration.
  • Describe net movement down a gradient.
  • Explain one factor affecting diffusion rate.

Remember

  • Particles in gases and solutions move randomly.
  • A concentration difference produces net movement from higher to lower concentration.
  • Diffusion does not require energy supplied by cellular respiration.
  • At an even distribution, particles keep moving; net movement is approximately zero.

Words that help

diffusion — Net spreading from higher to lower concentration through random particle motion.

concentration — Amount of a substance in a given volume.

gradient — A difference in concentration between places.

net — The overall result after movement in both directions is considered.

Check this idea: Particles do not decide where to go. They continue moving after concentrations become equal.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 4

Staff answers · Arrival · Stretch

1. Why is a membrane relevant to cell exchange?

It separates cell contents from surroundings and controls movement across it.

2. What additional information is needed to compare concentration?

The volume occupied by each amount.

3. Would every gas particle travel in the same direction?

No. Motion is random in many directions.

4. Why must model counts be compared in equal volumes?

Equal volumes make particle number a valid concentration comparison.

Staff key · Shared work

Supports diffusion: Particles move randomly.; Net movement is down a concentration gradient.; Particles still move at an even distribution..

Needs correcting: Particles decide to spread out.; Particles stop when evenly spread.; Diffusion always needs respiration energy..

Worked example

  1. In one short interval, 9 particles cross left to right and 4 cross right to left.
  2. Net movement = 9 − 4 = 5 particles left to right.
  3. Both directions occurred; “net” summarises their difference.
  4. The supplied counts illustrate one interval, not a universal diffusion rate.

Evidence choices

Left count falls from 18 to 13; right rises from 6 to 11. What is the net transfer?
Five particles to the right.

Concentrations become equal. What happens to particles?
They keep moving randomly, with no overall preferred direction.

Staff answers · You do · Standard

1. Use the start counts. Which region has higher concentration?

Left: 18 particles compared with 6 in the same volume.

2. State the direction of net movement between the start and later states.

Left to right. Left loses 5 while right gains 5.

3. Explain diffusion using random motion and the concentration difference.

Particles move randomly in both directions; overall more move from higher to lower concentration.

4. Predict one change that could increase diffusion rate.

A higher temperature, steeper concentration gradient or larger exchange surface area, with other factors controlled.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 4

Staff answers · You do · Stretch

1. Would 18 versus 6 alone establish concentration if volumes differed?

No. Concentration depends on amount per volume; the volumes must be considered.

2. Calculate the net transfer and explain why individual crossings are not known.

Net transfer is 5 left to right. The snapshots do not tell how many crossed in each direction.

3. At equal concentrations, explain what continues and what becomes zero on average.

Random motion and two-way crossings continue; average net concentration-driven movement is zero.

4. Explain why higher temperature can increase diffusion rate.

Particles have greater average kinetic energy and move faster, so spreading can occur more rapidly.

Before you teach: Use the particle model or paper tokens. No perfume, aerosol or tasting demonstration is needed. Avoid moving pupils around to act as particles when space or regulation makes that unhelpful.
