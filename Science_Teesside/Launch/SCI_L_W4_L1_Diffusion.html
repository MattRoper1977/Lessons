I do · watch the model

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. The text and manual controls provide a still-image route.

STEP 1 OF 6

Compare equal volumes: the left side begins more concentrated.

Watch random movement

Choose the starting distribution. Dots cross both ways; this is a qualitative model, not measured diffusion data.

Initially 12 left and 4 right, or 8 each. Counts fluctuate in this small random model. A momentary count is not the definition of equilibrium.

Watch the 30-second captioned explainer

Silent, with visible captions. The video repeats the six teaching steps. Pause or replay; no internet is needed.

Read the full explainer transcript
  1. Compare equal volumes: the left side begins more concentrated.
  2. Individual particles move randomly, not towards a goal.
  3. Some cross left to right; some cross right to left.
  4. More cross from the higher-concentration side overall.
  5. As the difference falls, net movement becomes smaller.
  6. At balance, random motion continues even when net movement is zero.

I do 2

3 minutes

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. During a model interval, 9 particles cross right and 4 cross left.
  2. Net crossing = 9 − 4 = 5 to the right.
  3. This does not mean only five particles moved.
  4. A later interval of 6 each way has zero net crossing.

Watch for this mistake

Particles stop when both sides are equally concentrated.

Individual particles keep moving; opposite flows balance on average.

Knowledge organiser

Knowledge organiser

LAUNCH SCIENCE · KNOWLEDGE ORGANISER

Diffusion: follow the particles

Learning objective: I can explain net diffusion using random particle movement and a concentration difference.

Success looks like

  • Identify a concentration difference in equal spaces.
  • Describe net movement from higher to lower concentration.
  • Explain why particles still move when there is no net movement.

Keep these ideas

  • Particles in liquids and gases move randomly.
  • Diffusion has net movement down a concentration gradient.
  • Particles can move both ways while the net movement is in one direction.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

particle — A tiny piece of a substance, such as a molecule.

concentration — How much of a substance there is in a given volume.

net movement — The overall movement after opposite movements are compared.

gradient — A difference in concentration between regions.

Worked example

During a model interval, 9 particles cross right and 4 cross left.

Net crossing = 9 − 4 = 5 to the right.

This does not mean only five particles moved.

A later interval of 6 each way has zero net crossing.

A useful correction
Individual particles keep moving; opposite flows balance on average.
