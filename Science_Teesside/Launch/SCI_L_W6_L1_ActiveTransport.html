Active transport: against the gradient

Learning intention

I can explain how cells move substances against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration.

Success looks like

  • Identify a low-to-high concentration movement.
  • State the energy requirement.
  • Use an example without confusing it with osmosis.

Point, speak, sign, arrange cards, draw, write or direct an adult. You may pass or leave an answer blank.

Starter · 3 min

Notice, choose, explain

Mineral ions enter a root cell even when there are fewer ions per volume outside. Can diffusion alone explain this direction?

Think quietly, point to evidence or share a first idea. You may pass.

Teacher reveal · check the first idea
No. Movement from lower to higher concentration is against the gradient and requires an active transport mechanism.
Words that help: active transport · mineral ion · respiration · carrier protein

Watch the 24-second captioned explainer

Silent video with captions built into each frame. The complete text is below.

Read the complete explainer transcript
  1. There are fewer mineral ions per equal volume outside the cell than inside.
  2. Moving an ion into the cell is movement against its concentration gradient.
  3. A membrane transport protein provides a route for this transport.
  4. Energy from respiration is required for active transport.
  5. The cell can build or maintain a higher internal concentration.
  6. If energy supply is insufficient, this active uptake is reduced; do not treat the model as every cellular process.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. Soil solution: 2 model ion units per volume. Root cell: 8 units per equal volume.
  2. Direction required: soil → root cell, from lower to higher concentration.
  3. This is against the gradient, so diffusion alone does not explain it.
  4. Active transport uses energy from respiration to support the uptake.

Check a common mistake

Active does not mean “any movement”. Use the concentration direction and energy requirement to identify active transport.

Before the reveal, predict the next step or explain why it follows.

Knowledge organiser

  • Identify a low-to-high concentration movement.
  • State the energy requirement.

Remember

  • Active transport can move substances from a lower concentration to a higher concentration.
  • It requires energy transferred by respiration.
  • Root hair cells can take up mineral ions from dilute soil solutions.
  • Water uptake by osmosis is not the same process as mineral-ion uptake by active transport.

Words that help

active transport — Movement against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration.

mineral ion — A charged mineral particle dissolved in a solution.

respiration — Cell reactions that transfer energy from substances such as glucose.

carrier protein — A membrane protein involved in moving particular substances across.

Check this idea: Active does not mean “any movement”. Use the concentration direction and energy requirement to identify active transport.

Knowledge organiser · Active transport: against the gradient

Learning intention: I can explain how cells move substances against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration.

Success looks like

  • Identify a low-to-high concentration movement.
  • State the energy requirement.
  • Use an example without confusing it with osmosis.

Remember

  • Active transport can move substances from a lower concentration to a higher concentration.
  • It requires energy transferred by respiration.
  • Root hair cells can take up mineral ions from dilute soil solutions.
  • Water uptake by osmosis is not the same process as mineral-ion uptake by active transport.

Words that help

active transport — Movement against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration.

mineral ion — A charged mineral particle dissolved in a solution.

respiration — Cell reactions that transfer energy from substances such as glucose.

carrier protein — A membrane protein involved in moving particular substances across.

Check this idea: Active does not mean “any movement”. Use the concentration direction and energy requirement to identify active transport.

Staff answers · Arrival · Stretch

Its net direction is down, not against, the concentration gradient.

Partial permeability.

Concentration is amount per volume, not particle number alone.
