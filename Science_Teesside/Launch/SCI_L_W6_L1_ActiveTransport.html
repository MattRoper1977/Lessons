Starter

2 minutes

Starter · notice the evidence

A cell takes mineral ions from a dilute solution into a more concentrated cell. Can diffusion alone explain that net movement?

Point to a clue. Give a first idea, then check it against the organiser or model.

Check the first idea
No. This is against the ion concentration gradient and needs an energy-dependent transport process.

I do

4 minutes

I do · watch the model

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. The text and manual controls provide a still-image route.

STEP 1 OF 6

Compare the ion concentration on each side of the membrane.

Energy-dependent pump model

Equal-size regions begin with 3 ions outside and 9 inside. Each model step moves one ion against the gradient.

Outside: 3 · Inside: 9 · Total: 12

The model counts transfer, not all random movement. Turning energy off does not mean molecules stop moving.

Watch the 30-second captioned explainer

Silent, with visible captions. The video repeats the six teaching steps. Pause or replay; no internet is needed.

Read the full explainer transcript
  1. Compare the ion concentration on each side of the membrane.
  2. The outside begins lower and the inside higher.
  3. The model protein moves an ion towards the higher-concentration side.
  4. That net transfer is against the gradient.
  5. Cellular energy is needed; respiration supplies that energy.
  6. The protein model is a simplification, not a tiny conscious worker.

I do 2

3 minutes

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. Outside: 2 relative concentration units. Inside: 8.
  2. Net ion movement: outside → inside.
  3. That means lower → higher concentration.
  4. Conclusion: active transport, using cellular energy, not diffusion alone.

Watch for this mistake

All movement into a cell is active transport.

Identify the substance, gradient and energy use; direction into a cell alone does not identify the process.

LAUNCH SCIENCE · KNOWLEDGE ORGANISER

Active transport: against the gradient

Learning objective: I can explain active transport using a concentration gradient, a membrane and cellular energy.

Success looks like

  • Recognise movement from lower to higher concentration.
  • State the need for energy from respiration.
  • Distinguish a transport process from a deliberate choice by a cell.

Keep these ideas

  • Active transport can move a substance from lower to higher concentration.
  • It uses energy supplied through cellular respiration.
  • Specific membrane transport proteins are involved.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

active transport — Movement of substances across a membrane against their concentration gradient using cellular energy.

respiration — Cellular reactions that transfer energy from nutrients.

transport protein — A membrane protein involved in moving particular substances.

against a gradient — From lower to higher concentration for the substance considered.

Worked example

Outside: 2 relative concentration units. Inside: 8.

Net ion movement: outside → inside.

That means lower → higher concentration.

Conclusion: active transport, using cellular energy, not diffusion alone.

A useful correction
Identify the substance, gradient and energy use; direction into a cell alone does not identify the process.

Before teaching

Use the membrane pump model, counters or printed cards. No living samples or chemicals needed.

The pump is a qualitative teaching model. In organisms, energy coupling and transport proteins vary; the model does not claim that every transport protein directly uses ATP in the same way.
