LAUNCH · Science

Copy, separate, divide

Autumn 2 · Week 1 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can describe the cell cycle and use chromosome evidence to explain mitosis.

Success criteria

  • Place DNA copying before mitosis.
  • Use chromosome positions to name mitosis stages.
  • Explain how each daughter cell receives a full set.

Use Next to begin. Ask for word help or your usual support when needed.

I do · 5 min

Separate the three events

Preparation comes before mitosis.

Interphase: the cell grows, makes more subcellular structures and copies DNA.

Mitosis: copied genetic material separates into two nuclei.

Cytokinesis: the cytoplasm divides into two cells.

Teaching diagram; not to scale.

Interphase

Grow and copy DNA.

Mitosis

Divide the nucleus.

Cytokinesis

Divide the cytoplasm.

I do · 4 min

Read the chromosome evidence

The positions change through mitosis.

Prophase: copied chromosomes condense; the nuclear membrane breaks down.

Metaphase: chromosomes line up near the middle.

Anaphase: sister chromatids separate to opposite ends.

Telophase: new nuclei form around the two sets.

Teacher response guidance

  • Six-stage order: interphase, prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase, cytokinesis.

  • Each daughter has three chromosomes: one copy of every starting chromosome.
  • Limits: simplified count, colours, 2D layout, no molecular detail, rods in interphase track information rather than visible condensed chromosomes.

Arrival · From one cell to two

Recap nucleus; genetic instructions; growth may include increasing cell number. Do not imply every mature human cell has a nucleus.

Starter · Share a full set

Colours distinguish two chromosome identities, not maternal/paternal homologues. This is not a real human chromosome count.

I do · Separate the three events

Mention DNA appears uncondensed in interphase; rods here track information and are not a microscope view. A copied X-shaped chromosome has two sister chromatids. Replication doubles DNA amount, not the chromosome number counted by centromeres at that moment.

We do · Control the cell-cycle model

The sequence advances only by pupil control. Prophase/metaphase/anaphase/telophase appear in order; the activity checks selected frame identity and invariant replication timing.

I do · Read the chromosome evidence

Prophase image is an early snapshot and may retain nuclear boundary; explain its breakdown across prophase. Avoid equating a drawn X with two chromosomes before separation. Molecular detail and phase durations omitted.

We do · Count information, not just shapes

“Genetically identical” describes the intended outcome barring mutation/error. No claim that all cells have identical sizes, functions or gene expression.

Independent · A new cell model

Fresh three-chromosome evidence. R is anaphase; copied sister chromatids have separated and count as chromosomes. Do not ask for a single misleading whole-cell chromosome count across all stages.

Exit · Explain the full sequence

Require evidence-based explanation. The pupil may deliver a structured spoken answer with the diagrams in view.

Misconceptions

DNA copying is a mitosis stage. DNA replication happens in interphase before mitosis.

An X is always two chromosomes. Before sister separation it is one duplicated chromosome with two chromatids.

Mitosis alone splits the whole cell. Mitosis divides the nucleus; cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm.

Every body cell is exactly the same. Successful daughter cells receive the same genetic information; size, role and expression can differ.
