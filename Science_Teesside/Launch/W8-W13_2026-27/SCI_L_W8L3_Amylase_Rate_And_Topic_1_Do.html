Amylase Rate, Graph and Topic 1 Consolidation

Learning objective

I can calculate and graph relative amylase rates, judge what the evidence supports, and use enzyme and transport ideas in an exam-style explanation.

Success looks like

  • Show relative-rate working using 1 ÷ time with the unit s⁻¹.
  • Read or plot the pH–rate pattern and limit the conclusion to the evidence.
  • Link a method limitation to an improvement, then distinguish digestion from diffusion.

We will observe, work together, then use the evidence ourselves.

Arrival task

Arrival task

Choose a route. Think first, then check the answers together.

1. Time and rate

With the same starting starch and endpoint, which is faster: 90 s or 180 s?

Shorter time for the same measured change means faster breakdown.

Answer: 90 s is faster.

2. Read the endpoint

Blue-black means starch detected. Orange-brown means starch not detected. Which colour meets our endpoint?

Endpoint = first sampled time with starch not detected.

Answer: Orange-brown.

3. Show the working

Relative rate = 1 ÷ time. Use a calculator to find 1 ÷ 100.

Press 1 ÷ 100 =. Use time in seconds; rate unit is s⁻¹.

Answer: 1 ÷ 100 = 0.0100 s⁻¹ (0.01 s⁻¹ is also correct).

4. Match the instruction

DESCRIBE means state the pattern. EVALUATE means judge the evidence. Which asks for a strength and a limitation?

The command word tells you what the answer must do.

Answer: EVALUATE.

1. Time and rate

Explain why 90 s gives a larger relative rate than 180 s under matched conditions.

Relative rate = 1 ÷ time, for the same starting amount and endpoint.

Answer: The same measured change takes less time; 1/90 is larger than 1/180.

2. Read the endpoint

At 60 s starch is detected; at 90 s it is not. What endpoint value is used in the rate calculation?

Use the first observed endpoint, not an invented time between samples.

Answer: 90 s, while acknowledging the endpoint was reached after 60 s and by 90 s.

3. Show the working

Calculate relative rate for an endpoint of 120 s; show the equation, substitution and unit.

Relative rate = 1 ÷ time. A value to three significant figures is sufficient here.

Answer: Relative rate = 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹.

4. Match the instruction

Distinguish DESCRIBE from EXPLAIN when answering about a graph.

Describe = state the pattern. Explain = link that pattern to scientific reasons.

Answer: Describe states how values change; explain uses biology, such as pH affecting the active site, to account for the pattern.

1. Time and rate

A trial takes twice as long to reach the same endpoint. What happens to its reciprocal relative rate?

Compare 1/t with 1/(2t). Starting amount and endpoint must be matched.

Answer: Its relative rate halves.

2. Read the endpoint

Why is 1/90 s⁻¹ an endpoint-based estimate rather than a direct instantaneous rate measurement?

The endpoint records one sampled stage of change; rate may vary during a reaction.

Answer: It summarises time to an observed endpoint, with sampling and colour limits. It does not continuously measure the amount changed per instant.

3. Show the working

For 120 s, compare 1/t with 1000/t. May the two values share one unlabelled graph axis?

The two conventions differ by a scale factor of 1000. This lesson uses 1/t only.

Answer: 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹; 1000/120 = 8.33 on that stated scaled convention. They must not be mixed on one unlabelled axis.

4. Match the instruction

A graph has one result at each pH. Name one extra thing an EVALUATE answer needs beyond describing its peak.

Evaluate = use a strength or limitation to reach a supported judgement.

Answer: For example: one trial per pH limits repeatability evidence, so repeat matched trials before treating the peak as secure.

Draft explanation: “Enzymes are killed at pH 4. pH 7 is the exact optimum for every amylase, and diffusion breaks starch into sugar.”

Reference: enzymes are biological catalysts, not living organisms. Digestion breaks molecules down; diffusion is net movement from higher to lower concentration. pH can affect enzyme activity.

Which revision is supported?

In these single runs, rate rises from 0.00417 s⁻¹ at pH 4 to 0.0111 s⁻¹ at pH 7, then falls to 0.00667 s⁻¹ at pH 8. pH may affect the active site and catalysis. pH 7 is highest among those tested; repeats and more nearby pH values would help refine the inference. Word help

enzyme

A biological catalyst that speeds a reaction without being used up.

substrate

The substance an enzyme acts on.

active site

The region where the substrate binds and the reaction is catalysed.

specificity

An enzyme acts on particular substrates because of its complementary active site.

endpoint

The agreed observable point at which timing is recorded.

control variable

A factor kept the same to make the comparison valid.

denaturation

A change in an enzyme’s three-dimensional structure that disrupts the active site and its function. Unsuitable pH or high temperature can cause this.

Teacher answers · W8L3

Shared activities

Starter · reverse the time: 90 s is faster than 180 s for matched amounts and the same endpoint. 1/90 ≈ 0.0111 s⁻¹; 1/180 ≈ 0.00556 s⁻¹. Doubling the time halves the relative rate. Worked rate and graph point: Relative rate = 1/time = 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹. Plot pH 6 at 0.00833 s⁻¹; pH has no unit. Build an evidence chain: Calculation checks: a reciprocal value; shorter time gives larger rate. Pattern/conclusion: pH 7 has the highest first-series rate among tested values; rate rises to pH 7 then falls at pH 8. Evaluation: colour judgement, shorter sampling intervals, and transparent treatment of repeats/anomalies. Graph and claim repair: First-series rates are 0.00417, 0.00556, 0.00833, 0.0111 and 0.00667 s⁻¹ for pH 4–8. The separate pH 7 repeat is 0.00833 s⁻¹, plotted with a distinct symbol at x = 7. These values do not prove an exact universal optimum or permanent denaturation. Topic 1 process labels: Starch chemically breaking into smaller sugars is enzyme digestion. Net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration is diffusion. Movement does not itself chemically break starch into sugars.

Supported arrival

  • 1. 90 s is faster.
  • 2. Orange-brown.
  • 3. 1 ÷ 100 = 0.0100 s⁻¹ (0.01 s⁻¹ is also correct).
  • 4. EVALUATE.

Supported independent task

  • 1. pH 6: 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹. pH 7: 1/90 = 0.0111 s⁻¹.
  • 2. Plot (6, 0.00833) and (7, 0.0111), pH on x-axis, relative rate/s⁻¹ on y-axis. Accept equivalent pupil-directed placement.
  • 3. pH 7 is fastest in the first series, because 0.0111 s⁻¹ is the highest measured relative rate (or 90 s the shortest time).
  • 4. The exact endpoint between samples is unknown. A shorter fixed interval could narrow the timing window if authorised and feasible; it does not remove every uncertainty.
  • 5. Starch breaking into smaller sugars: digestion. Particles moving down a concentration gradient: diffusion.

Supported exit

  • 1. 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹; pH 7 is faster in the first series (90 s).
  • 2. The first statement is supported. Only pH 4–8 in these conditions were tested; the repeat also shows variation.

Standard arrival

  • 1. The same measured change takes less time; 1/90 is larger than 1/180.
  • 2. 90 s, while acknowledging the endpoint was reached after 60 s and by 90 s.
  • 3. Relative rate = 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹.
  • 4. Describe states how values change; explain uses biology, such as pH affecting the active site, to account for the pattern.

Standard independent task

  • 1. Relative rate = 1/time. pH 6: 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹. pH 7: 1/90 = 0.0111 s⁻¹. The shorter time produces the larger reciprocal rate.
  • 2. x-axis pH (no unit); y-axis relative rate/s⁻¹. Points 4:0.00417, 5:0.00556, 6:0.00833, 7:0.0111, 8:0.00667. Rate rises from pH 4 to 7, then falls at pH 8.
  • 3. pH 7 has the highest first-series rate, 0.0111 s⁻¹ versus e.g. pH 4 at 0.00417 s⁻¹. pH can alter the active-site environment/shape and affect substrate binding and catalysis. The graph does not prove permanent denaturation or an exact universal optimum.
  • 4. Strength: matched starting amounts/temperature and a common endpoint support comparison. Limitation: 30 s intervals or subjective colour judgement. Improvement: shorter fixed intervals if authorised, or consistent agreed colour judgement; link it to the limitation. The pH 7 repeat gives 120 s, so variation weakens confidence from one series.
  • 5. Digestion is chemical breakdown catalysed by enzymes; amylase breaks starch into smaller sugars. Diffusion is net particle movement from higher to lower concentration. Moving particles does not itself change starch molecules chemically.

Standard exit

  • 1. pH 6: 0.00833 s⁻¹; pH 7: 0.0111 s⁻¹. pH 7 is faster in the first series under these matched conditions.
  • 2. Variation: repeat matched trials across all pH values to judge the pattern. Or 30 s timing gap: shorter fixed intervals narrow the endpoint window if authorised.

Stretch arrival

  • 1. Its relative rate halves.
  • 2. It summarises time to an observed endpoint, with sampling and colour limits. It does not continuously measure the amount changed per instant.
  • 3. 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹; 1000/120 = 8.33 on that stated scaled convention. They must not be mixed on one unlabelled axis.
  • 4. For example: one trial per pH limits repeatability evidence, so repeat matched trials before treating the peak as secure.

Stretch independent task

  • 1. pH 6 = 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹. First pH 7 = 1/90 = 0.0111 s⁻¹. Separate repeat pH 7 = 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹; it equals the first pH 6 rate. Keep both pH 7 observations visible.
  • 2. First-series graph uses the five supplied/calculated coordinates. Repeat belongs at (7, 0.00833), as the same IV condition measured again, with its own symbol/key. Do not silently replace or average raw points.
  • 3. The claim is too broad. pH 7 is highest only in the first series among pH 4–8 tested. A 120 s repeat shows variation and matches the first pH 6 time. Untested values and other amylase sources/conditions remain unknown. pH effects on active sites are a biological explanation, not direct molecular observations from this graph.
  • 4. Matched starting amounts/temperature and common endpoint strengthen validity. A 30 s interval limits timing precision; a shorter fixed interval could narrow that gap if feasible/authorised. It does not remove subjective colour judgement, variable starting conditions or all repeat variation.
  • 5. Digestion chemically breaks large molecules down; diffusion moves particles down a concentration gradient. They are different processes. The graph measures an endpoint in starch breakdown, not membrane transport, concentration gradients across a gut wall or transport proteins.

Stretch exit

  • 1. Repeat rate = 1/120 = 0.00833 s⁻¹, matching first-series pH 6. It shows variation and weakens an exact-optimum claim.
  • 2. A shorter authorised interval narrows the timing window, or repeats clarify variation. Lower activity alone does not establish irreversible structural change; the graph does not measure active-site structure or recovery.
