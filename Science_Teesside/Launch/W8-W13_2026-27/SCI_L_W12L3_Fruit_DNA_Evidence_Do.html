Full transcript

Break up the tissue. Gently mash soft fruit in a sealed bag with the centre-approved extraction solution.

Release the contents. Detergent helps disrupt membranes. Salt assists the extraction and precipitation process.

Remove large debris. Filter the mixture. The liquid contains dissolved and suspended material released from the fruit.

Observe the interface. An adult carefully layers cold ethanol. White, stringy material may appear; it is a crude DNA-containing extract.

Work through one example

Worked example

After filtering, a liquid looks clear. Does that prove it contains only DNA?

Step 1

The observation is appearance: the liquid looks clear.

Step 2

Filtering removed large debris, not every dissolved substance.

Step 3

Other molecules may remain even when they are not visible.

Step 4

Therefore a clear filtrate is not evidence of pure DNA.

Teacher-approved practical or observation route

Teacher/technician must approve the local method, concentrations, quantities, PPE and disposal. Use soft fruit prepared by an adult, sealed bags and a tray. Check allergies without requiring public disclosure. No tasting. Adult handles cold ethanol; keep it away from flames, sparks and heat. Do not use human samples or mouth pipetting. Observation and supplied-data routes are equal alternatives.

  1. Gently mash soft fruit in a sealed bag with the centre-approved extraction solution.
  2. Detergent helps disrupt membranes. Salt assists the extraction and precipitation process.
  3. Filter the mixture. The liquid contains dissolved and suspended material released from the fruit.
  4. An adult carefully layers cold ethanol. White, stringy material may appear; it is a crude DNA-containing extract.
Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

The observation is consistent with a DNA-containing precipitate from the fruit extract. The water comparison helps show that strands did not simply appear in every tube. It does not identify all substances or prove purity.

Learning objective

Explain the roles of extraction steps and distinguish an observation of a crude extract from a claim about purity.

Key words

Extraction — Separating material from cells.

Membrane — A boundary around a cell or organelle.

Detergent — Helps disrupt membranes and release cell contents.

Filter — Separates larger debris from liquid.

Precipitate — Material that comes out of a solution.

Crude extract — A mixture that has not been purified.

Remember

• Mashing breaks up fruit tissue.

• Detergent helps disrupt cell and nuclear membranes.

• Filtering removes larger pieces of tissue.

• Cold ethanol helps DNA precipitate; visible material may contain other substances.

Success looks like

• Explain release, filtering and precipitation steps.

• Record an observation without claiming purity.

• State one practical limit or useful comparison.

Arrival

1. Release: Membranes.

2. Filter: Larger tissue debris.

3. Observe: An observation.

4. Safety: The trained adult, following the centre’s risk assessment.

Independent / application

1. Explain: Mashing breaks tissue; detergent disrupts membranes; filtering removes large debris; ethanol helps DNA precipitate.

2. Record: For supplied data: “The supplied record states white strands appeared at the boundary”; this is consistent with a crude DNA-containing extract.

3. Evaluate: Appearance does not show purity. Keep conditions consistent and include a comparison; do not claim this alone chemically identifies DNA.

Two-check exit

1. Role: It helps disrupt membranes, releasing cell contents including DNA.

2. Limit: The crude extract may contain other cell material; appearance alone does not establish purity.
