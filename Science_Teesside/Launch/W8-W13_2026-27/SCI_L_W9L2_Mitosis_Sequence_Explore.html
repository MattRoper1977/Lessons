I do

LAUNCH · GCSE BIOLOGY FOUNDATION · W9 · LESSON 2 · 5 MINUTES

Watch the model

During interphase the cell grows and copies its DNA. Each replicated chromosome has two joined sister chromatids.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

This schematic uses four chromosomes, not the human number. A replicated X is one chromosome with two sister chromatids. After chromatids separate, each is a chromosome. DNA copying does not mean each daughter cell has twice the chromosome number. The full cycle includes interphase, mitosis and cytokinesis. Interphase chromosome symbols show DNA copies schematically; the chromosomes are not normally visibly condensed at this stage.

32-second silent captioned explainer · optional

No sound or autoplay. Use instead of part of the modelling time, not as an extra activity.

Full transcript

Prepare and copy. During interphase the cell grows and copies its DNA. Each replicated chromosome has two joined sister chromatids.

Line up. During metaphase the replicated chromosomes line up at the centre of the cell.

Separate copies. During anaphase sister chromatids separate. One copy of each chromosome moves to each end.

Divide the cell. Nuclei reform and the cytoplasm divides. Two daughter cells each receive the same chromosome set.

Read the card clues

Chromosomes condense: Prophase makes chromosomes easier to see.

Nuclear membrane breaks down: This happens during early mitosis.

Chromosomes line up centrally: Metaphase comes before separation.

Sister chromatids move apart: Anaphase sends one copy to each pole.

Two complete sets at opposite ends: The sets have reached the ends as telophase begins.

Nuclear membranes reform: Telophase forms two nuclei.

Unlabelled cell models and observations

Cards P, Q, R and S describe a cell. Put them in order and give the evidence, not just a remembered letter sequence.

CardVisible observation
PTwo nuclear membranes are reforming
QChromosomes have condensed
RChromosomes line up across the centre

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

Q → R → S → P: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase. Interphase happens before Q; cytokinesis completes division after nuclear separation.

LAUNCH · W9 · Lesson 2 · Shared resource

Learning objective

Sequence prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase using visible chromosome evidence.

Key words

Cell cycle — Growth, DNA copying and cell division.

Interphase — The cell grows and copies its DNA before mitosis.

Chromosome — A long DNA molecule packaged with proteins.

Mitosis — Division of the nucleus into two genetically identical nuclei.

Cytokinesis — Division of the cytoplasm to form two cells.

Diploid — Two sets of chromosomes, one set from each parent.

Remember

• DNA is copied before mitosis, during interphase.

• Prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase describes mitosis.

• Cytokinesis produces separate daughter cells.

• Mitosis supports growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

Success looks like

• Order prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase.

• Link each stage to chromosome evidence.

• Distinguish an observation from an uncertain stage label.

3. Distinguish: Sister chromatids separate; each becomes a chromosome.

4. Count: 4 chromosomes.

Independent / application

1. Order: Q, R, S, P.

2. Justify: For example, metaphase has chromosomes aligned at the centre; anaphase has separated copies moving towards opposite poles.

3. Label: Interphase before prophase; cytokinesis completes separation after nuclear division.

Two-check exit

1. Evidence: Metaphase.

2. Order: Telophase; new nuclear membranes form around the chromosome sets.
