Punnett Squares: Construct the Prediction

Sequence outcome: Explain monohybrid inheritance using genetic diagrams and Punnett squares.

🎯 Learning objective: I can construct a Punnett square from parental genotypes and explain what its outcomes predict.
✅ Success criteria
  • I can write one allele in each parental gamete.
  • I can complete all four offspring genotype cells accurately.
  • I can convert genotype outcomes into phenotype predictions without treating them as guaranteed.

WHAT THIS MODEL HELPS US SEE

The grid accounts visibly for every possible pairing of one allele from each parent's gametes.

WHAT THIS MODEL DOES NOT SHOW

It assumes a simple monohybrid cross, equally likely gametes and complete dominance; it predicts probability, not the exact order or number of offspring.

Step 1

Write the parental cross Pp × Pp, then place P and p as the two possible gamete alleles from each parent.

Step 2

Draw a 2 × 2 grid and bring one allele from the top and one from the side into every box.

Step 3

Check the four genotypes: PP, Pp, Pp and pp. Do not skip repeated outcomes.

👩‍🏫 I Do 2 · connect / extend

Connect the model to the second idea

Connection: the model is useful when its visible pattern is translated into a scientific explanation.
Connection 1

Count genotypes: 1 PP : 2 Pp : 1 pp.

Connection 2

Apply the phenotype rule: PP and Pp are purple, pp is white, so the predicted phenotype ratio is 3 purple : 1 white.

Connection 3

Interpret as probability for each offspring: 3/4 purple and 1/4 white; the next offspring is not controlled by previous outcomes.
