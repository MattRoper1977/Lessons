Punnett squares: construct the prediction

I do

LAUNCH · GCSE BIOLOGY FOUNDATION · W13 · LESSON 2 · 5 MINUTES

Watch the model

For this one-gene, complete-dominance model, both parents are heterozygous: Aa × Aa.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

This is a simple monohybrid complete-dominance model. Dominant does not mean common, stronger, healthier or better. Treat each fertilisation as an independent event. Avoid deterministic single-gene examples such as eye colour or tongue rolling.

32-second silent captioned explainer · optional

No sound or autoplay. Use instead of part of the modelling time, not as an extra activity.

Full transcript

Describe the parents. For this one-gene, complete-dominance model, both parents are heterozygous: Aa × Aa.

Separate into gametes. Each parent can produce gametes carrying A or a. Put one allele in each margin box.

Combine one from each. Each offspring box combines one allele from the top with one from the side: AA, Aa, Aa and aa.

Read the probability. The chance of aa is one out of four, or 25%, for each fertilisation. Four offspring do not have to follow this pattern.

Worked example

Construct BB × bb in a simple one-gene model.

Step 1

A BB parent can contribute only B gametes.

Step 2

A bb parent can contribute only b gametes.

Step 3

Combine one from each: every predicted offspring is Bb.

Step 4

The predicted chance of Bb is 100% under these model assumptions.

LAUNCH · W13 · Lesson 2 · Shared resource

Learning objective

Construct a monohybrid Punnett square and explain how gametes combine to predict offspring genotypes.

Key words

Allele — A version of a gene.

Genotype — The allele combination for a gene.

Phenotype — An observable characteristic.

Dominant — Expressed in a heterozygote in this simple model.

Recessive — Expressed when two recessive alleles are present in this model.

Gamete — A sex cell with one allele for each gene in the model.

Remember

• Use the same letter for alleles of one gene: A and a.

• AA and aa are homozygous; Aa is heterozygous.

• A Punnett square predicts probabilities, not a fixed family pattern.

• Most real human characteristics involve more than one gene and the environment.

Success looks like

• Put one allele in each gamete margin.

• Combine one allele from each parent in every box.

• Read a genotype probability from the completed square.

1. Construct: Margins A,a and A,a; boxes AA,Aa,Aa,aa.

2. Compare: Aa,Aa,aa,aa; probability aa = 2/4 = 1/2 = 50%.

3. Explain: A from one parent and a from the other can combine in two ways, both producing Aa.

Two-check exit

1. Construct: AA, Aa, Aa, aa.

2. Interpret: 2/4 = 1/2.
