LAUNCH · GCSE BIOLOGY FOUNDATION · W10 · LESSON 2 · 4 MINUTES

Stem cells and meristems: compare potential

Learning objective

Compare embryonic stem cells, adult stem cells and plant meristems without overstating their potential.

40 minutes · one route · start from what you show today. Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

Supported · 4 questions

1. Definition

What is a stem cell?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Unspecialised; can divide and differentiate.

Answer guidance

An unspecialised cell that can divide and develop into specialised cell types.

2. Location

Where are meristems found?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Look at growing root and shoot tips.

Answer guidance

At growing root and shoot tips, and other plant growth regions.

3. Potential

Which usually has wider potential: an embryonic stem cell or an adult blood stem cell?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Compare many cell types with blood-cell types.

Answer guidance

An embryonic stem cell.

4. Word care

Does “adult stem cell” mean it can become any cell in the body?

Help beside the question
Read or point first. Use the idea of limited potential.

Answer guidance

No; most adult stem cells have a more limited range of possible cell types.

I do

LAUNCH · GCSE BIOLOGY FOUNDATION · W10 · LESSON 2 · 5 MINUTES

Watch the model

Mitosis increases cell number. New cells can grow before they divide again.

Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

Do not equate growth only with mitosis. Meristems are regions, not the name of every plant stem cell. Differentiation changes cell structure and gene activity; it does not normally remove the other genes.

32-second silent captioned explainer · optional

No sound or autoplay. Use instead of part of the modelling time, not as an extra activity.

Full transcript

More cells. Mitosis increases cell number. New cells can grow before they divide again.

Longer cells. Plant cells can elongate. A longer root is not proof that every cell has divided.

Different jobs. Differentiation produces cells with specialised structures, such as root hair cells or neurones.

Where growth begins. Meristems contain cells that keep dividing. New cells can elongate and differentiate.

Work through one example

Worked example

An adult blood stem cell produces several blood-cell types. What does that show?

Step 1

Identify the source: an adult blood stem cell.

Step 2

The observed range is blood-cell types.

Step 3

This supports differentiation within that range.

Step 4

It does not show that the cell can form every body tissue.

LAUNCH · GCSE BIOLOGY FOUNDATION · W10 · LESSON 2 · 4 MINUTES

Match a source to a justified claim

Compare potential and use. Keep “can produce a cell type” separate from “is proven safe as a treatment”.

SourceModel potentialWhat this does not prove
Embryonic stem cellsMany body-cell typesA treatment is safe in people
Adult blood stem cellsDifferent blood-cell typesThey can form every organ
Plant meristemNew plant cells and tissuesIt is an animal treatment
Worked explanation · reveal after thinking

Potential describes possible differentiation. Clinical safety and usefulness require separate evidence; plant meristems must not be treated as human stem-cell therapies.

Knowledge organiser

LAUNCH · W10 · Lesson 2 · Shared resource

Learning objective

Compare embryonic stem cells, adult stem cells and plant meristems without overstating their potential.

Key words

Growth — An increase in size or mass.

Division — One cell produces daughter cells.

Elongation — A cell becomes longer.

Differentiation — A cell develops specialised structures and functions.

Stem cell — An unspecialised cell that can divide and differentiate.

Meristem — A region containing dividing, unspecialised plant cells.

Remember

• Animals grow through cell division and differentiation.

• Plants grow through division, elongation and differentiation.

• Embryonic stem cells can form many cell types; most adult stem cells have more limited potential.

• Meristems occur at growing root and shoot tips.

Success looks like

• Compare embryonic and adult stem-cell potential.

• Locate meristems at root and shoot tips.

• Separate differentiation potential from treatment proof.

Route answers · Standard

LAUNCH · W10 · Lesson 2 · Standard

Arrival

1. Definition: An unspecialised cell that can divide and develop into specialised cell types.

2. Location: At growing root and shoot tips, and other plant growth regions.

3. Potential: An embryonic stem cell.

4. Word care: No; most adult stem cells have a more limited range of possible cell types.

Independent / application

1. Compare: Embryo: many cell types; adult bone marrow: blood-cell types; root/shoot meristems: dividing plant cells.

2. Correct: Most adult stem cells have limited potential; adult blood stem cells mainly produce blood-cell types.

3. Apply: Dividing plant cells can provide new cells that differentiate into the required tissues under suitable conditions.

Two-check exit

1. Compare: Embryonic stem cells can form many cell types; most adult stem cells have a more limited range.

2. Locate: Meristem cells divide to provide new cells, which can elongate and differentiate.
