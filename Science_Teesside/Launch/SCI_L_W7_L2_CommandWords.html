Learning intention

Success looks like

  • Use the supplied evidence.
  • Improve a response without adding irrelevant detail.

  1. Underline the command word and the scientific focus.
  2. Look for evidence, numbers, units and marks.
  3. Choose a response structure that fits the verb.
  4. For explain, connect a feature or cause to an effect.
  5. For compare, discuss both things on the same feature.
  6. For evaluate, weigh support and limitations before judging.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. Describe: as solution concentration rises from 0.0 to 0.6 mol dm⁻³, mean mass change falls from +15% to −15%.
  2. Explain: in more concentrated external solutions, water has net movement out of tissue cells by osmosis.
  3. Compare: osmosis concerns water through a partially permeable membrane, whereas active transport moves substances against gradients using energy.
  4. Evaluate: the consistent main repeats support a trend, but limited concentrations and possible blotting error restrict precision.

Check a common mistake

Success looks like

  • Recognise what the question asks.
  • Use the supplied evidence.
  • Improve a response without adding irrelevant detail.

Remember

  • Identify usually needs the requested name or feature.
  • Describe a data pattern using the relevant variable changes.
  • Explain links a cause or feature to a consequence.
  • Evaluate considers supporting evidence and limitations rather than giving praise or criticism alone.

Words that help

describe — Give an accurate account of a feature, event, process or pattern.

explain — Give a reason or show how something happens.

compare — State similarities and/or differences on the same feature.

evaluate — Weigh evidence and limitations to make a judgement.

Check this idea: The same scientific knowledge may need a different response when the command word changes.

Knowledge organiser · Command words: make the science visible

Learning intention: I can adapt a scientific response to identify, describe, explain, calculate, compare and evaluate.

Success looks like

  • Recognise what the question asks.
  • Use the supplied evidence.
  • Improve a response without adding irrelevant detail.

Remember

  • Identify usually needs the requested name or feature.
  • Describe a data pattern using the relevant variable changes.
  • Explain links a cause or feature to a consequence.
  • Evaluate considers supporting evidence and limitations rather than giving praise or criticism alone.

Words that help

describe — Give an accurate account of a feature, event, process or pattern.

explain — Give a reason or show how something happens.

compare — State similarities and/or differences on the same feature.

evaluate — Weigh evidence and limitations to make a judgement.

Check this idea: The same scientific knowledge may need a different response when the command word changes.

Staff key · Shared work

Describe: State how the graph changes as concentration rises.; Report that mean mass change falls from +15% to −15%..

Explain: Link thin walls to a short diffusion path..

Compare: Give a similarity and a difference between transport processes..

Evaluate: Judge a conclusion using repeats and method limitations.; Explain why one unusual repeat weakens confidence..

Worked example

  1. Describe: as solution concentration rises from 0.0 to 0.6 mol dm⁻³, mean mass change falls from +15% to −15%.
  2. Explain: in more concentrated external solutions, water has net movement out of tissue cells by osmosis.
  3. Compare: osmosis concerns water through a partially permeable membrane, whereas active transport moves substances against gradients using energy.
  4. Evaluate: the consistent main repeats support a trend, but limited concentrations and possible blotting error restrict precision.

Evidence choices

Which response answers “describe the trend”?
Percentage mass change decreases as concentration increases.

Which response answers “evaluate the method”?
Repeats reveal variation, but inconsistent blotting could bias masses.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 7

Staff answers · You do · Standard

1. Describe the pattern using two values.

As concentration rises from 0.0 to 0.6 mol dm⁻³, mean mass change falls from +15% to −15%.

2. Explain why tissue loses mass in the most concentrated solution.

Water has net movement out of cells by osmosis through partially permeable membranes into the more concentrated external solution.

3. Compare diffusion with active transport using direction and energy.

Diffusion is down a gradient without respiration energy; active transport is against a gradient and requires respiration energy.

4. Evaluate the claim: “These data give the exact concentration inside every potato cell.”

The trend and repeats support an approximate zero-change estimate, but only four concentrations were tested, blotting was not documented and tissue cells vary; the exact universal claim is not supported.

1. Describe the crossing from gain to loss without treating the estimate as a measurement.

The mean changes from +5% at 0.2 to −5% at 0.4, so zero lies between them; about 0.3 by interpolation.

It explains the molecular movement causing the observed mass decrease, rather than only reporting a lower value.

Test closer concentrations around the crossing with replicates and standardised blotting to improve precision and reduce method variation.

Staff preparation · Command words: make the science visible

Before you teach: Use these original classroom questions with the official command-word definitions as the reference. The visible sentence frames support learning and are not exam-board mark schemes.

Start from what pupils show: use the four arrival checks. Do not assume earlier work was taught or completed. Model first where needed; invite a reason or transfer where it is familiar.

Starter4–7 min

Responsive next example: If an answer is correct science but wrong form, retain its correct point and model the missing connection, comparison or judgement.

During the first We do: receive one pupil choice or question in their preferred mode, explain how it affected the example or support, and continue the science. No extra participation sheet is needed; passing is permitted.

Feedback: give a specific next step and make room for a pupil response. A click, saved response or completed sheet is not automatically evidence that the pupil was heard. Use school codes only for what actually happened.

Reference codes from the supplied chassis: VF verbal feedback; WS worked with support; I independent; NS + specific next step; // self-edit; ? read back; R responded after the adult has genuinely received and engaged with the response; E purposeful evidence. Do not award these automatically.
