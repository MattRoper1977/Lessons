LAUNCH SCIENCE · WEEK 7 · 40 MINUTES

Cell science: connect the evidence

Learning intention

I can connect microscopy, exchange and transport ideas to explain how cells obtain substances they need.

Success looks like

  • Connect a structure to its function.
  • Use a calculation accurately.
  • Choose a transport explanation from evidence.

Point, speak, sign, arrange cards, draw, write or direct an adult. You may pass or leave an answer blank.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. Evidence: a root cell has a long projection and takes up ions from a dilute soil solution.
  2. Structure link: the projection increases contact area.
  3. Transport link: uptake from lower to higher ion concentration needs active transport.
  4. Energy link: respiration supplies the required energy. These links form an explanation, not just a list.

Check a common mistake

A string of topic names is not yet an explanation. The words on the connecting arrows must explain the relationship.

Knowledge organiser

Knowledge organiser

Learning intention: I can connect microscopy, exchange and transport ideas to explain how cells obtain substances they need.

Success looks like

  • Connect a structure to its function.
  • Use a calculation accurately.
  • Choose a transport explanation from evidence.

Remember

  • Microscopy gives evidence about structures, while size calculations establish scale.
  • Exchange surfaces support movement by providing area, a short path and maintained gradients.
  • Diffusion, osmosis and active transport explain different kinds of movement.
  • A concept map is stronger when its arrows are labelled with meaningful links.

Words that help

structure — A part or arrangement of an organism or cell.

function — The job a structure does.

evidence link — A reason showing how two ideas are connected.

model limitation — Something a representation leaves out or simplifies.

Check this idea: A string of topic names is not yet an explanation. The words on the connecting arrows must explain the relationship.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 7

Knowledge organiser · Cell science: connect the evidence

Learning intention: I can connect microscopy, exchange and transport ideas to explain how cells obtain substances they need.

Success looks like

  • Connect a structure to its function.
  • Use a calculation accurately.
  • Choose a transport explanation from evidence.

Remember

  • Microscopy gives evidence about structures, while size calculations establish scale.
  • Exchange surfaces support movement by providing area, a short path and maintained gradients.
  • Diffusion, osmosis and active transport explain different kinds of movement.
  • A concept map is stronger when its arrows are labelled with meaningful links.

Words that help

structure — A part or arrangement of an organism or cell.

function — The job a structure does.

evidence link — A reason showing how two ideas are connected.

model limitation — Something a representation leaves out or simplifies.

Check this idea: A string of topic names is not yet an explanation. The words on the connecting arrows must explain the relationship.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 7

Staff key · Shared work

Microscopy and size: Image size ÷ actual size; Resolution reveals detail.

Diffusion and exchange: Oxygen moves down its gradient; Thin alveolar wall shortens the path.

Osmosis: Water crosses a partially permeable membrane.

Active transport: Ions move against a gradient using energy.

Worked example

  1. Evidence: a root cell has a long projection and takes up ions from a dilute soil solution.
  2. Structure link: the projection increases contact area.
  3. Transport link: uptake from lower to higher ion concentration needs active transport.
  4. Energy link: respiration supplies the required energy. These links form an explanation, not just a list.

Evidence choices

A 16 mm image represents an 80 µm object. What is its magnification?
200×.

A potato gains 0.20 g from an initial 2.00 g. Which statement is justified?
Its mass increases by 10%.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 7

Staff answers · You do · Standard

1. Solve station 1 and explain your unit conversion.

16 mm = 16,000 µm; magnification = 16,000 ÷ 80 = 200×.

2. Explain station 2 using two linked ideas.

Oxygen diffuses from higher concentration in alveolar air to lower in blood; a thin surface shortens the path or blood flow maintains the gradient.

3. Calculate percentage mass change at station 3, then explain it.

+10%; consistent with net water entry by osmosis through partially permeable membranes from a more dilute external solution.

4. Create four labelled connections linking the stations.

Accept correct links such as microscope → observes cells; membrane → permits selective exchange; oxygen → diffuses down gradient; water → osmosis; ions → active transport requiring respiration.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 7

Staff answers · You do · Stretch

1. The image is halved in size. What stays the same and what changes?

Actual size remains 80 µm; image size becomes 8 mm and magnification becomes 100×.

2. Why is ventilation not itself diffusion across the wall?

Ventilation moves bulk air; diffusion is molecular transfer down a gradient across the surface.

3. Name one reason why the mass result alone does not prove every detail of the mechanism.

Inconsistent blotting or weighing could affect mass; it is evidence interpreted with biological knowledge, not direct observation of molecules.

4. Add a limitation to one model and explain why it matters.

For example, particle dots are not to scale and the diagram does not predict each molecule’s exact path.

Staff preparation · Cell science: connect the evidence

Before you teach: Provide four evidence stations as cards or on screen. Pupils can remain in one place and direct an adult; physical rotation is optional. Use the organiser to support an unknown starting point.

Start from what pupils show: use the four arrival checks. Do not assume earlier work was taught or completed. Model first where needed; invite a reason or transfer where it is familiar.

Responsive next example: Use gaps to choose one re-modelled example. Do not label a pupil as having missed a unit because their map is sparse.

During the first We do: receive one pupil choice or question in their preferred mode, explain how it affected the example or support, and continue the science. No extra participation sheet is needed; passing is permitted.

Feedback: give a specific next step and make room for a pupil response. A click, saved response or completed sheet is not automatically evidence that the pupil was heard. Use school codes only for what actually happened.

Reference codes from the supplied chassis: VF verbal feedback; WS worked with support; I independent; NS + specific next step; // self-edit; ? read back; R responded after the adult has genuinely received and engaged with the response; E purposeful evidence. Do not award these automatically.
