Starter · notice the evidence

Which clue is more useful for choosing a process: “into the cell” or “against the concentration gradient using energy”?

Point to a clue. Give a first idea, then check it against the organiser or model.

Check the first idea
The gradient and energy clue is more useful: it identifies active transport. “Into the cell” alone is incomplete.

3 minutes

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. Case: a potato piece changes from 5.00 g to 5.50 g.
  2. Percentage = (5.50 − 5.00) ÷ 5.00 × 100 = +10%.
  3. A gain is consistent with net water entering cells under suitable solution conditions.
  4. The mass change alone does not tell us every detail of the method or prove who collected it.

Watch for this mistake

A correct process name is enough even when the stated conditions do not fit.

Knowledge organiser

LAUNCH SCIENCE · KNOWLEDGE ORGANISER

Cells and transport: connect the ideas

Learning objective: I can connect microscopy, calculations and transport using evidence from this unit.

Success looks like

  • Retrieve key ideas without assuming earlier lessons were completed.
  • Choose a transport process using the stated conditions.
  • Use a calculation or explanation to support a claim.

Keep these ideas

  • Microscopy helps observe cells, but image size and resolution are different.
  • Calculations need matching units and an identified unknown.
  • Transport depends on the substance, gradient, membrane and energy conditions.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.
