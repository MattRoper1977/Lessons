I do 2

3 minutes

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. Image length = 16 mm; actual length = 40 µm.
  2. 16 mm = 16 000 µm.
  3. Magnification = 16 000 ÷ 40 = ×400.
  4. Check: 40 × 400 = 16 000 µm.

Watch for this mistake

An answer without units or method is always enough.

Show relevant working and give length units where needed; magnification itself has no length unit.

Magnification exam skills

Learning objective: I can answer magnification questions with a clear method, suitable units and a sensible check.

Success looks like

  • Read the command word and identify the unknown.
  • Set out a correct conversion and calculation.
  • Use a scale-bar ratio and explain an estimate.

Keep these ideas

  • Identify whether the unknown is magnification, image size or actual size.
  • A scale-bar ratio can be used even when the whole image is resized proportionally.
  • Use stated image lengths in this pack, not a ruler on a phone screen.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

calculate — Find a numerical answer and show relevant working.

estimate — Find an approximate value from evidence.

scale bar — A labelled line representing a real length.

working — The steps that connect the data to the answer.

Worked example

Image length = 16 mm; actual length = 40 µm.

16 mm = 16 000 µm.

Magnification = 16 000 ÷ 40 = ×400.

Check: 40 × 400 = 16 000 µm.

A useful correction
Show relevant working and give length units where needed; magnification itself has no length unit.
