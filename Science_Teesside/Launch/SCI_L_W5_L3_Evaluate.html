Osmosis evidence: graphs, anomalies and judgement

Learning intention

I can interpret repeated osmosis results, estimate the zero-change point and evaluate the method.

Success looks like

  • Calculate and interpret means.
  • Estimate, rather than overclaim, a zero-change concentration.
  • Connect a limitation to a useful improvement.

Point, speak, sign, arrange cards, draw, write or direct an adult. You may pass or leave an answer blank.

Read the complete explainer transcript
  1. Look at replicate percentage changes at each concentration.
  2. Add the repeat values and divide by the number of repeats to calculate a mean.
  3. Plot concentration against mean percentage mass change.
  4. Locate where the trend crosses zero change.
  5. Use nearby measurements to estimate the crossing concentration.
  6. Evaluate spread, sample size, controls and possible measurement errors before making a judgement.

I do · Worked example · 4 min

I do · a worked example

  1. At 0.2 mol dm⁻³, three changes are +4%, +5% and +6%.
  2. Mean = (4 + 5 + 6) ÷ 3 = +5%.
  3. At 0.4 mol dm⁻³, the mean is −5%. A straight-line estimate crosses zero halfway between.
  4. Estimated zero-change concentration = 0.3 mol dm⁻³. Test closer concentrations around it rather than claiming exact cell contents.

Check a common mistake

An anomalous result is not automatically wrong. Keep a record, investigate possible causes and justify any exclusion.

Before the reveal, predict the next step or explain why it follows.

Knowledge organiser

Knowledge organiser

Learning intention: I can interpret repeated osmosis results, estimate the zero-change point and evaluate the method.

Success looks like

  • Calculate and interpret means.
  • Estimate, rather than overclaim, a zero-change concentration.
  • Connect a limitation to a useful improvement.

Remember

  • Plot solution concentration on the x-axis and percentage mass change on the y-axis.
  • Zero percentage change marks no net mass change in the measured tissue.
  • A crossing between tested concentrations is an estimate, not an exact measurement.
  • Do not discard an awkward result simply to make a graph look tidy.

Words that help

mean — The total of values divided by the number of values.

anomaly — A result that does not fit the main pattern and needs investigation.

interpolation — Estimating between measured values.

evaluation — A judgement that weighs evidence and limitations.

Check this idea: An anomalous result is not automatically wrong. Keep a record, investigate possible causes and justify any exclusion.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 5

Knowledge organiser · Osmosis evidence: graphs, anomalies and judgement

Learning intention: I can interpret repeated osmosis results, estimate the zero-change point and evaluate the method.

Success looks like

  • Calculate and interpret means.
  • Estimate, rather than overclaim, a zero-change concentration.
  • Connect a limitation to a useful improvement.

Remember

  • Plot solution concentration on the x-axis and percentage mass change on the y-axis.
  • Zero percentage change marks no net mass change in the measured tissue.
  • A crossing between tested concentrations is an estimate, not an exact measurement.
  • Do not discard an awkward result simply to make a graph look tidy.

Words that help

mean — The total of values divided by the number of values.

anomaly — A result that does not fit the main pattern and needs investigation.

interpolation — Estimating between measured values.

evaluation — A judgement that weighs evidence and limitations.

Check this idea: An anomalous result is not automatically wrong. Keep a record, investigate possible causes and justify any exclusion.

Staff answers · Arrival · Stretch

1. Calculate the change from 2.0 g to 2.1 g as a percentage.

+5%.

2. Find the mean of −4, −5 and −6.

−5.

3. Why is concentration the independent variable?

Its values are chosen and changed by the investigator.

4. Why is “human error” not a useful improvement by itself?

It does not identify a cause or describe a change that would reduce it.

Staff key · Shared work

Evidence-based practice: Record all repeats.; Investigate an unusual result.; State that an interpolated crossing is estimated..

Needs improving: Delete a result because it looks untidy.; Call two measurements absolute proof.; Ignore inconsistent blotting..

Worked example

  1. At 0.2 mol dm⁻³, three changes are +4%, +5% and +6%.
  2. Mean = (4 + 5 + 6) ÷ 3 = +5%.
  3. At 0.4 mol dm⁻³, the mean is −5%. A straight-line estimate crosses zero halfway between.
  4. Estimated zero-change concentration = 0.3 mol dm⁻³. Test closer concentrations around it rather than claiming exact cell contents.

Evidence choices

The anomaly row is −4%, −5%, +12%. What should happen first?
Check the record and method, retaining the original readings.

The line crosses zero near 0.30 mol dm⁻³. How should the conclusion be phrased?
An estimate between measured concentrations, with uncertainty.

Staff answers · You do · Standard

1. Calculate the four main means.

+15%, +5%, −5%, −15%, in concentration order.

2. Plot the means or direct an adult to place the points.

Points: (0.0,15), (0.2,5), (0.4,−5), (0.6,−15). Axes need labels and units.

3. Estimate the zero-change concentration.

About 0.3 mol dm⁻³ by interpolation between 0.2 and 0.4.

4. Evaluate the extra-check row and suggest an improvement.

+12% is inconsistent with −4% and −5% and with the main trend. Check labelling, blotting and weighing; repeat with new tissue and retain the original record.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 5

Staff answers · You do · Stretch

1. Compare the spread of the main rows.

Each main row spans 2 percentage points; this is similar spread, not proof of absence of systematic error.

2. Explain why the y-axis must include negative values.

Some tissue samples lost mass, producing negative percentage changes.

3. Why should you not report this as an exact cell concentration?

It is an interpolated tissue-level estimate from limited data and method assumptions, not a direct exact intracellular measurement.

4. Calculate the mean with all three extra-check values and explain the problem.

(−4 − 5 + 12) ÷ 3 = +1%. It differs strongly from the main −5% mean; investigate before deciding how to use it.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 5

Staff answers · Exit · Standard

1. What does a zero percentage mass change show?

No net measured mass change under the conditions, not no water movement.

2. Give one justified improvement to locate the crossing more precisely.

Test more closely spaced concentrations around 0.3 mol dm⁻³, with replicates and controlled conditions.

MADE BY MATT · LAUNCH Science · Week 5

Staff answers · Exit · Stretch

1. Why can water still move at the zero-change point?

Water moves in both directions; transfers balance on average.

2. Explain why that improves the estimate.

It reduces the gap between measured points near zero and shows whether the assumed trend fits there.

Staff preparation · Osmosis evidence: graphs, anomalies and judgement

Before you teach: Provide graph paper, calculators and the supplied dataset. The interactive graph is an alternative to precise drawing, not a substitute for teaching axis choice. No new practical is needed.

Start from what pupils show: use the four arrival checks. Do not assume earlier work was taught or completed. Model first where needed; invite a reason or transfer where it is familiar.

Responsive next example: If a pupil calls the crossing exact, point to the gap between measured concentrations and ask what was estimated rather than measured.

During the first We do: receive one pupil choice or question in their preferred mode, explain how it affected the example or support, and continue the science. No extra participation sheet is needed; passing is permitted.

Feedback: give a specific next step and make room for a pupil response. A click, saved response or completed sheet is not automatically evidence that the pupil was heard. Use school codes only for what actually happened.
