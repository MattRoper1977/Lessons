Full transcript

Cells. Connect DNA copying, chromosome separation and two daughter cells.

Evidence. Use numbers and a comparison before making a stem cell claim.

Genetic information. Connect nucleus, chromosome, DNA and gene; pair A–T and C–G.

Inheritance. Use gametes and a Punnett square, then interpret chance cautiously.

LAUNCH · GCSE BIOLOGY FOUNDATION · A2 W7 · LESSON 2 · 4 MINUTES

Work through one example

Worked example

Calculate 8/25 as a percentage and interpret it carefully.

Step 1

8 ÷ 25 = 0.32.

Step 2

0.32 × 100 = 32%.

Step 3

This describes the observed fraction in that sample.

Step 4

It does not by itself prove a general population probability.

Explain: DNA is copied before mitosis and chromosome copies separate into complete matching sets. Calculate: 7 ÷ 20 × 100 = 35%. Evaluate: short follow-up cannot establish long-term safety; more appropriate evidence is needed.

Learning objective

Use command words, selected evidence and a clear scientific link to improve exam-style responses.

Key words

Describe — Say what happens or what the evidence shows.

Explain — Connect a statement to a scientific reason.

Calculate — Use values and show the working.

Evaluate — Weigh evidence and identify a limitation.

Evidence — A value, observation or source supporting a claim.

Independent event — An outcome that does not determine the next outcome.

Remember

• Copy DNA before mitosis; each daughter cell retains the chromosome set.

• Stem cell evidence needs benefits, risks and uncertainty.

• A gene is a section of DNA; DNA has complementary base pairs.

• A genetic cross predicts a chance for each fertilisation.

Success looks like

• Respond to the command word.

• Select a value or mechanism that supports the answer.

• Improve a claim by adding an appropriate limitation.
