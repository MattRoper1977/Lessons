Silent, with visible captions. The video repeats the six teaching steps. Pause or replay; no internet is needed.

Read the full explainer transcript
  1. Read both lengths and their units.
  2. Convert 30 mm to 30 000 µm.
  3. Write magnification = image ÷ actual.
  4. Substitute 30 000 ÷ 75.
  5. Calculate ×400; do not add a length unit.
  6. For actual size, divide the image size by magnification instead.

I do 2

3 minutes

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. An image is 18 mm at ×300.
  2. Actual size = 18 ÷ 300 = 0.06 mm.
  3. 0.06 × 1000 = 60 µm.
  4. Check: 60 µm × 300 = 18 000 µm = 18 mm.

Watch for this mistake

Divide 30 mm by 75 µm without converting.

Convert first: 30 mm = 30 000 µm, then 30 000 ÷ 75 = ×400.

Calculating magnification

Learning objective: I can calculate magnification or actual size after putting lengths in the same unit.

Success looks like

  • Convert millimetres and micrometres.
  • Use magnification = image size ÷ actual size.
  • Show a calculation and check whether its unit makes sense.

Keep these ideas

  • 1 mm = 1000 µm.
  • Magnification = image size ÷ actual size, in matching units.
  • Actual size = image size ÷ magnification.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

image size — The measured size of the image or drawing.

actual size — The size of the real object.

micrometre (µm) — One thousandth of a millimetre.

ratio — A comparison made by dividing one quantity by another.

Worked example

An image is 18 mm at ×300.

Actual size = 18 ÷ 300 = 0.06 mm.

0.06 × 1000 = 60 µm.

Check: 60 µm × 300 = 18 000 µm = 18 mm.

A useful correction
Convert first: 30 mm = 30 000 µm, then 30 000 ÷ 75 = ×400.
