I do 2

3 minutes

I do 2 · make the reasoning visible

  1. Teaching root case: outside nitrate = 1 relative unit; inside = 6.
  2. Net uptake is 1 → 6, against the nitrate gradient.
  3. Active transport uses cellular energy to support this uptake.
  4. Water uptake is a separate question; compare water conditions before naming osmosis.

Watch for this mistake

Water and mineral ions both enter roots by osmosis.

Osmosis is water movement. Mineral-ion uptake against its gradient uses active transport.

LAUNCH SCIENCE · KNOWLEDGE ORGANISER

Root hairs and the gut

Learning objective: I can apply transport ideas to mineral uptake in root hairs and nutrient absorption in the small intestine.

Success looks like

  • Separate water uptake from mineral-ion uptake.
  • Explain uptake against a stated concentration gradient.
  • Link a large surface area and energy supply to absorption.

Keep these ideas

  • Water can enter root hair cells by osmosis.
  • Mineral ions can be taken up against their gradient by active transport.
  • The small intestine has a large absorption area from villi and microvilli.

Schematic teaching model, not to scale. Use stated measurements, not screen size.

Word help

root hair cell — A specialised root surface cell with a long extension.

mineral ion — A charged dissolved particle needed in small amounts by an organism.

villus — A small projection lining the small intestine.

absorption — Movement of digested nutrients across the gut lining into the body.

Worked example

Teaching root case: outside nitrate = 1 relative unit; inside = 6.

Net uptake is 1 → 6, against the nitrate gradient.

Active transport uses cellular energy to support this uptake.

Water uptake is a separate question; compare water conditions before naming osmosis.

A useful correction
Osmosis is water movement. Mineral-ion uptake against its gradient uses active transport.
