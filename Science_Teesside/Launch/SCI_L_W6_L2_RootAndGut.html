Learning intention

I can explain how root hairs and the small intestine are adapted for absorption and select a transport process from evidence.

Success looks like

  • Link a structural feature to absorption.
  • Distinguish water, mineral ions and sugars.
  • Use a supplied gradient to justify the process.

Starter · 3 min

Notice, choose, explain

A root takes up both water and mineral ions. Does that mean both enter by osmosis?

Think quietly, point to evidence or share a first idea. You may pass.

Teacher reveal · check the first idea
No. Osmosis concerns water; mineral ions may be taken up by active transport.

I do · a worked example

Knowledge organiser

Knowledge organiser

Learning intention: I can explain how root hairs and the small intestine are adapted for absorption and select a transport process from evidence.

Success looks like

  • Link a structural feature to absorption.
  • Distinguish water, mineral ions and sugars.
  • Use a supplied gradient to justify the process.

Remember

  • A root hair increases surface area in contact with soil solution.
  • Water enters root cells by osmosis when the external solution is more dilute.
  • Mineral ions can enter root hairs by active transport against their concentration gradients.
  • Villi increase intestinal surface area; thin surfaces and blood flow support absorption. Sugar can be absorbed against a concentration gradient using active transport mechanisms.

Words that help

root hair — A long projection from an epidermal root cell that increases contact area.

villus — A finger-like projection of the small-intestine lining.

absorption — Movement of substances into cells or blood.

adaptation — A feature related to a function.

Check this idea: Not every absorbed substance uses the same mechanism. Name the substance and use the concentration evidence before choosing a process.

Knowledge organiser · Root and gut: two absorption stories

Learning intention: I can explain how root hairs and the small intestine are adapted for absorption and select a transport process from evidence.

Success looks like

  • Link a structural feature to absorption.
  • Distinguish water, mineral ions and sugars.
  • Use a supplied gradient to justify the process.

Remember

  • A root hair increases surface area in contact with soil solution.
  • Water enters root cells by osmosis when the external solution is more dilute.
  • Mineral ions can enter root hairs by active transport against their concentration gradients.
  • Villi increase intestinal surface area; thin surfaces and blood flow support absorption. Sugar can be absorbed against a concentration gradient using active transport mechanisms.

Words that help

root hair — A long projection from an epidermal root cell that increases contact area.

villus — A finger-like projection of the small-intestine lining.

absorption — Movement of substances into cells or blood.

adaptation — A feature related to a function.

Check this idea: Not every absorbed substance uses the same mechanism. Name the substance and use the concentration evidence before choosing a process.

Staff answers · You do · Stretch

1. Explain why the same root hair can use both.

Different substances move via different mechanisms: water by osmosis and ions against gradients using respiration energy.

2. Why is “it reaches for food” misleading?

Roots take up water and mineral ions; plants make sugars by photosynthesis, rather than absorbing their whole food from soil.

3. Why should “all gut absorption is active” be rejected?

Different substances and gradients can involve different mechanisms; the claim overgeneralises the supplied case.

4. Compare one shared feature and one difference between a root hair and a villus.

Both provide increased area. A root hair is a cell projection contacting soil; a villus is a tissue projection containing blood vessels.
