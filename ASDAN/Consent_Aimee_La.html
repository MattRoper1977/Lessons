Before we start

How we'll talk about this today

⚠️ A heads-up

Today's lesson talks about an unwanted sexual experience that happens to a character on a bus. It is handled sensitively, but it is a real and serious topic.

Our ground rules

  • We talk about Aimee, the character — not about ourselves.
  • You never have to share anything personal.
  • You can step out for a moment — just go, no questions asked.
  • We listen, we don't judge, and what's said here stays kind.

If you want to talk to someone

  • Any trusted adult in school
  • Our safeguarding lead (your teacher will say who)
  • Childline — 0800 1111 (free, any time)
  • childline.org.uk for 1-2-1 chat

There's no wrong reason to ask for support.

I Do + We Do · consent

Why it's a violation of consent — and what that teaches us

Aimee did not agree to any sexual interaction — in any way.

Consent must be freely given, informed and enthusiastic. None of that was true here.

Consent matters even when no words are exchanged. No words does not mean "yes".

So the behaviour was harmful, disrespectful and against the law. Everyone has the right to their own body — that's bodily autonomy.

Bridge · how people respond

Feelings can come later

Minimising — joking or "I'm fine" can be a way of coping or feeling in control.

Freezing — the body's response to shock isn't always "fight". It's common and not the person's fault.

Turn & talk: Why might someone say "I'm fine" when they really aren't? Reacting differently never means it wasn't serious.

Key messages

  • Consent must be freely given, informed and enthusiastic.
  • If there's no clear "yes", there's no consent — words or no words.
