Key vocabulary

WordWhat it means
ConsentFreely agreeing to something, and meaning it.
Freely givenA real choice — no pressure, fear or tricks.
InformedYou know what you're actually agreeing to.
EnthusiasticA clear, willing yes — not just going along with it.
SpecificAgreeing to one thing isn't agreeing to everything.
ReversibleYou can change your mind at any time.
Bodily autonomyYour right to decide what happens to your own body.
MinimisingPlaying something down — e.g. joking it 'wasn't a big deal'.
Trauma responseHow the body/mind reacts to shock — e.g. freezing, or feelings coming later.
BystanderSomeone who is present and sees what happens.

The scene in four steps

  1. On the bus: a man behaves in a sexual way towards Aimee without her permission. She is shocked and freezes.
  2. Straight after: she acts as if nothing happened and makes a joke of it.
  3. Telling Maeve: Maeve takes it seriously and encourages her to report it.
  4. Later: the impact shows — Aimee is frightened and anxious about getting on buses.

Revision prose

Key distinction: a clear, willing "yes" is consent. Silence, freezing or going along out of fear is NOT consent. No words spoken does not mean agreement.

Memory hook: "No clear yes = no consent."

Common mark-losers: writing only "she didn't say yes" without explaining why that matters; assuming a calm or joking reaction means no harm was done.

Self-quiz: cover this and say aloud — (1) three key words for consent, (2) one reason Aimee minimised it, (3) what "bodily autonomy" means.

Key messages & support

Remember

  • Consent must be freely given, informed and enthusiastic.
  • No clear "yes" means no consent — words or no words.
  • People react in different ways, and that's never their fault.
