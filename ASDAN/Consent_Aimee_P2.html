I Do · the bystander effect

Why did no one step in?

On the bus, other people were nearby — but no one stepped in or checked on Aimee.

This is the bystander effect: the more people who are present, the less likely any one person acts — everyone assumes someone else will.

People also freeze: unsure what they saw, scared, or worried about getting it wrong.

Doing nothing isn't really neutral — it can leave a person feeling alone. But you never have to confront anyone: the 5 D's are safe ways to help.

🧠 Key word: bystander effect — diffusion of responsibility across a crowd.

I Do + We Do · writer's purpose

Why did the writer tell this story?

To show that this can happen anywhere — even somewhere ordinary, like a daytime bus.

To show there's no single "right" way to react — minimising, freezing and fear-later are all real.

To challenge assumptions about who this happens to and what it "looks like".

To encourage people to take it seriously, report it, and support each other — the way Maeve and the girls support Aimee.

Independent · ~10 min

Pull it all together: write the summary 🦉

Choose your level. Use the structure to help you.

Foundation

Finish each sentence:

"On the bus, a man behaved towards Aimee without her ____."
"Afterwards she ____ it, but later she felt ____."
"The other people did ____."
"The writer wanted us to ____."
Middle

Write a paragraph covering all four:

  • what happens & why it breaks consent
  • how Aimee responds (then & later)
  • what the bystanders did
  • why the writer told the story
Start: "In this storyline, a man behaves towards Aimee without her consent on the bus…"
Higher

Write a structured summary in three short paragraphs: (1) the event & consent, (2) the responses & bystanders, (3) the writer's purpose. Use the words consent, bystander effect and bodily autonomy.

✍️ Top tip: a strong summary explains why things matter, not just what happens.
