Newly authored continuation for Aut2·W3, current calendar absolute W11. No dedicated HTML lesson for this cell was found in the source tree; originalPath identifies a related donor, not an existing exact-week replacement.

Prior: Retrieve a previous trial of a mark, edge or tone. New pupils can compare the supplied studies without relying on an earlier finished piece.

Next: Add the selected panel to an agreed class display, with a label in the pupil’s words. Discuss real cultural references only when a specific checked source is present.

Preparation

  • Provide paper, pencils and optional scrap-paper collage shapes. Print the same-shape studies and three drawing frames.
  • The brief is an invented class light festival display. There is no required claim about a specific faith festival and no fixed event date.

Care and safety

  • Use usual supervised scissor and glue routines. Paper-only option; no candles, flashing lights or electrical build.

Teacher response guidance

  • A/B: same pale circle and placement; dark B has stronger light/dark contrast around its boundary.
  • C/D change both size and position, so do not claim to isolate which one caused a response.
  • Blue makes everyone calm is an unsupported universal claim.
  • Independent: varied valid responses; expect three light forms, chosen ground/spacing and a reason or directed choice.

Evidence of learning

Check whether a pupil tries and chooses light/colour/composition effects and makes an artwork. A response can be spoken or directed; do not infer independent tool use when an adult handled tools.

Access and independence

Large bold forms, a limited material choice and movable shapes preserve design decisions. Support motor demands without deciding the placement for the pupil.

Lundy Loop

Offer a private or spoken response. Receive the pupil’s exact evidence and name it back. Let that evidence guide one real next teaching step. No forced personal disclosure.

Arrival · Look back, then look closely

All needed tones are on this slide. Accept a pointed comparison before technical vocabulary.

Starter · Same shape, two grounds

Both are original constructed studies. The art aim is visibility, not a universal judgement that dark backgrounds are always better.

I do · Make a light shape

Keep the SoW festival theme through an explicitly invented class arts event. This is not teaching that traditions share one meaning. Use pencils or collage; real lights are not needed.

We do · Try two versions

The tool provides specific design feedback, not an art score. The same colours can prompt different feelings. Ask about seen relationships and intended effect.

I do · Test before you fix it

Teacher models one physical move in addition to the screen version. No claim that irreversible media always destroy a piece; this is a useful trial strategy.

We do · Say what you can see

No colour/emotion rule. Let peers describe their own response without diagnosing another person. A practical suggestion can be rejected with a reason.

Independent · Make your own panel

Fresh task changes a single focal circle to a three-shape path. Blank frames and shape tiles are provided. The standard route requires making art, not only changing screen controls.

Exit · Give your picture a voice

Accept speech, pointing or annotated image. Retain authorship and honest support notes. Do not convert completed art into automatic award attainment.

Misconceptions

A dark ground is always the best artwork. It makes this pale edge clearer, but another ground may fit a different aim.

One colour has the same feeling for everyone. Keep personal responses as personal; ask about intended effect and visible evidence.

Any light design is an authentic festival object. These are original class studies. Specific cultural claims need specific sources and context.
