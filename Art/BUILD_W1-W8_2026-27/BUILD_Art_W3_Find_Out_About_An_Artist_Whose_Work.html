One Artist, Three Facts, One Picture I Can Point At

BUILD · Art · Week 3. Trinity Arts Award Discover. One Artist, Three Facts, One Picture I Can Point At.

Objective: choose one artist from the approved card pack, find three facts about their work, and point at the part of one picture that shows each fact.

Success evidence: three facts written on the artist sheet, each with a part of the picture ringed to show it.

Every artist comes from the approved pack. You look at the work, not at the artist's private life.

40 minutes. Offline.

Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

A fact about the work, not about the person

A fact about the work is something you can see in the picture.

Uses thick black lines is a fact. Was born in Leeds is not, because you cannot see it.

Both might be true. Only one of them helps you make something.

Today every fact you write has to be findable in the picture in front of you.

That is the rule for the whole lesson, and it is the only rule.

Do not let a pupil research the artist's personal life. The approved card carries what is needed and nothing else.

You need one artist card, one picture card, a pencil and the artist sheet.

Your artist card stays on your desk all lesson. It is not a prize to swap.

Everything you write comes from the picture card next to it, and nowhere else.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Two things people say about a picture

One person says: I like it.

The other says: the lines are thick and black, and they go all the way round every shape.

Both are honest. Only the second one tells you how to make something like it.

I like it is a feeling. Thick black lines is an instruction.

Today you write the second kind, three times.

You are allowed to like it as well. Just not instead.

  • 1 · look at one part.
  • 2 · say what you can see there.
  • 3 · ring that part on the sheet.

Look at your own picture now, for ten seconds, without writing.

Then look at one small part of it. Small parts are where facts live.

I Do

I Do · model

Finding three facts in one picture

My artist card says Alma Thomas. My picture card is one of her paintings.

First I look at the colour. Fact one: the colours are bright and there are lots of them.

I ring the top corner where three bright colours sit next to each other.

Then I look at the shapes. Fact two: the paint is in short blocks, not long lines.

I ring one block near the middle.

Then I look at the gaps. Fact three: white gaps show between the blocks.

Three facts, three rings. Anyone can check my work by following a ring.

I did not say whether I liked it once.

Ring before you write, every time. A written fact with no ring is the thing this lesson is trying to stop.

Watch where I look. I do not look at the whole picture and think.

I put my finger on one part and stay there until I can say something about it.

We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort them: can you see it?

Six sentence cards are on your table. Sort them into two groups.

One group is things you can see in a picture. The other group is things you cannot.

Uses red a lot. Was famous. Paints in circles. Liked cats. Uses thin lines. Sold a painting.

Then match each card in the first group to a part of the picture on your table.

When your table has sorted all six, hold up the one you found hardest.

Hold one up even if your table did not agree.

One of the six cards is nearly a fact. Say which one, and what word is missing from it.

I Do

I Do 2 · connect

Three facts is better than ten

You could write ten facts about a picture. Most people would write ten weak ones.

Three facts you can ring is stronger than ten you cannot.

Take one from colour, one from shape and one from the gaps between.

That way your three facts do not all say the same thing in different words.

So check your three before you finish. Are they about three different things?

If two of them are about colour, change one and look at the gaps instead.

If you cannot find a gap fact, look at the edges of the picture instead.

Edges count as a part. So do corners, and so does the biggest empty area.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Follow the ring

Work in twos. Swap sheets, but keep your own picture card in front of you.

Read your partner's first fact, then find the ring it goes with.

Say whether you can see the fact there. Just yes or not yet.

If it is not yet, point at where you think the ring should be.

Then swap back and fix that one ring only.

Say yes or not yet. Do not say wrong; it stops people looking again.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Three facts, three rings

Work on your own. Use your own artist card and picture card.

Ring three different parts of the picture, one at a time.

Write one fact next to each ring, saying what you can see there.

Take one fact from colour, one from shape and one from the gaps.

If you finish early, add one sentence saying which fact you would use in your own work.

  • Supported · two rings and two facts, said aloud for an adult to write.
  • Standard · three rings and three facts, from three different parts.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say which fact you would use in your own work, and how.

Number your rings one, two and three so the facts and the rings stay paired.

A fact with no number next to it cannot be checked, however true it is.

Exit ticket

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE: the learner may pause or choose a private response.

VOICE: the evidence statement remains in the learner’s chosen communication mode.

AUDIENCE: a familiar adult receives and names back exactly what was heard or seen.

INFLUENCE: one real next action is recorded: retain, add method detail, or leave open.

Read one fact to an adult

Choose your strongest fact and read it to the adult at your table.

Point at the ring while you read it.

They read the fact back exactly and look where you pointed.

If they cannot see it, say the fact again with one more word in it.

Then choose what happens next: keep this artist, swap the picture, or add a fourth fact.

Write your choice at the bottom of the sheet before you leave.

The sheet and both cards go in the arts log folder together.
