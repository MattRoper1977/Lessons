Start Making And Keep The Process

Explore · Part C · Create. Start making and keep the process.

Today you will turn your loose idea into the start of your artwork. Learn how to place and join dry paper, then keep a clear record of what you made.

We are making a new print and collage. A print is a mark pressed from one surface to another. A collage joins pieces to make one image.

Bring forward the tests and idea from C1. Keep them beside today’s work so a reader can follow your steps. The final artwork will join this record when it is finished.

You can point, speak, type or write to show your choices. You may make a pattern or a made-up place. Your art does not have to tell a story about your life.

This deck supports Explore Part C, Create. Part C requires an artwork, a record of the PROCESS, and the final work. The new print-and-collage activity is distinct from the drawing and paper sculpture in Part A and the live or active exploration in Part B. Do not relabel a finished A or B work as C. 4 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Look at two loose shapes on the board. One sits beside the other. Now one moves partly over the other. Which shape seems to be in front? Point to the edge that helps you tell.

Where we place a piece changes the image. A big gap may split the page. An overlap may link two parts. We can move loose pieces to try these effects before we glue.

What could go wrong if we glue at once? We may hide a mark we meant to show. We may fill a space we meant to leave clear.

Today we will test the layout, save that state, then join the pieces. Glue is a making step, not a race to cover the card.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

Watch me use dry prints to start a fresh collage. I tear around one printed shape. I turn the paper as I tear so my hands can follow the edge. A rough edge can be part of my choice.

I put the large piece down first. Then I place a small piece partly across it. I leave the print I like in view. I move the small piece back and compare the gap.

Before I fix anything, I save a view of this loose layout. I make a small sketch as another way to keep it. The sketch shows where the two pieces meet.

I lift one piece and turn it over on a scrap mat. I spread a thin coat of glue across the back, close to its edges. I place it back, hold the card still, and press from the middle out.

I lift a corner gently to check the join. If it rises, I add a little glue there and press again. I keep glue away from the print surface.

Model explanations

  1. Press the piece down, then check the dry edge. — Fourth. Press gently. Once the join is ready, check for a loose corner.
  2. Try the dry pieces and choose their overlap. — First. A loose trial lets you move shapes before they are fixed.
  3. Put a thin coat of glue on the chosen piece. — Third. Add glue after you have kept a record of the chosen loose layout.
  4. Keep a sketch or image of the loose layout. — Second. This saves a real making stage that the join will change.

This model supports discussion. A well-explained different connection can be useful too.

We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Use two dry scraps to practise a join before working on your main piece. Place them with an overlap. Turn one over, add a thin glue coat and press it into place.

Check the edge with an adult or partner when it is ready. Does it lie flat? What would help a loose corner? Add glue only where it is needed.

Keep this small test or a clear image of it. Say what the test taught you about the join. Now look at where that skill could help on your own card.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Set out the first part of your own collage. Move the loose pieces until the shapes and gaps suit your idea. Save this layout before you glue it.

Join a section using the steps you have practised. Keep the edge of a print you want people to see. Stop to look at how the new join affects the image.

  • Core: use large dry pieces, an easy-grip glue stick and a helper to steady the card. Choose the layout yourself. Keep a process sample and your own words; retain the final artwork when ready.
  • Extend: place and join your chosen pieces, keeping a record before and after the join. Add your own choice note. Keep these process records and the final artwork.
  • Challenge: compare two overlaps before fixing one. Say how your choice changes the image. Keep both layout trials, your process record and the final artwork.

Record a real step today. A picture of the finished adult model is not a record of your making. Your work can be at an early stage and still show a clear choice.
