My finished arts review

Tuesday 9 February 2027 P1 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Add a review to my arts log.

I can add my own checked review to my arts log.

Today I can

I can say what I tried and learned.

I can check my review against real work.

I can keep my review and one next step.

In your book or arts log. You may point, speak, sign, draw or direct an adult.

Words to use

review: Look back and say what you think.

choice: Something you chose to do.

change: Something you did in a new way.

log: A record of your arts work.

Arrival

Arrival and your arts log

0 to 3 minutes

Find your review and real examples.

Read or hear your own words again.

Choose one part to check today.

What to say

Recover Monday’s review, the actual pieces or witness notes and the six-outcome grid. If absent last time, use an honest review of the available work today. Keep missing earlier evidence marked as a gap. Do not let the pupil’s review become a test of memory.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies a real review starting point and a manageable task.

Materials-free fallback

Read actual prior notes or use today’s labelled recollection. Give time to confirm or correct the account.

Check and response

A review written today must use today’s date. Earlier work keeps its original date where known.

Starter

Starter and your arts log

3 to 6 minutes

Picture prompts help you tell the story of your work.

What did you try? What changed?

What will you try next?

Use the stars for helpful features and the tool for a change or next step.

What to say

Revisit the eye or ear, stars and tool. Ask the pupil to choose the most useful prompt for their review. A next step is a small action, such as trying a different gap or practising a pause. It is not a promise to be perfect.

What good looks like

The pupil chooses a prompt and identifies a possible small next action.

Materials-free fallback

Read the prompt labels aloud and accept a spoken or gestured choice.

Check and response

A feasible next step names something to do. Try a softer start is clearer than be the best.

I do

I do and your arts log

6 to 12 minutes

See how the adult checks a review.

Does the work show the change?

The maker can correct the scribed words.

Step by step model
  1. Find Choose actual work or a real witness note.
  2. Notice Point to a helpful feature.
  3. Explain Say one choice or change and its reason.
  4. Check Read the review back and match it to evidence.

Adult example: I learned that a longer pause changed my ending. My next step is to test a shorter pause too. The real versions support my review.

What to say

Use yesterday’s adult model or make and date a fresh one. Read: I made the pause longer. Compare the actual versions. Then deliberately read a mistaken scribe sentence: I made every sound louder. Ask the maker to correct it. Replace it with the confirmed wording. Model a final next step and show how one grid row links to the real dated example, while an unseen row stays not yet seen.

What good looks like

The adult demonstrates checking, correction and honest evidence selection without completing pupil rows for them.

Materials-free fallback

Use a real short silent demonstration and oral comparison. Give an agreed signal to stop the read-back at an inaccurate word.

Check and response

Check: Who owns the review, the adult who writes or the pupil whose ideas it records? The pupil. Ask them to correct the example.

We do

We do and your arts log

12 to 18 minutes

Help match a comment to the real example.

Choose shown, more to do or not yet seen.

A tick needs a real comment or record.

Check together

We cannot find a record for one week. What should we do?

What to say

Use the adult example and a practice grid row outside the pupil grid. Hinge: We cannot find any earlier record. Should we A tick shown anyway, or B mark not yet seen and say what is missing? Choose B. Distinguish a gap from a failure. A pupil may have taken part even when a record is unavailable. Do not assume either completion or non-participation.

What good looks like

The pupil recognises that honest assessment uses actual evidence and can leave an outcome unconfirmed.

Materials-free fallback

Give the two choices orally. Use a spoken status and scribed comment if ticks are inaccessible.

Check and response

If A is chosen, ask a parallel check: Can a blank folder show the change we want to review? No. We can make a truthful note and choose a next step.

What to say

Use about seven minutes to complete and check the pupil’s review. It may be short, scribed, signed or drawn with an explanation. Use the remaining seven minutes to revisit rows 1 to 3 and review rows 4 to 6. For each outcome, choose one status and add a specific evidence location, date and short comment. Row 6 refers to this actual review. Retain support and pupil words. Keep one next step achievable. Do not manufacture missing earlier work or assess every practical skill again in fourteen minutes.

What good looks like

A dated pupil-owned review is added to the log. All six verbatim outcomes have an honest status and comment, including gaps. No adult model substitutes for pupil evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Conduct the review orally with real records and the pupil’s chosen words. An adult transcribes and reads back at the next opportunity; until then mark the written log as pending.

Check and response

A valid entry can be: I used colour for a calm idea. I changed a gap after my review. I learned to test a choice. Next I will try a bigger shape. The pupil must supply their own true details.

Review

Review and your arts log

32 to 37 minutes

Hear or read your final review.

Check one strength and your next step.

Keep the review and evidence together.

What to say

Invite a private share with the supporting adult. Check that the words belong to the pupil and that the evidence matches. A public display is not needed. Record actual support and any unfinished item. Use the final two minutes to tidy and store the log.

What good looks like

The pupil confirms the review and can identify its evidence and next step using their preferred response mode.

Materials-free fallback

Use a private oral or signed review, with adult read-back of any record.

Check and response

The assessment is of what the evidence shows and what the pupil can explain. Handwriting, speech volume and public confidence are not arts success criteria.

What to say

Record the real location of the review and the pupil’s next step. Confirm that the six-week grid has comments rather than automatic ticks. Spring 1 is complete as a teaching sequence; this does not certify an award or fill an evidence gap.

What good looks like

The pupil and adult can find the final review and understand one next step.

Materials-free fallback

Record the location and confirmed next step in a precise witness note, marking any transcription still pending.

Check and response

Completed teaching, completed evidence and an award decision are different. Keep each statement true.

Teacher guidance

Prepare

Actual arts log and own work from Weeks 1 to 5, including the Week 3 plan where relevant.

Six-outcome evidence sheet; keep actual dates, locations, pupil words and support.

Book and ordinary writing tools if available; oral or signed responses and adult scribing are valid.

Picture prompt strip from the accepted pack, read with its labels. No new art materials are needed.

Catch up

Use the actual available evidence. Date any new review or research today. Mark unavailable earlier work as a gap and never reconstruct it as old evidence.

Access

Show one prompt at a time. Model a short example, then fade to a sentence start. Revisit the same words and use the actual work beside the prompt. Chunk the six-week grid into two groups of three. Allow thinking time.

Read all prompts aloud. Accept pointing, signing, speaking, drawing or pupil-directed adult recording. Offer a choice before an open question. Read scribed words back and let the pupil correct them.

Keep review private and about choices in the work. Use invented or abstract ideas and avoid questions about personal adversity. No grades, rankings or public performance. Offer a calm pause and an agreed return cue.

Offer a seated, silent route and a large print view. Use a description or precise witnessed sequence where a visual piece is inaccessible. The pupil can direct adult handling. Keep actual support visible in the record.

Plan in any language. Read the source note or prompt slowly and check meaning. Accept oral ideas before writing. Translate with support where available while retaining the pupil’s intended meaning.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose the agreed quiet space or return cue with an adult. Start again with one short prompt.
