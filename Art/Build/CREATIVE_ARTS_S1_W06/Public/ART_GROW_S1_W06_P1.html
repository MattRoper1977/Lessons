An artist I want to learn about

Monday 8 February 2027 P6 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Research an artist or performer who inspires me (Bronze).

I can research one artist and record checked facts in my own words.

Today I can

I can choose an artist and a question.

I can record facts about their career and work.

I can name my source and explain my interest.

In your book or arts log. You may point, speak, sign, draw or direct an adult.

The scheme names Bronze. This research goes in your Explore log now and can support later progression.

Words to use

research: Find out about a question using a source.

source: Where a fact or account comes from.

career: The work a person does over time.

inspire: Give someone an idea or reason to try.

fact: Information that we can check.

view: What someone thinks or feels about the work.

Research is this week’s task for GROW. Only extra depth is optional. The current award level stays Explore.

Starter

Starter and your arts log

3 to 6 minutes

Read or hear notes M1 and T1.

Choose Henri Matisse or Alma Thomas.

What do you want to find out?

Source M1 Henri Matisse

Henri Matisse made paintings and drawings. Later, he made art with cut paper.

His helpers painted sheets of paper. He cut shapes from the sheets. He chose where the shapes went.

Pins held the shapes in place. He could try a new layout before fixing them. For large works, he asked helpers to move the shapes.

How did Matisse test and change a layout?

Printed research note M1 in this pack. Based on a museum studio-method account titled Henri Matisse The Cut Outs. Checked 30 October 2026.

Source T1 Alma Thomas

Alma Thomas was an art teacher. She made art too. She retired from teaching in 1960. She went on to make more paintings.

Her later art has small marks of bright colour. The marks often form a pattern. Her abstract art uses marks and shapes.

Plants and gardens gave her ideas. Space travel gave her ideas too.

What changed in her career, and what features appear in her later paintings?

Printed research note T1 in this pack. Based on a museum collection biography titled Alma Thomas. Checked 30 October 2026.

What to say

Read the short original fact notes. Both artists have long-established public careers. Invite a genuine choice based on a method, career change or idea that interests the pupil. They choose one artist for the record. Another long-established public artist or performer is valid only with an adult-checked factual source and the same record fields. If neither supplied artist inspires them yet, let them research the more interesting method and record an honest response rather than inventing admiration.

What good looks like

The pupil chooses one subject and a question, such as how the artist changed a method or found ideas. A reason can be tentative and truthful.

Materials-free fallback

Offer the two names with a brief spoken description and let the pupil point, sign or say a choice.

Check and response

Both choices are valid. The pupil’s reason must be their own. Do not score one artist as better than another.

I do

I do and your arts log

6 to 12 minutes

Watch how the adult reads one fact.

Hide the note. Say its meaning in new words.

Check the fact and name the source.

Step by step model
  1. Choose Choose one artist and a question.
  2. Find Read or hear the source note. Pick a career fact and a work fact.
  3. Explain Cover the note. Explain the meaning in your own words.
  4. Check Check accuracy, name the source and add your own response.

Adult example only: Matisse could test where to place cut shapes. That method interests me because I can try a layout before fixing it. Source M1 supports the fact; the reason is my own.

What to say

Model with M1: read the account of cut shapes and movable arrangements. Cover it and say, in fresh wording, that Matisse could try new positions for pieces before fixing them. Reopen the note and check the meaning. Name M1 and its printed title. Then say a personal view separately: I like the chance to test a layout. Show that swapping just one word is too close to copying. Write no quotation and reproduce no artwork. Model one career fact and one method fact as different kinds of finding.

What good looks like

The adult demonstrates read, cover, explain, check and source. A fact and a personal response remain distinct.

Materials-free fallback

Read a single fact aloud, pause, ask the pupil to explain it, then read the source again to check meaning. A scribe records the pupil’s wording.

Check and response

Check: Matisse cut painted paper is a source-supported fact. I find movable shapes useful is a personal view. Either needs accurate labelling.

We do

We do and your arts log

12 to 18 minutes

Sort a fact from a personal view.

Help make a short own-word note.

Keep the source label with the fact.

Check together

Which is a checkable fact from T1?

What to say

Use T1. Compare A Alma Thomas continued developing her painting after retiring from teaching and B Her repeated marks give me an idea for my piece. Ask which is a checkable fact. Choose A; B is a personal response. Build a new sentence together and check it against the note. Hinge: is changing one word in a copied sentence enough to make the research your own? No. Explain the meaning without looking, then check accuracy. Adult model sentences remain examples, not pupil evidence.

What good looks like

The pupil separates fact from response and understands a repeatable own-word research process.

Materials-free fallback

Use spoken A and B choices and accept a signal. Scribe the pupil’s explanation and read it back.

Check and response

If B is chosen as fact, ask: Can the source prove what gives you an idea? No. Parallel check: retired in 1960 is a fact; I like this method is a view.

What to say

Use about ten minutes for the research record. Read or hear the chosen note, cover it, express the meaning, check it and name the source. Keep one checked career fact and at least one fact about the work or method. Add the pupil’s own response to a specific feature and one remaining question. Pupils may scribe or dictate. Use the last four minutes to locate evidence for grid rows 1 to 3 and mark honest gaps. The supplied note is an access source, not the pupil’s completed research. Do not require copying its sentences.

What good looks like

A dated record identifies one artist, a question, at least two accurate own-word findings, a source and a personal reason. Earlier grid rows have evidence comments or gaps.

Materials-free fallback

Read the note aloud in small parts. The pupil gives an oral or signed response. Scribe exact confirmed wording when possible and mark any later transcription as pending.

Check and response

Accept, for example, a correctly rephrased career change and one technique detail. If the pupil repeats the note exactly, cover it and ask them to explain the idea to an adult.

Review

Review and your arts log

32 to 37 minutes

Check facts against your chosen note.

Which words are your own view?

Keep one question for Wednesday.

What to say

Ask the pupil to point to the source for each fact and to identify their personal response. Correct an error together without replacing the pupil’s voice. Note one question or feature to consider next time. Use the final two minutes to store the source prompt, research record and actual log evidence.

What good looks like

Facts are checked and separate from interpretation. A genuine question supports the second lesson.

Materials-free fallback

Read the fact and source aloud and ask for confirmation or correction through the pupil’s chosen response mode.

Check and response

We can check a method from the source. We cannot infer an artist’s private feelings from the method or impose a universal meaning on a colour.

Teacher guidance

Prepare

Actual arts log and own work from Weeks 1 to 5, including the Week 3 plan where relevant.

Six-outcome evidence sheet; keep actual dates, locations, pupil words and support.

Book and ordinary writing tools if available; oral or signed responses and adult scribing are valid.

Research notes M1 and T1, read aloud if needed. No pupil internet access or login is needed.

Catch up

Use the actual available evidence. Date any new review or research today. Mark unavailable earlier work as a gap and never reconstruct it as old evidence.

Access

Show one prompt at a time. Model a short example, then fade to a sentence start. Revisit the same words and use the actual work beside the prompt. Chunk the six-week grid into two groups of three. Allow thinking time.

Read all prompts aloud. Accept pointing, signing, speaking, drawing or pupil-directed adult recording. Offer a choice before an open question. Read scribed words back and let the pupil correct them.

Keep review private and about choices in the work. Use invented or abstract ideas and avoid questions about personal adversity. No grades, rankings or public performance. Offer a calm pause and an agreed return cue.

Offer a seated, silent route and a large print view. Use a description or precise witnessed sequence where a visual piece is inaccessible. The pupil can direct adult handling. Keep actual support visible in the record.

Plan in any language. Read the source note or prompt slowly and check meaning. Accept oral ideas before writing. Translate with support where available while retaining the pupil’s intended meaning.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose the agreed quiet space or return cue with an adult. Start again with one short prompt.
