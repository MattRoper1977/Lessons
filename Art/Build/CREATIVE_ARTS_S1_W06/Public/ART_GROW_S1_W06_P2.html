My research and arts learning

Wednesday 10 February 2027 P5 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Research an artist or performer who inspires me (Bronze).

I can explain how an artist inspires me and keep an honest record of my learning.

Today I can

I can explain checked facts in my own words.

I can give a specific reason and an original next idea.

I can share privately and review all six outcomes.

In your book or arts log. You may point, speak, sign, draw or direct an adult.

The scheme names Bronze. This research goes in your Explore log now and can support later progression.

Words to use

research: Find out about a question using a source.

source: Where a fact or account comes from.

career: The work a person does over time.

inspire: Give someone an idea or reason to try.

fact: Information that we can check.

view: What someone thinks or feels about the work.

Arrival

Arrival and your arts log

0 to 3 minutes

Find your research note and the source.

Keep the same chosen artist if it still fits.

Read your question and facts again.

What to say

Recover Monday’s record and the chosen source note. If missing, create a short honest research record now using the supplied source, dated today. Do not invent a previous visit, interview or study session. Reconfirm that the current log is Explore and the exact scheme outcome names Bronze for progression.

What good looks like

The pupil has a real starting record and source, or an honest current catch-up record.

Materials-free fallback

Read the source and actual research aloud. Accept signed or oral responses with accurate adult scribing.

Check and response

A record made today is valid when dated today. It does not prove a missing Monday activity.

Starter

Starter and your arts log

3 to 6 minutes

Which fact did you check?

What feature interests you?

What is still a question?

Source M1 Henri Matisse

Henri Matisse made paintings and drawings. Later, he made art with cut paper.

His helpers painted sheets of paper. He cut shapes from the sheets. He chose where the shapes went.

Pins held the shapes in place. He could try a new layout before fixing them. For large works, he asked helpers to move the shapes.

How did Matisse test and change a layout?

Printed research note M1 in this pack. Based on a museum studio-method account titled Henri Matisse The Cut Outs. Checked 30 October 2026.

Source T1 Alma Thomas

Alma Thomas was an art teacher. She made art too. She retired from teaching in 1960. She went on to make more paintings.

Her later art has small marks of bright colour. The marks often form a pattern. Her abstract art uses marks and shapes.

Plants and gardens gave her ideas. Space travel gave her ideas too.

What changed in her career, and what features appear in her later paintings?

Printed research note T1 in this pack. Based on a museum collection biography titled Alma Thomas. Checked 30 October 2026.

What to say

Revisit the pupil’s question. Let them identify what the note answers and what remains unknown. If it does not answer a question, say so. Help narrow the research to the artist’s career and work. Do not invent an answer about private feelings, intentions or events.

What good looks like

The pupil can identify a source-supported finding and a remaining uncertainty.

Materials-free fallback

Offer one fact and one unanswered question orally. The pupil chooses the one supported by the note.

Check and response

The supplied notes explain public career or method facts. They do not answer every question about the artist.

I do

I do and your arts log

6 to 12 minutes

Hear a fact and a personal reason.

See how an artist’s method can lead to a new idea.

The research uses words, not copied art.

Step by step model
  1. Choose Choose one artist and a question.
  2. Find Read or hear the source note. Pick a career fact and a work fact.
  3. Explain Cover the note. Explain the meaning in your own words.
  4. Check Check accuracy, name the source and add your own response.

Adult example only: Thomas used repeated coloured marks. The gaps interest me. I could vary pauses in my own silent sequence. Source T1 supports the painting fact; the silent idea is mine.

What to say

Model with T1: Thomas used repeated coloured marks in later abstract painting. My response is that changing the gaps between my own marks could suggest a fresh start. That is my idea, not a reproduction or a claim about Thomas’s feelings. Show how to record source T1, the fact, the specific feature, my reason and a possible next action. Briefly tell the adult listener what I found and note that real private sharing. This is a model only.

What good looks like

The model joins accurate research to a personal reason and an original next idea, without copying a work.

Materials-free fallback

Describe an original pattern of short and long pauses as a possible personal response. The pupil need only explain an idea today; a new finished artwork is not required.

Check and response

Check: Can I be inspired by a method while making my own different piece? Yes. A copied picture or invented quotation is unnecessary.

We do

We do and your arts log

12 to 18 minutes

Check a model research record.

Does it name a source and use new wording?

Does the reason point to a feature?

Check together

Which record is useful research?

What to say

Compare A I copied the note and everyone must like this artist with B I explained two facts, named the note and gave my own reason about a feature. Choose B. Rehearse a short private explanation to an adult. The listener asks one factual question; the researcher may say I do not know yet. This supports communication without requiring a peer comment or a public show.

What good looks like

The pupil recognises own-word findings, source identification and personal reason as the useful evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Use the two options orally. The adult can be the listener and the pupil can point to or direct their recorded answer.

Check and response

If A is chosen, cover the note and help the pupil explain one meaning. Parallel check: Is saying a feature interests me the same as saying everyone must like it? No.

What to say

Use about seven minutes to complete the research record in the pupil’s own words, checking any final facts against the chosen source. Add a specific reason the artist interests or inspires the pupil, and one original next idea. If inspiration remains uncertain, record that honestly and identify what they would explore next. Use about two minutes for a private explanation to one adult or a freely chosen listener. Record the actual date, what was shared and one response, or mark sharing pending if the pupil opts out. Use about five minutes to check all six grid rows, reusing Monday’s comments and adding rows 4 to 6. Row 6 points to the actual research. Record help and honest gaps. No new art reproduction or copied text is needed.

What good looks like

The final record has one chosen artist, a question, checked career and work facts, source details, a feature-specific personal response, an original idea and actual sharing status. All six outcomes have truthful comments.

Materials-free fallback

The pupil gives an oral, signed or directed explanation. An adult records exact words and actual sharing, with read-back. If writing materials are absent, record later and keep the transcription pending meanwhile.

Check and response

Research can support later arts inspiration evidence when it shows genuine enquiry, learning and personal response. A private adult share is a valid classroom route. Any future qualification judgement checks the whole portfolio and current requirements.

Review

Review and your arts log

32 to 37 minutes

Check that the research sounds like you.

Read one fact, one reason and your next idea.

Keep real evidence for each grid row.

What to say

Use the source note to check facts and the pupil to check personal wording. Read scribed words back. Confirm one next step and any missing evidence. Make no automatic award or full-level claim. Use the final two minutes to store the log and grid together.

What good looks like

The pupil recognises their own research and can locate the supporting source and half-term evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Read short parts aloud and let the pupil confirm, correct or indicate an uncertainty.

Check and response

An uncompleted share remains pending. A source note in the folder is not by itself the pupil’s research or a completed arts award.

Teacher guidance

Prepare

Actual arts log and own work from Weeks 1 to 5, including the Week 3 plan where relevant.

Six-outcome evidence sheet; keep actual dates, locations, pupil words and support.

Book and ordinary writing tools if available; oral or signed responses and adult scribing are valid.

Research notes M1 and T1, read aloud if needed. No pupil internet access or login is needed.

Catch up

Use the actual available evidence. Date any new review or research today. Mark unavailable earlier work as a gap and never reconstruct it as old evidence.

Access

Show one prompt at a time. Model a short example, then fade to a sentence start. Revisit the same words and use the actual work beside the prompt. Chunk the six-week grid into two groups of three. Allow thinking time.

Read all prompts aloud. Accept pointing, signing, speaking, drawing or pupil-directed adult recording. Offer a choice before an open question. Read scribed words back and let the pupil correct them.

Keep review private and about choices in the work. Use invented or abstract ideas and avoid questions about personal adversity. No grades, rankings or public performance. Offer a calm pause and an agreed return cue.

Offer a seated, silent route and a large print view. Use a description or precise witnessed sequence where a visual piece is inaccessible. The pupil can direct adult handling. Keep actual support visible in the record.

Plan in any language. Read the source note or prompt slowly and check meaning. Accept oral ideas before writing. Translate with support where available while retaining the pupil’s intended meaning.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose the agreed quiet space or return cue with an adult. Start again with one short prompt.
