A feeling in colour or sound

Monday 4 January 2027 · P5 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Use colour/sound to express a feeling.

I can use a colour or sound to show a feeling.

Today I can

  • I can choose a feeling for my art.
  • I can try two marks or sounds.
  • I can show my choice in my arts log.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

feeling

How a person might feel, such as calm or glad.

mark

A dot, line or shape you make.

beat

A steady pulse you can tap or show.

choice

Something you pick for a reason.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

You can point, speak, sign or pass.

Choose a made-up feeling: calm or full of energy.

You do not need to tell a story about your life.

What to say

We can make art about a made-up feeling. Pick calm or full of energy, or use another word. You can show your choice in silence.

What good looks like

A pupil chooses or confirms a feeling. There is no demand for personal disclosure.

Materials-free fallback

Point left for calm or right for energy. Confirm the choice aloud without interpreting it.

Check and response

Both choices are valid. Accept a pupil's own feeling word and any culturally different colour association.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Watch a slow hand wave. Watch two quick hand taps.

Which might show calm?

Can someone choose the other one? Say or show why.

Ready

Watch or direct a small move, a repeat, a larger move and a pause. The adult models the movement. No sound or personal story is needed.

What to say

I move slowly, then I tap twice. I think the slow move could show calm. Your idea may differ if you can show a reason.

What good looks like

The pupil links a feature such as speed or space to a feeling. An alternative interpretation is accepted.

Materials-free fallback

Use just silent hand movements. Pupils may watch or direct the adult with one or two fingers.

Check and response

A slow wave might show calm because it moves gently. Quick taps could show calm for someone who enjoys a steady beat. Meaning depends on the maker.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch two tiny tests.

Colour: try a smooth line, then short marks.

Sound: try a slow soft beat, then a faster beat.

Pick one way to work. You do not need to do both.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Say: My made-up feeling is calm. Draw one long curve in the air.

15 to 30 seconds Show short marks. Say: I tried two ways. I choose the curve because it flows.

30 to 45 seconds Tap four soft slow beats, pause, then four quicker beats. Say: I can test sound too.

45 to 60 seconds Point to the chosen test. Say: I keep my real choice and reason in my log.

Which feature could fit your own idea?

What to say

My made-up feeling is calm. I try a smooth blue line. Next I try short blue marks. I choose the smooth line because it flows. For sound I try four slow soft taps, pause, then four quicker taps. I prefer slow taps for my calm piece. These are my choices, not rules.

What good looks like

The adult models two tests and a reason. Show that another colour, silence or different marks can express the same feeling.

Materials-free fallback

Trace a smooth line in the air and show short silent taps with the fingers. The pupil can direct the adult's hand movement.

Check and response

The key is an intentional choice and reason. Blue does not always mean calm. Quiet sound is an option, and silence is a valid artistic choice.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Choose calm or energy for a class test.

Tell the adult: slow or fast? Smooth or short?

Try two versions. Keep one.

Finish: We chose ___ because ___.

For example: We chose slow marks because they feel still. Accept a different reason if it links a visible or heard feature to the intended effect.

Other reasons can be valid. Show the feature that fits your idea.

What to say

You choose the feeling. I will change only the speed or the marks. Which version fits your idea? Point to the choice, then I will read your reason back.

What good looks like

Every pupil makes a choice. The class compares two deliberate versions and gives a feature-based reason. The adult does not supply the pupil's feeling.

Materials-free fallback

Make two silent hand paths. The pupil can point to a version or direct a partner, with adult confirmation.

Check and response

For example: We chose slow marks because they feel still. Accept a different reason if it links a visible or heard feature to the intended effect.

Create

Create

18–32 minutes

In your book or arts log, choose a feeling.

Try two tiny colour tests or two sound tests.

Keep one and make a short piece.

Say or show: I chose ___ because ___.

Keep your tests. You can ask an adult to write your words.

Supported

Pick calm or energy, or point to your own word.

Try two lines or beats with an adult.

Choose one. Direct the adult if you want.

Finish: I chose ___ because ___.

Independent

Choose a feeling and paint or sound.

Try two small versions. Keep both tests.

Make a short piece with your choice.

Add your choice and reason to your arts log.

Aspire

Use the same core task.

Change one feature, such as space, speed or a pause.

Say how the new version changes the feeling.

Keep your own reason in your arts log.

Choose one route within the same 14 minutes. Every route keeps your own choice and arts-log evidence.

What to say

Work in paint or sound. With paint, try two small marks on scrap before your piece. Keep water and paint on the mat. With sound, tap softly below talking volume, use four beats and a pause, and stop on my raised hand. Choose silence if sound is too much. Point to the version you keep and tell me why.

What good looks like

A short painting or sound pattern with two genuine trials, a pupil choice and a reason. A simple piece is complete for this lesson. An adult may handle materials under the pupil's direction.

Materials-free fallback

Use a silent four-move finger pattern or an air drawing. A pupil may direct the adult. The adult records the sequence and exact confirmed words on the supplied log. No materials or device is needed for participation.

Check and response

Example sound piece: four slow soft taps, a pause, repeated once. Example painting: spaced curved blue marks. Each is a teacher model, not a required answer or pupil evidence.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Stop and put tools down.

Show one mark, beat or pause that you chose.

Your partner says: I notice ___.

Tidy your space. You can share with just the adult.

Teacher model only: My feeling is calm. I tried short marks and a long curve. I chose the curve because it flows.

Teacher model only: I made four quick soft taps, then a pause. My piece shows energy without loud noise.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Tell the maker one thing you notice, not what they must be feeling. Ask: Is that the feeling you chose? Listen to their answer. We have two minutes to cap paint, wipe the mat and leave the piece flat to dry. Keep each pupil's tests for next lesson.

What good looks like

A respectful observation linked to a feature and a maker's confirmed intention. Tools are secure and work is stored flat. The pupil may share privately.

Materials-free fallback

The adult repeats a silent pattern and asks the pupil to confirm one feature. No peer audience is required today.

Check and response

A useful observation is: I notice big gaps between the marks. A guess about a pupil's private life is not required or recorded.

Exit

Exit

37–40 minutes

Arts log: I tried ___.

I chose ___ because ___.

Next time I will ___.

Point to, sketch or describe your real work.

My arts log

My feeling or idea is ___.

My art form is paint / sound / silent movement.

My two tests were ___.

I chose ___ because ___.

Next time I will ___.

Type instead

Your typed words stay on this page until you save or close it. Save a local copy before closing.

Keep actual work or a dated witness record with your log. Sound and movement can use a written sequence. A recording device is optional.

What to say

Show what you actually made or tried. I can write your words, read them back and let you change them. Our log keeps your idea and one real next step. You may say the piece is still a test.

What good looks like

A dated arts-log entry records the form, trial, chosen feature and reason. Staff record the support actually used. No model answer is entered as pupil evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil confirms a choice by gesture. Adult makes a short witnessed record and reads it back at the next available recording opportunity.

Check and response

Example only: I tried two beats. I chose slow taps because I wanted calm. Next time I will add a pause.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four books or arts logs and four pencils.

Painting option: five small sheets, mats, five brushes, a small shared set of washable paint and one stable water pot per pair. Prepare two adult test swatches.

Sound option: hands or fingertips, no instrument or recording device required. Use soft knee taps only if comfortable.

Six scrap pieces for tests, drying space and cloths. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

No-materials option: silent hand patterns or pupil-directed adult movement.

Teaching approach

Model a small choice, two trials and a reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

One colour always means one feeling Ask the maker what the colour means to them. Accept different personal and cultural meanings.

Loud sound is needed to show energy Use faster quiet taps, silent movement or a pause. All work stays below talking volume.

A neat picture is the only good outcome Look for an intentional choice and a reason. Marks or a brief sound pattern can express an idea.

This lesson contributes to the Explore record. A whole award decision needs the full portfolio. Any optional next level evidence is additional.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. You can watch or use silent movement when you return.

Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You do not need to explain a personal feeling to the group.
