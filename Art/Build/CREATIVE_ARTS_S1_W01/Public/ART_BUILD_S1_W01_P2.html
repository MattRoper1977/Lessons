My feelings piece and arts log

Tuesday 5 January 2027 · P1 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Use colour/sound to express a feeling.

I can make a short piece and tell you about my choice.

Today I can

  • I can find my test and chosen feeling.
  • I can use a mark or sound on purpose.
  • I can add my work and words to my arts log.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

feeling

How a person might feel, such as calm or glad.

repeat

Do the same part again.

pause

A short time with no sound or move.

Look or listen again and say what you notice.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find your test from last time.

Point to the mark or beat you chose.

You may keep the feeling or choose a new one.

What to say

Find your own tests. If you were away, we can make two tiny tests now. We keep today's real date and say what happened. A feeling can be made up.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies a real previous choice or makes a short honest catch-up trial. No earlier evidence is invented.

Materials-free fallback

Adult recalls only a choice that was actually witnessed. If none exists, offer two silent patterns to try now.

Check and response

Either continuing or making a fresh trial is valid. Log the actual work and date.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Watch four soft taps with a pause.

Watch the same taps with no pause.

What changed? Which version fits your idea?

Ready

Watch or direct a small move, a repeat, a larger move and a pause. The adult models the movement. No sound or personal story is needed.

What to say

The taps stay soft. This time I change only the pause. Show me what changed and tell me which you would choose for a made-up feeling.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies the pause and makes an intentional choice, rather than copying an expected feeling.

Materials-free fallback

Use four silent finger moves, hold still, repeat. Pupils may just watch and point.

Check and response

The pause changed. It might create calm, waiting or surprise. The maker's reason determines the intended effect.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch a tiny piece grow from a test.

Paint: repeat a chosen mark.

Sound: repeat a four-beat pattern.

I can keep a choice or change it.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Point to a real adult test. Say: I choose this curve for calm.

15 to 30 seconds Repeat three curves with spaces. Say: I keep space because it feels still.

30 to 45 seconds Show four soft taps and a pause twice. Say: I can show a sound in a beat plan.

45 to 60 seconds Say: I kept this feature because it fits my idea. I keep my real work and words in my log.

Which feature could fit your own idea?

What to say

I use yesterday's curve test to make three spaced curves. I keep the space because I want calm. If I choose sound, I repeat four soft taps and a pause twice. I check my idea, then keep a sketch or a beat plan and my own words. These are adult examples.

What good looks like

A clear link from trial to piece, one deliberate feature and a short review. The piece can stay small. Show how to log a sound using written beat words or simple dots and a gap.

Materials-free fallback

Make three air curves or a silent four-move sequence. Say what an adult could record as a witnessed performance.

Check and response

The log may contain a sketch, written pattern, work-only image or witnessed description. A recording device is not needed.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Help make a class piece for a made-up character.

Pick one mark or four-beat pattern.

Try a change. Choose keep or change.

Finish: We chose ___ because ___.

For example: We kept the growing gaps because they feel open. Other justified choices are valid. Hope does not have one fixed colour or sound.

Other reasons can be valid. Show the feature that fits your idea.

What to say

Our made-up character feels hopeful. You direct my marks or soft taps. We can change the space or add a pause. Which should we keep and why? Each pupil's own work comes next.

What good looks like

Pupils test one small change and confirm an intention. The shared class model is labelled as a model and is separate from individual evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Adult follows pupils' silent hand directions. Use a gesture for keep or change.

Check and response

For example: We kept the growing gaps because they feel open. Other justified choices are valid. Hope does not have one fixed colour or sound.

Create

18–32 minutes

Use your test to make a small painting or sound piece.

Repeat your mark or short pattern.

Check one feature. Keep it or change it.

Keep a sketch, beat plan or adult witness note.

Add: I chose ___ because ___.

Tell or show your choice for the log.

Aspire

Complete the core piece and log.

Try one part in a new way.

Explain which version fits your idea best.

Keep the test and your reason, even if you keep the first version.

Choose one route within the same 14 minutes. Every route keeps your own choice and arts-log evidence.

What to say

Choose paint, sound or the silent option. Use your own idea. Make it small enough to finish a first version today. Check one feature, then keep or change it for a reason. I can move materials under your direction. Sound stays below talking volume and stops on my raised hand.

What good looks like

A real first piece or clearly labelled unfinished work, a deliberate feature, a keep/change decision and pupil-owned log. Adult help supports access without supplying the choice.

Materials-free fallback

Create a silent hand sequence or air drawing, or direct the adult. Record the order, intended feeling and confirmed reason on the arts-log sheet when available.

Check and response

A complete lesson response may be four soft taps and a pause repeated twice, with a simple beat plan and reason. A painting can use only a few intentional marks.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Show your work to an adult or partner.

Say: I chose ___ because ___.

Listener: I notice ___.

Tidy and store your work.

Teacher model only: I made a small painting. I chose spaced curves for calm. I kept the gaps because they feel still.

Teacher model only: I made a sound piece with four taps and a pause. I changed to a longer pause because the character is waiting.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Ask the maker before you comment. Describe a mark, sound or pause, then let the maker explain. You can share privately. We use the last two minutes to wipe mats, cap paint and place work flat. Keep the maker's own review.

What good looks like

The maker explains one feature and receives a respectful observation. No compulsory performance or personal disclosure. Work and evidence remain individual.

Materials-free fallback

The adult watches a silent sequence and reads a short description back for pupil approval. A private audience is enough today.

Check and response

For example: I chose a long pause because the character is waiting. I notice the pause comes after each four taps.

Keep actual work or a dated witness record with your log. Sound and movement can use a written sequence. A recording device is optional.

What to say

Your log can be spoken, drawn or written. I will scribe your exact words and read them back if needed. Show the work or plan the words refer to. Today contributes to your Explore record. We will keep building it across the term.

What good looks like

A dated entry links actual work with the pupil's intention, decision and next step. Staff state what was witnessed and what help was used, without claiming the whole award is complete.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil confirms the sequence and reason using gestures. Adult records that response, with the actual support and date, at the next recording opportunity.

Check and response

Example only: I made a calm sound piece. I kept slow taps because they feel still. Next I would like to try two sounds.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four previous tests, arts logs and pencils. Offer a catch-up test if a pupil was absent.

Paint option: four sheets, five brushes, mats, stable water pots, a small amount of washable paint and cloths.

Sound option: fingertips or quiet hand movement, no instrument required. Provide a quiet listening space.

Adult model: one test and one tiny developed example. Drying space for paintings.

No-materials option: silent finger sequence, air drawing or pupil-directed adult movement.

Teaching approach

Model a small choice, two trials and a reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

Change means the first version failed A trial helps the maker choose. Keeping a feature for a reason also counts as a review.

Only a recording can show a sound piece Keep a simple beat plan and a dated witness description, confirmed by the pupil.

The audience decides the maker's feeling Listeners describe what they notice. The maker explains the intended feeling.

This lesson contributes to the Explore record. A whole award decision needs the full portfolio. Any optional next level evidence is additional.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. You can watch or use silent movement when you return.

Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You do not need to explain a personal feeling to the group.
