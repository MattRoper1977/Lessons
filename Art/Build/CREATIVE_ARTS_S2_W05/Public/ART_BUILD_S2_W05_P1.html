Our story is made up.

Pick act, speak, sign or direct.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Today is our first lesson of the week and our chance to share a short story. A book can tell a story; we tell ours with moves and short lines. Choose a role or a quiet directing job. Agree the stop signal and who from the class or invited staff is actually here. There are no outside guests.

What good looks like

Each pupil chooses or confirms a role and audience route. The invented story, space and stop signal are clear.

Fallback

Offer one seated hand move or let the pupil direct the adult. A private share with one class member or invited staff member is available.

Check and response

A short silent part or a directed part is valid. Record what the pupil actually chooses; do not treat a pass as a performance.

3 minutes

Find our real story plan.

Show first, next and last.

Try the start cue once.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use the genuine plan and choices made earlier. Our den story can be: first we want a den, next the space is small, last we choose a small den. If work is missing or the pupil was away, make that fresh three-part plan now and date it today. Do not claim a previous rehearsal.

What good looks like

Pupils recall a real sequence or make a fresh dated one. The start cue is practised calmly.

Fallback

Point wide, bring hands close, then make a small roof shape. The pupil may cue the adult for each part.

Check and response

First is the wish for a den; next is the small space; last is the new small-den plan. A different original three-part story is valid if the order is clear.

Share our made up story

6 minutes

Watch the two class examples.

See a clear start.

See the change and the end.

Which cue helps us?

Compare the performance examples

The new den with hand pictures

First the group point to a big space. Next they show that the space is small. Last they make a small roof with their hands. A hand cue helps them hold each picture for three counts.

What comes first? What comes last?

The new den with short lines

One voice says, We want a den. Next a voice says, The space is small. Last a voice says, We can make a small den. Each person stays in their own space.

Which line tells us the change?

Watch the adult model

Point wide. Say: We want a den. Give a calm start cue.

Bring hands close. Say: The space is small. Hold for three comfortable counts.

Make a small roof with hands. Say: We can make a small den.

Give an end cue and relax. Ask a real watcher to name one feature.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Demonstrate the two invented den examples. I use a hand cue, hold each comfortable pose for three counts and relax. I keep my own space and breathe normally. Check after the model: which part shows the space changed? Explain that the teacher model is not the pupil's evidence.

What good looks like

Adult demonstrates a complete safe tiny story and an explicit check. Pupils identify the middle small-space part.

Fallback

Use seated hand shapes. Ask the pupil to choose wide or close hands for the middle part; adult follows their direction.

Check and response

Close hands show the space is small in this model. The final roof shape shows the response. Meaning should be confirmed by the maker.

We do

Share our made up story

6 minutes

Agree first, next and last.

Give each person a real part.

Try a start and end cue.

Keep our own space.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Choose roles such as mover, voice or cue keeper. Try one calm rehearsal. Hinge: if a pupil wants a quiet share, should we force a class show or offer one class member or invited staff member? Choose the quiet share. If unclear, model the private route and ask again. Confirm the actual audience before the share.

What good looks like

Every pupil has an agreed access route and real contribution. Pupils understand that a private share within the allowed audience is valid.

Fallback

Adult performs under pupil direction. Use a small hand cue and let the pupil choose one listener from the class or invited staff.

Check and response

Offer the private share. Never force public acting or add outside guests. If the pupil passes entirely, record the real choice and a next action, not a completed performance.

Keep a note of your real part.

Supported

Tell or show your real part.

Independent

Use our real three-part story plan.

Share your part with clear start and end cues.

Ask one watcher what they noticed.

Log what you did, who watched and your choice.

Aspire

Do the same core share.

Make the change and new plan clear.

Use a calm cue to help the group.

Explain one choice and keep real feedback.

Teacher guidance

What to say

This is the performance slot. Groups share in turn to the class and any invited staff who are actually present. Each pupil may share a short section privately within that audience or direct an adult. Use the den sequence or a short original story. Before starting, confirm the cue and chosen part. A watcher names one feature they really saw or heard. Record the real date, audience, individual contribution and help; if no share occurs, mark it pending and name the next action.

What good looks like

A real performance or private directed share occurs with the allowed audience, or an honest pending record is kept. Each pupil's own part is identified separately from the group story.

Fallback

Pupil directs a seated adult to show three hand pictures to one class member or invited staff member. Record direction and the real listener; no device is required.

Check and response

Example only: I gave the start cue. One class member watched our three pictures and noticed the pause. Only record that if it happened. Do not copy this teacher model into a pupil log.

Review

Share our made up story

5 minutes

Say what you really shared.

Who watched or listened?

What did they really notice?

Store your plan and note.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Ask each maker about one real contribution and one actual watcher notice. If no feedback was given, write no feedback yet and choose a later action. Use the final two minutes to clear the space and store the plan and actual note for tomorrow's review.

What good looks like

The pupil confirms actual audience and contribution; no invented praise, feedback or attendance is added. Records are stored.

Fallback

Use a point or sign to confirm the role and listener. Adult reads a short witnessed note back and lets the pupil correct it.

Check and response

A useful observation is: I noticed the end pose. A pupil who directed the end cue is recorded as director. Missing feedback stays missing until it is received.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Keep a dated log of the real share or a pending note. I can write your exact words and read them back. Tomorrow we will review our skills and sort our real arts-log evidence. No checklist will turn a missed share into a completed one.

What good looks like

A dated entry distinguishes actual work, directed work and pending work. The pupil confirms a choice and reason.

Fallback

Pupil points to the actual role and confirms a short adult note. If the share was not completed, record one practical next action.

Check and response

Example only: I directed the small-roof pose to show the new plan. My audience was one invited staff member. Record only today's actual work.

Safety

All props and story spaces are imagined. Keep movement within each person's own clear space; no den building, objects to climb on, stage combat, lifting, touching, blindfolds or breath holding. Hold a comfortable pose for about three seconds, breathe normally, then relax. Keep exits clear. Quiet sound stays at talking level or below. Record directed work as direction; do not claim the pupil physically or vocally performed an adult's action.
