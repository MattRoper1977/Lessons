beat

Change a plan to fit a new need.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or ask an adult to write your words.

Arrival

Devise a story about a new plan

3 minutes

Choose a role: mover, voice, narrator or director.

You can work seated or use silent gestures.

Keep your own clear space.

Our story is invented. You do not need a personal story.

Teacher guidance

What to say

We will devise a short scene about a group that changes its plan. Agree a calm open-hand stop cue. Choose an acting, voice or directing role that fits you. No contact, running or loud sound is needed.

What good looks like

Each pupil confirms a role and access route. Adult checks safe space and the stop cue.

Fallback

Offer a silent director role: the pupil chooses the cue or pose and an adult performs it.

Check and response

A creative contribution can be a deliberate cue, a pose or a choice of order; it does not require public speech.

Starter

Devise a story about a new plan

3 minutes

Story seed: A group plan a show in a wide space.

The space becomes smaller.

They pause, ask for ideas and change the moves.

They finish the show in the smaller space.

What changed? What did the group do?

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

This is a made-up story called A smaller show space. Nothing really blocks our room. What is the change? Which choice helps the group respond? We will show a manageable change, not act out personal distress.

What good looks like

Pupils separate the changed space from the group's response of pausing, asking and adapting.

Fallback

Use two prompts: What got smaller? What did they change? Offer hands wide then close as a visual cue.

Check and response

The space got smaller. The group changed wide moves into small moves after asking for ideas. They did not need to force the old plan to work.

I do

Devise a story about a new plan

6 minutes

Watch two short examples.

One uses a narrator. One uses silent cues.

Find four beats: plan, change, choice, result.

Which feature makes the choice clear?

Compare the performance examples

A smaller show space

Four roles plan a show with wide arm moves. The imagined space becomes smaller. A planner asks the group for ideas. They rehearse small arm moves and finish together. A narrator names each change.

Which action lets the audience see the problem?

A change told in silence

The group begin with wide poses. A silent palm cue marks a smaller space. One performer pauses and looks at the group. Each offers a small move. They choose one and repeat it together, then hold the final pose.

How does this group show a choice without speech?

Watch the adult model

Show wide arms within your safe space. Say: Our first plan has wide moves.

Bring hands close. Say: The imagined space is smaller. Pause.

Ask: What could we try? Offer a small hand wave, then repeat it.

Hold a final small pose. Say: We changed the move. Our four beats show the new plan.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Read or demonstrate both fictional comparison cards with calm hand movements. In one example I say, What could we try? In the other, I pause, invite a small hand move and repeat it. Which feature shows the group choosing a new plan? Check: does a wide first pose belong before or after the small-space cue?

What good looks like

Pupil identifies four beats and compares narration with pause, invitation or repetition. Adult checks the story order before independent work.

Fallback

Read one card and show only two contrasting hand shapes. Ask the pupil to place the change before the new move.

Check and response

The wide first pose goes before the small-space cue. Repeating a chosen small move can make the group's decision visible without words.

We do

Devise a story about a new plan

6 minutes

Build four beats as a group.

Plan: agree the first idea.

Change: show the smaller space.

Choice: ask for help or test a small move.

Result: show the new plan working.

Choose cues and roles.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Each person offers one small idea or chooses one offered by a peer. With up to five pupils, use mover, narrator, director or cue roles; roles may be shared. Test a short four-beat version. Does the audience see what changed and what the group chose to do?

What good looks like

Group creates a workable four-beat outline with agreed accessible roles and a clear response to change.

Fallback

Keep the supplied story and offer two choices at the choice beat: ask a helper or test a small move. Pupil selects and directs it.

Check and response

A clear choice could be a narrator question followed by small moves, or a silent pause followed by offered hand moves and an agreed repeated one.

Create

Devise a story about a new plan

14 minutes

Devise a scene of about 30 to 60 seconds.

Keep the four beats in order.

Each person contributes a choice.

Try it twice. Test one cue, pause or move.

In your book or arts log, keep the outline and your part.

Supported

Use the supplied four-beat story.

Choose an accessible role.

Make one cue, pose or voice choice with help.

Rehearse the four beats.

Tell or show your choice for the arts log.

Independent

Devise four clear beats as a group.

Agree roles and safe cues.

Try a scene of 30 to 60 seconds twice.

Keep one tested feature for a reason.

Record your own part and a next step.

Aspire

Complete the same scene and log.

Test two ways to show the choice beat.

Ask which helps the audience see the response.

Use the feedback to explain your choice.

Optional next level stretch

Compare the two fictional examples. Choose a skill for your own role, such as cue timing or clear narration. Describe a starting point, practise once and note what changes.

Keep the comparison, personal skill goal, first trial and a dated reflection in your arts log. Use your own words or confirmed dictated words.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use three minutes for roles and the outline, five for two calm trials, two to decide what to keep, and four to log each person's contribution. Use the supplied story or a similar invented low-stakes change. If sound is chosen, use quiet original words or hand taps below speaking volume. Stop on the agreed cue.

What good looks like

Real scene or directed scene has a clear change, response and result. Each pupil identifies an actual creative contribution and a trial.

Fallback

Use seated hand gestures and adult narration for the four beats. Pupil directs one beat and confirms the outline and reason.

Check and response

Example only: I chose a pause before the small move so the audience could see the character thinking. Our group tested narration and silence, then kept the narrator.

Review

Devise a story about a new plan

5 minutes

Show a trial to a peer or the adult.

Watcher: What changed? What helped?

Maker: Confirm or correct that reading.

Choose one feature to improve next lesson.

Store the outline and settle.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Give descriptive feedback using I noticed ... . The maker can correct the audience's reading. Agree one next step linked to clarity rather than confidence. Use the last two minutes to store outlines, clear the space and settle.

What good looks like

Audience reads the change and response. Maker records a specific next step while retaining the first attempt.

Fallback

Adult watches privately and reads back a short exact observation for pupil confirmation.

Check and response

Useful feedback: I saw the space shrink, but the new plan was rushed. Next step: hold the chosen small move before the final pose.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Keep your own words and the support used. An adult may write and read them back. Mark a trial as a trial. Do not copy a model reflection or claim another pupil's decision.

What good looks like

Dated individual reflection explains an actual choice, trial and next step.

Fallback

Pupil points to a beat and confirms a choice; adult records the observed action and confirmed words.

Check and response

Example only: I directed the pause at the choice beat. We kept a silent hand cue because it helped us start together. Next time I will make the final pose clearer.

Safety

Check a clear personal space before any movement. Walk only; no running, lifting, falling, climbing, throwing or actual building. Keep all imagined props imaginary. Use an agreed open-hand stop cue. Do not move another pupil or their chair.
