What to say

We will improve one part of our invented scene. Choose a peer, small group or trusted adult as audience. If work is missing or you were absent, use the four-beat story and make a fresh version today. Do not add an earlier date to new evidence.

What good looks like

Pupil retrieves a genuine starting point or begins a fresh dated attempt and confirms an accessible role.

Fallback

Check and response

A fresh attempt is valid. It must not be described as a performance that happened in the previous lesson.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

In our story the space gets smaller. The response is the group asking for ideas and trying small moves. What did our watcher actually notice? If we have no feedback yet, I will watch a brief trial now and describe one thing I see. Choose one useful target.

What good looks like

Pupil distinguishes event from response and chooses one target grounded in a genuine observation.

Fallback

Offer two visible targets: hold the choice pose longer or give a clear silent cue.

Check and response

The change is smaller space. The response is the new plan. A target should help the audience see that link.

Compare the performance examples

A smaller show space

Four roles plan a show with wide arm moves. The imagined space becomes smaller. A planner asks the group for ideas. They rehearse small arm moves and finish together. A narrator names each change.

Which action lets the audience see the problem?

A change told in silence

The group begin with wide poses. A silent palm cue marks a smaller space. One performer pauses and looks at the group. Each offers a small move. They choose one and repeat it together, then hold the final pose.

How does this group show a choice without speech?

Watch the adult model

Show a rushed choice beat with small hand moves.

Repeat with a held pause before the same hand move.

Ask the watcher what became clearer. Keep their actual comment.

Say: I changed the pause. It helped the choice stand out. My next step is a clear end cue.

Teacher guidance

What to say

First I rush through the new idea. Now I pause, ask What could we try? and repeat a small move once. For this test I am changing the pause length only. Tell me if that gives the audience time to see the choice. A later test could change the cue, but we will not change everything at once.

What good looks like

Adult models controlled refinement and an audience check. Pupil links the tested feature to clarity.

Fallback

Use one hand gesture and a counted silent pause. Pupil chooses the shorter or longer pause.

Check and response

Holding a pause can mark thinking and separate the change from the new plan. A shorter pause may be preferred if the group can still make the choice clear.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use one kind specific comment: I could see ... when ... . Makers, decide whether to keep the tested change. Feedback informs your choice; it does not take the choice away from you. Make sure every pupil has a real creative contribution and knows the stop cue.

What good looks like

Group tests one feature and makes an evidence-based decision. Watcher comment is precise and maker confirms the choice.

Fallback

Adult watches a seated trial and gives one concrete observation; pupil chooses keep or change.

Check and response

Example: I could see the decision when you held the offered move before copying it. The group might keep that hold because it makes agreement visible.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use five minutes to rehearse and three to share with the chosen small audience. Use two minutes for the audience questions and four for individual logs. A private directed demonstration is valid. Watchers describe features and keep comments kind. Keep sound below speaking level and all movement within each person's own space.

What good looks like

Short performance or directed scene clearly shows a change and response. Each pupil retains an individual account of their contribution, real feedback and a next step.

Fallback

Use four seated hand poses and adult narration. The pupil directs the improved cue or pause, and a trusted adult watches privately.

Check and response

Example only: I changed the cue from a spoken Next to a held hand. The watcher saw us start the new move together. I will keep that cue and next work on a clear final pose.

Review

Refine and share a story about change

5 minutes

Audience: Name the change and the new plan.

Makers: Explain one choice you made.

Say what helped the audience follow the story.

Thank the audience.

Store work and settle.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Ask each maker to confirm their own part. If the audience missed a beat, keep that as useful feedback rather than pretending it worked. No rating of confidence or personal disclosure is needed. Use the last two minutes for calm storage and clearing the space.

What good looks like

Reflection compares intention and audience reading honestly, including an unfinished feature if relevant.

Fallback

Adult reads back a brief observation and asks pupil to confirm or correct the own-contribution record.

Check and response

Useful reflection: The audience saw the smaller space but missed the request for help. Our next step is to hold the choice beat longer.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Date the log and record actual support. Keep the first attempt and this later version if possible. Your arts log can show useful change even when the scene is still in rehearsal. This is evidence towards the award, not a claim that all its requirements are complete.

What good looks like

Dated reflection records a specific before-and-after feature, real feedback and an honest next step.

Fallback

Pupil points to the changed beat and confirms an adult-written record. Record what was observed, not an inferred feeling.

Check and response

Example only: I directed a longer pause. The watcher saw the new plan clearly. Next I will practise giving the final cue without an adult prompt.
