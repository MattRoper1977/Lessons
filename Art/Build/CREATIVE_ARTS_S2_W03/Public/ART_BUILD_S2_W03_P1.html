Three parts of a story

Monday 8 March 2027 · P5 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Sequence a short drama with a group.

I can put a short story in order with my group.

Today I can

  • I can show the start, change and end.
  • I can use my part or cue.
  • I can keep my own choice in my arts log.
pose

A shape you hold still.

cue

A sign to start or stop.

order

What comes first, next and last.

role

The part you do in a group.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or ask an adult to write your words.

Arrival

Three parts of a story

3 minutes

Choose to act, speak, point or direct.

Keep a clear space. Stay in your own space.

This is a made-up story. You can sit.

Teacher guidance

What to say

We will make a tiny story in three parts. You can be a mover, a voice or a director. You may sit and use your hands. We will not touch, run or build a real den. Show an open hand to stop.

What good looks like

Each pupil confirms an accessible role and the stop cue. Adult checks clear space.

Fallback

Offer two roles: show a hand pose or tell the adult which pose to show.

Check and response

A pupil may direct one pose while another pupil performs it. That is a valid role if the choice is the pupil's.

Starter

Three parts of a story

3 minutes

Our story is The new den.

Start: We want a den in a big space.

Change: The space is too small.

End: We choose a small den.

Point to the end.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I will read three parts. Hold up one finger for the start, two for the change and three for the end. Which part shows the new plan? We can change a plan and still tell a good story.

What good looks like

Pupils identify the ending as the small den and notice the space changes.

Fallback

Read only the start and end. Ask the pupil to point to the one that shows the new plan.

Check and response

The small den is the end. The small space is the change. The group changes its plan rather than trying to force a big den into the space.

I do

Three parts of a story

6 minutes

Watch three still poses.

One: Point at a big space.

Two: Hold hands close. Look and pause.

Three: Point at a small den.

Hold each pose for three slow counts.

Compare the performance examples

The new den with still pictures

The group point at a big space. Next they show a small space with their hands. Last they point to a new small den. They hold each pose for three counts.

What comes first? What comes last?

The new den with a voice cue

A voice says Start, Next, End. The group make one pose at each cue. In the middle a helper says, Can we change it? The group point to a new plan.

Which word helps the group change pose?

Watch the adult model

Point wide. Say: We want a den. Hold for three counts.

Hands close. Pause. Say: The space is small.

Ask: Can we change it? Make a small roof with hands.

Point to the three parts. Say: Start, change, end. I kept that order.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Watch me tell the story with hands only. First I point wide. Next I hold my hands close to show the space is small. I pause and say, Can we change it? Last I show a small roof with my hands. Now I mix up the order. Does the new plan still come last? Tell me which pose to move.

What good looks like

The adult contrasts ordered and mixed beats. A pupil identifies or directs a correction. Check understanding before group practice.

Fallback

Use just three spoken cues and hand shapes at the desk. The pupil can hold up one, two or three fingers.

Check and response

Start then change then end makes the new plan clear. If the small-den pose comes first, the audience may not see why the plan changes.

We do

Three parts of a story

6 minutes

Pick a role in our group.

Choose a pose for each part.

Use Start, Next, End as cues.

Try all three in order.

Which pose shows the change?

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Let us use one idea from each person. A mover may show a pose, a voice may give a cue, and a director may choose the pose. With four pupils two can share a pose role. Everyone keeps their own space. Try our three parts. Can we see the small space before the new den?

What good looks like

Each pupil offers or confirms a creative choice. Group holds three poses in order and uses an agreed cue.

Fallback

Adult reads the cues while pupils choose hand shapes. A pupil may direct the adult to show their chosen shape.

Check and response

A close hand shape can show the small space. A small roof shape may show the new den. Other clear, safe choices are valid.

Create

Three parts of a story

14 minutes

In your group, make three parts.

Choose your role and one idea.

Try the story twice.

Keep the order: start, change, end.

In your book or arts log, draw or tell your three parts.

Add: My choice was ___.

Supported

Use the three story parts.

Pick a role with the adult.

Choose one pose or cue.

Try the three parts with help.

Tell or point to your own choice.

Independent

Agree your role and one idea.

Make the start, change and end.

Use one cue for each part.

Try twice and keep the order.

Keep your own choice in your arts log.

Aspire

Use the same three-part task.

Test a spoken cue and a silent cue.

Choose which helps the order most.

Say why and keep that choice.

Teacher guidance

What to say

For the first three minutes choose roles and poses. Rehearse for four minutes. For three minutes test a cue or change a pose so the story is clear. Use the last four minutes to keep a three-part sketch or adult-written record and your own choice. No actual den is built. Ask for a pause if you need one.

What good looks like

Group performs or directs an ordered three-part story. Each pupil has an identifiable contribution and keeps an individual record.

Fallback

Use the supplied story exactly, with hand poses and an adult caller. The pupil chooses one pose or cue and confirms the three-part record.

Check and response

Example only: I chose close hands for the change. Our start is wide space, our change is small space, and our end is a small den. A pupil may use their own safe ending if it follows the change.

Review

Three parts of a story

5 minutes

Show the three parts to a partner or the adult.

The watcher says: I saw ___ first.

Did the new plan come last?

Choose one thing to keep.

Sit and put books away with care.

Teacher guidance

What to say

The watcher describes the order, not how brave anyone was. Say I saw ... first, then ... . Maker, confirm or correct that account. Use the final two minutes to settle and store the sequence for tomorrow. No public performance is required.

What good looks like

Audience identifies order and pupil confirms one choice to retain. Record honest uncertainty where the change was not clear.

Fallback

Adult watches one hand sequence privately and asks which pose the pupil wants to keep.

Check and response

Useful feedback: I saw the wide space first and the small den last. A useful next step: hold the middle pose a little longer.

Exit

Three parts of a story

3 minutes

Arts log: My role was ___.

My choice was ___.

Our story went first ___, next ___, last ___.

Next time I will ___.

Type instead

Write in your book first, or use these boxes. Save before closing.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I can write your words and read them back. Tell me if I have got your idea right. Keep your own choice, not my sample. Write or confirm today's date and the help you used.

What good looks like

Dated log records individual contribution, sequence and a real next step.

Fallback

Pupil points to one of the three poses; adult records the confirmed choice and what the pupil directed.

Check and response

Example only: My role was director. I chose the small roof. Next time I will hold the change pose longer.

Safety

Check a clear personal space before any movement. Walk only; no running, lifting, falling, climbing, throwing or actual building. Keep all imagined props imaginary. Use an agreed open-hand stop cue. Do not move another pupil or their chair.
