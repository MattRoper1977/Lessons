My own response to an artist

Tuesday 19 January 2027 · P1 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Find out about an artist who expresses feelings.

I can link my own art to an artist's idea.

Today I can

  • I can recall two facts about the artist.
  • I can make my own piece using a new layout.
  • I can say how the artist's idea helped me.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

artist

A person who makes art.

line

A long mark.

shape

A space with an edge, such as a circle.

response

Your own work made after you explore an idea.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find your real trials from last time.

Listen to the artist card again if needed.

Tell or show one fact and your chosen layout.

What to say

Find your own tests. If you were away or the tests are missing, hear the short card and make a quick fresh trial now. We use today's real date. Matisse is the artist; the new response will belong to you. You may use an invented feeling and share privately.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies an actual prior choice or makes an honest catch-up trial, and recalls an artist detail with rereading if needed.

Materials-free fallback

Read the card aloud and offer three silent hand positions close, then apart. The pupil chooses one or directs a new layout.

Check and response

A fresh trial and a reread card are valid. Do not invent a previous piece, quotation or pupil response.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Which are facts from the artist card?

Cut painted paper. Moved the shapes.

Does a shape tell us the artist's private mood?

What to say

Ask the pupil to name or point to two facts: cut painted paper and moved shapes. Then ask whether our description tells us exactly how he felt. A viewer can have a personal reading while being honest about what the facts show.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies or confirms two accurate method details and knows that the description does not prove a private emotional state.

Materials-free fallback

Give one spoken fact at a time and use an agreed yes or no gesture. Allow the pupil to listen again before responding.

Check and response

Both method details are facts in the card. No, a shape alone does not tell us the artist's private mood. Reread and model one fact versus one personal reading if needed.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch the adult use an artist's idea in a new way.

Move three own shapes. Choose a new layout.

Make a small piece for a made-up feeling.

Say what is the artist link and what is your own choice.

Artist description to read aloud

Henri Matisse

Cut-outs · Later work

Henri Matisse made art with line, colour and cut paper. He used line and colour to show feelings. He cut shapes from painted paper. He moved the shapes to try a new layout. We can read his art in our own way. We do not know his mood from it.

Notice: What did he cut? What could he move?

How could moving your own shapes change the way your piece feels?

He cut shapes from painted paper and moved the shapes to try a layout. In my own art, I could move shapes apart to suggest calm, or put them close to suggest energy. Those are my own readings. They do not tell us what he felt.

Other readings can be valid. Point to a detail and say why.

No artwork by this artist is shown. We use a description in our own words. A personal reading starts “This suggests ... to me”.

Which feature could fit your own idea?

What to say

Matisse's method of trying arrangements helped me. I use my own three torn shapes and move them into a rising path. I choose this layout for an invented feeling of hope. My shapes and layout are my own design. I may draw round them or use a little glue under each shape once I choose. I do not recreate an existing composition. In a silent response I can place three hand shapes low, middle and high. I explain the method link without pretending the artist made my piece.

What good looks like

The adult makes an original short response and clearly separates a general borrowed method from the new subject, shapes, arrangement and interpretation.

Materials-free fallback

Use or direct three silent hand shapes at different heights, or describe their order. A pupil who prefers stillness can make every choice verbally.

Check and response

Ask: 'Which came from learning about the artist: moving shapes to test a layout, or my exact new shape path?' Expected: the method of testing a layout. The exact path is the adult's own example.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Help make one new class response.

Choose a layout and a made-up feeling.

Check the artist link and our own choices.

Finish: The artist's idea helped us ___.

B is honest. If a pupil chooses A, point out the different maker, shapes and layout. Parallel check: 'Can I write my own name or code on my own response and name the artist as the idea link?' Expected: yes. Continue when the pupil can identify the method link and their own choice.

Other reasons can be valid. Show the feature that fits your idea.

What to say

You direct our original shapes. We can change spacing, line or position for our own idea. Hinge: Which is an honest log: A 'I made the artist's real work', or B 'I tried moving my own shapes after hearing about his method'? Ask why the record is honest.

What good looks like

The pupil makes an intentional new choice and distinguishes an original response from a copied or falsely attributed artwork.

Materials-free fallback

Use a pupil-directed silent sequence or spoken layout. Ask the same A or B question orally and let the pupil confirm a choice.

Check and response

B is honest. If a pupil chooses A, point out the different maker, shapes and layout. Parallel check: 'Can I write my own name or code on my own response and name the artist as the idea link?' Expected: yes. Continue when the pupil can identify the method link and their own choice.

Aspire

Complete your own response and log.

Try one new layout with the same shapes.

Say which layout best fits your idea and why.

Explain a different way someone could read it.

Choose one route within the same 14 minutes. Every route keeps your own choice and arts-log evidence.

What to say

Use your selected trial or choose a fresh layout and record why. For paper art, move three own torn shapes before fixing. Use a little glue underneath, press gently on the mat and keep fingers clear of wet paint. Drawing is equally valid. For a sound response, choose three soft taps with your own pauses and order; the link is testing spacing in your own form. Keep sound below talking volume and stop on a raised hand. A small piece is enough. An adult may handle materials under the pupil's direction. The pupil decides the feeling, layout and review.

What good looks like

A real original response, two accurate artist details recalled with support if needed, and a clear link from the artist's general method to a pupil-owned feature. The log distinguishes intention and observation. Record unfinished work honestly rather than claiming a final piece.

Materials-free fallback

Make or direct a silent layout or movement response, or give an original spoken description. The adult records the actual ordered actions, the pupil's stated emotion, artist link and decision when a recording method is available.

Check and response

Example only: I moved my own shapes, as the description said the artist could do. My layout rises because I chose hope. I kept the space at the end because it seems open to me. A different reason or art form is valid.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Show an adult or partner your own response.

Name the artist and the idea that helped you.

Say: My own choice was ___ because ___.

Tidy and keep the response with your log.

Teacher model only: Matisse moved cut shapes to test a layout. That helped me move my own shapes before fixing them. I chose a rising path because it suggests hope to me.

Teacher model only: I made a silent hand response with a wide gap at the end. The artist's method gave me the idea to test spacing. My sequence and intended feeling are my own.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Ask for the artist link and a choice in the actual response. A listener can notice a feature and offer a different reading. The maker decides what to keep. Sharing can be private. Use the last two minutes to cap glue, return tools, collect scraps and store work flat. Keep the real response, trials and log together.

What good looks like

The pupil explains a method link and a personal choice without claiming the class models or their response are works by the artist. Tools and evidence are stored safely.

Materials-free fallback

Witness a silent response privately and read a short factual description back for the pupil to confirm. Keep the pupil's interpretation separate from the adult's observed actions.

Check and response

A useful review is 'The artist moved shapes to test a layout. I moved my own shapes apart because I wanted calm.' It does not need to tell a personal life story.

Keep the artist facts and the response in their proper roles. Ask the pupil what they found out and how it helped their own work. Scribe exact words if needed, read them back and allow correction. A dated log gives or confirms the artist name, two facts, an original response or witnessed sequence, and a method link with a personal choice and reason. No quotation or model answer is passed off as pupil evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four real trial sets, four books or arts logs, four pencils and eight small scrap pieces for fresh trials if needed.

Paper route: twelve pupil-owned torn shapes or scrap to tear, four small bases, four mats and two glue sticks. Shapes can stay loose until a layout is chosen. No cutting tool is required.

Drawing route: four sets of familiar drawing tools. Optional paint uses five brushes, four mats, two stable water pots, a little washable paint and cloths.

Adult model: three original shapes and a new layout based on the method of trying arrangements. The artist card is read aloud; no actual artwork is displayed or copied.

Quiet sound needs only fingertips; the materials-free route uses silent hand positions, pupil-directed adult movement or an original spoken description. Keep four individual storage places and a flat drying area.

Teaching approach

Read the artist description or model a small original plan. Describe a feature, a choice and a reason. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

An original response must look like the artist's actual work Borrow a general method such as testing a layout, then use the pupil's own shapes, arrangement, subject and reason.

The teacher decides the feeling in the pupil's piece The maker names or confirms the intended emotion. An adult supports access and records the pupil's own explanation.

The log can describe work that we meant to make Retain actual work or a precise witness note. State the true stage and next step; do not invent a finished piece or earlier trial.
