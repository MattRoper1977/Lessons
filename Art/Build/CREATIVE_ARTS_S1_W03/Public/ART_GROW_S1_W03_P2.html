My plan ready for making

Weekly outcome: Plan an artwork on the theme of resilience.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find the plan you chose and your reason.

Today you will develop the plan. Making comes later.

What to say

Find your selected rough plan and the reason from Monday. Today we will make it clear enough to use in a later making lesson. If the plan is missing, make two tiny honest options now and choose one. Record today's real date rather than inventing an earlier plan.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies an actual selected idea or makes a brief dated catch-up choice. They understand that the task is planning, not full artwork making.

Materials-free fallback

Read back the ordered idea note. Pupil may describe the chosen idea or direct an adult to show it with small silent gestures.

Check and response

An honest fresh plan is valid. Keep the pupil's own intention and choice.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

What does your chosen feature mean?

What does another person need to know to help you make it?

What to say

Say the feature you chose and what it could suggest. Imagine a helpful adult reading your plan later. What would they need to know about the layout or order, tools, space or finish? We will add notes, not ask them to guess your intention.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies an intention and one missing practical detail. A different audience reading is allowed while the maker keeps control of the plan.

Materials-free fallback

Adult asks two spoken choices at a time, such as order or tools. Pupil points, signs or gives one short answer.

Check and response

Useful missing detail: where a path turns, how many marks repeat, when a pause comes, which tools are needed, or how the sequence ends.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch a rough idea become a usable plan.

Notice labels, counted tools, ordered steps and a time plan.

A small plan can be clear.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Point to the chosen rough plan. Say: I keep my idea and add labels so someone can follow it.

15 to 30 seconds Label start, turn and new route. Say: I need one sheet and two colour tools. A silent sequence needs no art tools.

30 to 45 seconds Say three ordered making steps. Show the draft forty-minute split: five set-up, twenty making, ten checking and five tidy.

45 to 60 seconds Read the plan back and add one missing note. Say: This is a usable plan for later making, not a finished artwork.

Which feature could fit your own idea?

What to say

My path plan has labels: start, block, turn and new route. I note one small sheet and two colour tools. My steps are place the main lines, add the turn, then check the meaning. My draft first making period is five minutes to set up, twenty to make, ten to check or change, and five to tidy: forty minutes. For a silent sequence I need no art tools. I note a clear seated space, four ordered actions and a stop cue. Staff can adjust the draft schedule before making.

What good looks like

The model connects intention, labelled layout or sequence, counted resources, access, ordered steps and a workable draft time plan. Pupils see a plan can stay simple. No artwork is claimed to be complete.

Materials-free fallback

Read the same plan aloud. Pupil may direct an adult to place imagined parts or state four sequence actions. Use an oral materials list, access choice and time plan.

Check and response

Check: Is a list saying make it nice enough? No. A usable plan says what comes first, which resources or no-materials route is used, and how the maker will check and finish.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Help check a class plan for missing details.

Add one label, step or resource note.

Keep the maker in charge of the idea.

Check your plan

A plan has a title but no steps or tools. What should the maker do?

Choose, then explain your reason.

What to say

Our original plan says repeat, pause, change, return, but it does not say how to stop. Let us add an ending cue. Hinge: a plan has a title but no steps or tools. Should we A start full making and guess, B add the missing details before making, or C copy someone else's plan? Explain.

What good looks like

The pupil selects B and adds a practical detail that supports the maker's intention. Feedback concerns clarity and feasibility rather than forcing a different idea.

Materials-free fallback

Use an oral four-part plan. Pupil directs the adult to insert one missing action, pause, access note or stop cue. Choose A, B or C aloud or by pointing.

Check and response

If A or C is chosen, model the confusion from one missing step. Parallel check: If a silent plan has no ending cue, should we add a clear stop before the making lesson? Yes. Continue when the pupil identifies a useful detail.

What to say

Use the chosen idea, not a new full artwork. Add labels showing what the parts mean. Write or say the materials and quantities. For a silent piece, say no art tools and plan the space, actions and stop cue. Put making steps in order and choose a manageable first forty-minute schedule. Use the adult model if helpful. Note a quiet or supported access option. Check that the later making is possible, then keep the plan for next week.

What good looks like

An annotated layout or ordered sequence, resource quantities or no-materials choice, space/access note, ordered steps, draft time budget and meaning check form a usable plan. Staff approve tools and space before later making. Any unfinished planning is labelled honestly.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil directs an adult through the layout or sequence and gives an oral tools/no-tools list, order, access and time plan. Adult retains exact confirmed notes. No materials or device are needed to participate.

Check and response

A workable simple plan could use one sheet, two colour tools and three ordered steps. A silent plan could use four small seated actions, a raised-hand stop and no art tools. These are options, not compulsory designs.

Optional stretch what to say

Ask for one practical question about your plan. Decide what to keep or change and say why. Explain how your tools, time and access choices fit the idea. Set a check before making.

Optional stretch what good looks like

The pupil evaluates whether the plan can communicate the intention under real constraints, explains a feedback decision and identifies a readiness check before making.

Optional stretch materials-free fallback

Adult reads the full oral plan back and retains the practical question, pupil's decision, reasons and next check in exact confirmed words.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Ask an adult or partner to read your plan back.

What is clear? What still needs a note?

You choose what to change. Keep the real plan.

Teacher model only: My plan shows a path finding another route. I labelled the turn, counted one sheet and two colour tools, and put my steps in order. I added a label after feedback. I still need the adult to approve the space.

Teacher model only: My silent piece has four parts: repeat, pause, change, return. I need no art tools. I chose small seated actions and a raised-hand stop. My draft time plan leaves time to practise and tidy.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Read or say the plan as if you will use it later. The listener describes one clear detail and asks one practical question. The maker decides whether to add or keep a feature and explains why. Use the final two minutes to tidy and store the selected plan, earlier options and log together.

What good looks like

The pupil checks the plan against intention and feasibility, considers feedback and retains ownership of a revision or reasoned decision to keep it. No full making or completed result is claimed.

Materials-free fallback

Adult reads the exact oral plan back. Pupil confirms it or directs one change. A private audience is enough.

Check and response

Useful feedback: I know the four actions but not the ending cue. Useful response: I added a raised-hand stop so the ending is clear.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For five pupils: five books or arts logs, five pencils and ten small planning spaces for two rough ideas each. Use a third space only if useful.

Optional visual route: six drawing tools including the adult model, five blank layout areas and a small set of colour tools for planning labels. No paint, cutting or fixing is required this week.

Sound or movement plans: five four-part sequence spaces, or five spoken/witness records. Fingertips and seated actions are enough for a brief planning demonstration.

Adult examples: two or three labelled original rough plans, one selected plan and one materials-and-time plan. These are teacher models, not pupil evidence.

For lesson two retain each pupil's chosen rough plan, reason and any feedback. No recording device or specialist art kit is required.

Teaching approach

Read the artist description or model a small original plan. Describe a feature, a choice and a reason. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

Resilience means never pausing or asking for help. A pause, support or a different route can be part of finding a way forward. Use invented or abstract ideas, not personal hardship.

Two ideas must be almost the same design. Change the main subject, arrangement or sequence so the plans offer a real choice. Changing only a colour may not make a genuinely different idea.

A plan is a finished artwork. Keep rough sketches, labels or short sequence notes as plans. The full making happens later. Do not claim completed artwork this week.

A more complex idea is always better. Choose an idea that communicates an intention and can be made with the time, space, materials and access available. A small original piece can work well.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. You can watch or use silent movement when you return.

Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You do not need to explain a personal feeling to the group.
