Arrival

3 minutes

Choose act, direct or watch first.

Point, speak, sign or pass.

Use made-up roles today.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Choose how to join in. We will tell a made-up story. You can sit, direct me or try a pose. Our open hand means stop.

What good looks like

Every pupil has a participation choice and knows the stop cue.

Fallback

Offer two choices: direct me or use one hand. A pupil may watch first.

Check and response

Acting, directing and a seated pose are all valid. Watching first is a preparation choice; record a later contribution accurately.

Starter

A story in three still pictures

3 minutes

Watch a still pose: look at an empty shelf.

What might be lost?

Watch a smile and a point. What may come next?

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

My empty hands and look show a missing prop. My point shows an idea. Can we tell which comes first? These are invented signs, not rules about feelings.

What good looks like

Pupils notice a concrete feature and link it to a story part.

Fallback

Show the two poses seated. Ask the pupil to point to first.

Check and response

Looking at the empty shelf can begin the story. A point can show a plan. Accept another clear order if the pupil explains it.

I do

A story in three still pictures

6 minutes

Watch first, next and last.

First: the prop is lost.

Next: the helper looks in a box.

Last: the prop is found.

Compare the performance examples

The lost prop

A helper looks at an empty shelf. The helper pauses with open hands. A friend points to a box. They both look in the box. They smile when the prop is found.

What shows the problem?

A new plan

Two makers hold an imaginary large sheet. They stop when it will not fit. One points to a fold. They fold the sheet in mime, then carry it together with space between them.

What changes after the pause?

Watch the adult model

Look at an empty shelf with empty hands. Say: First, the prop is lost.

Point into an imaginary box. Say: Next, I search.

Hold an imaginary prop. Say: Last, I find it.

Turn the last pose towards the viewer. Say: I changed this so you can see the end.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I show three still pictures. First I look at the empty shelf. Next I point into a box. Last I hold an imaginary prop and smile. I hold each pose for two seconds and relax. I try the last pose facing away, then facing you. Facing you makes my choice easier to see.

What good looks like

Adult models a complete three-part invented story, a comfortable freeze and one deliberate improvement.

Fallback

Mime with hands on a table or describe each part while pointing to three spaces.

Check and response

The problem is a missing prop. Looking in a box is the action. Finding it is the ending. Ask: Which pose shows the end? Check before moving on.

We do

A story in three still pictures

6 minutes

Choose a helper and a friend.

Pick first, next and last.

Try three still poses with space.

Which pose helps us see the end?

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Let us use the lost prop story. You choose where I look first. A partner or adult can be the friend. We move only at walking pace and do not touch. After each cue, relax. If the ending is unclear, change one hand or face direction.

What good looks like

Each pupil chooses a story detail. The group rehearses a shared cue and improves one pose.

Fallback

One pupil directs the adult through three seated poses. Ask the next pupil to choose a different final pose.

Check and response

A clear end might show the found prop. If pupils cannot order the parts, return to two parts: lost, found; then add the search. This is the hinge decision.

A story in three still pictures

14 minutes

In your book, mark first, next and last.

Use our story or make a safe new one.

Try three poses. Keep space.

Change one pose to help the viewer.

Tell an adult what you chose.

Supported

Pick lost prop or folded sheet.

Choose first, next and last with an adult.

Make a seated pose or direct the adult.

Point to one pose to keep in your log.

Independent

Plan first, next and last in your book.

Rehearse three poses with a partner.

Ask what the viewer can see.

Change one pose and log your reason.

Aspire

Use the same three-part task.

Try two ways to show the ending.

Choose the clearer one. Say why.

Give your partner a calm start cue.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Work in a pair, or in a small group with an adult. Choose the lost prop or a folded sheet that now fits. Give each person a pose or a directing job. Take turns choosing. Rehearse, ask one viewer what they saw, then change one pose. I will note your real choice.

What good looks like

Each pupil makes or directs an intentional pose. The sequence has a beginning, action and ending, with one tested change.

Fallback

Use three small sketches or table-top hand poses. Adult acts only the pupil directions. A pupil may show just two parts first, then choose a third.

Check and response

Example only: empty hands, point in box, hold found prop. A new safe invented sequence is equally valid. Record what the pupil actually did, not the example.

Review

A story in three still pictures

5 minutes

Choose one pose to show.

You may show just the adult.

Say: I notice ___.

Relax and clear your space.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Choose a partner or a private adult audience. The viewer says what they saw, then asks the maker to confirm. Keep feedback about the story. In the last two minutes, return chairs safely and store the plan.

What good looks like

Pupil shares a choice with an agreed audience and hears one specific observation.

Fallback

Adult describes the directed pose and asks the pupil to confirm the intended part.

Check and response

Useful feedback: I saw you point in the box. That helped me see the search. Avoid guessing private feelings.

Type instead

Write in your book first, or use these boxes. Save before closing.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Show your plan or describe your real pose. I can write your words and read them back. You can change them. We will use this sequence again tomorrow, or make a fresh one if needed.

What good looks like

Dated entry records the pupil role, chosen pose, reason and actual support.

Fallback

Offer first, next or last as choices. Scribe only the confirmed response.

Check and response

Example only: I pointed in the box to show a search. Next time I will face the viewer.

Safety

Clear bags and chairs from the acting area. Mark personal space by agreement; no touch, lifting, chasing, falls, climbing, weapons or thrown objects. Mime props. A still pose may be seated and lasts only a comfortable few seconds.
