Starter

Share a real arts choice

3 minutes

Listen to two viewer responses.

Which helps the maker?

You are good, or Your pause showed the change.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

The second response names a choice and its effect. We can use that information. A maker may disagree and explain why. We avoid judgements about the person.

What good looks like

Pupil identifies specific feedback linked to a visible choice.

Fallback

Offer the two phrases orally with one repetition and thinking time.

Check and response

Your pause showed the change is more useful because it explains what the viewer noticed. It is still the viewer's interpretation.

The lost prop

A helper looks at an empty shelf. The helper pauses with open hands. A friend points to a box. They both look in the box. They smile when the prop is found.

What shows the problem?

A new plan

Two makers hold an imaginary large sheet. They stop when it will not fit. One points to a fold. They fold the sheet in mime, then carry it together with space between them.

What changes after the pause?

Watch the adult model

Show the adult model plan and name the contribution.

Explain the pause and give a reason.

Read an invented viewer response clearly labelled Teacher model only.

Confirm the response and state a specific next step.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I use an adult model plan. I explain: I directed the folded-sheet ending and added a pause to show a new plan. My viewer says the pause helped. I confirm that was my aim. If the viewer saw something else, I ask which detail led to that reading. My next step is to try a clearer facing.

What good looks like

Adult models source, contribution, choice, reason, viewer response and next step.

Fallback

Read the model aloud. Pupil points to the item while adult uses the sentence frame.

Check and response

The model shows a real share structure, not pupil evidence. Check: Which sentence tells us the maker's choice?

We do

Share a real arts choice

6 minutes

Rehearse your share with a partner or adult.

Name the item, your part and a choice.

Viewer says: I notice ___.

Maker confirms or explains.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Take turns in the maker and viewer roles, or use an adult viewer. Ask permission before touching the work. If a pupil cannot explain the item yet, return to one choice and one reason. Do not write words they have not confirmed.

What good looks like

Every pupil rehearses a short explanation or directs a rehearsal. Feedback stays specific and optional.

Fallback

Adult reads a pupil-confirmed phrase and asks a single choice question.

Check and response

If the pupil can identify a choice, proceed to share. If not, make a fresh two-pose choice so there is a real contribution to explain. This is the hinge decision.

Aspire

Complete the same real share.

Ask a precise question about your artistic choice.

Compare your aim with the viewer response.

Justify a next step and plan a way to check it.

Optional next level stretch

Compare your aim with what the viewer saw. Will you use one idea they gave you? Say why. Set one skill to try. Say how you will check it.

Keep your real item, your own reason and the viewer's words. Add your skill goal and a plan to check it.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use two or three minutes to prepare, then share in a pair, small group or privately. A fresh route is a two-pose lost prop drama, acted seated or directed. Try two endings and choose one. The viewer gives a specific response. Keep the pupil's own decision about using it. Do not demand a public performance or recording.

What good looks like

Each pupil shares actual arts evidence or a new witnessed contribution, receives an audience response and records a reasoned next step.

Fallback

Pupil directs the adult performance and selects an ending by gesture. Adult states the confirmed choice to the agreed viewer. Pupil confirms the feedback record.

Check and response

Example only: My viewer saw that the prop was found. I will keep the final pose but pause longer. If sharing is declined, record preparation and plan another opportunity honestly.

Review

Share a real arts choice

5 minutes

Name a choice you want to keep.

Name a small change, if one would help.

Check your viewer note is true.

Put the item back in your log.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Read the note back to the maker. The maker can correct it. Separate what was shown from what is planned. In the last two minutes, store the item safely and clear the performance space.

What good looks like

The maker confirms an accurate record, including disagreement or an uncompleted share where applicable.

Fallback

Read one short sentence and offer keep / change.

Check and response

A true note might say shared privately with an adult, or prepared but not yet shared. Neither should be changed into a public performance claim.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Record the audience type, your actual contribution, feedback and next step. Use no personal names in this template. Keep missing evidence marked as a gap. This is progress towards the award, not a completion claim.

What good looks like

A dated sharing record preserves pupil voice and distinguishes completed evidence from future plans.

Fallback

Adult scribes a short oral or gestured response and reads it back.

Check and response

Example only: I shared my two-pose drama with one adult. They noticed the ending. I will keep the pause because it helped the story.

Safety

Clear bags and chairs from the acting area. Mark personal space by agreement; no touch, lifting, chasing, falls, climbing, weapons or thrown objects. Mime props. A still pose may be seated and lasts only a comfortable few seconds.
