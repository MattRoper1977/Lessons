Teacher guidance

What to say

Today we review real evidence before sharing it. A missing log is a gap to plan for, not a reason to invent an entry. You choose the audience. No one has to explain a personal experience.

What good looks like

Pupils have an agreed audience option and access to old logs or a fresh route.

Fallback

Adult offers a clean page and a short invented drama task.

Check and response

Work present today can be reviewed. Work that cannot be found is recorded as unavailable, not completed.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

A title alone does not show what the maker did. A dated note such as I directed a two-pose story tells us more. We can add a confirmed explanation today without changing the old date.

What good looks like

Pupil distinguishes a claim from evidence and identifies one helpful detail.

Fallback

Read two short oral examples and offer shows my choice / does not show it yet.

Check and response

A real record with a date, contribution and pupil explanation is stronger than a blank page or model answer. A new explanation uses today's date.

Compare the performance examples

The lost prop

A helper looks at an empty shelf. The helper pauses with open hands. A friend points to a box. They both look in the box. They smile when the prop is found.

What shows the problem?

A new plan

Two makers hold an imaginary large sheet. They stop when it will not fit. One points to a fold. They fold the sheet in mime, then carry it together with space between them.

What changes after the pause?

Watch the adult model

Point to the dated adult model drama plan. Name the actual contribution it describes.

Read a reason for the pause. Mark the model clear.

Show a blank sample. Mark missing rather than inventing an entry.

Mime the fresh two-pose fold story and date the new witnessed record today.

Teacher guidance

What to say

This invented adult sample has a drama plan and a dated note. I can tell who chose the pause and why. I mark it clear. This blank page needs evidence. This claim of a show has no record; I mark missing. These samples cannot become your evidence. I read the lost prop and new plan cards as invented performance examples only.

What good looks like

Adult models an honest audit, explains source/date and keeps examples separate.

Fallback

Use three spoken labels with one adult sample at a time.

Check and response

Clear means enough detail to understand a real contribution; needs a note means a real item exists but lacks explanation; missing means no item is available. Check with one item before independent sorting.

Teacher guidance

What to say

We can use an adult sample if no pupil wants to share. Read the record, ask the maker for permission and check its meaning. We do not guess that everyone attended. If it needs a note, the maker gives words we can confirm.

What good looks like

Group applies the audit questions without inventing attendance, intentions or work.

Fallback

Use only the fictional adult sample. Pupil chooses the audit category and a useful question.

Check and response

If the contribution is unclear, ask the maker once. If still unclear, mark needs a note. If no real record exists, use today's fresh task rather than recreate an old claim.

For each, find a date and your part.

Supported

Point to your part or explain it aloud.

Keep a dated note and choose a viewer.

Independent

Audit up to three available items.

Record each date, your part and one choice.

Select an item and one honest gap.

Plan a short share with an agreed audience.

Aspire

Complete the same real-evidence audit.

Compare two items for what they show.

Explain which best shows a choice and why.

Set one precise evidence or skill goal.

Optional next level stretch

Compare two real items. Explain which gives clearer evidence of your choice and why. Identify one skill goal and plan how you will check it.

Keep item references, your own comparison, the reason and a specific future check. Date new notes today.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use your existing arts log if available. Keep original dates and add new notes with today's date. Select rather than decorate. For a fresh route, make or direct a two-pose story: a folded sheet will not fit; a new fold makes it fit. Try a pause and choose the clearer version. I witness the actual contribution. Then plan a share for Wednesday.

What good looks like

Each pupil selects genuine evidence or makes a witnessed fresh drama entry, confirms a contribution and identifies a feasible next step.

Fallback

Adult reads available records one at a time. Pupil points to one real choice. If none is available, direct the adult's two poses and confirm a dated observation.

Check and response

Fresh example only: I directed the second pose and chose a pause to show the plan changed. Do not label it as a past lesson or live arts encounter.

Review

Find the story in our arts logs

5 minutes

Explain one selected item to your chosen viewer.

Say: My part was ___.

Viewer asks: What helped your idea?

Store the work for Wednesday.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Share a small real detail privately or with a peer. The viewer asks a question, not a judgement. Confirm what was understood. Save the selected item and the audit note; use the last two minutes to reset the space.

What good looks like

Pupil explains an actual contribution and confirms the viewer's reading.

Fallback

Adult reads the pupil-confirmed note aloud and records whether sharing occurred.

Check and response

An explanation can be spoken, signed, sketched or scribed. Record a private share accurately; if declined, keep sharing as a future step.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Keep the item reference, actual date, your words and the help used. A gap can stay open. Our record shows what happened, rather than claiming the award is finished.

What good looks like

Evidence index separates available items, missing items and today's new work.

Fallback

Offer one item code and one short confirmed phrase.

Check and response

Example only: Item one is my three-part plan. It shows I chose the ending. My next step is to explain why I chose it.

Safety

Clear bags and chairs from the acting area. Mark personal space by agreement; no touch, lifting, chasing, falls, climbing, weapons or thrown objects. Mime props. A still pose may be seated and lasts only a comfortable few seconds.
