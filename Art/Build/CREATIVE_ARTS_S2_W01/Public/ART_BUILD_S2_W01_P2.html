Teacher guidance

What to say

We can build on yesterday or make a fresh lost prop story. Missing work is not a problem to hide. We record what we do today. Choose how to join in and check the open-hand stop cue.

What good looks like

Every pupil has access to a usable story, including absent pupils.

Fallback

Adult recalls the lost prop model in three hand poses. Pupil chooses a beginning or end.

Check and response

A recovered plan and a fresh plan are both valid. Do not backdate a new plan.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I show the empty shelf. A helper might say: Where is the prop? Or the helper can point in silence. Which helps our story?

What good looks like

Pupil recalls a pose and selects a possible short line or action.

Fallback

Offer two lines: Where is it? or Here it is! Ask which fits this pose.

Check and response

Where is it? fits the problem; Here it is! fits the ending. Silent mime can communicate either.

I do

A story with a short role-play

6 minutes

Watch a pose, a line and a move.

I say: Where is the prop?

I look in the box.

I say: Here it is! Then I freeze.

Compare the performance examples

The lost prop

A helper looks at an empty shelf. The helper pauses with open hands. A friend points to a box. They both look in the box. They smile when the prop is found.

What shows the problem?

A new plan

Two makers hold an imaginary large sheet. They stop when it will not fit. One points to a fold. They fold the sheet in mime, then carry it together with space between them.

What changes after the pause?

Watch the adult model

Show empty hands. Say: Where is the prop?

Mime looking in a box. Keep the body in personal space.

Pause, show an imaginary prop and say: Here it is!

Repeat the last part facing the viewer. Say: I chose a pause so the end was clear.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I keep the same order. I start in my empty-hands pose, say one short line at talking level, mime looking in the box and end in my found-prop pose. I try rushing, then add a pause. The pause gives the viewer time. I do not need to shout.

What good looks like

Adult models brief role-play bounded by two poses and tests a pause.

Fallback

Do the full sequence seated with hand mime and a whispered or adult-spoken line.

Check and response

The first line shows the problem. The move shows the search. The final line and pose show the result. Ask: Which bit shows the search?

We do

A story with a short role-play

6 minutes

Pick one line or silent action.

Try it between two poses.

Give a calm cue.

Ask: Could you follow the story?

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

You choose the line or mime. I will wait for the cue. Let us try a short pause before the end. The viewer says first and last. If the order is unclear, return to three still pictures and add only one move.

What good looks like

Each pupil contributes a line, action or directing decision. The group checks whether a viewer follows the order.

Fallback

Adult performs the pupil choices. Pupil points when the adult should pause.

Check and response

A viewer naming lost then found shows a clear order. If not, keep the freezes and add one action only. This is the hinge decision.

Log what you chose.

Supported

Use the lost prop plan.

Choose one short line or a silent move.

Act seated or direct an adult.

Keep one choice in your arts log.

Independent

Use or make a three-part plan.

Add a line, mime or pause.

Rehearse and ask for one clear note.

Try a change and log what helped.

Aspire

Keep the same core story task.

Try a silent version and a spoken one.

Choose which tells the story best.

Explain the effect of your pause or cue.

Teacher guidance

What to say

In a pair or adult-supported group, agree one line or move per role. A directing job counts when it includes a real choice. Rehearse for a short agreed audience. Ask: Which part was clear? Change a pause, facing or cue. A silent version is complete.

What good looks like

The role-play retains a clear three-part story, an intentional addition and one revision informed by feedback.

Fallback

Make a seated hand drama. Adult voices the pupil-selected line. Pupil may direct a start and a pause without speaking.

Check and response

Example only: Where is the prop? Search mime. Here it is! A quiet original line or silent action is equally valid.

Review

A story with a short role-play

5 minutes

Share a small part or the whole story.

Choose your audience.

Say: I saw ___ in your story.

Put plans away with care.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Choose a peer, a small group or the adult. Listen to one story detail. Ask the maker if the meaning was understood. In the last two minutes, reset chairs and store the plan beside the log.

What good looks like

Pupil has an agreed sharing moment and receives a specific, respectful observation.

Fallback

Adult repeats the chosen action, then reads the viewer note for confirmation.

Check and response

A useful note is: Your pause helped me see the ending. Comment on a choice, not on acting confidence or personal traits.

Type instead

Teacher guidance

What to say

Keep your own words about the action or line you chose. I will date the record today and note the help used. If you did not share today, we write that honestly and plan a later private share.

What good looks like

Record distinguishes performance, creation and sharing actually completed, with an honest next step.

Fallback

Offer point, short phrase or dictated sentence. Read it back.

Check and response

Example only: I added a pause. The viewer saw the prop was found. Next time I will face front.

Safety

Clear bags and chairs from the acting area. Mark personal space by agreement; no touch, lifting, chasing, falls, climbing, weapons or thrown objects. Mime props. A still pose may be seated and lasts only a comfortable few seconds.
