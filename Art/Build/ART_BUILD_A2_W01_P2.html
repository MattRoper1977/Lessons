Art · BUILD · Autumn 2 · Week 1

Make — My festival light pattern

Learning goal

I can make a light pattern on dark paper.

Today I can

  • I can choose shapes and two or three bright colours.
  • I can use the same bit again.
  • I can check one thing in my plan.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write.

You can ask an adult to move your shapes.

You may pass.

I · S · C

Independent

Make a small light pattern on dark paper.

First, plan one or two shapes.

Choose two or three bright colours.

Show the bit you will use again.

Make that bit. Then use it again.

Use all the colours you chose.

Review: Pick one thing in your plan: a shape or a colour.

Find it in your work. Does it match? Show a match or a change.

I · S · C

Supported

Choose a dot or a ring.

Choose two bright colours.

This is your plan: one shape in each colour.

Show your plan with an adult.

Make that pair on dark paper.

Make the same pair once more.

Review: Pick one colour in your plan.

Find it in your work. Does it match?

I · S · C

Choice

Choose to draw, move cut shapes or direct an adult.

Plan a dot, ring, ray or lamp shape.

Choose two or three bright colours.

Put one mark in each colour in an order.

That is the bit to use again.

Make it on dark paper. Make the same bit once more.

Review: Pick your shape or one colour from the plan.

Show where it is in your work. Does it match?

Say, sign or show one word or reason for your choice.

I · S · C

Aspire

Plan a small light pattern on dark paper.

Pick one or two shapes and two or three bright colours.

Show the bit you will use again.

Choose where it will go: one row or two rows.

Make your bit at least twice.

Leave space so the shapes are clear to see.

Review: Choose one thing in your plan to check.

Show a match or a change. Show why you chose that space.

TA Brief

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Current stage

1. Set out dark paper, the full shape/colour choices, the four word-and-symbol cards and the simple plan space; do not assume attendance at P1.

2. Use the count-in and clear stop cue; every pupil may direct silently or pass. Keep cut shapes ready and support any scissors use.

3. In We do, each pupil chooses a part before the shared example is completed; allow an adult to carry out the pupil's instructions.

4. In You do, offer one route; note the pupil's own shapes, colours, repeat and check rather than praise alone.

5. Record one specific choice or changed part with the response mode and prompts, and file the evidence in the evidence app the same day under ART-BUILD-A2-W01-P2.

Shared preparation, access and safety

Prepare the simple word, shape and colour cards described here before the lesson; no external image, named resource or screen interaction is needed. Read pupil wording aloud, one short prompt at a time. All response choices apply to EVERY question, demonstration response, shared task, route, review and Exit: pupils may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. A pupil may direct an adult to place a shape or move a card. Offer a silent director role throughout; do not require making marks, touching materials or speaking to the group. Never cold call. Any pupil may pass. A pass needs no book entry.

Use one Arrival tier and one Exit tier per pupil. Keep Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until the response. The three Exit checks are brief choices or short responses, not three extended written answers. Allow private thinking before any shared reveal. An adult may record the pupil's own choice, gesture or exact words. No board copying, cutting or gluing is needed in the timed stages.

Festival context: light and colour are ways some people celebrate. People within a tradition may celebrate in different ways. These are original, made-up classroom pictures of light shapes, not copies of any tradition's sacred images. Never ask about a pupil's belief, family, worship or loss. Discuss the shapes and colours in these pictures, not personal disclosure.

Materials safety and access: use pencils, pastels or chalk, coloured and dark paper, and optional glue. Any scissors are used with adult care; prepare cut shapes in advance so cutting is never required. Use no flames. Keep chalk on the paper and do not blow dust. Announce each short task, use a predictable short count-in with a matching hand gesture, and show a flat hand for STOP. At STOP, pause marks or card moves and rest tools on the table. A pupil can choose the silent director role, a quiet place or a pause without explaining why. Offer large, clearly spaced shapes and describe their colour and position; do not infer lack of art understanding from a visual or motor access need.

Kit and stimulus: Each pupil needs a small piece of dark paper, bright yellow, pink and light blue pencils, pastels or chalk, or ready-cut coloured paper shapes with optional glue. Use only two or three bright colours in each artwork. Provide DOT / RING / RAY / LAMP cards: dot = a filled round spot; ring = a round line with an empty middle; ray = one short straight line; lamp = a plain oval outline with three short rays above it, with no flame. A lamp here is only a made-up drawn sign for a light. Add START / STOP cards and a small plan space with SHAPES / COLOURS / BIT TO REPEAT. Say colour names aloud and describe card positions. An adult can draw or place shapes exactly as the pupil directs. No earlier lesson or completed work is needed. Use a retained P1 plan if available, or make a fresh brief plan within the lesson. Prepare four must-know word cards, each with the word and a simple matching symbol, read aloud: plan (a small plan card), shape (a dot and a ring), repeat (one pair, an arrow, the same pair), pattern (a row of marks). Show them from the Opening.

Adult-made Example A and its plan: Draw or arrange four large, evenly spaced marks in one row on dark paper: yellow dot / pink ring / yellow dot / pink ring. Beside it, make a plan card showing a yellow dot followed by a pink ring, with the words TWO COLOURS: YELLOW, PINK and USE THE PAIR AGAIN. This is an original classroom example, not a named artwork or a sacred image. The finished row matches the plan.

Adult-made Example B: Make another row on dark paper: yellow dot / pink ring / yellow dot / pink dot. It uses the same two colours as A, but its second pair has a dot where the plan calls for a ring. Prepare the last pink dot as a loose paper shape and a pink ring to swap in, so the adult can show one simple change without covering a pupil's work. Adult cutting happens before the lesson.

Adult-only EHCP note: Possible outcome areas are communication and interaction; cognition and learning; sensory and/or physical needs; social, emotional and mental health. Link observations only to an actual individual target; do not infer an outcome from participation alone. Regulation strategy: use the announced count-in and visible STOP cue, with an agreed silent return to the next small task if the pupil needs a pause.

Same-day evidence — ART-BUILD-A2-W01-P2: The assigned adult records one chosen book item or a brief observation of the pupil's own response, response mode and actual prompt used, and files it in the evidence app the same day under ART-BUILD-A2-W01-P2. A photo is optional and needs the accompanying observation; a photo alone does not show understanding. Do not require a public display.
