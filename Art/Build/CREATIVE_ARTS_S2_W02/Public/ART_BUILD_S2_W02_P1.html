Today I can

  • I can try two ways to say a line.
  • I can choose one small move.
  • I can show my choice and why.
role

The part you play in a story.

voice

The sound you use to say words.

pace

How fast you speak or move.

pause

A short stop.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or ask an adult to write your words.

Arrival

Choose a voice and a move

3 minutes

Pick a role: Keeper or Helper.

You can sit, point, speak, sign or direct.

This is a made-up story.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Our Keeper comes to work at a table. The table has moved. That is our made-up change. You can play the Keeper or help me play it. We will keep our hands in our own space and stop at my open hand.

What good looks like

Pupils choose or confirm a role or a directing job. The stop signal and working space are clear.

Fallback

Offer the two role words and ask for a point. The adult performs while the pupil directs.

Check and response

Either role or a directing role is valid. Do not require a pupil to share a real experience of change.

Starter

Choose a voice and a move

3 minutes

Hear: My table has moved.

Hear it fast. Hear it slow.

Point: fast or slow?

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I say the same line in two ways, both at a quiet talking level. First fast, then slow. Which one was slow? We have changed pace, not the words.

What good looks like

Pupils identify a contrast in pace by word, gesture or choice.

Fallback

Say just "It has moved" twice with a very clear contrast. Use one finger for fast and two for slow.

Check and response

The second version is slow in this model. A quiet voice can be fast or slow. Loudness and pace are different.

I do

Choose a voice and a move

6 minutes

Watch the two Keeper versions.

Hear one line in two ways.

Watch one small hand move.

Which way might you choose?

Compare the performance examples

The changed table

The Keeper says, "My table has moved." The words come fast. The hands stop by the chest. The Keeper then asks, "Can you help?"

Are the first words fast or slow?

The changed table

The Keeper says the same words slowly. One open hand points to an empty spot. There is a short pause. The Keeper then asks, "Can you help?"

What has changed in this version?

Watch the adult model

Say the Keeper line quickly at quiet talking level. Keep hands by the chest.

Say the same line slowly. Open one hand towards an empty spot.

Say: I choose slow words because my Keeper looks first. Show a short pause.

Ask pupils to choose fast or slow and perform their choice once.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I read or show example A, then B. I choose the slow line, "My table has moved," and an open hand to the empty spot. I want my Keeper to take time to look. Another Keeper could choose fast words. Check: did I change the words, the pace, or both?

What good looks like

Adult compares two versions and gives one reason. Check after the model: pupils identify that pace changed and the words stayed the same.

Fallback

Use only the short line and an open hand. Point to fast or slow and let the pupil choose the adult's next version.

Check and response

The words stayed the same; the pace changed. Both choices can work. A character's feeling cannot be known just from a hand shape.

We do

Choose a voice and a move

6 minutes

Choose fast or slow for our Keeper.

Choose one small move.

Try the line and move once.

Say: We chose ___ because ___.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Let us choose the pace first. Now choose an open hand or hands resting still. I perform the class choice, then we try the other pace. Hinge: which helps our Keeper look before asking for help? Give a reason. If the reason is unclear, I show the slow version with a pause and we choose again.

What good looks like

Each pupil makes or confirms a choice. The class links a feature to an intention; no feeling is imposed.

Fallback

Adult offers two choices and performs the confirmed direction. A pupil can point rather than act.

Check and response

A slow line and pause might show looking before asking. A fast line could also fit if the pupil explains a different intended Keeper. Accept a reason about a visible or heard feature.

Create

Choose a voice and a move

14 minutes

In your book or arts log, pick a role.

Try the line fast, then slow.

Try a small move with the line.

Keep one voice and one move.

Say or show why.

Supported

Pick Keeper or Helper.

Tell the adult: fast or slow.

Choose an open hand or still hands.

Try both ways. Keep one. Say or show why.

Independent

Pick your role and line.

Try fast and slow words.

Add one small move or pause.

Keep a voice and move. Log your reason.

Aspire

Do the same core task.

Change the pause or one hand move.

Say how the new choice changes the role.

Keep a note of both real tries.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use the Keeper line, "My table has moved," or the Helper line, "We can find a new spot." Practise each at two paces at talking level or below. Add one small hand move or a clear still pause. Keep the version you choose. Work with a partner, adult or alone. A silent director can choose the adult's voice and move. Record those directions as your evidence.

What good looks like

Two actual pace trials, one chosen pace and movement or stillness, and a confirmed reason. A directed performance is labelled as directed rather than performed by the pupil.

Fallback

Pupil points to a pace and a hand choice. Adult performs both trials and asks which to keep. Record the pupil's exact confirmed choice.

Check and response

Example only: I chose slow words and an open hand because my Keeper looks first. Any deliberate safe choice with a reason is valid. Do not copy this model as the pupil's work.

Review

Choose a voice and a move

5 minutes

Show your line and move, or direct it.

A partner says: I notice ___.

Tell us your choice.

Put your work in a safe place.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Share with one partner or just me. The listener names one thing they saw or heard: "I notice a pause." The maker says whether that was planned. Use the final two minutes to put pencils away and store each real log for tomorrow.

What good looks like

A factual observation is shared respectfully and the maker confirms an intention. Work is kept for lesson two.

Fallback

Adult describes the performance neutrally and asks for yes, no or a changed word. No peer audience is required.

Check and response

Useful feedback: I heard the words get slower. Avoid claims about what the pupil or character must feel.

Type instead

Write in your book first, or use these boxes. Save before closing.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Point to or tell me your real choice. I can write your words and read them back. Mark if you performed or directed. Keep your two trials in a brief note so we can use them next time.

What good looks like

Dated entry shows the role, two actual trials, chosen voice and movement, reason, and support used.

Fallback

Use one confirmed choice at a time. Adult writes a short witnessed note with the pupil's own words or gesture meaning.

Check and response

Example only: I directed the Keeper. I chose slow words and a still pause. Next time I will add the help line. Record only what happened today.

Safety

Clear a small working space. Keep both feet supported if seated. Use hands within each person's own space. No touching another performer or taking a prop from them. Sound is optional; stop on the agreed signal. Pupils can direct the adult to perform their chosen pace and movement. Record that as directed evidence, not a vocal or physical skill the pupil personally demonstrated.
