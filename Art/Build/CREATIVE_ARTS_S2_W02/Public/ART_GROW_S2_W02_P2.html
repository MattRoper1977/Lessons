Teacher guidance

What to say

Use Monday's genuine plan and goal. If you were absent or cannot find it, make a fresh short comparison today and set a goal now. Date that starting point today. We keep the agreed quiet sound level, stop signal and private sharing option.

What good looks like

Prior evidence is retrieved accurately or a fresh starting point is recorded. No backfilled claim is made.

Fallback

Offer two gap lengths or turn cues; adult demonstrates and pupil confirms a goal.

Check and response

A new starting point is valid. Do not claim a previous trial or meeting of a goal without evidence.

Starter

Rehearse and refine a short performance

3 minutes

State or point to your skill goal.

Show the chosen cue or pause once.

Ask a partner to watch that feature.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Rehearse only the feature first. A two-count gap can be shown silently; a turn cue can be a small hand lift. Ask the listener to notice the selected skill rather than rate the whole performance.

What good looks like

Pupils recall or confirm one observable goal and ask for focused feedback.

Fallback

Adult performs under the pupil's direction and the pupil selects the feature for the listener to notice.

Check and response

A focused goal can be checked: start after the cue in two runs. "Be better" needs a more precise skill to notice.

A clear pause

The Keeper plans to work at a table. It has moved. The performer says, "The table has moved," pauses for one count, then says, "I can ask for help." One open hand shows the empty space.

Where does the performer pause?

A changed pattern

A sound maker creates four soft steady finger taps, one silent count, two slower taps, then four steady taps. The maker says the break marks a plan changing. This is a new class pattern with no tune or words from another work.

Watch the adult model

Show a brief first run, starting the return before the agreed cue.

Listener says: I notice the return began before the cue. Show a clearer hand cue.

Repeat and check what happened. Count the real successful run only.

Say: That is one success. My goal asks for two; I need one more real run or an honest next step.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I perform the original Keeper scene or sound pattern. The listener says, "The return began before your cue." I test a clearer hand cue, then repeat. I count one successful start, not two. Check: is my goal met if it asked for two runs? What real evidence do I still need?

What good looks like

Adult models honest checking, one refinement and a real second attempt. Pupils distinguish partial progress from a fully checked goal.

Fallback

Show two silent hand starts and ask pupils to point to together or early. Keep a visible count of actual runs.

Check and response

One successful start is progress but does not meet a goal of two successful starts. Complete a second real run or record the goal as still in progress.

Listener names the chosen skill.

Choose one change and repeat.

Check what really happened.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use the same short changed-table story. Let one person cue, one perform and one observe; in a pair, the adult can observe. Hinge: if a listener says "The gap was one count," but our plan says two, should we write two because it was planned? If anyone is unsure, model and count the real gap together before another try.

What good looks like

Pupils use feedback about an actual feature and record the trial honestly. They understand that a plan is different from observed evidence.

Fallback

Adult follows a pupil cue; another pupil points to one or two counts. Share privately if preferred.

Check and response

Record one count for that run. Try again if wanted. Never replace observed evidence with the intended plan. The maker decides what to change.

Optional next level stretch

Compare two runs of your scene or beat. What got clearer? Which change helped? Say what you kept and why. Set one small skill goal and a way to check it.

Keep a note of both runs and your own part. Add the real feedback, your choice and reason. Add your next skill goal.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use up to three short lines or a brief original beat-and-gap pattern. Keep the focus on your selected skill. Do a first run, hear one feature notice, then choose one adjustment in pace, pause, movement or cue. Try the adjusted version and check your goal. If the new choice is less useful, return to the first and explain why. Swap performer, director and listener roles so each pupil makes a real choice. Whole-class performance is optional.

What good looks like

A genuine rehearsal, feature-based feedback and tested refinement or justified kept choice are recorded. Each pupil contributes an intentional skill choice and checks the goal honestly. A directed piece is labelled as directed.

Fallback

Pupil directs the adult's two runs by selecting a gap or cue. Adult records the direction, observed result and exact confirmed reason. Silent hand movement can carry all cues.

Check and response

Example only: The first start was early. I made the cue clearer and we began together in the next run. My goal is partly met; I need one more successful run. Alternatively record two successes only if both occurred.

Review

Rehearse and refine a short performance

5 minutes

Share your final short version or direct it.

Listener: I notice ___.

Maker: I changed or kept ___ because ___.

Put plans and real notes in order.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Invite a chosen listener to name one feature they saw or heard. The maker links a kept or changed choice to the intended effect. If time permits one final check, do it; otherwise record the next step honestly. Use the last two minutes to clear the space and store logs.

What good looks like

Pupils communicate a reasoned choice and retain feedback that actually happened. Goal status is clear.

Fallback

Adult acts as private listener and reads the confirmed choice note back. No public performance or recording is required.

Check and response

Useful reflection links a change to its purpose: the longer pause gave the new plan a clear start. A goal still in progress is a valid honest outcome.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Keep the actual plan, trial notes and confirmed reflection together. Include your art form, what you performed or directed, listener notice, choice, goal status and support. Next week we will use a short sequence about overcoming a change; a clear cue or pause can carry forward.

What good looks like

Dated record shows real evidence of practice and reflection, an accurate goal judgement and a concrete next step.

Fallback

Prompt one item at a time. Record a short witnessed note and let the pupil correct it. Leave an honest gap rather than filling it with the teacher model.

Check and response

Example only: I directed the pause. One run had two counts and one had one count. I will practise holding two counts before the final line.

Safety

Clear a small working space. Keep both feet supported if seated. Use hands within each person's own space. No touching another performer or taking a prop from them. Sound is optional; stop on the agreed signal. Pupils can direct the adult to perform their chosen pace and movement. Record that as directed evidence, not a vocal or physical skill the pupil personally demonstrated.
