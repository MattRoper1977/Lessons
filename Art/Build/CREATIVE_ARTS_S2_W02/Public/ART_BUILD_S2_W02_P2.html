Pick act, speak, sign or direct.

We use the same made-up story.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Yesterday our Keeper found the table had moved. Find your choice of voice and move. If you were away or the note is missing, make a fresh fast and slow test now; date it today. You do not need to pretend to remember.

What good looks like

Pupils retrieve actual prior choices or make a clearly dated fresh test. Participation and stop routes are confirmed.

Fallback

Read the role lines and offer fast or slow. Adult makes the selected trial while the pupil directs.

Check and response

Missing work is a fresh starting point, not a failure. Record the real date and support.

Starter

Keep and change your character

3 minutes

Try your line and move once.

A partner watches or listens.

Say: I notice ___.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use a short warm-up at quiet talking level. One pupil says or directs a line; the other notices the pace, pause or small move. Then swap, or work privately with me.

What good looks like

Pupils recall a deliberate feature and give a neutral observation.

Fallback

Adult performs the pupil's confirmed choice and the pupil points to the feature they notice.

Check and response

An observation names a feature: I notice slow words or I notice an open hand. It need not judge the performer.

I do

Keep and change your character

6 minutes

Watch our three-line scene.

Keeper: My table has moved.

Helper: We can find a new spot.

Keeper: I will try that.

Hear a pause. See a small move.

Compare the performance examples

The changed table

The Keeper says, "My table has moved." The words come fast. The hands stop by the chest. The Keeper then asks, "Can you help?"

Are the first words fast or slow?

The changed table

The Keeper says the same words slowly. One open hand points to an empty spot. There is a short pause. The Keeper then asks, "Can you help?"

What has changed in this version?

Watch the adult model

Read all three lines, using a hand cue for each turn.

Repeat the last Keeper line with no pause, then with one short pause.

Say: I keep the pause because the new choice is clear.

Ask the pupil to cue the adult's three turns and confirm the pace.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I show the whole tiny scene once, with a pause before the last line. The Keeper looks to a new spot with one open hand. Now I rush the last line, then try the pause again. I keep the pause so the response is clear. Check: which part did I change?

What good looks like

Adult models a complete three-line scene, one revision and a reason. Pupils identify the pause as the changed feature.

Fallback

Read one line at a time. Use a hand cue for each turn. Let the pupil direct the pace of the last line.

Check and response

The pause before the final line changed. The story words stayed the same. A change is useful when it helps the intended scene.

We do

Keep and change your character

6 minutes

Pick Keeper or Helper.

Use a hand cue for each turn.

Try the three lines together.

Choose one part to make clear.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

I cue one turn at a time with an open hand. Use your chosen pace and move. Hinge: if both roles speak at once, what could help? Choose a hand cue or a louder voice. If anyone chooses louder, model the same sound level with a clear cue and try again.

What good looks like

Each role has a clear turn. Pupils choose a cue or pause to help turn-taking, not louder sound.

Fallback

Adult reads all lines while pupils direct turns with one, two and three fingers. Keep the same story order.

Check and response

A hand cue or pause helps us take turns. A louder voice does not tell us whose turn comes first. Rehearse the cue if turns are still unclear.

Try it again. Keep your choice.

Supported

Try one pause or move change.

Keep your choice. Tell or show why.

Independent

Use your chosen voice and move.

Try the scene with clear turns.

Get one notice from a listener.

Test one change and log your reason.

Aspire

Do the same core scene.

Keep the role clear when the plan changes.

Test two ways to cue the last line.

Explain which cue helps the scene most.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Work in pairs, with an adult, or as a director. Use the three original lines or make your own short help line. First try the scene. A listener names one feature. Choose one change in pace, pause, movement or turn cue. Try again. Keep a brief note of the first try and final choice. No performance to the whole class is needed.

What good looks like

A real first try and a revised try show one intentional skill choice. The pupil can say or show what changed and why. Directed evidence is labelled accurately.

Fallback

Adult performs both versions under pupil direction. Ask: keep the pause or change it? Record the confirmed choice and reason.

Check and response

Example only: We added a pause before "I will try that" so the new plan was clear. A pupil may keep the first version if they can explain why it works.

Review

Keep and change your character

5 minutes

Show a short part or direct it.

Listener: I notice ___.

Maker: I kept ___ because ___.

Store the real log.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Show one line or a short section to your chosen listener. The listener names a feature, and the maker explains one kept or changed choice. Reserve the last two minutes to return pencils and put logs in order.

What good looks like

Sharing is accessible and consensual. Feedback connects to a real feature, and the maker's choice is kept.

Fallback

The adult gives a neutral observation; pupil confirms by word, gesture or point. Sharing may be private.

Check and response

A useful reply is: I kept the slow line because it was clear. Both keeping and changing can be a reasoned decision.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Complete today's real log. Record what you performed or directed, one change you tested and your reason. I can scribe and read it back. Pick one next step for next week's group story.

What good looks like

Dated arts-log entry distinguishes the actual trial, choice, reason and support. A next step points towards clear group turns.

Fallback

Pupil selects one feature and confirms a short adult note. Leave an honest gap if a part was not tried.

Check and response

Example only: I used slow words and an open hand. I added a pause to help my turn. Next time I will watch the cue.

Safety

Clear a small working space. Keep both feet supported if seated. Use hands within each person's own space. No touching another performer or taking a prop from them. Sound is optional; stop on the agreed signal. Pupils can direct the adult to perform their chosen pace and movement. Record that as directed evidence, not a vocal or physical skill the pupil personally demonstrated.
