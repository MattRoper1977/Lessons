Teacher guidance

What to say

I show the same invented choice beat twice. In the first adult model, the new move follows at once. Next I pause, ask What could we try? at talking level and give the cue. A still pose lasts at most three comfortable seconds with normal breathing. I ask a real viewer whether the decision is easier to follow.

What good looks like

Adult models a small feedback-led revision and checks the audience interpretation directly.

Fallback

Use a silent seated version: pause, show two small hand choices, point to one, then repeat it.

Check and response

The pause can separate the change from the choice. It does not prove success on its own. Check the viewer's actual reading; if still unclear, simplify to two gestures.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Run the four beats with the agreed target. Ask the viewer to name the problem and the response. If they can, check how each cue works. If not, return to the unclear beat with a smaller gesture or short line before running again.

What good looks like

Group uses actual feedback to judge a revision and agrees accessible transitions for each role.

Fallback

Staff cue only the first beat. Pupils choose or direct the following cues and can answer by pointing.

Check and response

If the viewer identifies the smaller space and the new plan, retain useful detail. If not, simplify the choice/result link. This is the hinge decision.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use the established scene or the fresh four-beat route. Try the revision, ask for one specific response, then make a reasoned decision. Agree a provisional running order: ready check, start cue, scene, end cue and relax. Next week is performance then review, for the class and invited staff only. No public or family audience is planned. Keep an accessible role and staff-only alternative.

What good looks like

Each learner tests an actual refinement and records a real contribution. The group has a workable next-week plan with clear role and cue agreements.

Fallback

Use a short private staff rehearsal. A learner can direct one transition or choose a silent cue; record that action and agree a suitable future role.

Check and response

Example only: We added a pause before the new plan. The viewer saw the choice. I will give the end cue next week. If that cue is not settled, keep it as a next decision rather than a completed agreement.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Read back each learner's part, real response and next step. Confirm how to pause or stop. The plan is for next week; it is not a record of a performance already given. In the last two minutes, relax and store all notes with the outline.

What good looks like

Learners confirm accurate rehearsal evidence and an accessible preparation plan.

Fallback

Check role and cue one-to-one by gesture or short spoken choice.

Check and response

A useful readiness check covers roles, order, cues, safe space and how to stop. It does not require a polished or public performance.

Safety

Keep a clear personal space. Walk only; no running, lifting, falls, climbing, throws or contact. Props and scenery are imagined. Use an agreed open-hand stop cue. A freeze lasts at most three comfortable seconds, with normal breathing, then relax. Seated and hand-only versions are equal options.
