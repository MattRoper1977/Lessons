Teacher guidance

What to say

Use the scene about a smaller show space from last week when available. Missing plans or missed sessions do not become invented evidence. The fresh route is four beats: a wide show, a smaller space, a new choice, a small show. Agree an accessible role and audience.

What good looks like

Each learner has a real source plan or fresh route and a voluntary accessible role.

Fallback

Read the four fresh beats aloud and let the learner direct a seated hand version.

Check and response

A new scene or trial is dated today. Existing records keep their original date. No one needs to explain a personal change.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Ask each learner to point to or state their actual contribution. Read a genuine earlier comment if available; otherwise observe a short trial today. Our story is invented, and every role can make a creative choice.

What good looks like

Learner identifies a personal contribution and the group recovers the four-beat order.

Fallback

Offer one beat at a time. Use a labelled sketch or gesture instead of a spoken account.

Check and response

The choice beat shows the group asking or testing an idea. The result shows their new small move working. If those are mixed up, rehearse just those two.

Compare the performance examples

The small show with a cue keeper

A group rehearse plan, change, choice and result. The first cue comes before everyone is ready. In the next trial, a cue keeper checks each role, points to the word Start, then starts. The group begin the new small move together.

Which part changes in the second trial?

A pause that shows a choice

A group show wide moves, a smaller space, a choice and a new small move. A viewer misses the choice. The group add a quiet pause before asking What could we try? The viewer now notices that the group make a new plan.

What did the viewer miss at first?

Watch the adult model

Name plan, change, choice and result. Show the transition into the small move.

Give a cue too soon in the adult model. Ask which part was hard to follow.

Repeat the same transition after checking readiness, then give one calm cue.

Ask what the viewer saw. Name cue timing as the only planned change and retain the actual reply.

Teacher guidance

What to say

I use the invented cue-keeper example. The scene keeps the same four beats. First I rush the cue into the small move. Then I check the roles and give one calm cue. Ask a real viewer whether the change begins together. Do not script their answer. We can keep or reject a change after the trial.

What good looks like

Adult isolates a transition target and models testing it against real audience understanding.

Fallback

Use two seated hand roles and let a pupil point when the next cue should come.

Check and response

The target is cue timing, not a new plot. Check: Which feature changed, and what stayed the same? Repeat the transition if pupils cannot separate the two.

Choose one target as a group.

Try that part again.

Keep the viewer's real comment.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

Run a baseline before choosing a target. Each pupil can offer an idea by speaking, pointing or directing. Choose one manageable feature: cue, pause, voice or move. If the viewer cannot identify the change and response, simplify those two beats before adding detail.

What good looks like

The group agrees one target from an actual trial and records how learners took part in the choice.

Fallback

Use two options linked to the real comment. Adult models the first beat only, then pupils give the next cue.

Check and response

If the viewer names the smaller space and new small move, refine one feature. If not, return to the four-beat sequence. This is the hinge decision.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use the previous invented scene or the fresh four-beat route. Keep both trials short and comparable. Each learner makes or directs a real choice. Ask one agreed viewer from the class or invited staff. Use the actual response to decide what to keep. If a trial is unfinished, record that honestly.

What good looks like

Each learner has a witnessed contribution, a specific tested target and a pupil-confirmed record of the outcome.

Fallback

Rehearse seated with hands or let the pupil direct staff through the four beats. A private staff-only trial is valid.

Check and response

Example only: I waited for all roles before my cue. The viewer saw the small move begin together. A viewer may still miss the choice; keep that honest result as the next target.

Review

Rehearse the group story

5 minutes

What did the viewer understand?

Which change will we keep?

What still needs a try?

Store the plan and notes for Wednesday.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Separate the maker's aim from what the viewer saw. The group can disagree with a suggestion and give a reason. In the last two minutes, relax, reset the space and keep the current outline with the genuine rehearsal note.

What good looks like

Group chooses a supported next action and every learner can confirm their own record.

Fallback

Read a short real viewer phrase and ask keep, change or try again.

Check and response

A useful decision links the feature to the viewer response. A general claim that it was better needs a detail.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Record today's date, your actual part, the target and the real response. An adult can scribe and read your words back. This rehearsal is progress towards next week's performance; it does not close the award.

What good looks like

Log records individual contribution and support alongside the group trial and keeps future actions separate.

Fallback

Ask one question at a time and confirm the learner's response before recording.

Check and response

Example only: Our target was the choice beat. I added a pause. The viewer saw us think before the new move. Next I will check the end cue.

Safety

Keep a clear personal space. Walk only; no running, lifting, falls, climbing, throws or contact. Props and scenery are imagined. Use an agreed open-hand stop cue. A freeze lasts at most three comfortable seconds, with normal breathing, then relax. Seated and hand-only versions are equal options.
