Rehearse and choose one change

Scheme outcome: Rehearse and refine our performance.

Teacher guidance

What to say

We will rehearse The new den. You can keep or change your role. If the old plan is missing, we make a fresh three-part plan today. Our audience is the class and invited staff only, and you can rehearse with a staff member privately.

What good looks like

Each learner has a safe role and an available plan or a fresh route.

Fallback

Read the three parts aloud: want a den, space too small, choose a small den. Let the pupil point or direct one hand pose.

Check and response

A role may be acting, a seated pose, silent movement or directing. A missing old plan stays missing; date today's new work today.

Ready

Use a small move, a gesture, words or directing. Pause whenever you need.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use your real story from last week if it is here. This three-part den story is our fresh route if needed. Tell me which part you chose. You do not need to tell a story from your life.

What good looks like

Pupils identify their actual contribution and recover a workable order.

Fallback

Offer first, next or last, then show the chosen part seated.

Check and response

The new den is an invented story. The order is want a den, find the space too small, choose a smaller plan.

I do

Rehearse and choose one change

6 minutes

Watch the same story twice.

First, I rush the last cue.

Next, I wait until all are ready.

I change just one thing.

Which cue is clear?

Compare the performance examples

The den with a clear cue

The group point to a wide space. Their hands move close to show a small space. The director waits until all are ready, then points to the word Start. The group show a small den. Each still pose lasts three comfortable seconds.

What does the director check?

The den with a clear end

The group try the same three parts. A watcher cannot see the last pose. The group turn their hands towards the watcher and try again. The watcher sees a small roof. The story order stays the same.

What did the group change?

Watch the adult model

Show wide hands, close hands and a small roof. Say: First, next, last.

Give the last cue before the model is ready. Say: That cue was too soon.

Repeat. Check readiness and give one calm cue. Hold at most three comfortable seconds.

Ask: Which ending could you follow? Keep the watcher's actual reply and name the one change.

Teacher guidance

What to say

In our first adult model, I give the last cue too soon. Then I try the same three parts again. I wait until the performers are ready before the last cue. I keep each freeze to three comfortable seconds and breathe as usual. The watcher says which version they could follow. This is a model, not your evidence.

What good looks like

Adult contrasts two trials with a single change in cue timing and uses a concrete watcher question.

Fallback

Use three hand poses from a chair. Ask the pupil to point to the cue that gives time to get ready.

Check and response

A cue after checking readiness can help the group change together. Check: What did I change? If the pupil names a different feature, repeat only the last transition slowly.

We do

Rehearse and choose one change

6 minutes

Try our three parts once.

A watcher says: I saw ___.

Choose one cue or pose to change.

Try that part again.

Keep it or try once more.

Which is a useful record of your work?

Teacher guidance

What to say

We will do the story once before deciding. The watcher names a part they saw or could not yet see. We pick one change, such as waiting for the cue or turning the last pose. Let each pupil make a real choice. If the order is still unclear, rehearse first, next and last before adding a change.

What good looks like

The group bases one manageable target on a real trial and each pupil contributes or confirms a decision.

Fallback

Adult is the watcher and models only the first part; pupils direct the next cue or choose between two poses.

Check and response

If the watcher can name the first and last parts, test one feature. If not, return to the three-part order. This is the hinge decision; do not pile on several targets.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Use the real plan or the fresh den story. Rehearse once, choose a cue or pose change, then try again. A director can choose when the cue comes; a seated or silent performer can choose a clear hand shape. The watcher is a class member or invited staff member. Keep the real comment, even if the change does not help yet.

What good looks like

Each learner makes or directs an actual rehearsal contribution, tests one change and records a confirmed choice.

Fallback

Use a staff-only seated trial. The pupil directs the adult with a point to the next cue word; record the instruction actually given.

Check and response

Example only: I waited for the group before my cue. The watcher saw us end together. A pupil may instead discover that the cue needs another try; that is useful evidence too.

Review

Rehearse and choose one change

5 minutes

Tell a watcher what you changed.

Watcher: I saw ___.

Choose one thing to keep.

Relax and store your plan.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Ask the maker whether the comment matches their aim. Use the last two minutes to relax, reset chairs and store the plan and feedback for tomorrow. The record should say what actually happened, not that the show is finished.

What good looks like

Learner confirms actual feedback and identifies one feature to keep or revisit.

Fallback

Staff member reads back the pupil's choice and the witnessed response. The pupil can correct either.

Check and response

A useful comment names a cue, pose or clear story part. Good acting alone does not tell the maker what helped.

Teacher guidance

What to say

Date the rehearsal today. I can write your words, read them back and let you change them. Keep the real target for tomorrow. If we did not finish a trial, say that and plan the next step.

What good looks like

Dated log separates the actual role, tested change, response and next step.

Fallback

Offer cue or pose as a choice and scribe the confirmed words.

Check and response

Example only: I tried a slow cue. The watcher saw the last pose. Tomorrow I will check we all wait for the cue.

Safety

Keep a clear personal space. Walk only; no running, lifting, falls, climbing, throws or contact. Props and scenery are imagined. Use an agreed open-hand stop cue. A freeze lasts at most three comfortable seconds, with normal breathing, then relax. Seated and hand-only versions are equal options.
