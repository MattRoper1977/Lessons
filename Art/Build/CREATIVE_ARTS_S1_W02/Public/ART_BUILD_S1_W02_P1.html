A small plan for a feeling

Monday 11 January 2027 · P5 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Create art linked to an emotion (links PSHE).

I can plan a small piece about a feeling.

Today I can

  • I can choose a feeling for a made-up piece.
  • I can try two ways and choose one.
  • I can plan a start and an end.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

emotion

A feeling, such as joy, calm or worry.

plan

An idea for what you will make.

start

The first part.

end

The last part.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

You can point, speak, sign or pass.

Our art can be about a made-up person.

Pick joy, calm or worry. You may choose your own word.

What to say

An emotion is a feeling. Our art can show a feeling in an invented story. A made-up person might wait for a turn, get help or hear good news. Pick one feeling for your piece. You do not need to tell us about your own life. You may work quietly and show me in private.

What good looks like

The pupil chooses or confirms an emotion for an invented piece. The adult accepts a pupil's own word without asking for personal history.

Materials-free fallback

Name two choices slowly. A pupil can point, sign, direct an adult or indicate a choice with an agreed gesture.

Check and response

Joy, calm, worry or another pupil-chosen feeling is valid. An adult may explain the word but does not decide what the pupil must feel.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

A made-up person waits for a turn.

What might they feel? More than one idea can fit.

Which marks or moves could show your idea?

Ready

Watch or direct four repeated moves with steady gaps. The adult controls the pace. No sound or personal story is needed.

What to say

Our made-up person waits for a turn. They might feel calm, glad, unsure or cross. We do not know until the maker tells us. Show one line, quiet tap or silent move that could fit your idea.

What good looks like

The pupil offers an emotion and one possible artistic feature. Different readings of the same fictional situation are accepted.

Materials-free fallback

The adult shows a smooth hand path and a short zigzag path. The pupil chooses one, directs a new path or describes it in words.

Check and response

For example, a still hand could show calm or waiting. A quick zigzag could show worry or excitement. Ask the maker for the intended meaning rather than marking one feeling correct.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch two tests for the same feeling.

Paint: try a curve, then a short zigzag.

Sound: try soft slow taps, then soft quick taps.

Choose one way to work. Plan a start and an end.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Say: My invented piece is about hope. Touch the brush lightly to scrap and pull a short curve. Or trace it in the air.

15 to 30 seconds Try a zigzag beside the curve. Say: I choose the curve because it seems open. These are my two tests.

30 to 45 seconds Show one curve at the start and three spaced curves at the end. For sound, tap four soft slow beats, pause, then repeat. Say: My plan has an order.

45 to 60 seconds Point to the trial and plan. Say: I keep my feeling, choice and reason. Tomorrow I will use this plan to make my piece.

Which feature could fit your own idea?

What to say

My invented piece is about hope while waiting. I touch the brush lightly to scrap paper and pull a short curve. I try a zigzag beside it. I choose the curve because it seems to open out. My small painting will start with one curve and end with three spaced curves. If I choose sound, I test four slow soft taps, then four quicker soft taps. I choose slow taps, a pause, then the same taps again. I keep the volume low. I am planning an order, not just choosing a colour. These are adult examples, not rules for your work.

What good looks like

The adult shows two small tests and links a choice to an intended emotion. The plan states a beginning and an ending. A sound plan names the actions in order, so the pupil can return to it next lesson.

Materials-free fallback

Use an air drawing or silent finger moves. Show the beginning, pause and ending with the hands. A pupil may direct the adult instead of making the movement.

Check and response

Ask: 'Have I chosen a feeling and an order, or only picked a tool?' Expected: a feeling and an order. If unclear, point to the beginning and ending, then ask the pupil to choose what comes first.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Choose one feeling for a class piece.

Try two small versions. Say what changed.

Choose the first part and the last part.

Finish: We chose ___ because ___.

B keeps the feeling, action and order. If a pupil chooses A, model that the same colour could make many different pieces. Parallel check: 'Which reminder helps with sound: quiet, or four soft taps, pause, four taps?' Continue when the pupil identifies the ordered reminder.

Other reasons can be valid. Show the feature that fits your idea.

What to say

You choose the emotion for our invented piece. I will make two versions while keeping the tool or volume the same. Tell me which marks or pace fit your idea. Then direct the start and end. Hinge: Which helps us return to the idea tomorrow: A keep only a colour word, or B keep the feeling, chosen marks or actions, and their order?

What good looks like

Each pupil makes or confirms a choice. Pupils compare a controlled change, such as curve versus zigzag or slow versus quick, and identify a useful ordered plan. The shared class model stays separate from individual evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Pupils direct two silent hand sequences. The adult repeats their chosen start and end, then reads the oral plan back for confirmation.

Check and response

B keeps the feeling, action and order. If a pupil chooses A, model that the same colour could make many different pieces. Parallel check: 'Which reminder helps with sound: quiet, or four soft taps, pause, four taps?' Continue when the pupil identifies the ordered reminder.

Create

Create

18–32 minutes

In your book or arts log, choose a feeling.

Try two small marks, sounds or silent moves.

Choose one way. Make a tiny trial piece.

Plan your start and end for next time.

Keep your tests and say why you chose them.

Supported

Pick a feeling, or point to your own word.

Try two marks or moves with an adult.

Choose one. Make a tiny test piece.

Point to your start and end. Tell the adult your reason.

Independent

Choose a feeling for an invented piece.

Try two ways. Keep both tests.

Make a tiny trial with a start and an end.

Keep your plan and reason in your arts log.

Aspire

Complete your trial and plan.

Try one small change in the last part.

Say how it changes the effect.

Choose the end that best fits your idea.

Choose one route within the same 14 minutes. Every route keeps your own choice and arts-log evidence.

What to say

Choose paint, original sound or a silent route. For paint, use a small amount on the brush tip and test on scrap. Keep paint and water on the mat. Rinse and wipe the brush before a new colour. For sound, use four gentle fingertip taps and a pause, below talking volume. Stop on my raised hand. Change only the mark shape or pace in your two tests so you can notice its effect. Choose the version for your piece and try a tiny ordered version. In the log, keep a small plan or spoken sequence for tomorrow. A pupil may direct an adult's hands and materials.

What good looks like

Two real tests, a small original trial and a plan with a start and end. The pupil names the intended feeling and gives a feature-based reason. The plan is manageable for the next 40 minute lesson. A test may stay unfinished if the log records its true state.

Materials-free fallback

Use a silent hand sequence or an air drawing, or direct the adult. Confirm an ordered plan aloud. The adult records the pupil's actual sequence, emotion and reason at the next available recording opportunity. Art materials and a device are not needed for participation.

Check and response

Example only: a hopeful piece starts with one small curve and ends with three open curves. A quiet sound version begins with four slow taps, pauses, then repeats. A pupil may choose a different pattern and explain it. Do not substitute this adult example for pupil evidence.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Show your trial to an adult or partner.

Say: My piece is about ___.

Say: I chose ___ because ___.

Tidy and keep your plan for next time.

Teacher model only: My piece is about hope. I tried curves and zigzags. I chose curves because they seem open. My piece starts with one curve and ends with three.

Teacher model only: My invented person feels calm while waiting. I chose four soft slow taps, a pause, then the same taps again. I will keep that order.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

The maker explains the emotion. The listener says one thing they notice, such as a gap, a curve or a pause. The listener can read it differently. Ask the maker before touching the work. Use the final two minutes to cap paint, wipe mats and put the work flat to dry. Keep each pupil's own tests and ordered plan together.

What good looks like

A real feature is noticed and the pupil's intention remains their own. Tests, trial and plan are safely stored. Private sharing is equally acceptable.

Materials-free fallback

The pupil directs a repeat of their silent sequence to the adult. The adult describes one observed feature and confirms the intended emotion with the pupil.

Check and response

A useful comment is 'I notice the last curves have more space.' Do not infer a private event or tell the maker what they must feel.

Exit

Exit

37–40 minutes

Arts log: My piece is about ___.

I chose ___ because ___.

First I will ___. At the end I will ___.

Keep your real test or a note of what you did.

My arts log

My piece is about ___.

My two tests were ___.

I chose ___ because ___.

First I will ___.

At the end I will ___.

Type instead

Your typed words stay on this page until you save or close it. Save a local copy before closing.

Keep actual work or a dated witness record with your log. Sound and movement can use a written sequence. A recording device is optional.

What to say

Tell me the feeling, one choice and the order for next time. You can draw, speak, sign or point. I can write your own words, read them back and let you correct them. We keep the actual date and support used. This plan helps tomorrow's making.

What good looks like

A dated arts-log entry links the pupil's emotion, chosen feature, reason and ordered plan to the real trial. A witness note describes what was actually seen or heard.

Materials-free fallback

Confirm the feeling and ordered sequence orally. The adult keeps a short witnessed note when a recording method is available, with the actual date and support.

Check and response

Example only: My piece is about hope. I chose curves because they seem open. First I will make one small curve. At the end I will make three curves.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four books or arts logs, four pencils and eight small scrap pieces for two tests each.

Paint route: four small trial sheets, five brushes including the adult model, four mats, two stable water pots, a small shared amount of washable paint and cloths. Use familiar materials and check access needs.

Sound route: fingertips or soft knee taps only if comfortable. No instrument or recorded sound is needed. Provide a silent option.

Adult model: two tiny tests and a short ordered plan. Keep four labelled trays or safe spaces for individual work and a flat drying space.

Materials-free route: silent hand patterns, air drawing or pupil-directed adult movement. Record an oral plan when paper becomes available.

Teaching approach

Model the chosen technique in small steps. Compare a real first try and a later try. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

The maker must tell a real personal story Use an invented person or an abstract idea. A pupil can pass on public sharing and explain privately.

Choosing a colour is a complete plan Add the mark or action and the beginning and ending. A short spoken sequence is a valid plan.

A feeling has one correct colour or pace Ask the maker for their intention. Accept a different interpretation when it links a feature to a reason.

This lesson contributes to the Explore record. A whole award decision needs the full portfolio. Any optional next level evidence is additional.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. You can watch or use silent movement when you return.

Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You do not need to explain a personal feeling to the group.
