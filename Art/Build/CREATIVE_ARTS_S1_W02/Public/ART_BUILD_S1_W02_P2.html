Making my emotion piece

Tuesday 12 January 2027 · P1 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Create art linked to an emotion (links PSHE).

I can make a small piece linked to a feeling.

Today I can

  • I can use my plan to make a piece.
  • I can show a start and an end.
  • I can say how one choice fits my feeling.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

emotion

A feeling, such as joy, calm or worry.

order

Which part comes first, next and last.

repeat

Do the same part again.

review

Look or listen again and say what you notice.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find your real trial and plan.

Point to the feeling, start and end.

If you were away, make a small plan today.

What to say

Find your own trial and plan from yesterday. Tell or show me the emotion, first part and last part. If a plan is missing or you were away, choose a feeling and make a short trial now. We record today's date and do not invent an earlier test.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies their actual plan or makes an honest short restart. The intended emotion and order are clear enough for a manageable piece.

Materials-free fallback

The adult reads back only a witnessed prior sequence. If no record exists, offer two silent patterns and let the pupil choose and plan one now.

Check and response

A fresh plan is valid. Keep the date and reason for the restart honest. A pupil may change the plan if they can show the new choice.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Watch a start, a pause and an end.

Watch the end first, then the start.

What changed? Which order fits your idea?

Ready

Watch or direct four repeated moves with steady gaps. The adult controls the pace. No sound or personal story is needed.

What to say

I show one small air curve, pause, then three open curves. Now I reverse the order. The same parts can feel different in a new order. Which order might fit hope, calm or another feeling? Explain your own reading.

What good looks like

The pupil notices that the order changed and links a chosen order to an intention. No feeling is treated as a fixed answer.

Materials-free fallback

Use seated finger movements or describe 'one curve, then three curves'. A pupil can direct the order without moving.

Check and response

The parts are the same but their order changed. Growing from one mark to three might suggest hope to one maker; another reading is valid with a reason.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch a plan become a small piece.

Make the start. Leave a space or pause.

Make the end. Check the chosen feeling.

Keep or change one part for a reason.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Show the actual model plan. Say: My piece is about hope. My plan starts with one curve and ends with three.

15 to 30 seconds Make the first curve with a light brush touch or in the air. Leave a gap. Say: I follow my chosen order.

30 to 45 seconds Add the three ending curves. Or perform four soft slow taps, pause, then repeat and stop. Say: My piece has a start and an end.

45 to 60 seconds Point to one chosen feature. Say: I keep the space because it fits my idea. I keep my real work and review together.

Which feature could fit your own idea?

What to say

My plan says hope: one small curve, then three open curves. On the left I make the first curve. I leave space, then make the ending on the right. I look back at the plan. I can keep this ending or change the space if that helps my idea. In sound, I perform four soft slow taps, pause, then repeat, and stop. I keep a simple action plan so the work can be described. This is an adult model.

What good looks like

The adult uses the real model plan to make a coherent small piece with an identifiable beginning and ending. A keep or change decision links to the intended emotion, not only to neatness.

Materials-free fallback

Perform or direct a silent hand sequence with a clear beginning and ending. The adult can describe the ordered actions without needing a recording device.

Check and response

Ask: 'Did my piece use my chosen order?' If unclear, point to the two planned parts and match them to the actual piece before moving on.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Help make one small class piece.

Check its start and end against the plan.

Say one part to keep or change.

The maker makes the final choice.

B. The maker can listen, then keep or change a feature for a reason. If unclear, model two different readings of the same curve. Parallel check: 'Can the maker keep the curve and explain why?' Expected: yes. Return to individual making when the pupil can choose a feature and a reason.

Other reasons can be valid. Show the feature that fits your idea.

What to say

You direct our class piece for a made-up person. I will follow the chosen order. Tell me what you notice before offering a change. Hinge: A listener reads the piece differently. Must the maker A change it at once, or B hear the idea and then choose for their own purpose?

What good looks like

The pupil checks the work against the plan and makes a reasoned keep or change decision. The pupil understands that feedback can inform a choice while the maker keeps control.

Materials-free fallback

Direct the adult through a silent sequence. Use an agreed gesture to choose keep or change. The adult confirms the pupil's reason in their own words.

Check and response

B. The maker can listen, then keep or change a feature for a reason. If unclear, model two different readings of the same curve. Parallel check: 'Can the maker keep the curve and explain why?' Expected: yes. Return to individual making when the pupil can choose a feature and a reason.

Create

Create

18–32 minutes

In your book or arts log, find your plan.

Make your small painting, sound or silent piece.

Give it a clear start and end.

Check one part. Keep or change it and say why.

Keep your work and your own words.

Supported

Find your plan or make a short one now.

Make the first part, then the last part.

You may direct an adult.

Point to a choice. Tell or show why you kept it.

Independent

Use your plan to make a small piece.

Give it a clear start and end.

Check one part. Keep or change it for a reason.

Add your real work and words to the arts log.

Aspire

Complete your small piece and review.

Try the end in one new way.

Which end fits your feeling best? Say why.

Keep both tests and your final choice.

Choose one route within the same 14 minutes. Every route keeps your own choice and arts-log evidence.

What to say

Make a piece small enough for this lesson. Use your planned order or record a new choice. With paint, use a small amount, keep water on the mat, and rinse and wipe the brush before a new colour. Let wet marks dry before painting over them. With sound, use gentle taps below talking volume and stop on my raised hand. You may practise quietly before showing me. Check one feature, such as space, mark shape, pace or pause. Keep it or change it for a reason linked to the emotion. An adult may handle materials under your direction.

What good looks like

An original emotion-linked piece with a deliberate beginning and ending, a pupil-owned choice and a brief review. If unfinished, retain the real stage and next step rather than naming it finished. A sound or movement piece may be brief and still complete for the lesson.

Materials-free fallback

Make or direct a silent hand piece or air drawing with a beginning and ending. The adult records the observed order and the pupil's confirmed emotion, choice and review when a recording method is available. No art materials, instrument or device is required for participation.

Check and response

Example only: the pupil keeps a gap between two parts because it suggests waiting to them. Another pupil may remove a gap to show joy. Both are valid purposeful choices.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Share with an adult or a partner.

Say: My piece is about ___.

Show how one part fits your feeling.

Tidy and keep your piece with your log.

Teacher model only: I made a small painting about hope. It starts with one curve and ends with three open curves. I kept the gap because it seems like waiting.

Teacher model only: My quiet sound piece is about calm. It starts with four slow taps. After a pause I repeat them. I kept the soft pace because it feels still to me.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

The listener describes one feature before asking the maker about the intention. The maker says what they chose and why. You may share privately or pass on a public performance. Use the final two minutes to cap paint, wipe mats and store work flat. Sound and movement pupils keep their sequence plan or dated witness note beside their log.

What good looks like

The pupil links a real feature to the intended emotion and hears a respectful observation. Actual work, plan and review remain together. Interpretation belongs to the maker.

Materials-free fallback

The adult witnesses a silent sequence privately and reads a short description back for the pupil to confirm or correct.

Check and response

Useful review: 'My piece is about calm. I kept the slow pace because it feels still to me.' Avoid judging a pupil's private emotional state from the work.

Type instead

Keep actual work or a dated witness record with your log. Sound and movement can use a written sequence. A recording device is optional.

What to say

Show the actual piece, plan or witnessed sequence that your words refer to. Say what you kept or changed and why. I can scribe your words, read them back and let you correct them. Record the real date, whether the piece is finished or a first version, and the help used. This entry contributes to the ongoing Explore log.

What good looks like

A dated entry identifies the emotion-linked piece and a specific artistic decision. It distinguishes pupil explanation from adult observation and records the true state of the work.

Materials-free fallback

The pupil confirms the feeling, sequence and decision by gesture or speech. The adult makes an accurate dated observation note at the next recording opportunity.

Check and response

Example only: I made a small painting about hope. I kept the open curves because they seem to grow. Next I would like to try longer marks.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four real trials and plans, four books or arts logs and four pencils. Have eight scrap pieces for honest catch-up trials or one changed ending.

Paint route: four small sheets, five brushes including the adult model, four mats, two stable water pots, familiar washable paint and cloths. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Sound route: quiet fingertips or comfortable soft knee taps, no instrument or recording device required. Offer a quiet practice space and a silent route.

Adult model: the actual short plan and a small piece made from it. Keep four individual storage places and a flat drying space.

Materials-free route: silent hand sequence, air drawing or pupil-directed adult performance. Keep a dated observed sequence and the pupil's confirmed words when recording is available.

Teaching approach

Model the chosen technique in small steps. Compare a real first try and a later try. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

A listener's different reading makes the piece wrong The listener describes a feature. The maker explains the intention and decides whether to change anything.

More paint or louder sound makes a stronger piece A small clear choice can work. Keep sound gentle and use a little paint; change pace, space or order instead.

A piece must be finished to keep a log entry Record the actual stage honestly, with a next step. A first version or witnessed silent sequence can show genuine learning.

This lesson contributes to the Explore record. A whole award decision needs the full portfolio. Any optional next level evidence is additional.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. You can watch or use silent movement when you return.
