My skill before and after

Wednesday 13 January 2027 · P5 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Develop a skill and record it in my arts log.

I can practise the same technique and compare my real before and after tests.

Today I can

  • I can keep the same technique and test conditions.
  • I can practise, then keep an honest after test.
  • I can compare a feature and record my next step.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

skill

Something you learn and practise.

technique

A way of using a skill.

repeat

Do the same action again.

gap

The space or time between two things.

steady

Keeping a similar pace.

compare

Look or listen for what stayed the same and what changed.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find your before test and chosen technique.

Keep the same four-part test and cue.

What to say

Find the before test, goal and cue from Monday. We will keep the same technique and a similar size, pace guide and support. If the record is missing, make a fresh dated first test now. Do not invent an earlier result. If access requires a new form, keep the old record and make a new baseline for the new form.

What good looks like

The pupil recognises the chosen technique, test and cue. Any missing record, changed help or new access form is noted honestly before a comparison is planned.

Materials-free fallback

Read back the adult witness note and let the pupil confirm the four-action pattern. If no reliable record exists, observe a fresh baseline today.

Check and response

Compare only matching tasks. A new baseline can still support useful learning today.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Point to one feature in your first test.

Say your goal. Do not change the first record.

What to say

What does the real first test show about gaps or pace? Point to one feature. What was your small goal? We keep the first record exactly as it was so the comparison stays honest.

What good looks like

The pupil describes a specific starting feature without altering or improving the baseline. Staff distinguish the pupil's intention from the observed action.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil confirms one feature from the spoken witness note. Use small gestures or direct the adult to show it.

Check and response

For example: The gap after mark two was smaller. My last action came sooner. Already steady is also a valid starting observation.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch one short practice step.

See the same four-part test before and after.

The after test can stay the same.

Ready

Watch or direct four repeated moves with steady gaps. The adult controls the pace. No sound or personal story is needed.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Show the labelled adult before test. Say: I keep this real starting point and the same four-part task.

15 to 30 seconds Practise one feature slowly. Say: I keep my mark or action and use the same cue.

30 to 45 seconds Show an actual matching adult after test. Say: I compare gaps or pace. I do not need to claim it is better.

45 to 60 seconds Point to a specific feature. Say: This stayed similar. My next step is to practise the final gap. These are adult examples, not your evidence.

Which feature could fit your own idea?

What to say

Here are two real adult model tests using the same mark, size and cue. I compare the gaps, not a new colour or the number of marks. For actions I keep the same four actions and slow cue, then compare their pace. I can say more even, about the same or more varied. I give one feature as my reason.

What good looks like

The adult demonstrates a fair comparison of the chosen feature and an honest outcome. The pupil sees that a changed art form or easier test cannot prove the original technique improved.

Materials-free fallback

Describe two adult four-action examples or perform them silently. Say which feature is being compared. Label adult examples as models.

Check and response

Check: Which comparison is fair? A four marks before and ten after, B quiet taps before and a picture after, or C the same four-part test with similar conditions? C. If unclear, name the one feature and repeat matching examples.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Try the chosen practice cue once.

Ask for a comment about gaps or pace.

Choose one next practice step.

Check your plan

After practice, the result looks similar. What should we do?

Choose, then explain your reason.

What to say

Practise the same technique once. The listener says what they notice about the chosen feature. Hinge: after practice the result looks similar. Should we A pretend it improved, B record what is similar and choose a next step, or C hide the first test? Explain your choice.

What good looks like

The pupil selects B and gives a specific next practice action. Feedback describes gaps or pace and leaves the maker in control. The adult models only the first step when needed.

Materials-free fallback

Use matching silent patterns or pupil-directed adult actions. An adult voices the observed feature and records the pupil's chosen next step.

Check and response

If A or C is chosen, model an unchanged outcome without blame. Parallel check: If the last action still comes early, can the log say that and plan another try? Yes. Continue when the pupil accepts an honest comparison.

Use the same cue. Pause between rounds.

Make the same four-part after test. Keep both tests.

Compare a clear feature in both tests.

Explain how your practice cue helped, or why it did not yet help.

Keep a fair record

Keep each real stage. The last try may look or feel the same.

Use both tests to review your goal. Say what changed or stayed the same. Explain one comment you used or chose not to use. Set a next goal. Say how you will check it.

What to say

Practise your small goal twice, with a calm pause. Then make the after test: the same four marks or actions, a similar scale or pace guide, the same cue and comparable help. Mark the actual date and label it after. Note any different support. Compare the chosen feature, even if it stayed similar or became less controlled.

What good looks like

A real after test follows practice and can be matched to the before record. The pupil identifies a feature, records actual support and gives an honest comparison. Adult help is visible, not presented as independent skill.

Materials-free fallback

Observe the same four silent actions or pupil-directed adult sequence using the retained cue. Keep a dated witness note of before, after, support and the pupil's explanation. No device is needed.

Check and response

If form or support must change for access, explain it in the log. A new-form baseline and later matching test can show progress in that form, not a direct improvement claim over the old-form test.

Optional stretch what to say

Use both tests to review your goal. Say what changed or stayed the same. Explain one comment you used or chose not to use. Set a next goal. Say how you will check it.

Optional stretch what good looks like

The pupil evaluates the chosen technique against their original goal, uses a specific feature as evidence, explains a feedback decision and proposes a fair next check.

Optional stretch materials-free fallback

Adult witnesses the matching live tests and records the same review prompts in the pupil's own confirmed words. No recorded media is required.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Compare your real before and after tests.

What stayed the same? What changed?

Choose one next step and store the records.

Teacher model only: My skill was even gaps in four repeated marks. Before, one gap was small. After, the gaps looked more alike. I used the same mark and cue. Next I will try the technique in a short pattern.

Teacher model only: My skill was a steady four-action pace. Before, the last action came early. After, it still came early. The start stayed steady. Next I will practise just the final gap.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Look at or recall the two matching tests. Say one detail about the gaps or pace. It is fine to say unchanged, or that you need more practice. A partner may give a feature-based observation. Use the final two minutes to tidy, cap wet materials, keep work flat and store both tests with the log.

What good looks like

The pupil gives a reasoned comparison, accepts a truthful result and chooses a manageable next step. Private sharing is enough. The before and after records remain distinct.

Materials-free fallback

Adult reads both precise observations aloud. Pupil confirms, corrects or adds their own view. Keep adult observation separate from pupil voice.

Check and response

Useful: Three gaps look more alike now. The last action still came early. I used more help today. Do not force better as the only valid answer.

Record what the real evidence shows, your view and the support used. You can draw, speak, sign or use a scribe. I will read your words back. Your log shows chosen skill, practice and a before-and-after comparison. The next level task is optional.

What good looks like

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For five pupils: five books or arts logs, five pencils and fifteen small test spaces or sheets for before, practice and after records. Reuse books to avoid extra sheets.

Optional visual route: six drawing tools including the adult model. If using paint, use six brushes, five mats, three stable water pots, a small amount of familiar paint and cloths. Dry marks need no paint.

Quiet or silent route: fingertips or a small comfortable seated action. No instruments or recording device are required.

Adult model: a four-mark line or a four-action pattern, plus a visibly labelled original before and after demonstration. Keep the adult example separate from pupil evidence.

Retain each pupil's chosen technique, baseline and cue for the second period. An adult can make a precise dated observation note when writing materials are unavailable.

Teaching approach

Model the chosen technique in small steps. Compare a real first try and a later try. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

Practice always makes the second version better. Record what actually happened. It may improve, stay similar or need more practice. A clear observation is useful evidence.

More marks or faster taps show more skill. The chosen target is control. Keep four marks or actions in the test and focus on the gaps or pace.

A fair before and after comparison can use completely different tasks. Use the same chosen technique and four-part test. Keep similar conditions and note help. If access requires a new form, keep the old record and make a fresh baseline for that form.

A polished adult example can replace a pupil's first try. Keep the pupil's real baseline, attempt and words. Label all adult samples as teacher models.

This lesson contributes to the Explore record. A whole award decision needs the full portfolio. Any optional next level evidence is additional.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. You can watch or use silent movement when you return.

Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You do not need to explain a personal feeling to the group.
