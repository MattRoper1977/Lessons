Two stars and one small wish

Monday 1 February 2027 · P5 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Review and improve my piece.

I can find two useful parts and choose one small change.

Today I can

  • I can use a real piece of my own work.
  • I can give two stars with clear reasons.
  • I can choose and test one small wish.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Choose your own real work from weeks 2 to 4.

It can be paint, a print, sound or a silent piece.

You can share with just an adult.

What to say

Find one piece you actually made, or the real note of your sound or silent sequence. Choose something from weeks two to four that you can review today. We look at choices in the work, not at how good a person is. There are no scores, rankings or competitions. A made-up feeling or abstract idea is enough. If work is missing, make a tiny honest fresh piece now and date it today before reviewing it.

What good looks like

The pupil chooses actual own work or makes a small dated fresh piece. The adult identifies the true medium and makes private sharing available without asking for personal disclosure.

Materials-free fallback

Use a real witnessed movement note, or let the pupil direct a short silent sequence now. State honestly whether this is an earlier piece or a fresh one made today.

Check and response

A fresh piece is a valid honest starting point. It must not be labelled as missing work from an earlier week.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Look at the eye, two stars and tool pictures.

The stars are useful parts in your work.

The wish is one small change you can make.

What to say

Read each picture label aloud. The eye reminds us to look or listen. Star one and star two each name a different feature that helps the maker's idea. They are observations, not points or prizes. The tool reminds us to choose a wish we can act on, then check it. A star needs more than 'nice'. A wish needs more than 'make it better'.

What good looks like

The pupil can identify the purpose of the picture prompts and distinguish a specific feature from a score or vague praise.

Materials-free fallback

Read the four labels and model them through a short silent sequence. The pupil may point to a picture, choose a spoken label or direct the adult.

Check and response

Useful star: 'The slow ending helps my calm idea.' Useful wish: 'Add a longer pause before the end.' Vague praise or a general judgement about the pupil is not a feature-based review.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch the adult review a real class model.

Star 1 names one useful part. Star 2 names another.

The wish names one small action.

Watch a test on spare paper or in a short rehearsal.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Show a real adult example. Say: I notice this work before I change it. My first star is one feature and why it helps.

15 to 30 seconds Point to a second different feature. Say: My second star has its own reason. These are observations, not points or prizes.

30 to 45 seconds Choose one small wish. Try it on spare paper or in a short silent rehearsal while keeping the original state. Say: I can actually do this action.

45 to 60 seconds Compare the real test with the start. Say: I record what happened. I keep the original and choose one action for next time.

My own work review

Use your real current piece or actual movement. These picture prompts do not judge your work for you.

Choose a picture prompt. Look, listen or try your own work, then give your own response.

What to say

Use an actual adult example, not a pupil's piece. For a calm sound model, star one is the gentle volume because it suits the idea; star two is the slow ending because it feels still to me. My wish is to add a longer pause before that ending. I perform the original, then rehearse the changed pause and note what really happened. For visual art, I might value a clear curve and an open space, then test wider spacing on a spare version. I keep the original state and label the test as a test. The model is not pupil evidence.

What good looks like

Two different features are linked to the maker's intention, followed by one achievable action and a real small test. Before and test states use the same art form and remain honestly identified.

Materials-free fallback

Actually perform a short silent curve-and-pause sequence. Name two observed features, then try a longer pause in a second performance. The pupil can direct the adult's actions and compare them.

Check and response

Ask: 'Is my wish a small action I can do, or only a word like better?' Expected: a small action. If unclear, compare 'make it better' with 'add a pause before the end'.

Other reasons can be valid. Show the feature that fits your idea.

What to say

Use the picture prompts in order. Ask what the maker intended, then accept two feature-based stars. Let pupils choose one small, feasible wish. Hinge: Which wish can we act on today: A 'be the best', or B 'make the pause before the ending longer'? We will test the action and record the true effect. Keep our class review separate from pupils' own evidence.

What good looks like

The pupil selects B, gives or confirms two distinct observed features, and directs a real test of one achievable change. No pupil is ranked against another.

Materials-free fallback

Use a short adult silent sequence and the same four spoken or pictured prompts. The pupil directs one changed pause or size while keeping the rest of the sequence the same.

Check and response

B is specific and manageable. If A is chosen, model one small action and ask a parallel check: 'Which can I do: be perfect, or move these two marks farther apart on a spare test?' Continue when the pupil identifies a real action.

Review and make one improvement

Use your current work. Make a real small improvement and keep an honest before-and-after record. These controls do not complete the task or fill your log.

What to say

Start with what the piece is intended to show. Use the pictures and words to name two different helpful features, such as a clear mark and a gap, or a gentle volume and a pause. Choose one achievable wish linked to that intention. Keep the actual original state before testing. For a drawing or painting, use spare paper or a small copy for the test. For a dry fold print, do not force a change onto the original: make a small new trial on fresh paper with changed spacing, following the safe fold-print steps. For sound or movement, perform the real original sequence, then rehearse the one chosen change. Compare like forms. If the old form cannot be accessed, make a tiny fresh piece in the accessible form and review that honestly today. An adult may handle materials under the pupil's direction.

What good looks like

A review of actual own work has two feature-based stars, one doable wish and a real small test. The original remains available as the before state. The test has the same form as the work being reviewed; a new form is labelled as a fresh piece, not a false before-and-after improvement.

Materials-free fallback

The pupil performs or directs a short silent sequence, uses two spoken or pictured stars, chooses one wish and actually rehearses that change. The adult records the real actions, pupil reasons and support when a recording method is available.

Check and response

Example only: Star one is a clear curve because it fits calm. Star two is the gap because it gives the curve space. My wish is a wider final gap. I test it on spare paper and say what changed. The pupil may choose a different justified feature.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Tell an adult your two stars and one wish.

Show what your real test changed.

Choose the one action for next time.

Tidy and keep the original and test together.

Teacher model only: Star one is a clear curve because it fits calm. Star two is an open gap because it gives the curve space. My wish is to make the final gap wider. I test that on spare paper.

Teacher model only: I review a silent sequence. Star one is a slow curve. Star two is a clear stop. My wish is a longer pause before the stop. I actually rehearse that pause and record what happened.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Listen to the maker's reasons without diagnosing their feelings. Ask what the test actually changed and whether the action is still suitable for the intended piece. A test may need another small adjustment; keep that truth visible. No improvement is invented. Use the final two minutes to put tools away, leave wet trials flat and keep each original, test and log together. Sharing with just an adult is enough.

What good looks like

The pupil links the two stars and chosen wish to actual features, reports the real test result and selects a feasible next action. Work and records are safely stored.

Materials-free fallback

An adult witnesses the before and test silent sequences and reads a short comparison back. The pupil confirms the action to use next time or chooses a smaller one.

Check and response

A useful review names a feature and its effect, such as a longer pause making the ending clearer to the maker. A test that did not help is recorded honestly and leads to a smaller achievable wish.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four chosen real pieces from weeks two to four, or accurate sound/movement records; four books or arts logs and four pencils. Keep original work and any chosen trial together in four labelled spaces.

Provide the four picture-and-word prompts for each pupil, or display/read them aloud. The meaning must not depend on colour alone. Adult models are separate from pupil work.

Visual tests: eight small spare sheets, familiar drawing tools and materials actually needed by the chosen piece. Paint uses a small amount of familiar washable non-toxic paint, five brushes, four mats, two stable water pots, cloths and drying space if needed.

Fold-print tests use fresh small paper, three pea-sized dabs on one half, a clear fold/edge border, gentle pressing and flat drying. Do not force wet paint or glue onto a dry original print.

Sound or silent review needs a quiet seated space, a clear stop cue and actual sequence notes if available. Keep sound below talking volume. No recording device is required; pupils may direct an adult.

Teaching approach

Model a review of your own example. Notice a real feature, choose a small improvement, make it, then check the actual result. Pupils use their own current work in its actual medium. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

Stars are scores or a contest between pupils Each star names a helpful feature in the maker's own work. There are no ratings, rankings or comparisons of personal worth.

A wish can just say make it better Choose one small action linked to the intention, such as widening a gap or adding a pause. Try the action and record its real effect.

A dry fold print must be painted over to improve it Keep the original. A new version on fresh paper can test a changed spacing. Retain both and label them as original and new trial.

Missing earlier work can be described as if it is here Make a tiny honest fresh piece today, label the actual date and review it. Never invent work or observations from an earlier week.
