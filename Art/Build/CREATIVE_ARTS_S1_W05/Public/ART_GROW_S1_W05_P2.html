One real revision and its effect

Wednesday 3 February 2027 · P5 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Review and improve my work.

I can make one chosen change and record what it actually did.

Today I can

  • I can use my real review and choose one revision.
  • I can make that change and keep a true before and after record.
  • I can explain the actual effect, support and next step.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

review

Look or listen again and say what you notice.

intention

What you want your work to suggest.

feature

A part you can notice in the work.

feedback

A comment about the work that may help a choice.

revise

Make a change for a reason.

effect

What a feature seems to do.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find your real piece, review and test.

Name the one change you chose.

What to say

Recover the real plan, current work, review and test from Monday. State the one feature you intend to revise. If records are missing, use a new small dated starting point and an honest self-review now. Do not invent an earlier piece, comment, test or photo.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies an actual starting state and a chosen manageable revision. Missing earlier evidence remains visible and any catch-up work is dated today.

Materials-free fallback

Read back the actual live-sequence note. Pupil confirms it or directs a new short starting sequence if needed.

Check and response

Use what really exists. A new current starting point supports today's revision without proving an earlier history.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Show what the work is meant to suggest.

How could your one change help that idea?

What to say

Look at the intention from the real plan or today's honest catch-up plan. Which feature will change? How could the change affect the resilience idea? Keep the aim small enough to do today. A change of access may be needed, but it is not proof of artistic improvement.

What good looks like

The pupil names one achievable revision and a possible intended effect. They keep the same medium where possible or establish a fresh baseline if access requires another form.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil states, signs or directs the intended change to a quiet or silent sequence. Adult records the real starting actions before the revision.

Check and response

A clear aim might be make one turn easier to see, shorten a pause or replace one original word. The reason should relate to the maker's intention.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch one real change being made.

Compare the actual before and after feature.

The result may help or need another try.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Point to the actual adult starting feature and review. Say: I choose one line to revise.

15 to 30 seconds Make the real small change. Say: I have changed the line, not just planned a change.

30 to 45 seconds Compare both actual records. Say what the change did, including an unhelpful effect if that is what happened.

45 to 60 seconds Record the actual result, support, feedback route and next step. Say: This is an adult model. Your log must show your own real revision.

What to say

Here is my actual starting feature. I shorten this line to make the turn easier to see. That is a real revision, not just a promise to change. I compare the before note and the actual new line. I explain whether the effect helped. If it did not help, I retain the true test and choose another next step. For a silent piece I change one pause and perform both actual versions. These are adult models.

What good looks like

The adult makes one concrete revision and compares a real before state with its actual result. No better outcome is assumed. A reversed unsuccessful trial remains labelled as a real attempted change, not hidden.

Materials-free fallback

Perform or direct two actual quiet or silent versions with one revised feature. Adult notes the precise before and after actions, effect and decision.

Check and response

Check: Which shows revision? A saying I might change later, B changing one actual feature and recording its effect, or C copying an adult's finished example. B. If unclear, model the difference between planning and doing one change.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Use one real review comment or self-review.

Direct one change in a class example.

Say what actually changed.

One private peer comment is optional. Both agree and either may stop.

Check this choice

A real revision did not help the intended effect. What should the maker do?

Choose, then explain your reason.

What to say

Use the class example's intention and one real feature observation to direct a small change. This is still adult model work, not your evidence. Optional peer feedback follows the same consent rule: both agree, one chosen part, one private comment and either may stop. Hinge: a real revision did not help the intended effect. Should we A pretend it improved, B hide the test, or C keep the true test and plan a next step? Explain. If a peer comment was already given on Monday, use that actual comment and do not ask for a second. Only if none has been used may the one optional comment be offered today.

What good looks like

The pupil chooses C and describes the actual attempted change without inventing success. Self or staff review remains enough. Any optional peer comment is consensual, private and feature-based.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil directs an adult to change a quiet or silent example. Use self-review or a staff observation, then read the actual effect back for confirmation.

Check and response

If A or B is chosen, model an honest unsuccessful trial. Parallel check: If a longer pause feels less clear, can the maker record that and try a smaller change next? Yes. Continue when a truthful result and next step are accepted.

What to say

Make the chosen change now in the actual piece or sequence. Retain a genuine before note or observed starting version and the real revised result. You may direct adult handling. Compare the changed feature with your intention and explain the effect. If the test is unhelpful, keep its record and choose a next step; you may restore a previous feature for a reason, but the actual attempted revision remains visible. Do not treat a different medium alone as an improvement.

What good looks like

One real purposeful revision is made and logged. The before and after states are actual, support is recorded and the pupil gives a reasoned account of the effect. The work can remain unfinished. A planned or merely retained feature alone does not replace the revision action.

Materials-free fallback

Perform or direct the planned quiet or silent sequence, change one action, order detail, pause or original phrase, and perform or direct the revised version. Adult witnesses both actual versions and scribes the pupil's view and support.

Check and response

A revision can help, stay similar or reveal another need. The action and real effect are kept even when a later version restores the earlier feature. Never recreate an old photo and label it as earlier evidence.

Optional stretch what to say

Use both real records to judge your revision. Link the effect to your intention. Explain how a comment or self-review shaped your choice. Say what helped or did not help. Set a next step.

Optional stretch what good looks like

The pupil evaluates a concrete revision using actual evidence, distinguishes intention from observed effect and explains a reasoned response to feedback or self-review.

Optional stretch materials-free fallback

Adult witnesses both actual versions and retains the pupil's confirmed evaluation and support in precise notes. No recorded media or peer comment is required.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Show the actual changed feature and both real records.

Did the change help your intention? Say why.

Keep the real result, support and next step.

Teacher model only: Before, the line hid the turn. I shortened the line. After, the turn looked clearer to me. I used self-review, and no peer comment was used. Next I will check the spacing.

Teacher model only: I made a real pause change, but the restart felt rushed. I kept the test note and restored the earlier pause for now. My actual revision attempt is recorded. Next I will test a smaller change.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Describe the chosen revision and a feature that supports your view. Say what helped or did not help. Keep the actual current version and truthful state, finished or still being made. Record self, staff or the one agreed private peer comment as actually used; state if peer feedback was declined, unavailable, stopped or not used. Use the final two minutes to tidy and store the work and records together.

What good looks like

The pupil evaluates an actual revision against intention, records support and gives a manageable next step without automatic praise or completion claims. Optional feedback remains private and accurately labelled.

Materials-free fallback

Adult reads the real before and after sequence notes back. Pupil confirms, corrects or adds their own view. Private oral review is sufficient.

Check and response

Useful: I shortened the pause. The restart felt clearer to me. Or: The new line did not help, so I kept its test record and will try a different gap next.

Teacher guidance

Teacher only private feedback choice

The peer option is off by default. Ask the pupil privately. Ask a potential peer privately too. A pupil can decline or withdraw without a public explanation.

Current feedback rules

Default: Off. Self-review or a staff comment is a complete route.

Consent: Both the maker and the peer agree before one chosen part is shown privately.

Limit: At most one private peer comment across the two periods. Reuse a real earlier comment instead of asking for another.

Withdraw: Either person may stop. Close the chosen work and use self or staff review.

Comment: One feature and its possible effect. No marks, rankings or judgement of the person.

Honest log: Record used, not used, declined, unavailable or stopped as true. Never invent a comment.

Private route. The peer option is off. No peer agreement or comment is recorded by this control.

Use a private self review or ask for one specific staff observation if the pupil wants it. Log the route and actual words honestly. Do not label a staff or self comment as peer feedback.

If no peer comment was used earlier, ask whether both pupils want one private comment. Reuse a real earlier comment instead of asking for another. Agree one work feature to discuss. Keep the work off public display. Stop and return to private self or staff review if either pupil declines or withdraws.

Only an actual, freely given comment may be logged. Opening this prompt does not establish consent, a completed comment or an improvement.

Preparation

For five pupils: five actual selected plans, five actual pieces or precise live-sequence records, five arts logs and five pencils. Keep the Week 4 process evidence with each pupil's own work.

Prepare only resources needed for the small chosen revisions. A dry visual route might use five small test spaces and ten colour tools shared in pairs. Retain before and after notes without covering the original record.

If the actual medium needs paint: six familiar brushes, five mats, three stable water pots, small amounts of familiar non-toxic paint and cloths. Keep wet work flat and away from electrical items. No new tool is compulsory.

If the piece uses paper parts: up to ten prepared scraps and three glue sticks for small tests. Use torn or prepared parts if cutting is a barrier, following local procedures.

For original sound, words or silent movement: a quiet seated space, the actual sequence or text note, and a start and stop cue. No recording device, instrument or copied text is required.

Optional private peer route: one short feedback prompt per pair, with an adult checking consent on both sides. A peer, public share or recorded media is not required for the lesson.

Adult model: one original piece or sequence, its short plan, a real starting-state note and one actual feature revision. All samples remain labelled teacher models.

Teaching approach

Model a review of your own example. Notice a real feature, choose a small improvement, make it, then check the actual result. Pupils use their own current work in its actual medium. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

A comment tells the maker what they must do. The maker considers a feature-based comment and chooses a change for their intention. Feedback is advice, not an instruction to please someone.

Everyone must show work to a peer. Peer feedback is optional. Both people agree, one chosen part is shown privately, and either can stop. A staff comment or self-review completes the core task.

A revision must make the result better immediately. Make a purposeful change and record its actual effect. An unsuccessful test is retained honestly with a next step. Do not claim improvement without evidence.

A missing old image or comment can be made up later. Use the real current work and notes. Make a new dated starting point if needed. State when a peer comment was not used, declined or unavailable.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. You can watch or use silent movement when you return.
