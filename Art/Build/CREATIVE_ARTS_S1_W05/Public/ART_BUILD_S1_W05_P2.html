Making one real improvement

Weekly outcome: Review and improve my piece.

I can make one real change and say how it helps my piece.

Today I can

  • I can find the real work and my chosen wish.
  • I can make one real change in the same art form.
  • I can keep the before and after states and a reason.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find your real piece, two stars and one wish.

Look at your actual test from last time.

Choose the one change you will make today.

What to say

Use the actual piece and review from yesterday. Ask the maker to point to the two helpful features and the chosen wish. Check that the wish is one small action, not a score or vague judgement. If the work or review is missing, make a tiny fresh piece and a short honest review now, dated today. Do not invent missing earlier states. Keep a before state before changing anything.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies actual own work, a feasible wish and an honest before state, or creates a dated fresh starting piece that can be revised in the same lesson.

Materials-free fallback

Read the witnessed silent sequence and review, or let the pupil direct a short fresh sequence now. Observe and note that starting state before the chosen change.

Check and response

A fresh small piece and one real revision today are valid. The record must distinguish that from work recovered from an earlier week.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Which wish names an action?

Make it perfect, or add a pause before the end?

How will you know what changed?

What to say

A useful wish tells us what to do. Compare a vague judgement with adding one pause or widening one gap. Ask how we will retain the before state and observe the after state. The two versions should use the same art form so the comparison is fair. A new art form is a fresh response, not a false revision of a different medium.

What good looks like

The pupil selects a specific action and identifies a real before-and-after comparison in the same form.

Materials-free fallback

Perform a short silent sequence, then ask the pupil to direct one longer pause. Name the original and revised actions aloud and keep a precise witness note when recording is available.

Check and response

Adding a pause is an action. Before and after can be two pieces, a sketch and actual changed piece, two work-only images or two precisely witnessed same-form sequences. A device is optional.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Show the actual adult before state and chosen wish. Say: I keep this real starting point before I change one part.

15 to 30 seconds Actually make one revised mark, fresh-print version or changed silent sequence. Say: I make my wish into an action, not just a sentence.

30 to 45 seconds Place the two actual same-form states together, or perform both witnessed versions. Say: This is the one feature that changed.

45 to 60 seconds Give a reason and honest effect. Say: I keep both states and name what really happened. A new fold print is labelled as a revised version.

My own work review

Use your real current piece or actual movement. These picture prompts do not judge your work for you.

Choose a picture prompt. Look, listen or try your own work, then give your own response.

What to say

Show a real adult before version and state its two feature-based stars. My one wish is a longer pause before the ending of my calm silent sequence. I perform the original, then actually make the revised sequence with that pause. I compare like with like and say the pause is clearer to me. For a fold print, I keep the original dry print and make a new version on fresh paper with wider spacing, using three small dabs on one half, a clear border, gentle pressing and flat drying. I label this a revised version, not a hidden alteration of the old print. I only claim an effect that the real results show.

What good looks like

The adult makes one actual chosen revision and retains both real states or precise observations. The reason links a feature to the intention, with no automatic claim that every change is better.

Materials-free fallback

Actually perform or direct the original silent sequence and the revised sequence. Keep all actions the same except the one chosen pause, size or spacing. The pupil can direct the adult without moving.

Check and response

Ask: 'Did I only write my wish, or did I make the changed sequence?' Expected: made the changed sequence. If unclear, show the before and after pause lengths again and name the observed difference.

Review and make one improvement

Use your current work. Make a real small improvement and keep an honest before-and-after record. These controls do not complete the task or fill your log.

What to say

Choose a manageable revision linked to the wish. For a painting or drawing, first keep a small sketch, work-only image or other accurate record of the before state, then make the one safe chosen change. For a dry fold print, keep the first print intact and make a new version on fresh paper with the chosen spacing or colour change. Use three pea-sized dabs on one half, a clear fold-and-edge border, gentle pressing and flat drying. Never force paint, glue or rubbing onto a dry original. For sound or silent movement, perform or direct the actual before sequence, then make the same sequence with the one intended change. Compare like forms. If the original medium is inaccessible, make a tiny fresh baseline in the accessible medium, date it today and actually revise that. Review the real effect; if a test did not help, select a smaller feasible action and carry it out rather than inventing improvement. Keep sound below talking volume and use the stop cue. Adult handling follows the pupil's direction.

What good looks like

One real intentional change is made to actual own work or a clearly labelled same-form revised version. Before and after states or precise witness records exist, with a pupil-owned reason. The record says what changed and whether it helped the intended effect, without inventing success or hiding an unchanged result.

Materials-free fallback

The pupil performs or verbally directs a short silent baseline and a revised sequence with one real changed pause, pace, size or order. The adult witnesses both and records the actual difference, intended effect, pupil reason and support when a recording method is available.

Check and response

Example only: I kept my first silent sequence. I made the pause longer in the revised one. I can show the difference, and the longer pause helps my calm idea. A physical fold-print revision keeps both actual prints. A changed medium is labelled as fresh work before revising it.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Show the real before and after states.

Say: I changed ___ to help ___.

Point to what actually changed.

Tidy and keep both states with your log.

Teacher model only: I kept the original silent sequence. I made its final pause longer. I chose that action to help calm. The after version has a pause I can notice more clearly.

Teacher model only: I kept my dry first fold print. I made a revised print on fresh paper with wider spacing. I kept both real prints and described the actual difference. I did not paint over or hide the first version.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Ask the maker to identify the actual changed feature and the intended effect. The maker may say more practice is needed; retain that truth. If there is no real change yet, help choose one small accessible action and actually try it now. Do not replace evidence with praise or a planned sentence. Keep private sharing available. Use the final two minutes to put tools away, leave wet work flat and retain both states or precise same-form witness records with the log.

What good looks like

The pupil demonstrates or confirms one actual revised feature, explains a reason and reviews its real effect. Before and after evidence is retained, and no pupil is ranked or pressured to disclose a private story.

Materials-free fallback

An adult witnesses a repeat of the original and revised silent sequence if needed, reads the difference back and lets the pupil correct the record.

Check and response

A useful review says what changed and why it may help the intended idea. It can honestly note that further practice is needed. The work, not a general adult judgement, supports the statement.

What to say

Date the actual revision. Record the original state, one change made, the pupil's reason, the real after state and a next step. Keep the actual work or a precise witness comparison. Mark a new fold print as a revised version and retain the first version. Scribe exact words if useful, read them back and allow correction. State the true medium and support; no improvement is invented and no planned change is counted as made.

What good looks like

A dated arts-log entry shows a real before-and-after revision in the same form, with a specific reason and honest evaluation. It records pupil choices separately from adult observation.

Materials-free fallback

Take an oral or gestured log after witnessing both actual silent versions. The adult retains precise action notes and the pupil's confirmed reason when recording is available.

Check and response

Example only: Before, my sequence ended straight after the curve. I added a long pause. I chose it to help calm. After, I noticed the ending felt clearer to me. Next I will practise the same pause again.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four actual chosen pieces or accurate witnessed sequences, four two-stars-and-one-wish reviews and tests, four books or arts logs and four pencils. Have four labelled spaces to retain both real states.

Provide the four picture-and-word prompts, or display and read them aloud. Prepare one actual adult before version and a real same-form revision for the demonstration.

Use only the resources needed for each agreed wish. Visual routes may need eight small spare sheets and familiar drawing tools. Paint uses a small amount of familiar washable non-toxic paint, five brushes, four mats, two stable water pots, cloths and flat drying space.

Fold-print revisions use fresh paper and the safe four-step method. Keep dry originals intact. Prepare four new sheets plus four spares if needed; use three small dabs, clear borders and gentle pressure.

Sound or silent revision needs a quiet seated space and a clear stop cue. A pupil may direct an adult. A recording device is optional; precise witnessed before-and-after action notes are enough. Keep sound below talking volume.

Teaching approach

Model a review of your own example. Notice a real feature, choose a small improvement, make it, then check the actual result. Pupils use their own current work in its actual medium. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

Writing a wish means the work has improved A planned sentence is not a made change. Carry out one small action and keep the real before and after states or precise witness notes.

An old dry print must be physically altered A new version on fresh paper can be the revision. Retain the original, label the new version and compare the two actual prints.

Every change must be called better Record the observed effect honestly. If a change does not help, select and try a smaller achievable action. Do not invent a successful result.

A new medium can be compared as if it were the same piece Compare like forms. If access requires a new medium, create a fresh dated baseline in that form and then make a real revision to it.
