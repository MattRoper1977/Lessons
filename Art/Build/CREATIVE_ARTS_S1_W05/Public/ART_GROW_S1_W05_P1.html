Review and choose one change

Monday 1 February 2027 · P6 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: Review and improve my work.

I can review my real work and test one change for a reason.

Today I can

  • I can review my intention, features and real progress.
  • I can choose one small change and test it.
  • I can record the actual result and my next step.

In your book or arts log. You can point, speak, sign, draw or write. An adult can record your confirmed words.

Theme: Resilience & Change. You can use an invented idea, work quietly and share privately.

review

Look or listen again and say what you notice.

intention

What you want your work to suggest.

feature

A part you can notice in the work.

feedback

A comment about the work that may help a choice.

revise

Make a change for a reason.

effect

What a feature seems to do.

Arrival

Arrival

0–3 minutes

Find your real plan, piece and process record.

Use a quiet or private route if helpful.

What to say

Recover the real plan and work or precise live-sequence record. We review what actually exists. If the plan or piece is missing, agree a small honest plan and make a simple new starting piece or sequence now, dated today. It becomes today's starting point, not invented earlier work.

What good looks like

The pupil identifies their actual work, intention and starting condition. A new catch-up record is labelled with today's date and source. No old image or decision is fabricated.

Materials-free fallback

Read back the real sequence note or let the pupil direct a short current sequence. If no record exists, witness a small new starting sequence today.

Check and response

A real new starting point is valid. It cannot be labelled as evidence of an earlier stage.

Starter

Starter

3–6 minutes

Review your intention and one feature.

Name the form or tools, what is made and the help used.

What to say

Use five short review prompts. What did you want the work to suggest about resilience? Which feature helps that idea? What form or materials did you use? What is actually made so far? What help did you use? You can say that a part is unfinished.

What good looks like

The pupil gives or confirms a structured self-review grounded in the plan and actual piece. Staff distinguish intended meaning from observed features and note support honestly.

Materials-free fallback

Ask one prompt at a time through speech, sign or gesture. Adult reads a precise current-state note back for pupil confirmation.

Check and response

A specific feature might be a turn, repeated mark, pause or original phrase. The maker explains the intention; a neatness judgement is not the review.

I do

I do

6–12 minutes

Watch an adult review a real example.

See one small change being tested.

A test can help, stay similar or need another try.

One minute teacher demonstration

0 to 15 seconds Show the actual adult plan and piece. Say: I wanted a new route. I notice the turn is hard to see.

15 to 30 seconds Name the actual form, state and help. Say: I choose one small turn change, not a whole new design.

30 to 45 seconds Make a real small test and compare it. Say: This is what changed. I will describe the actual effect.

45 to 60 seconds Record the next revision and true feedback route. Say: I used self-review. No peer comment was used. This is an adult model.

What to say

My plan says the path should show finding another route. I notice the turn is hard to see. I used dry marks, and the small piece is made. My one test is to make the turn clearer on a small test area. I compare it with the real starting feature and say what changed. I do not claim better unless I can explain the effect. For a quiet sequence I could test one pause. These are adult models.

What good looks like

The adult uses intention, feature, form/materials, progress and help, then tests one achievable revision. The real starting point and actual effect are distinct. Pupils see that feedback and a test support a choice rather than force praise.

Materials-free fallback

Perform or direct two quiet or silent versions differing in one feature. Adult describes the actual change and effect. A device or art materials are not needed.

Check and response

Check: Should the maker change everything at once? No. A small change makes its effect easier to judge. If unclear, compare changing one pause with changing the whole sequence.

We do

We do

12–18 minutes

Use self-review or ask for one comment.

A private peer comment needs both people to agree.

Choose one change for your own intention.

You or the peer may stop. Self or staff review is enough.

Check this choice

Peer feedback is not used. Which route still completes the core task?

Choose, then explain your reason.

What to say

First review the work yourself. A staff comment can help. If you want one private peer comment, choose one part to show and ask the peer too. Either person can stop. The peer says one feature they notice and its possible effect, with no marks or rankings. Hinge: which route is valid? A force a peer to comment, B use self-review or a staff comment if peer feedback is not used, or C invent a peer comment. Explain. Use at most one peer comment across both periods; retain this actual comment for the second lesson if used.

What good looks like

The pupil chooses B and retains ownership of the change. If peer feedback is used, both agree, only one chosen part is shown privately and one feature-based comment is retained. If not used, the actual route is labelled without penalty.

Materials-free fallback

Use oral self-review or a private staff comment on the actual observed sequence. Record peer comment not used, declined, unavailable or stopped, as true.

Check and response

If A or C is chosen, state that feedback is optional and must be real. Parallel check: If either person says stop, do we stop and use staff or self-review? Yes. Continue when the pupil recognises a valid alternative.

What to say

Choose one change that could help your intention and can be done with available tools and access. Test only that feature. Keep the real current-state note and actual test, then say whether the effect helped, stayed similar or needs another try. You may direct an adult. Keep the same form when possible. If access requires a new form, make a new dated starting point in that form before the test.

What good looks like

One achievable revision is tested and retained with a reason and actual effect. The structured review leads to a clear next making step. No improvement is assumed and an access change alone is not presented as artistic progress.

Materials-free fallback

Perform or direct a planned quiet or silent sequence, then revise one pause, order detail or action. Adult witnesses both actual versions and retains precise notes and the pupil's reason.

Check and response

A test may make a turn clearer, lengthen a pause or change one original phrase. The pupil's actual form and intention determine the change. A real unsuccessful test remains useful evidence.

Optional stretch what to say

Use your review to explain one strength and one need. Link your test to the intended effect. Say how a real comment or your own review shaped the choice. Plan one next revision.

Optional stretch what good looks like

The pupil weighs actual evidence against intention, explains a feedback or self-review decision and proposes a specific feasible revision. Peer feedback is not required.

Optional stretch materials-free fallback

Adult witnesses the real versions and records the same review in exact confirmed pupil words, clearly naming the actual feedback route.

Review

Review

32–37 minutes

Compare the real feature and test.

What did the change do? What will you try next?

Keep the comment route and your decision honest.

Teacher model only: My plan meant to show another route. I notice the turn is hard to see. I tested a shorter line. The turn looked clearer to me. I used self-review and no peer comment. Next I will revise that line in the piece.

Teacher model only: My quiet sequence has a restart. I tested a longer pause, but it felt too slow to me. I will try a smaller pause change next. I keep the actual test, even though it did not help.

Teacher examples only. Keep your own work, words and choices as evidence.

What to say

Use one feature as evidence for your view. Say what changed, what helped or did not help, and the small revision you intend to make in the second period. Record self, staff or one private peer comment as the route actually used. A peer route can be declined or stopped. Use the final two minutes to tidy and store the actual work, test and log.

What good looks like

The pupil links a real test to the intention and chooses a manageable next revision. The feedback route is accurate and no person judgement or public display is introduced.

Materials-free fallback

Adult reads both observed versions and the pupil's decision back. A private oral review is enough. Retain a precise note rather than inventing media.

Check and response

Useful: The longer pause made the restart clearer to me. Or: The wider turn did not help, so I will try a shorter line. Both are honest test reviews.

Teacher guidance

Teacher only private feedback choice

The peer option is off by default. Ask the pupil privately. Ask a potential peer privately too. A pupil can decline or withdraw without a public explanation.

Current feedback rules

Default: Off. Self-review or a staff comment is a complete route.

Consent: Both the maker and the peer agree before one chosen part is shown privately.

Limit: At most one private peer comment across the two periods. Reuse a real earlier comment instead of asking for another.

Withdraw: Either person may stop. Close the chosen work and use self or staff review.

Comment: One feature and its possible effect. No marks, rankings or judgement of the person.

Honest log: Record used, not used, declined, unavailable or stopped as true. Never invent a comment.

Private route. The peer option is off. No peer agreement or comment is recorded by this control.

Use a private self review or ask for one specific staff observation if the pupil wants it. Log the route and actual words honestly. Do not label a staff or self comment as peer feedback.

If no peer comment was used earlier, ask whether both pupils want one private comment. Reuse a real earlier comment instead of asking for another. Agree one work feature to discuss. Keep the work off public display. Stop and return to private self or staff review if either pupil declines or withdraws.

Only an actual, freely given comment may be logged. Opening this prompt does not establish consent, a completed comment or an improvement.

Preparation

For five pupils: five actual selected plans, five actual pieces or precise live-sequence records, five arts logs and five pencils. Keep the Week 4 process evidence with each pupil's own work.

Prepare only resources needed for the small chosen revisions. A dry visual route might use five small test spaces and ten colour tools shared in pairs. Retain before and after notes without covering the original record.

If the actual medium needs paint: six familiar brushes, five mats, three stable water pots, small amounts of familiar non-toxic paint and cloths. Keep wet work flat and away from electrical items. No new tool is compulsory.

If the piece uses paper parts: up to ten prepared scraps and three glue sticks for small tests. Use torn or prepared parts if cutting is a barrier, following local procedures.

For original sound, words or silent movement: a quiet seated space, the actual sequence or text note, and a start and stop cue. No recording device, instrument or copied text is required.

Optional private peer route: one short feedback prompt per pair, with an adult checking consent on both sides. A peer, public share or recorded media is not required for the lesson.

Adult model: one original piece or sequence, its short plan, a real starting-state note and one actual feature revision. All samples remain labelled teacher models.

Teaching approach

Model a review of your own example. Notice a real feature, choose a small improvement, make it, then check the actual result. Pupils use their own current work in its actual medium. Keep each pupil's own choice and reason. You do not need to paint or perform expertly. Judge intention and explanation. Use the least help needed and record the support actually used. Scribe the pupil's own words, read them back and allow correction. Plan in any language.

Sound stays below talking volume. Use a raised open hand to stop. Offer the silent option before sound, seated movement and a private audience. No personal disclosure is required. Use familiar non-toxic materials and local procedures. Keep wet materials away from electrical items.

Prompt ladder

  1. Point to the instruction or place.
  2. Ask a short question.
  3. Rephrase the instruction.
  4. Provide a scaffold or choice.
  5. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Common misconceptions

A comment tells the maker what they must do. The maker considers a feature-based comment and chooses a change for their intention. Feedback is advice, not an instruction to please someone.

Everyone must show work to a peer. Peer feedback is optional. Both people agree, one chosen part is shown privately, and either can stop. A staff comment or self-review completes the core task.

A revision must make the result better immediately. Make a purposeful change and record its actual effect. An unsuccessful test is retained honestly with a next step. Do not claim improvement without evidence.

A missing old image or comment can be made up later. Use the real current work and notes. Make a new dated starting point if needed. State when a peer comment was not used, declined or unavailable.
