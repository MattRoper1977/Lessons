Arrival

A choice and a new start

0–3 minutes

Find your chosen Week 3 plan.

Say what your piece will show.

Choose a quiet way to work and share.

What to say

Retrieve the annotated plan, tool quantities, access notes and intended effect. Pupils may use an invented seed, a path or their own abstract idea. If the plan is missing, use the brief catch-up in the cover plan. Do not claim it was made last week.

What good looks like

Pupil identifies their actual plan and a first making step.

Materials-free fallback

Read back the retained oral sequence. If absent, the pupil chooses a simple new sequence and confirms it.

Check and response

Resilience may involve help, rest or a different route. It does not mean ignoring discomfort.

Starter

A feature to notice

3–6 minutes

Check your tools, space and first step.

You have 14 minutes to make today.

What small part can you make in that time?

What to say

Check real supplies against the Week 3 plan. Its draft time split was provisional. Today the shared taught structure gives fourteen minutes of making, with a further fourteen next lesson. Scale the first section to fit; note this change in the log. Preserve the maker intention.

What good looks like

Pupil sets a manageable first section and confirms actual resources.

Materials-free fallback

Choose a short silent sequence that can be made and repeated within fourteen minutes.

Check and response

A smaller piece can still express the whole idea. Record a changed time or resource choice honestly.

I do

The method in small steps

6–12 minutes

Look back at the plan before each step.

Make a small first section.

Check the feature that shows your idea.

Record one decision as you work.

Step by step demonstration
  1. Point to the adult plan: start, block, turn and open space. State the intended idea.
  2. Make the first line and block or show the first two planned actions.
  3. Check the turn. Leave a clearer gap or pause and say why.
  4. Record the actual decision and the unfinished next step. Keep the plan with the work.
Original examples to compare

Plan and first section

Original class models, not pupil evidence.

Teacher example only: I made the start and turn. I left a gap because it makes the new route clear. The ending still needs making.

Teacher example only: I changed a loud sound idea to silent movement. I kept the pause and turn because they show a new way forward.

What to say

Use an original adult plan: a line meets a block and turns towards open space. Point to the start, block and new route on the plan. On paper make the first line and block, then test the turn. My line almost touches the block, so I leave a small gap to make the new route clear. I note: I left a gap because I wanted the turn to show. For silent movement, start a forward hand path, stop, turn sideways and continue. Explain which version is actually being made.

What good looks like

Adult shows plan-to-making links and a real practical decision. The adult sample is never used as pupil evidence.

Materials-free fallback

Model the same sequence silently with one hand. Pupil can direct position, pause or route without moving.

Check and response

CfU: Which note records a decision: I worked hard, or I left a gap to show the turn? The second. Model a specific feature if the answer is vague.

We do

A shared choice

12–18 minutes

Help check a first section against a plan.

What matches the intended idea?

Choose one small change if needed.

Say: I chose ___ because ___.

Check together

A material on the plan is unavailable. What should the maker do?

What to say

Invite each pupil to identify one feature. Hinge: a planned material is unavailable. A: pretend it was used; B: choose an available method, keep the idea and record the change; C: copy another pupil piece.

What good looks like

Pupils select B and explain how an available method can keep the intention.

Materials-free fallback

Offer silent movement or pupil-directed adult actions and ask which part still expresses the planned idea.

Check and response

B. If unsure, model an unavailable paint colour replaced with pencil marks. Parallel check: a larger sheet is unavailable; can a smaller sheet work if the layout is adjusted and logged? Yes. Start making once each pupil identifies an achievable next step.

Your own making

Use your Week 3 plan to make a first section.

Keep your chosen idea visible or clear in the sequence.

Independent

Make the planned first section.

Aspire

Complete the core making task.

Test two small versions of one feature.

Choose the version that fits your intention.

Keep the comparison and your reason.

Optional next level stretch

Use your Week 2 skill goal while making. Compare two small versions of a feature. Ask for one useful comment. Explain which version fits your intention and why. Note a time, tool or access limit.

Keep the actual tests or witness descriptions, skill goal, feedback, pupil decision and reason.

Your Explore work is complete without this task. Retain additional evidence for a later level review; this task does not complete a higher award.

What to say

Use the pupil chosen art form. Visual route: follow the labelled layout using familiar drawing tools, paper shapes or approved paint. Sound route: use the planned short original pattern below talking volume, stop on a raised hand. Silent route: use the planned order of actions, with comfortable movement. In the fourteen minutes, use about two to set up, nine to make and three to check and note a decision; adapt to access needs. Make a first version rather than expanding the task. Staff scribe only confirmed words.

What good looks like

A genuine first section or whole short first version follows the plan. The pupil records one decision, any change and the actual remaining work.

Materials-free fallback

Perform or direct the planned silent sequence. Adult records exact actions, order, pauses, support and confirmed intention. No media capture is needed.

Check and response

Example only: I changed to a small sheet. I left more space round the turn to show another way forward. The final section is still to do.

Review

A check and a reason

32–37 minutes

Compare your work with the plan.

Ask for one useful observation.

Decide what you will keep or change next.

Store the work safely.

What to say

A listener names a feature, then asks if it fits the maker idea. The maker can accept or decline a suggestion with a reason. Use the final two minutes for caps, water, scraps and safe storage. Keep the work with the plan; note any wet areas or loose parts.

What good looks like

Pupil states what is actually made and one manageable next step.

Materials-free fallback

Repeat the actual sequence once, then read the witness description back for correction.

Check and response

A useful comment is: I notice a pause before the turn. The maker decides whether that helps their intention.

Exit

Your arts log

37–40 minutes

Arts log: I used my plan to make ___.

I decided ___ because ___.

I changed ___ from my plan because ___.

Next lesson I need to ___.

Type instead

Your responses save on this device. They are not sent anywhere.

What to say

Record the date, actual work, decision, plan change or no change, support and next making step. If the work is incomplete, keep that clear. Preserve the pupil first version or a precise witnessed description.

What good looks like

Dated record shows an actual making process rather than repeating last week planning entry.

Materials-free fallback

Use oral responses read back to the pupil, then retain a dated witness note.

Check and response

A pupil may say I kept the plan because the new route was clear. Do not force a change that they do not need.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For up to five pupils: five Week 3 plans, arts logs and actual Week 2 skill records; five pencils and small workspaces.

Check each plan materials list in advance. Drawing fallback: five sheets plus scrap and five familiar drawing tools. Paper route: clean scrap and shared glue sticks. Use familiar materials and local procedures.

For paint only if planned and approved: mats, stable water pots, brushes, a small amount of familiar washable paint and cloths. Keep wet work away from electrical equipment.

Sound needs no instrument: quiet fingertips and a stop signal. Silent movement or directed adult actions need no materials. Offer seated, quiet and private options.

Set aside five safe storage places for unfinished work with its plan and actual log. No phone or media recording is required.

Catch up

If prior work is missing, offer a brief honest restart: choose an invented idea and a small achievable form, state a first step and needed tools or no-materials route, then make a first version. Date it today and label it a new start. Do not claim missing earlier evidence.

Access

Give one step at a time, point to the current instruction and model the first step only. Keep the plan or test visible. Check understanding by asking the pupil to show the next step.

Allow pointing, sign, speech, gesture or an adult scribe. Give processing time. Read scribed words back for confirmation. Use I chose ... because ... .

Use invented or abstract ideas. Offer a quiet pause, private audience and a clear return cue. No personal disclosure, forced touch or public performance.

Offer seated silent movement, larger comfortable actions or pupil-directed adult making. Allow looking without touch. Keep sounds below talking volume.

Plan in any language. Model the key words with objects or gestures; accept a drawing or oral sequence before fuller sentences.

Prompt ladder

Point; short question; rephrase; scaffold or choice; model the first step only, then return the task.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You may watch or use silent movement when you return.
