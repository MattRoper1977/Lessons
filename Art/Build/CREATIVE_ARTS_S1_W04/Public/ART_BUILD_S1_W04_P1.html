Arrival

A choice and a new start

0–3 minutes

You can point, speak, sign or watch first.

Pick a made-up feeling for your art.

Today we will try texture rubbing.

What to say

Show a safe piece of ridged card and a smooth card. We will put paper on top and rub a crayon across it. The bumps can make marks show through. This is a new way of making marks for our lessons. You may watch, direct me or use the silent option.

What good looks like

Pupil chooses an invented feeling or confirms one. No personal story is needed.

Materials-free fallback

Offer two silent hand textures: a smooth glide or small uneven stops. Pupil can direct the adult.

Check and response

Any chosen feeling is valid. A texture has no fixed emotional meaning.

Starter

A feature to notice

3–6 minutes

Look at a smooth surface and one with ridges.

Which has bumps?

You may look without touching.

What to say

Show the two surfaces. Ask permission before offering touch; do not place a pupil hand on an object. Describe flat and ridged. Ask how a rough-looking mark might suggest an invented feeling.

What good looks like

Pupil notices an actual surface feature before suggesting a feeling.

Materials-free fallback

Adult makes a smooth hand path and a path with small stops; pupil identifies the difference.

Check and response

Ridges are raised lines. Either texture can express calm or energy if the maker gives a reason.

I do

The method in small steps

6–12 minutes

Put safe card on the table.

Lay thin paper on top. Hold its edge.

Use the side of a crayon. Rub lightly.

Lift the paper. Look at the marks.

Step by step demonstration
  1. Put ridged card on the table. Lay thin paper over it. Hold the paper edge.
  2. Place the crayon on its side. Make four light back-and-forth sweeps.
  3. Lift the paper. Notice the ridges. Make a small second test over smooth card.
  4. Compare the actual marks. Choose a test and explain a possible feeling.
Original examples to compare

Two texture tests

Original class models, not pupil evidence.

Teacher example only: the ridged marks suggest energy to me. Another maker may read them as calm.

Teacher example only: I used a smooth glide and stop-start hand movement. I chose the stops for surprise. This is movement evidence, not a rubbing.

What to say

1. Put a clean, dry, flat piece of ridged card on a clear table. 2. Lay a small sheet of thin paper on it. Hold the paper at the edge, outside the rubbing area. 3. Lay a broad wax crayon on its side. Move it gently back and forth for four short sweeps. 4. Lift the paper and show the pattern. Make a second test on smoother card with the same crayon. If little shows, check paper contact, use thinner paper or try a little more pressure. Stop before paper tears. Heavy pressure can hide the pattern. Keep the two real tests.

What good looks like

Adult demonstrates each step slowly and compares the actual prints, even if one is faint. This is rubbing over a surface, different from drawing each line or moving loose shapes.

Materials-free fallback

Model a new texture translation: one continuous hand glide, then four small stop-start moves. Hold each end pose for a slow count of two. Pupil chooses the movement texture. Call this silent movement, not a completed rubbing.

Check and response

CfU: Is the textured card above or below the paper? Below. If unsure, lift the paper and reset it together, then ask again.

We do

A shared choice

12–18 minutes

Help the adult make two tests.

Which marks can you see?

Choose a test for a made-up feeling.

Say: I chose ___ because ___.

Check together

The paper starts to tear. What should the maker do?

What to say

Pupils direct where the paper and crayon go. Compare the ridged and smoother tests. Ask each pupil for one choice. Hinge: the paper is tearing. A: press harder; B: stop and try lighter pressure. Show hands or point.

What good looks like

Each pupil makes a choice and identifies a feature. They choose B for the tearing paper.

Materials-free fallback

Compare the smooth glide and stop-start motion. Pupil directs a lighter or smaller version if the movement is uncomfortable.

Check and response

B. If A is chosen, show a gentle sweep on fresh scrap. Parallel check: to protect the next sheet, press hard or lightly? Lightly. Proceed when the pupil can choose a gentle action or direct one.

Your own making

Independent

Make two texture tests with the same crayon.

Aspire

Complete the same two tests.

Try a lighter sweep on a small spare area.

Say what changes in the marks.

Choose a version for a reason.

What to say

Give each pupil two small sheets and two safe flat surfaces, or let them take turns at a stable shared tray. Repeat the four rubbing steps. Keep the colour the same while testing textures so the texture difference is clearer. The pupil chooses the preferred test. Adult help may hold paper or move the crayon under the pupil direction. Do not rub on skin, walls, fittings or sharp objects. Use familiar materials and the setting procedures.

What good looks like

Two actual tests with a pupil-selected texture and a reason, or two witnessed silent movement textures with the same choice and reason. Completion does not require a tidy or dark rubbing.

Materials-free fallback

Make a smooth glide and a stop-start hand path, with a still end pose. Choose one texture for the invented feeling. Pupil may direct the adult or use eye-pointing. Staff record the actual movement method, not rubbing.

Check and response

Example only: I chose ridges because the broken marks feel busy to me. A different reading is valid. Record what was actually tried.

Review

A check and a reason

32–37 minutes

Show the two tests to an adult.

Point to the texture you chose.

Say what you notice.

Tidy and keep the tests for next time.

What to say

Share privately if preferred. Ask the pupil what the texture suggests to them. The listener says I notice, then checks the maker meaning. In the last two minutes collect crayons, stack clean card, collect scraps and keep tests in each pupil tray.

What good looks like

The choice stays with the pupil; both tests are stored and no private feeling is inferred.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil repeats or directs the two motions. Adult notes their sequence and chosen version for tomorrow.

Check and response

A useful observation names repeated ridges, faint patches or smooth movement rather than judging the maker.

Exit

Your arts log

37–40 minutes

Arts log: I tried ___.

My two tests were ___.

I chose ___ because ___.

Next time I will ___.

Type instead

Your responses save on this device. They are not sent anywhere.

What to say

Date the real work. Scribe the pupil own words if requested, then read them back. Note the actual help and method. Keep the tests for the next lesson. If only a rehearsal happened, record rehearsal honestly.

What good looks like

Dated entry retains technique, two trials, choice, reason and support actually used.

Materials-free fallback

Pupil gives or confirms an oral log. Staff record it at the next opportunity with the true activity date.

Check and response

Example only: I tried texture rubbing. I chose the ridged test because it feels full of energy.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For four pupils: four books or arts logs, eight small sheets of thin paper, four broad wax crayons and four clear table spaces.

Provide four clean pieces of ridged card and four smooth pieces of card, with safe edges. Shared trays can be used in pairs. No cutting tools, wet paint or adhesive are needed.

Prepare two small real rubbings for demonstration, four storage trays and spare paper. Check that the surfaces give a visible mark before teaching.

No-materials route: seated or standing silent hand movement. No device, body contact or personal story is required.

Catch up

If prior work is missing, offer a brief honest restart: choose an invented idea and a small achievable form, state a first step and needed tools or no-materials route, then make a first version. Date it today and label it a new start. Do not claim missing earlier evidence.

Access

Give one step at a time, point to the current instruction and model the first step only. Keep the plan or test visible. Check understanding by asking the pupil to show the next step.

Allow pointing, sign, speech, gesture or an adult scribe. Give processing time. Read scribed words back for confirmation. Use I chose ... because ... .

Use invented or abstract ideas. Offer a quiet pause, private audience and a clear return cue. No personal disclosure, forced touch or public performance.

Offer seated silent movement, larger comfortable actions or pupil-directed adult making. Allow looking without touch. Keep sounds below talking volume.

Plan in any language. Model the key words with objects or gestures; accept a drawing or oral sequence before fuller sentences.

Prompt ladder

Point; short question; rephrase; scaffold or choice; model the first step only, then return the task.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You may watch or use silent movement when you return.
