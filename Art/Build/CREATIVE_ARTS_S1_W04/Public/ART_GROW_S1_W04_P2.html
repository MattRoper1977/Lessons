Arrival

A choice and a new start

0–3 minutes

Find your plan, first version and log.

Read the next step you chose.

Check what you can finish today.

What to say

Reconnect the actual first version with Monday log. Let the pupil confirm or revise the next step. If absent, use the catch-up route and keep the reduced scope clear. Do not fabricate work from Monday.

What good looks like

Pupil identifies existing work and a manageable continuation.

Materials-free fallback

Read back the actual action sequence and next step, then pupil confirms or corrects it.

Check and response

A short piece can be enough. Record any unfinished work honestly.

Starter

A feature to notice

3–6 minutes

Show the part that suggests resilience.

What still needs making or checking?

Choose one target for today.

What to say

Ask the pupil to name a visible or performed feature and its intended effect. Choose one completion target within the fourteen-minute making time. It can be completing an ending, clarifying a turn or making a sequence repeatable.

What good looks like

The target is specific and achievable without changing the maker intention.

Materials-free fallback

Select one part of the silent sequence to complete or make clearer.

Check and response

Resilience can be shown as getting help, pausing or finding another way. Avoid personal disclosure prompts.

I do

The method in small steps

6–12 minutes

Look at the real first version.

Complete one planned part.

Check the effect. Try one small change.

Keep the version that fits your idea.

Step by step demonstration
  1. Show Monday first version beside the plan and read the real next step.
  2. Complete the next section or final actions.
  3. Try one small change in the gap or pause. Compare both real versions.
  4. Choose a version, explain why and record whether the actual piece is complete.

What to say

Use the adult first section from Monday. Complete the new route into open space. Check whether the turn is clear. Try a longer gap on scrap or pause in the sequence; compare with the first version. I keep the short pause because it feels like a brief check before moving. I record the change from my plan and my reason. For visual work, keep the trial beside the piece; for movement, keep a precise witnessed sequence.

What good looks like

Adult completes a part, compares an actual change and records the maker decision. A changed choice is not automatically an improvement.

Materials-free fallback

Perform the original short pause and a longer pause in the same hand sequence. Choose one and explain its effect.

Check and response

CfU: Does a longer pause always improve the piece? No. It depends on the intended effect. Ask which version fits the maker idea.

We do

A shared choice

12–18 minutes

Compare the first version with a small change.

What effect does the change have?

The maker chooses what to keep.

Say what is really finished.

Check together

Only the start is made. What should the log say?

What to say

Compare the two adult versions. Hinge: only the start of a piece is made. A: record finished; B: record what exists and the next step. Ask every pupil to choose, with private pointing allowed.

What good looks like

Pupil selects B and gives an accurate completion statement.

Materials-free fallback

Use two live silent versions and identify exactly which parts were performed.

Check and response

B. If A, show the incomplete ending and model an honest record. Parallel check: a witness saw only the first two actions; may the record claim all four? No. Proceed when each pupil can state an actual next action.

Your Explore work is complete without this task. Extra evidence can inform a later level review; it does not certify a higher award.

What to say

Use about two minutes to retrieve and set up, nine to complete or develop the target, and three to check and record. Adapt to access needs. The pupil can use visual art, original quiet sound or silent movement as planned. Stop sound on a raised hand. Keep wet work stable. Avoid a wholesale restart unless the pupil can explain why and accept a smaller result. If the piece is already complete, use the time to refine one feature or rehearse a repeatable sequence, recording the real decision.

What good looks like

Pupil develops an actual piece and explains at least one decision, including a justified decision to keep a feature. The true completion stage is recorded.

Materials-free fallback

Develop or direct a short silent sequence. Staff witness the final order, pauses, changes, help and pupil meaning. No recorded media is required.

Check and response

Example only: I completed the turn and ending. I tried a longer pause but kept the short pause because the route still feels steady.

Review

A check and a reason

32–37 minutes

Show a chosen part privately or to a partner.

Ask: What do you notice?

Check it against your intended idea.

Tidy and store the piece with your plan.

What to say

Invite a respectful observation rather than praise alone. The maker states whether the observed feature matches the intention. They may keep the piece without further change. In the final two minutes tidy materials and store work safely; keep trials and logs.

What good looks like

Pupil links actual features to intention, and stores evidence without claiming a later share or encounter.

Materials-free fallback

Adult repeats the final sequence and reads back the witness description for correction.

Check and response

The audience can read the piece differently. The maker explanation and actual features are both worth keeping.

Exit

37–40 minutes

Arts log: My piece shows ___ through ___.

Type instead

Your responses save on this device. They are not sent anywhere.

What to say

Retain dated actual work or the final witnessed sequence, plan, decision and changes. Record support. Pupils can draw, dictate or confirm by gesture. Keep optional stretch distinct from core Explore evidence. This is making and review, not a claim that the full award is complete.

What good looks like

The entry states the final current form, features, decisions, real completion stage and next step.

Materials-free fallback

Confirm an oral entry and record the exact witnessed version and pupil words at the next opportunity.

Check and response

Example only: My silent piece shows another way forward through a pause and turn. It is complete as a short sequence; I would practise the ending next.

Teacher guidance

Preparation

For up to five pupils: five real first versions with their Week 3 plans and Monday logs, five pencils and small workspaces. Retrieve the optional Week 2 skill records if using stretch.

Check each plan materials list in advance. Drawing fallback: five sheets plus scrap and five familiar drawing tools. Paper route: clean scrap and shared glue sticks. Use familiar materials and local procedures.

For paint only if planned and approved: mats, stable water pots, brushes, a small amount of familiar washable paint and cloths. Keep wet work away from electrical equipment.

Sound needs no instrument: quiet fingertips and a stop signal. Silent movement or directed adult actions need no materials. Offer seated, quiet and private options.

Set aside five safe storage places for unfinished work with its plan and actual log. No phone or media recording is required.

Catch up

If prior work is missing, offer a brief honest restart: choose an invented idea and a small achievable form, state a first step and needed tools or no-materials route, then make a first version. Date it today and label it a new start. Do not claim missing earlier evidence.

Access

Give one step at a time, point to the current instruction and model the first step only. Keep the plan or test visible. Check understanding by asking the pupil to show the next step.

Allow pointing, sign, speech, gesture or an adult scribe. Give processing time. Read scribed words back for confirmation. Use I chose ... because ... .

Use invented or abstract ideas. Offer a quiet pause, private audience and a clear return cue. No personal disclosure, forced touch or public performance.

Offer seated silent movement, larger comfortable actions or pupil-directed adult making. Allow looking without touch. Keep sounds below talking volume.

Plan in any language. Model the key words with objects or gestures; accept a drawing or oral sequence before fuller sentences.

Prompt ladder

Point; short question; rephrase; scaffold or choice; model the first step only, then return the task.

A calm pause

Put tools down. Choose a quiet position. Ask an adult for the agreed quiet space or return cue. You may watch or use silent movement when you return.
