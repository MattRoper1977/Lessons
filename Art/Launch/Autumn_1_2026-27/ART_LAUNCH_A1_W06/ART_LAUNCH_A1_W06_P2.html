Stage 6 · Independent studio practice (You do) · SUMMIT Pick your route: Supported · Independent · Aspire

Choose your studio route and create

Supported route: partner pilot trial, one board upgrade, and mounted peer tile

  1. Use your 3-tile board to guide your partner through making one sample tile.
  2. Swap roles and make a tile at your partner's station.
  3. Add one bold tip sticker to your Workshop board to make the hardest step clearer.
  4. Glue the tile your partner made onto your Pilot log sheet and tick what you improved.
Success check: Partner pilot completed, one upgrade added to board, and peer tile mounted on log.

Independent route: timed two-way pilot, bottleneck diagnosis, and kit adaptation

  1. Facilitate an 8-minute live pilot of your workshop with a peer, testing your safety briefing, 3-stage exemplars, and time plan.
  2. Participate in your partner's pilot and exchange structured diagnostic feedback on pacing and clarity.
  3. Modify your Workshop leadership Board and participant prep kit to solve one identified bottleneck.
  4. Mount the peer-made pilot tile onto your Pilot adaptation Log with a three-part reflection (Observation -> Bottleneck -> Adaptation).
Success check: Timed pilot delivered, board/kit adapted, and peer tile mounted with 3-part reflection.

Aspire route: stress-tested pilot, differentiated cue upgrade, and facilitation critique

  1. Lead your live pilot while tracking exact split-times across Step 1, Step 2, and Step 3.
  2. Refine both your physical prep kit and your visual cue system to eliminate dead time and support two confidence levels.
  3. Mount the peer pilot outcome alongside a critical evaluation of how live iteration improved your instructional design.
Success check: Split-timed pilot, upgraded dual-level cues, mounted peer tile, and critical evaluation.

Key words and definitions (SUMMIT)

  • pilot test: A live trial run of a workshop with a small group used to spot and fix problems before the main event.
  • bottleneck: A step where participants get stuck, wait for a tool, or take longer than planned.
  • iteration: Testing a plan, observing what happens, and making a targeted improvement.
  • pacing: Managing the time spent on each step so participants finish a resolved piece before time runs out.
  • visual cue: A bold arrow, colour code, or stage sample that clarifies an instruction instantly.

  • visual cue: A bold arrow, colour code, or stage sample that clarifies an instruction instantly.
