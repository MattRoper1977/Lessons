Visual cues to spot

Examine the Three-Box visual Analysis layout on screen (Zero biography padding!).

Stage 6 · Independent studio practice (You do) · SUMMIT Pick your route: Supported · Independent · Aspire

Choose your studio route and create

Supported route: 3-tone map, 6 cm close-up study, and symbol adaptation

  1. Shade the pre-outlined Box 1 Tonal map using dark, mid, and light pencil tones to match the print.
  2. Place your viewfinder on the print and copy the layered marks inside Box 2.
  3. In box 3, draw your own personal symbol using the same dark/mid/light layout and mark style.
  4. Add three pointer notes explaining the layer order.
Success check: 3-tone map, viewfinder close-up study, personal adaptation, and three pointer notes completed.

Independent route: three-part visual dissection and personal synthesis

  1. Execute a 3-value compositional Tonal map in Box 1 and annotate how contrast directs the viewer's eye.
  2. Complete a 6 cm multi-layered Process transcription in Box 2, numbering the exact sequence of media layers.
  3. Design an original Personal adaptation in Box 3 applying the practitioner's tonal structure and mark recipe to your identity theme.
  4. Write an analytical synthesis comparing the practitioner's visual intent with your own.
Success check: Tonal map, numbered process transcription, original adaptation, and analytical synthesis.

Aspire route: comparative dual-lens analysis and resolved mixed-media maquette

  1. Deconstruct the exemplar through both a 5-value Tonal map and a directional Vector diagram in Box 1.
  2. Execute a high-precision mixed-media transcription in Box 2 testing wet-over-dry resistance.
  3. Develop a resolved mixed-media adaptation in Box 3 with full critical commentary on how the practitioner's methodology elevates your portfolio.
Success check: Tonal/vector maps, mixed-media transcription, resolved adaptation, and critical commentary.

Key words and definitions (SUMMIT)

  • visual analysis: Examining how an artwork is constructed visually (tone, composition, mark-making) rather than copying biographical facts.
  • tonal map: A simplified 3-to-5 value thumbnail that reveals where the artist placed darks, mid-tones, and lights.
  • transcription: A focused study of a small section made to reverse-engineer the layer order and tool marks.
  • intent: The meaning, mood, or visual impact the practitioner aimed to communicate.
  • adaptation: Taking a structural or technical principle from a study and re-inventing it for your own subject.
