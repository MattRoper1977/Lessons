Opening

Art · LAUNCH · Autumn 2 · Week 1

A review with evidence

Learning goal

I can support an arts review with evidence and recommend it to an audience.

Today I can

  • I can describe a feature I can check.
  • I can explain and qualify my judgement.
  • I can recommend a work to an audience, with a reason.

Must-know words: feature, evidence, interpretation, audience

feature — a particular part of a work that you can identify.

evidence — a detail or reliable record that supports a statement.

interpretation — a meaning or effect you draw from the work.

audience — the people who view, hear or experience the work.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write.

You may pass.

I · S · C

Independent

ART-LAUNCH-A2-W01 — Week 1 — CLASSROOM PRACTICE

1. Choose Flow or Crossing and record its title.

2. Describe two features you can point to.

3. Explain how those features support your judgement of the work.

4. Add another possible interpretation or a limit to your review.

5. Recommend the work to a particular audience. Give a reason linked to the work.

6. Check one claim against the image. Share it with your chosen listener and confirm or correct what they heard.

Help: Feature → effect → judgement. 'Another viewer might ___. I recommend it to ___ because ___. My evidence is limited to ___.'

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

I · S · C

Supported

ART-LAUNCH-A2-W01 — Week 1 — CLASSROOM PRACTICE

1. Choose Flow or Crossing. Point to its title.

2. With the image visible, choose two feature cues that fit.

Flow cues: curved bands / small orange circle / dark straight strips.

Crossing cues: overlapping diagonal strips / cropped white circle / three curved blue bands.

3. Choose or give an effect. Link it to one of your features.

Effect words you may use: calm / movement / tension / structure. You can choose a different effect.

4. Complete: 'I think ___ because I can see ___. Another viewer might ___. '

5. Choose an audience: viewers who enjoy abstract images, or viewers seeking a detailed lifelike portrait. Give one reason for your recommendation.

6. Show one claim to your listener. Check what they heard and make one change if it helps.

Help: Show one choice at a time. The adult may reread a cue or scribe your words; you make the decisions.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

I · S · C

Choice

ART-LAUNCH-A2-W01 — Week 1 — CLASSROOM PRACTICE

1. Choose one work: Flow or Crossing.

2. Choose a feature that fits it: curved blue bands / overlapping dark diagonal strips. Point to it.

3. Choose an effect or give your own: calm / movement / tension / structure. Say, show or select why the feature creates that effect for you.

4. Choose the careful claim: 'This is my reading' / 'Every viewer must agree'.

5. Recommend it to viewers who enjoy abstract images or viewers seeking a lifelike portrait. Add one reason based on what the work shows.

6. Ask your listener to name back your feature and judgement. Confirm or correct the account.

Help: 'I chose ___ because ___. My feature is ___. It suggests ___ to me. I recommend it to ___ because ___. '

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

I · S · C

Aspire

ART-LAUNCH-A2-W01 — Week 1 — CLASSROOM PRACTICE

1. Compare Flow and Crossing, identifying one precise feature from each.

2. Explain which work more strongly supports your chosen judgement, using both features as evidence.

3. Evaluate a competing interpretation of one work. Explain what its evidence supports and where your view differs.

4. State what further context could change your judgement. Do not invent a maker's intention.

5. Recommend one work, or the comparison, to a clearly defined audience. Justify the fit.

6. Check a claim with your chosen listener. Confirm, qualify or revise it after they name back your meaning.

Help: 'Both readings account for ___. This evidence supports ___ more strongly because ___. I would need ___ before claiming ___. '

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

TA Brief

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Current stage

1. Prepare the exact two constructed artwork cues, category cards and sentence cards. Keep practice and genuine event records separate; this lesson makes no Unit 1 completion claim.

2. Read one prompt at a time. Offer point, speech, sign, symbols, drawing or writing, private sharing and pass. Never select an unwilling pupil to perform or read aloud.

3. Separate feature, interpretation and evidence about intention. Accept a different supported view; do not reward certainty or agreement with the adult.

4. Offer one You do route. Record the actual prompt and pupil decision. Check one claim against the work and let the pupil confirm the listener's account.

5. Use the agreed pause signal and quiet return step. File a concise record in the evidence app the same day under ART-LAUNCH-A2-W01 showing the review evidence, audience exchange and support used.

Shared preparation, access and safety

They are constructed classroom examples, not historical artworks or evidence of attendance at an arts event. Label this lesson's mini-gallery review CLASSROOM PRACTICE. Keep any genuine experience record separate; this lesson does not complete or sign off Unit 1.

Read one prompt at a time. Every task can be answered by pointing, speaking, signing, using symbols, drawing or writing. A pupil may pass. No cold calling or required public reading. Allow thinking time, then invite all pupils to show a choice together. An adult can move cards or scribe the pupil's exact words. Do not substitute an adult's interpretation for the pupil's. A pass needs no book entry.

Use one Arrival tier and one Exit tier for each pupil, not all three. Keep Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until after responses. Offer one You do route; the four routes are alternatives. Keep the artworks visible and invite evidence-based disagreement. Personal taste is not marked right or wrong. No personal story, belief, family detail or loss is requested.

Preparation — complete teaching-artwork cues

Prepare two plain rectangular images before the lesson. Show both throughout. No names, logos or branding appear on them. These descriptions are the specification for the classroom examples. Do not substitute a different image; keep these answer keys.

Artwork A, title Flow: a pale cream field; three broad blue and teal curved bands running from left towards right, with clear pale gaps; one small solid orange circle above the bands towards the upper right. The circle does not touch a band. Leave open pale space around the forms. No people, letters or objects are depicted.

Artwork B, title Crossing: an orange field; three dark charcoal diagonal strips crossing and overlapping near the centre; a large white circle at the right edge with part of the circle beyond the image, so it is cropped. The strips have straight edges. No people or letters are depicted.

Prepare four category cards: Feature / Interpretation / Recommendation / Unsupported claim. Prepare the four sentence cards printed in We do. Provide two plain audience cards: Viewers who enjoy abstract images / Viewers seeking a detailed, lifelike portrait. Read all cards aloud. Offer large, high-contrast copies and a spoken description of each artwork. If a pupil uses the description, note that access route; do not treat visual acuity as reviewing skill. Make a private adult-listener option available.

Adult-only note: Possible EHCP outcome areas are communication and interaction (expressing and checking meaning), cognition and learning (selecting evidence and organising an explanation), and independence (choosing a response mode and checking one claim). Observe only the relevant individual outcome actually shown; do not infer progress in every area from a finished review.

Adult-only note: Regulation strategy — agree a quiet pause signal at the start; keep the pupil's selected image and first step ready for a calm return within task time.

Same-day evidence — ART-LAUNCH-A2-W01: the assigned adult records the chosen artwork, the pupil's exact review or selected/annotated evidence, the response mode and actual support. File one book item, a before-and-after photograph of the upgraded claim or brief observation in the evidence app the same day under ART-LAUNCH-A2-W01. You do · 15 minutes · notes and answers

Adult note: All work here is CLASSROOM PRACTICE, using the fully specified mini-gallery. Allow two minutes to select evidence, nine to compose, two to check a claim and two to review it with an adult. These are inside the stage, not extra stages. Keep both works visible. A private spoken, signed, symbol or drawn review can carry the same reasoning as a written one; the adult records only the pupil's own meaning. No electronic recording is required.

Adult note: The listener names back one judgement and its evidence. The pupil confirms or corrects that account, then chooses one small revision or explains why the evidence already supports the claim. Mark an actual self-edit // where useful. A next-step response must be genuine, not an automatic completion tick. Give a brief written note against the review criteria (two accurate features, a supported judgement, a qualification, an audience reason) and name the improvement. The pupil upgrades the checked claim in green pen (or by the same response mode). If a longer written review is made, its response is deferred to the first 10 to 15 minutes of the next lesson.

Allow thinking time, then invite all pupils to show a choice together. An adult can move cards or scribe the pupil's exact words. Do not substitute an adult's interpretation for the pupil's. A pass needs no book entry.

Use one Arrival tier and one Exit tier for each pupil, not all three. Keep Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until after responses. Offer one You do route; the four routes are alternatives. Keep the artworks visible and invite evidence-based disagreement. Personal taste is not marked right or wrong. No personal story, belief, family detail or loss is requested.

Preparation — complete teaching-artwork cues

Prepare two plain rectangular images before the lesson. Show both throughout. No names, logos or branding appear on them. These descriptions are the specification for the classroom examples. Do not substitute a different image; keep these answer keys.

Artwork A, title Flow: a pale cream field; three broad blue and teal curved bands running from left towards right, with clear pale gaps; one small solid orange circle above the bands towards the upper right. The circle does not touch a band. Leave open pale space around the forms. No people, letters or objects are depicted.

Artwork B, title Crossing: an orange field; three dark charcoal diagonal strips crossing and overlapping near the centre; a large white circle at the right edge with part of the circle beyond the image, so it is cropped. The strips have straight edges. No people or letters are depicted.

Prepare four category cards: Feature / Interpretation / Recommendation / Unsupported claim. Prepare the four sentence cards printed in We do. Provide two plain audience cards: Viewers who enjoy abstract images / Viewers seeking a detailed, lifelike portrait. Read all cards aloud. Offer large, high-contrast copies and a spoken description of each artwork. If a pupil uses the description, note that access route; do not treat visual acuity as reviewing skill. Make a private adult-listener option available.

Adult-only note: Possible EHCP outcome areas are communication and interaction (expressing and checking meaning), cognition and learning (selecting evidence and organising an explanation), and independence (choosing a response mode and checking one claim). Observe only the relevant individual outcome actually shown; do not infer progress in every area from a finished review.

Adult-only note: Regulation strategy — agree a quiet pause signal at the start; keep the pupil's selected image and first step ready for a calm return within task time.

I do · method

Step 1 — Describe the evidence

'Flow has three broad curved bands and a small orange circle. The pale spaces keep the forms apart.'

Step 2 — Explain a judgement

'The repeated curves feel calm to me because my eye can follow them across the image. The orange circle adds a contrast in colour.'

Step 3 — Qualify and recommend

'Another viewer may find the open space empty rather than calm. I would recommend this to someone who enjoys simple abstract shapes, because the curves and circle give them a clear colour contrast to explore. My view is based on this image; I do not know its maker's intention.'
