Opening

Art · GROW · Autumn 2 · Week 1

Make — A small light artwork

Learning goal

I can make and review a small light artwork.

Today I can

  • I can make bright marks contrast with a dark ground.
  • I can repeat a shape or pair of shapes.
  • I can show one strength and one change.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write.

You can ask an adult to make your chosen marks.

You may pass.

Words for today: ground — the space around and behind the marks; contrast — a clear difference, such as bright marks on a dark ground; repeat — use a mark or group of marks again.

You do · choose one route

15 min · 22–37

Choose one route.

I · S · C

Independent

Plan a small light artwork for an imagined festival display.

Use a dark ground. Choose bright colours that stand out.

Choose a shape or pair to repeat: dots, rings, rays or your own simple marks.

Make or direct at least three repeats. Keep some dark space around the marks.

Check the work against your chosen colours and repeat.

Show one strength: “My ___ works because ___.”

Show one change: “I could change ___ to ___ because ___.”

Review: Test or direct a small part of that change. Keep the repeat clear.

I · S · C

Supported

Use a small piece of dark paper.

Choose your pair of marks.

A: Yellow ring / orange dot.

B: Light blue ring / light green dot.

Make or direct your pair three times with an adult.

Check: do your bright marks stand out?

Check: can you follow the same pair each time?

Choose one strength: clear contrast / clear repeat. Show where.

Choose one change: thicker ring outlines / wider gaps / your own change.

Review: Tell or show how your change could help. Test or direct one small part.

I · S · C

Choice

Choose to make marks, arrange paper shapes or direct an adult.

Use dark paper. Choose bright warm colours, cool colours or both.

Choose one repeat: dot/ring, ray/dot or your own simple pair.

Make or direct your pair at least three times.

Leave some dark ground visible between marks.

Show one strength in the contrast or repeat.

Show one change to a colour, shape, outline or gap. Give a reason.

Review: Test or direct a small part of your change. Check how it affects the repeat.

I · S · C

Aspire

Start with this complete plan on dark paper.

Yellow ring / orange dot, repeated three times with equal gaps.

Make or direct the plan. Check its contrast and repeat.

Name one strength and show its evidence.

Now plan one change: mark colour, outline thickness or gap size.

Keep the shape order and dark ground the same. Apply your change across the repeat.

Test or direct the change on a short second strip of dark paper.

Explain the change in effect and which version you prefer for the display.

Review: Does your test show the one change you planned? Correct it if needed.

In your book: Your small artwork or directed plan, one strength and one change.

TA Brief

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Current stage

1. Set out both complete model images and safe materials; introduce the task afresh without relying on P1 attendance.

2. Use the agreed count-in and STOP; pupils can direct marks or shapes, use any response mode or pass. Offer pre-cut shapes instead of required cutting.

3. During We do, check that every pupil makes a private second-mark choice before a shared version is chosen.

4. On the chosen route, record the pupil’s own art decision and reason; making directed marks or reading cards is support to note.

5. Give one specific review prompt, retain the strength and change with a small test or check, and file the evidence in the evidence app the same day under ART-GROW-A2-W01-P2.

Shared preparation, access and safety

Prepare the simple word, colour and image cards described here before the lesson; no external image, named resource or screen interaction is needed. Read pupil wording aloud, one short prompt at a time. All response choices apply to EVERY question, demonstration response, shared task, route, review and Exit: pupils may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. A pupil may direct an adult to make a mark or move a shape. Offer a director role throughout; do not require handling materials or speaking to the group. Never cold call. Any pupil may pass. A pass needs no book entry.

Use one Arrival tier and one Exit tier per pupil. Keep Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until the response. The three Exit checks are brief choices or short responses, not three extended written answers. Allow private thinking before any shared reveal. An adult may record the pupil's own choice, gesture or exact words. No board copying is needed in the timed stages. Mark-making is the default; prepared paper shapes are an equal alternative without required cutting or gluing.

Festival context: light and colour are used in some celebrations. People within a tradition may celebrate in different ways. These are original, made-up classroom images for an imagined light display, not sacred images or examples of a named tradition's art. Never ask about a pupil's belief, family, worship or loss. Discuss visible features and possible effects, not personal disclosure. No named artists or artworks are needed.

Materials safety and access: use pencils, pastels or chalk, coloured and dark paper, and optional glue and scissors with adult care. No flames or real lights are used. Prepare small dark-paper rectangles and optional pre-cut shapes before the lesson so time is spent choosing and making. The adult checks scissors, supervises their use and offers pre-cut shapes or a director role. Keep materials on the work surface, avoid blowing chalk dust and clean hands after use. Offer a pencil or paper shapes if a texture is uncomfortable. Announce a brief count-in before the first making step; mirror it with a visible gesture, then use the agreed flat-hand STOP cue for a pause. A pupil can choose a quiet place without explaining why. Give large images and read the exact descriptions; label colours as well as showing them. Record access through descriptions or adult-directed marks, and do not treat colour discrimination or fine motor control as the art understanding being assessed.

Kit and stimulus — complete adult image cues: Prepare A and B as two equal plain rectangles with no people, text within the image or named objects. A: a very dark blue ground; one central row of six evenly spaced, equal-size marks in this order: yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot. Each ring is a yellow circle outline with the ground visible in its centre; each dot is a solid orange circle. Leave clear dark gaps. B: copy A's marks, colours, sizes, order and spacing exactly, but use a pale yellow ground close in colour and lightness to the yellow rings. Check that A's yellow outlines stand out more clearly before teaching. Prepare one unfilled dark-paper row for the guided example, and a separate test scrap for the adult's small mark change. Supply word cards that give each word and its meaning: WARM (red, orange and yellow) / COOL (blue and green); CONTRAST (a clear difference, such as bright marks on a dark ground) / REPEAT (use a mark or group of marks again); STRENGTH / CHANGE; labelled bright yellow, orange, light blue and light green colour choices; ring and dot shape cues. For a ray cue, if chosen for a pupil's own work, draw four short straight marks spreading from a single centre. Each line is one ray; the group is an abstract light shape, not a flame. Red, orange and yellow are warm colours; blue and green are cool colours for this task. The terms describe colour groups, not temperature. The complete model, terms and materials are introduced here; attendance at P1 is not assumed. Do not ask for a prepared artwork or prior plan. Each pupil makes a fresh small plan within the task.

Adult-only EHCP note: Possible outcome areas are communication and interaction; cognition and learning; sensory and/or physical needs; social, emotional and mental health. Link observations only to an actual individual target; do not infer an outcome from participation alone. Regulation strategy: use the announced count-in and visible STOP cue, with an agreed silent return to the next small task if the pupil needs a pause.

Same-day evidence — ART-GROW-A2-W01-P2: The assigned adult records one chosen book item or a brief observation of the pupil's own response, response mode and actual prompt used, and files it in the evidence app the same day under ART-GROW-A2-W01-P2. A photo is optional and needs the accompanying observation; a photo alone does not show understanding. Do not require a public display. Arts evidence goes in the Explore log.

Festival context: light and colour are used in some celebrations. People within a tradition may celebrate in different ways. These are original, made-up classroom images for an imagined light display, not sacred images or examples of a named tradition's art. Never ask about a pupil's belief, family, worship or loss. Discuss visible features and possible effects, not personal disclosure. No named artists or artworks are needed.

Materials safety and access: use pencils, pastels or chalk, coloured and dark paper, and optional glue and scissors with adult care. No flames or real lights are used. Prepare small dark-paper rectangles and optional pre-cut shapes before the lesson so time is spent choosing and making. The adult checks scissors, supervises their use and offers pre-cut shapes or a director role. Keep materials on the work surface, avoid blowing chalk dust and clean hands after use. Offer a pencil or paper shapes if a texture is uncomfortable. Announce a brief count-in before the first making step; mirror it with a visible gesture, then use the agreed flat-hand STOP cue for a pause. A pupil can choose a quiet place without explaining why. Give large images and read the exact descriptions; label colours as well as showing them. Record access through descriptions or adult-directed marks, and do not treat colour discrimination or fine motor control as the art understanding being assessed.

Kit and stimulus — complete adult image cues: Prepare A and B as two equal plain rectangles with no people, text within the image or named objects. A: a very dark blue ground; one central row of six evenly spaced, equal-size marks in this order: yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot. Each ring is a yellow circle outline with the ground visible in its centre; each dot is a solid orange circle. Leave clear dark gaps. B: copy A's marks, colours, sizes, order and spacing exactly, but use a pale yellow ground close in colour and lightness to the yellow rings. Check that A's yellow outlines stand out more clearly before teaching. Prepare one unfilled dark-paper row for the guided example, and a separate test scrap for the adult's small mark change. Supply word cards that give each word and its meaning: WARM (red, orange and yellow) / COOL (blue and green); CONTRAST (a clear difference, such as bright marks on a dark ground) / REPEAT (use a mark or group of marks again); STRENGTH / CHANGE; labelled bright yellow, orange, light blue and light green colour choices; ring and dot shape cues. For a ray cue, if chosen for a pupil's own work, draw four short straight marks spreading from a single centre. Each line is one ray; the group is an abstract light shape, not a flame. Red, orange and yellow are warm colours; blue and green are cool colours for this task. The terms describe colour groups, not temperature. The complete model, terms and materials are introduced here; attendance at P1 is not assumed. Do not ask for a prepared artwork or prior plan. Each pupil makes a fresh small plan within the task.

Adult-only EHCP note: Possible outcome areas are communication and interaction; cognition and learning; sensory and/or physical needs; social, emotional and mental health. Link observations only to an actual individual target; do not infer an outcome from participation alone. Regulation strategy: use the announced count-in and visible STOP cue, with an agreed silent return to the next small task if the pupil needs a pause.

I do · method

This is made-up art for an imagined festival light display.

We are choosing its effect; we are not copying a faith's image.

1. Choose a dark ground.

2. Plan a yellow ring, then an orange dot.

3. Repeat that pair three times. Leave dark gaps.

4. Check: can I follow ring, dot, ring, dot?

5. Review one strength and one change.

My strength: the bright marks contrast with the dark ground.

My change: I could make the ring outlines thicker so they show more clearly.

I test that change on a scrap. If I use it, I change each ring in the repeat.

I can choose a different effect. I give a reason I can show in the work.

Must-know words — five

contrast — a difference, such as bright marks against a dark ground.

warm — the red, orange and yellow colour group.

cool — the blue and green colour group used here.

pattern — an arrangement of marks or shapes; ours use a repeat.

repeat — use a mark or group of marks again.

Check: Which two features make my work fit our plan?
