Play with the count-in

Up and moving · two bands

Two-band challenge

Split the room into Band A and Band B. The teacher is the conductor.

Band A plays alone once. Band B plays alone once.

Then one count-in, and both bands play together for two repeats.

Points: each band that keeps going and lands together on beat 4 wins a point.

Play A alone once; then B alone once. Keep four equal positions.

Use one count-in, then join both parts for two repeats.

In your book: Record the count-in and your chosen part.

Check, respond and retry

Team game · groups

Lost and found

The group plays both parts. The teacher points at one player to freeze for a moment.

That player follows the pulse silently and rejoins on the next beat 1.

Points: the listeners award 1 point for a clean rejoin with no rushing.

A listener checks the shared tap on beat 4 and the full rests.

Respond to one next step, retry and say what stayed or changed.

Staff check after responding

A sounds on 1, 3 and 4; B sounds on 2 and 4. Both sound together on 4. They keep the same four-beat length, including silent positions. Recovery follows the pulse silently until the next beat-1 cue.

In your book: A specific listener comment and response.

Staff notes · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · Lesson 2

GROW uses Independent as the starting route. Offer Supported and optional Aspire without changing the weekly outcome. Use a pair and a trio for five learners; rotate player, pulse keeper and listener so every pupil learns a part and has a personal record. Agree a quiet stop cue. Model following one instruction at a time: look, wait, play, stop. Quiet finger taps, silent pointing or signs are valid access modes; offer a quiet space and a pause. Do not require solos or a public audience. Scribe exact words or confirmed choices, read them back and allow corrections. Plan in any language before sharing in the chosen mode. Give one precise success and one NS next step. The pupil responds in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode, then tries the part again. Record R only when Voice and Audience are present, one NS has been set and the pupil has responded. Use VF / WS / I / NS / // / ? / E / R accurately.

Retrieve each pupil’s actual first-lesson score and next step. Place A and B in aligned beat columns. Agree low volume and roles before sound starts. In the trio use player A, player B and listener/pulse keeper; rotate all roles. In the pair, an adult briefly keeps the pulse while both parts join, then fades. A pupil may direct or point a part silently. Keep code and judgement fields blank.

Use minutes 34–37 to check each pupil’s personal contribution, listener comment, chosen next step and actual retry. Read back exact scribing. Return cards and keep the score beside the real record in the arts log. If a pupil did not retry, record that honestly and plan the next opportunity.

Keep an individual note for each pupil even when the sound is shared. Observe a chosen part, common count-in, maintained full rests, any recovery and one response-led retry. The score and real comment can support taking part within the award. A group recording alone does not identify each learner’s understanding; use a brief individual explanation or checked choice.

Scheme outcome: Learn a part for a group performance.

Demonstration

0–15 seconds: Show A: TAP REST TAP TAP and B: REST TAP REST TAP. Point down the same numbered column in both scores. Explain: “The parts differ, but the beat positions line up.”

15–30 seconds: Give a shared 1, 2, 3, 4 count-in. Play A while the adult helper points B silently. On a second cycle the helper taps B. Keep the common pointer steady.

30–45 seconds: Model losing the place without blaming anyone. Stop your sound, follow the boxes silently and rejoin on the next beat 1 cue. Do not race to catch up or interrupt the other part.

45–60 seconds: The listener says: “The shared tap on beat 4 came at two times.” Choose: “We will watch beat 4 and keep the pulse.” Try both parts again, then ask whether the tap coincided. This is a class model, not recorded pupil progress.

Misconceptions and corrections

A player copies the louder or busier part: Reduce the other part’s volume or point it silently. Track the chosen score with one finger. Add the other sound only when the pupil can maintain the pattern.

The player speeds up to catch a lost place: Keep following the pulse silently. Offer a clear next-beat-1 cue and rejoin there; accept that recovery as a skill.

Feedback is only “good” or “bad”: Ask for an observable detail: start, rest, shared tap or repeat. Set one feasible next step, hear the response and compare another attempt.

Prompt ladder

Point to the relevant place or instruction.

Ask one short question.

Rephrase the instruction.

Provide a scaffold or a choice.

Model the first step only, then return the task.
