Play with the count-in

Up and moving · whole class

Human beat line

Four volunteers stand in a line as beats 1, 2, 3 and 4.

Beat 2 is the REST. That person holds up an open hand and stays quiet.

The teacher walks the line at an even pace. Each TAP person taps once as the teacher passes.

Go round twice without speeding up. Everyone else judges: thumbs up if the rest stayed a full beat.

Count in together: 1, 2, 3, 4. Wait until the next 1.

Point and play part A twice: TAP REST TAP TAP.

In your book: A checked count-in and two attempts of part A.

Team game · pairs

Spot the rest

One player plays part A twice. The listener looks away and listens.

The listener raises a hand exactly on the rest, beat 2.

Points: 1 point for each rest spotted right on time. Then swap jobs.

The listener checks beat 2 stays silent for a full beat.

Choose one next step, then repeat once with less adult pointing.

Staff check after responding

Start after the count-in. Taps occur on 1, 3 and 4; beat 2 is silent for the same length. The next cycle begins one beat after beat 4. Accept a steady silent performance with accurate pointing.

Staff notes · Autumn 2 · Week 2 · Lesson 1

GROW uses Independent as the starting route. Offer Supported and optional Aspire without changing the weekly outcome. Use a pair and a trio for five learners; rotate player, pulse keeper and listener so every pupil learns a part and has a personal record. Agree a quiet stop cue. Model following one instruction at a time: look, wait, play, stop. Quiet finger taps, silent pointing or signs are valid access modes; offer a quiet space and a pause. Do not require solos or a public audience. Scribe exact words or confirmed choices, read them back and allow corrections. Plan in any language before sharing in the chosen mode. Give one precise success and one NS next step. The pupil responds in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode, then tries the part again. Record R only when Voice and Audience are present, one NS has been set and the pupil has responded. Use VF / WS / I / NS / // / ? / E / R accurately.

Set out part A with beat numbers 1–4 and a clear repeat boundary. Agree the low volume and the red STOP signal. Check each pupil can see the count-in and can choose a comfortable action. Arrange a pair and a trio with predictable turns. Keep personal code fields blank. The adult model stays labelled class example. No track, recording or named artist is needed.

During minutes 34–37, keep each pupil’s actual practice note with the chosen score, read back any scribed words, and return cards. Record support used and one next step. Collect no personal disclosure. Put selected evidence in the existing arts log; record the real date only after teaching.

Observe each pupil reading or showing a part, waiting for the count-in, keeping the rest and repeating. Note the actual support and pupil response to one next step. Keep a short pupil-authored practice record and a score or description. This supports practice within the award; it does not certify a whole part.

Scheme outcome: Learn a part for a group performance.

Demonstration

0–15 seconds: Point across the four numbered boxes of part A: TAP REST TAP TAP. Say each word at an even pace. Explain that the four beats repeat as one short part.

15–30 seconds: Show the blue COUNT-IN card. Count 1, 2, 3, 4 at a calm even pace while the other hand waits. Say: “I start on the next 1, after the count-in.”

30–45 seconds: Play two cycles: TAP REST TAP TAP | TAP REST TAP TAP. Keep a small silent movement on every rest. Point to beat 1 at the repeat. Do not compress the silence.

45–60 seconds: Ask the listener which rest stayed in time. Model: “My rest was short. I will point to box 2 for a full beat.” Play one more cycle and show how the comment changed the next attempt.

Misconceptions and corrections

A rest is skipped and the next tap arrives early: Keep all four beat positions visible. Count 1–2–3–4 evenly while silently moving on beat 2. Replay slowly, then fade the adult count.

The pupil starts during the count-in: Use WAIT then START. Rehearse the count-in without playing; move the pointer to beat 1 only after the fourth count.

Repeating means going faster: Show the boundary after beat 4. Keep the next beat 1 the same distance away. Use eight steady silent pulses and let the pupil add the part.
