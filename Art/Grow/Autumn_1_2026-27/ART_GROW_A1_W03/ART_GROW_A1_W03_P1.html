Starter: spot the visual clues

Visual cues to spot

Examine the four composition levers on the studio planning board on screen.

  • Choice 1 (Focal point): Where does the main identity motif sit, and how big is it?
  • Choice 2 (Background pattern): How does a repeating geometric or organic pattern frame the motif?
  • Choice 3 (Colour palette): Two complementary colours plus one dark neutral for crisp edges.
  • Choice 4 (Medium pairing): Combining a dry line/pastel medium with a wet wash or collage ground.

Stage 6 · Independent studio practice (You do) · TRACK Pick your route: Supported · Independent · Aspire

Choose your studio route and create

Supported route: two thumbnails, three colour swatches, and labelled frame

  1. Sketch two quick layout ideas in the small top boxes (centred vs angled).
  2. Tick your stronger thumbnail and colour three swatch boxes (one dark, one bright, one light).
  3. Draw your main motif and background pattern in the large planning frame.
  4. Complete the four label stems around the border.
Success check: Two thumbnails, three colour swatches, and a labelled composition plan.

Independent route: four-choice studio blueprint with visual rationale

  1. Produce two distinct thumbnail sketches testing different focal-point positions and background patterns.
  2. Build a three-colour complementary or warm/cool swatch strip showing light, mid, and dark values.
  3. Develop your chosen layout in the main frame with clear tonal separation between subject and background.
  4. Write four concise annotations explaining your focal point, pattern rhythm, colour contrast, and media order.
Success check: Complete studio blueprint with thumbnails, value swatches, and four rationale notes.

Aspire route: three thumbnail variations, media-order test strip, and annotated plan

  1. Sketch three thumbnail compositions testing symmetry, rule-of-thirds asymmetry, and cropped framing.
  2. Create a live mixed-media test strip alongside your colour swatches to prove layer order.
  3. Draft a detailed composition plan with full annotations explaining how figure-ground contrast directs the viewer's eye.
Success check: Three thumbnails, live media test strip, and fully annotated composition plan.

Key words and definitions (TRACK)

  • composition: How shapes, lines, and colours are arranged inside the frame.
  • focal point: The main area of high contrast that pulls the viewer's eye first.
  • thumbnail: A small rapid sketch used to test layout ideas before working big.
  • complementary: Colours opposite each other on the colour wheel that create bold contrast.
  • balance: Arranging visual weight so the picture feels stable and purposeful.
