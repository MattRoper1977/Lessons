Technique studio tasters

Big question: How do four studio media change the mood and contrast of the same identity motif?

Learning goals for today

  • Record a clear technical annotation under each panel naming strengths and control tips.

Studio kit: A3 four-panel studio sheet, 2B/4B graphite pencils, diluted ink and soft brush, chalk pastels, toned paper offcuts and glue

Stage 6 · Independent studio practice (You do) · TRACK Pick your route: Supported · Independent · Aspire

Choose your studio route and create

Supported route: guided four-panel media sampler

  1. Use the light guide template to place your motif in all four studio boxes.
  2. Shade box 1 with 2B/4B pencil and paint Box 2 with two tones of diluted ink.
  3. Blend two chalk pastel shades in Box 3 and glue three cut paper tones in Box 4.
  4. Complete the stem under each box: 'This medium gives a ___ edge and ___ tone.'
Success check: All four media panels completed with a completed observation stem under each.

Independent route: comparative media battle and technical notes

  1. Draw your personal identity motif accurately across all four panels.
  2. Execute graded graphite shading (Panel 1), three-value ink wash (Panel 2), blended pastel (Panel 3), and torn/cut collage (Panel 4).
  3. Annotate each panel naming one technical strength and one handling challenge.
  4. Rank your top medium for your upcoming identity piece and state why.
Success check: Four distinct media studies completed with comparative technical annotations.

Aspire route: four media trials plus hybrid mixed-media fifth study

  1. Complete all four controlled media panels with wide tonal range from deep dark to bright highlight.
  2. In the central extension box, combine your top two media (e.g. collage base with ink wash and graphite line).
  3. Write a concise studio evaluation explaining how the two media interact on the paper surface.
Success check: Four single-medium panels plus a resolved hybrid mixed-media study and evaluation.

Key words and definitions (TRACK)

  • medium: The specific art material or tool used to make marks.
  • gradation: A smooth step-by-step shift from dark tone to light tone.
  • wash: Thin ink or paint diluted with water for translucent tone.
  • collage: Building an image by cutting, tearing, and layering paper shapes.
  • motif: A central visual shape or emblem repeated across studies.
