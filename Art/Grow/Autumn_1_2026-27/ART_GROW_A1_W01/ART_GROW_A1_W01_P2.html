Starter: spot the visual clues

Visual cues to spot

Examine the architectural diagram on screen highlighting solid beams vs open negative spaces.

  • Solid structure: vertical towers, horizontal deck, and diagonal cross-bracing.
  • Negative space: shaded sky triangles trapped inside the framework.
  • Line weight: heavy dark lines on near beams, lighter lines on distant girders.
  • Surface ground: subtle graphite frottage texture underneath the line drawing.

Stage 6 · Independent studio practice (You do) · TRACK Pick your route: Supported · Independent · Aspire

Choose your studio route and create

Supported route: negative-space stencil guide and line weight

  1. Rub a light graphite texture across your page using two textured plates.
  2. Use the triangle negative-space guide to map the inner gaps of a truss span.
  3. Join the beams around the gaps using a ruler and black fineliner.
  4. Thicken the front beam edges so they stand out over the texture ground.
Success check: Texture ground with accurate negative-space truss drawing and varied line weight.

Independent route: architectural study with frottage and negative space

  1. Create a zoned frottage ground combining two contrasting industrial textures.
  2. Map the overall proportions of an architectural span by sketching the negative-space sky shapes first.
  3. Develop the full structure using three distinct line weights (light guide, mid brace, heavy foreground girder).
  4. Add three technical annotations pointing to negative space, line weight, and surface texture.
Success check: Resolved architectural drawing over textured ground with three technical annotations.

Aspire route: multi-plane perspective study with rivet and shadow detail

  1. Prepare a layered tonal ground using graphite frottage and a pale ink wash.
  2. Construct a two-plane structural drawing showing near and far truss frames through negative-space mapping.
  3. Use hatched shadow under the deck and white chalk highlights on top flanges.
  4. Annotate how line weight and overlap establish spatial depth.
Success check: Two-plane architectural study with tonal depth, chalk highlights, and spatial notes.

Key words and definitions (TRACK)

  • negative space: The open air gaps and shapes trapped between solid beams.
  • truss: A rigid framework of steel or timber beams arranged in repeating triangles.
  • line weight: Varying line thickness from light construction lines to heavy foreground edges.
  • frottage: Taking a surface texture rubbing with graphite or wax over a relief surface.
  • proportion: The size relationship between height, span, and beam thickness.
