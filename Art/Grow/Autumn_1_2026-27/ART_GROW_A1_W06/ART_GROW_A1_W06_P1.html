Stage 6 · Independent studio practice (You do) · TRACK Pick your route: Supported · Independent · Aspire

Choose your studio route and create

Supported route: 6 cm pattern close-up, three rule ticks, and symbol response

  1. Place your card window over a clear pattern on the artwork card and copy the marks in Box 1.
  2. Tick the colour pair and mark style you used on the middle checklist.
  3. In box 2, draw your own identity symbol and fill it using the exact same two colours and mark style.
  4. Complete the link stem: 'I borrowed the ___ technique for my own symbol.'
Success check: Box 1 close-up study, checklist ticked, and Box 2 personal symbol response completed.

Independent route: paired transcription, personal response, and analytical annotations

  1. Use a viewfinder to execute an accurate 6 cm observational transcription in Box 1 showing layer sequence.
  2. Annotate box 1 across three lenses: Media layer Order, Tonal contrast, and Pattern rhythm.
  3. In box 2, design and render an original personal response applying those three technical rules to your own motif.
  4. Write a short summary explaining how the study informed your response.
Success check: Paired transcription and original response completed with three analytical annotations.

Aspire route: comparative dual-study and hybrid motif resolution

  1. Complete a precision observational study in Box 1 dissecting how the practitioner balances figure and patterned ground.
  2. In box 2, synthesise the practitioner's counterchange or layering method with your Week 4 identity motif.
  3. Write a critical commentary evaluating how adopting this method changes the visual impact of your work.
Success check: Precision study, synthesised personal response, and critical commentary.

Key words and definitions (TRACK)

  • deconstruct: To break an artwork down into its building blocks (line, tone, pattern, colour).
  • observational study: A careful drawing of a small section to learn how the marks were layered.
  • visual response: A new mini-artwork of your own that borrows a technique or colour rule from the study.
  • juxtaposition: Placing two contrasting textures or colours side by side for visual punch.
  • annotation: Short analytical notes connected to specific parts of your study.
