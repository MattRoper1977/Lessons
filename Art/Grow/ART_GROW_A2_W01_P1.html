Art · GROW · Autumn 2 · Week 1

Explore — Light and colour in festival art

Learning goal

I can describe and compare light and colour in art.

Today I can

  • I can find warm and cool colours.
  • I can compare bright marks on different grounds.
  • I can link a colour or pattern choice to its effect.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write.

You can ask an adult to make your chosen marks.

You may pass.

Aspire

TA Brief

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Current stage

1. Prepare A–C exactly, with labelled colour and art-word cards, so pupils can compare the same features through images or descriptions.

2. Use safe materials and predictable count-in/STOP cues; directing is available on every route and any pupil may pass.

3. Give private decision time before the We do reveal; note the pupil’s own comparison rather than a repeated group answer.

4. Keep warm/cool grouping, light-dark contrast and repetition distinct; treat effect words as reasoned interpretations, not fixed emotional facts.

5. Record a chosen feature, its reason and actual support; help the pupil make one precise review change and upload the evidence to the evidence app the same day, labelled ART-GROW-A2-W01-P1.

Shared preparation, access and safety

Prepare the simple word, colour and image cards described here before the lesson; no external image, named resource or screen interaction is needed. Read pupil wording aloud, one short prompt at a time. All response choices apply to EVERY question, demonstration response, shared task, route, review and Exit: pupils may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. A pupil may direct an adult to make a mark or move a card. Offer a director role throughout; do not require handling materials or speaking to the group. Never cold call. Any pupil may pass. A pass needs no book entry.

Use one Arrival tier and one Exit tier per pupil. Keep Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until the response. The three Exit checks are brief choices or short responses, not three extended written answers. Allow private thinking before any shared reveal. An adult may record the pupil's own choice, gesture or exact words. No board copying, cutting or gluing is needed in the timed stages.

Festival context: light and colour are used in some celebrations. People within a tradition may celebrate in different ways. These are original, made-up classroom images for an imagined light display, not sacred images or examples of a named tradition's art. Never ask about a pupil's belief, family, worship or loss. Discuss visible features and possible effects, not personal disclosure. No named artists or artworks are needed.

Materials safety and access: use pencils, pastels or chalk, and coloured and dark paper only; prepared colour and shape cards are enough for this explore lesson. No flames or real lights are used. Keep materials on the work surface and avoid blowing chalk dust. Offer a pencil or a director role if a texture is uncomfortable. Announce a brief count-in before the first visual task; mirror it with a visible gesture, then use the agreed flat-hand STOP cue for a pause. A pupil can pause in a quiet place without explaining why. Give large images and read the exact descriptions; label colours as well as showing them. Note when a response uses the description or labels, and do not treat colour discrimination or fine motor control as the art understanding being assessed.

Kit and stimulus — complete adult image cues: Make three equal plain rectangular images, labelled A, B and C, with no people, text within the image or named objects. A: a very dark blue ground; one central horizontal row of six evenly spaced marks, reading left to right as yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot. Each ring is a clear yellow circle outline with dark ground visible in its centre; each dot is a solid orange circle. Make the marks the same outer size, with clear dark gaps. B: exactly the same ground, shapes, sizes, spacing and order as A, but use bright light blue for each ring and bright light green for each dot. C: exactly the same marks, colours, sizes, spacing and order as A, but on a pale yellow ground. Make the yellow rings close in colour and lightness to C's ground, so their outlines are less distinct than on A. Check the actual paper examples before teaching; their yellow rings must clearly show this difference. Prepare word cards that each show the word and its meaning: WARM (red, orange, yellow), COOL (blue, green), CONTRAST (a difference that helps a mark stand out), PATTERN (an arrangement of marks; ours repeat) and REPEAT (use a mark again); and DARK / PALE and SAME / DIFFERENT cards. Colour cards: red, orange, yellow, blue and green, each with its written label. In this lesson red, orange and yellow are warm colours; blue and green are cool colours. These are art groupings, not a measure of temperature. A small dark-paper sample and the same mark-making colours allow a pupil to test a chosen pair of marks, or direct the adult to test it.

Adult-only EHCP note: Possible outcome areas are communication and interaction; cognition and learning; sensory and physical access; social, emotional and mental health. Link observations only to an actual individual target; do not infer an outcome from participation alone. Regulation strategy: use the announced count-in and visible STOP cue, with an agreed silent return to the next small task if the pupil needs a pause.

Same-day evidence — ART-GROW-A2-W01-P1: The assigned adult records one chosen book item or a brief observation of the pupil's own response, response mode and actual prompt used, and uploads it to the evidence app the same day, labelled ART-GROW-A2-W01-P1. Keep the coded book item, with a photo where useful, in the pupil's Explore log; a photo needs the accompanying observation; a photo alone does not show understanding. Do not require a public display.

Kit and stimulus — complete adult image cues: Make three equal plain rectangular images, labelled A, B and C, with no people, text within the image or named objects. A: a very dark blue ground; one central horizontal row of six evenly spaced marks, reading left to right as yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot / yellow ring / orange dot. Each ring is a clear yellow circle outline with dark ground visible in its centre; each dot is a solid orange circle. Make the marks the same outer size, with clear dark gaps. B: exactly the same ground, shapes, sizes, spacing and order as A, but use bright light blue for each ring and bright light green for each dot. C: exactly the same marks, colours, sizes, spacing and order as A, but on a pale yellow ground. Make the yellow rings close in colour and lightness to C's ground, so their outlines are less distinct than on A. Check the actual paper examples before teaching; their yellow rings must clearly show this difference. Prepare word cards that each show the word and its meaning: WARM (red, orange, yellow), COOL (blue, green), CONTRAST (a difference that helps a mark stand out), PATTERN (an arrangement of marks; ours repeat) and REPEAT (use a mark again); and DARK / PALE and SAME / DIFFERENT cards. Colour cards: red, orange, yellow, blue and green, each with its written label. In this lesson red, orange and yellow are warm colours; blue and green are cool colours. These are art groupings, not a measure of temperature. A small dark-paper sample and the same mark-making colours allow a pupil to test a chosen pair of marks, or direct the adult to test it.

Adult-only EHCP note: Possible outcome areas are communication and interaction; cognition and learning; sensory and physical access; social, emotional and mental health. Link observations only to an actual individual target; do not infer an outcome from participation alone. Regulation strategy: use the announced count-in and visible STOP cue, with an agreed silent return to the next small task if the pupil needs a pause.

I do · method

We are exploring made-up art for an imagined light display.

These examples do not stand for a whole culture or faith.

A: A yellow ring, then an orange dot. That pair repeats.

The marks use warm colours on a dark ground.

B: The same shapes in the same places use cool blue and green.

Both warm and cool marks can stand out on a dark ground.

C: The same warm marks as A have a pale yellow ground.

The yellow rings are less clear here. They blend into the ground more.

Contrast is the difference that helps a mark stand out.

A maker can choose colours and a repeat to create an effect.

Different viewers may describe the effect in different ways.

Must-know words — five

contrast — a difference, such as bright marks against a dark ground.

warm — the red, orange and yellow colour group.

cool — the blue and green colour group used here.

pattern — an arrangement of marks or shapes; ours use a repeat.

repeat — use a mark or group of marks again.

Check: Did A's shapes change when we made C, or did its ground change?
