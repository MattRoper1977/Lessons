🌱 Starter · observe / question before explanation

Can I make people believe it is already there?

Commit to one panel before the explanation. The diagnostic identifies a teaching need, not a pupil label.

Toy scale

The sculpture is too large or small for people, buildings or routes in the view.

Pasted on

Horizon, camera height or light direction contradicts the setting.

Belongs there

Scale, eye-level horizon, light and site relationship make the viewer look twice.

Fix: point to the visible clue that would move the first or second example towards the third.

👩‍🎨 I Do · model control / reasoning

Teacher model · decision, action, evidence

1 · Mark the horizon/eye level on the approved site view. Place a scale person before adding the sculpture.
2 · Position the maquette or paper silhouette at its own eye level. Match contact with the ground and one light direction.
3 · Protect Version A, change one variable and compare. Name what still reveals that this is a proposal image, not a completed installation.
Think aloud: “I am not hunting for a perfect sculpture. I am isolating one visible cause so I can explain my next move.”

Reclaimed Site Integration Lab

Reclaimed Site Integration Lab

Place the form at A, B or C, set one scale/view cue and reveal the public route. The site is fictional and not to scale.

Start with A. Make a prediction or observation before choosing the next state.
Transfer: Compare protected A with B. State what stayed fixed, the one changed variable and the planned versus observed effect.

Digital interaction is a rehearsal only. It stores nothing and is not portfolio evidence.

2 · Visual knowledge organiser

Key terms

integration
making a proposed artwork visually and conceptually belong in a site
horizon
the eye-level line that helps views share the same perspective
orientation
the direction a form faces
context
the physical, social and visual conditions around the work

Artist-method focus

Anish Kapoor with Cecil Balmond, Temenos — use verified sources to consider scale, void, engineering collaboration and public setting; do not recreate the artwork or infer permission/endorsement.

Equipment / safety / maintenance

Maquette; teacher-approved site image or fictional site sheet; paper silhouettes; tracing overlay; masking tape; pencil; optional camera.

Use a fictional, school-based or teacher-approved view. No industrial-site visit, material collection or restricted access is assumed. Photography is optional, consent-controlled and contains no faces or identifying information.

Visual workflow

  1. Choose a safe, approved site/view and identify its horizon.
  2. Place the camera or viewpoint at the sculpture’s eye level, not yours.
  3. Match one scale, orientation or light cue.
  4. Keep the baseline, revise one variable and name the remaining giveaway.
