Guidance

Recognise caring relationships and identify an action that shows care.

Unit: Caring relationships, feelings and helpful responses

Weeks 1–6 are formative. Week 7 is the assessing lesson. Keep actual responses, support, gaps and next steps in the evidence record.

One 40-minute introduce + explore lesson. The show-what-you-know task stays inside You do. This lesson does not claim completion of the unit award.

Purpose and evidence

A caring-action match and a fictional support map, with the learner’s explanation and support recorded.

The adult model is not learner evidence. Keep actual responses, mode, prompts, gaps and next steps. Accept one appropriate role in the core map; a second route extends the response.

Curriculum and practical link

Thread tags: SMSC S/M/So (C: none); British Values IL/MR (D/RL/T: none).

S: reflect on a fictional person’s needs and choices. M: consider caring and helpful actions. So: listen to different possible responses. IL: respect a choice to pause or use a response aid. MR: respect different families and feelings without ranking people.

This helps me recognise respectful support from people I learn or work with.

Reading and access

Use short chunks and read cards aloud without asking learners to read difficult text aloud. Invite a response, give thinking time, and allow a pass. Access support changes the response method. Record content prompts separately.

Preparation and safety

Every teaching prompt concerns a fictional young person. Never ask learners about their own bodies, families or experiences, or use a real person as the case. Read fictional cards without inviting comparison with home. No touching, body checking, forced eye contact, acting out distress or personal role-play. Allow a pass, quiet response or pause. If a learner raises a personal concern, follow the setting’s safeguarding procedure; do not turn the disclosure into lesson evidence. Staff use the setting’s agreed support arrangements.

Prepare the resources

Print the fictional family page whole. Cut the six story cards and the role and care cards if useful. Prepare a separate model set. Supply the chosen route pages, organiser and usual communication aids. Staff handle any cutting.

Read the supplied cases and support roles. Do not replace them with personal cases or infer safety from a title, face or family structure.

Keep the evidence honest

A pass, blank page, group answer or model is not completed learner evidence. Preserve the first response and any prompted change. Use the setting’s agreed arrangements for actual evidence in the evidence app.

Adult deployment and responsive feedback

The teacher models with a separate set, invites responses and checks the reason. The supporting adult reads, points, offers the usual aid or scribes exact words. Neither supplies the learner’s conclusion as evidence.

Stage decisions

  • Arrival: offer the chosen route and a calm start. Do not infer knowledge from readiness.
  • Starter: keep attention on the stated caring act, without family comparisons.
  • We do: hear a choice and reason, use corrective feedback, and invite a revision.
  • You do: fade prompts where possible. Mark I · S · C beside the actual task.
  • Review: give one specific next step, hear the learner’s change and respond.
  • Exit: receive one response in the usual mode and decide the next teaching step.

What to record

Record independent, supported or co-constructed work accurately. Record access aids separately from content cues. Preserve the first attempt, prompt and next try. Do not turn a disclosure into evidence.

Space Voice Audience Influence

Offer calm space and a pass. Receive the response in the chosen mode. Listen or read and reply. Use the response to change support or next teaching. No extra signature, form or duplicate answer is required.

Before teaching

Scan the actual books and work references. Ask the cover teacher and supporting adults what was attempted, what helped and what is unfinished. Existing next-step notes are history to check against actual work.

Use Arrival to decide

Use the supplied fictional cases. Look for a need-action link, not fast reading or handwriting. If the learner needs an access change, offer it. If the idea is not yet shown, model the listening card and check a fresh choice.

Return to learning
