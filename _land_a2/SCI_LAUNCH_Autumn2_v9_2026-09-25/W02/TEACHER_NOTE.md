## AfL hinge and misconceptions

**Question at We do 2:** A gut-lining cell and a nerve cell share inherited DNA but have different structures. Which explanation fits this new pair?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| A. They differentiated into specialised cells with different jobs. | yes | Uses the new case accurately. |
| B. One cell must have lost every inherited gene. | no | Assumes specialisation requires losing all inherited genes. |
| C. Both cells must perform exactly the same job. | no | Confuses shared DNA with identical cell functions. |
| D. Neither cell can have developed through cell division. | no | Assumes differentiated cells could not have arisen after cell division. |

**Decision:** About 3 in 4 or more correct: proceed; otherwise re-model a dividing stem cell and one specialised skin-cell branch with two paper cards, then ask the parallel hinge. No auto-grade. **Parallel hinge:** A muscle cell and a liver cell share inherited DNA. Must their jobs be identical? Answer: no; differentiation produces specialised cell types.

| Misconception | How to spot | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| New cells must lose their DNA to specialise. | Pupil chooses B, claiming one cell lost every inherited gene. | Both branches keep DNA; differentiation means a different job, not loss of all DNA. |
| Cell division alone is sufficient to make specialised tissue. | Pupil chooses C, claiming the two specialised cells must have the same job. | Place a second card for differentiation; in plants include elongation as a further growth step. |
