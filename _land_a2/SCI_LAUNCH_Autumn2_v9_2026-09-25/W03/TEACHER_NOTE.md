## F/M/H and named stretch

**We do F support:** Give three colour-coded headings and a fictional benefit/risk card; pupil supplies the missing risk and one request for evidence.  
**We do M:** Explain why a possible benefit is not evidence that a treatment works.  
**We do H stretch:** Ask whether “support more research” and “approve a treatment for use” require the same strength of evidence; justify the distinction.  
**Independent F:** Sort the fictional proposal statements under possible benefit, possible risk and evidence needed. Stem: A possible benefit is …; a possible risk is …  
**Independent M:** Explain why a proposal needs evidence of both effect and harm before a decision. Stem: The claim is not yet proven because we need evidence that …  
**Independent H:** Evaluate whether to support further research with a qualified reason, risk and evidence request. Stem: I would support/review further research if …; however, before treatment use I would need …  
**Go deeper:** If a careful new study showed no improvement, how should the panel change its recommendation and why?  
**Named early-finisher stretch — Research panel minute:** Separate further research from routine treatment; explain the evidence needed for each. This is a harder correction/evaluation, not a repeated item.

## AfL hinge and misconceptions

**Question at We do 2:** A new fictional cell study reports improved movement but does not report harmful effects. Which review is justified?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| A. The benefit can establish safety despite the missing report on harms. | no | Treats an observed benefit as proof of safety without harm data. |
| B. The missing report proves the study cannot have produced any benefit. | no | Treats missing harm reporting as proof that the reported benefit did not occur. |
| C. The improved movement proves the study will work for every person. | no | Generalises one study result to every person. |
| D. The benefit needs checking alongside missing risk evidence before a decision. | yes | Uses the new case accurately. |

**Decision:** About 3 in 4 or more correct: proceed; otherwise re-model a fictional claim card beside an empty evidence column and ask the parallel hinge. No auto-grade. **Parallel hinge:** Another fictional study reports harms but no benefit measure. Can it establish a favourable benefit-risk balance? Answer: no; both kinds of evidence are needed.

| Misconception | How to spot | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| A reported benefit in one study proves a treatment will work for everyone. | Pupil chooses C, claiming benefit for every person. | Ask for further evidence of benefit across people and settings. |
| A reported benefit establishes safety without harm evidence. | Pupil chooses A, treating benefit as proof of safety without harms data. | Review benefits and missing risk evidence together before a decision. |

## TA Focus and prompt ladder

At Independent choose at lesson start: teacher works with supported pupils and TA holds group, or TA supports and teacher holds group. Swap across the week. No public cold-calling is required; this is an invitation bank.  
**I do:** “Which statement is a possible result, and which is measured evidence?”  
**We do:** “The risk column is blank. What question could the panel ask?”  
**Independent:** “What is your reason for more research or a pause? Which evidence would test it?”  
**Least prompt first; no physical prompting:** Wait five seconds for the pupil to choose a column. → Point to a card heading and the last completed model sentence. → Ask “What would a careful panel need to find out?” → Model the first benefit card only; pupil places the risk card. → Place one card together, then invite a new evidence request independently.  
**Exit handoff:** adult states deepest prompt level, valid response mode and what pupil did independently; other adult chooses the next prompt.
