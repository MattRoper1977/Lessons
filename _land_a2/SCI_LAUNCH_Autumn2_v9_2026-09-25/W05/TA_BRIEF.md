Arrival (5 min): Look at this week’s letter tiles A and a. Point to one detail you notice. Staff: Offer a private, predictable start. Do not require previous knowledge.

Starter (3 min): Recall three ideas from earlier lessons. Show one answer at a time. Staff: All pupils respond; reveal the answers only after attempts. Revisit success criteria.

I Do (5 min): Watch the full model. Point to the step that answers today’s question. Staff: Model one complete example; check every pupil’s response to the show-me question.

We Do (7 min): Complete the missing step in the part-done model. Speak, then match the cards. Staff: Wait five seconds, gesture, then cue if needed. Support the route without supplying the missing answer.

We Do 2 (5 min): Choose one hinge answer. Show it at the same time as the group. Staff: Collect simultaneous responses, apply the decision rule and parallel hinge in the staff note.

Independent (8 min): Calculate the aa probability for Aa × aa. Evaluate an exact-count claim about real offspring. Staff: At lesson start choose one adult with supported pupils and one holding the group; swap during the week. Record independence and prompt level.

Review (4 min): Tell a partner what you changed after feedback. Mark a real edit // and read it back when asked ?. Staff: Give a precise VF or NS(+) only when it actually happens. Invite a true self-edit and read-back.

Exit (3 min): Show what you know with a model and explain one reason. Staff: Hear the answer privately in a valid mode and use it to change the next example or prompt.

Planned adaptation — communication interaction: Offer the key word card and a private partner turn before speaking to the group; accept point, sign or AAC.

Planned adaptation — semh: Check in before the top question and hinge; offer a private first answer, agreed pause and one-step return.

Planned adaptation — sensory physical: Provide an enlarged high-contrast model and keyboard focus; allow speech-to-text or exact scribing.

Planned adaptation — eal: Pre-teach defined science words with examples; let pupils plan in any language while keeping M/H reasoning open.

## Individual target opportunities

Cognition & learning: We do: Combine one top allele and one side allele in each second-row square; count two aa squares. Record the pupil’s own sequencing, attention or independence target, including the prompt level needed.

Communication & interaction: Check: respond to “New cross: Aa × Aa. What is the chance of aa for each offspring?” in the pupil’s agreed mode. Evidence an individual communication/turn-taking target without requiring speech.

SEMH: Arrival: Look at this week’s letter tiles A and a. Point to one detail you notice. Use the agreed predictable start or pause/re-entry plan; evidence the pupil’s own regulation or engagement target privately.

Sensory/physical: Independent: Calculate the aa probability for Aa × aa. Evaluate an exact-count claim about real offspring. Offer the agreed accessible input, enlarged material or adapted equipment; record the pupil’s target-related access/independence, not a diagnosis.

EAL: Model/shared rehearsal: use allele, genotype, dominant, recessive, Punnett square with the actual example; allow first-language planning and oral rehearsal before the independent response. Record the agreed language target; EAL is not SEN.

Capture E against the pupil's own IEDP target on the school's evidence app; the IEDP carries their EHCP-linked and Boxall targets, so one record serves every plan. Follow the pupil's IBSP where it differs from these routines.
