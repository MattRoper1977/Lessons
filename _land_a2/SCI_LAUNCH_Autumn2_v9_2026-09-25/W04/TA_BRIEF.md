Staff handling control — Short DNA cord/string-and-clip DNA model: short length only, counted out and back in, adult-supervised, removed at once if a pupil is dysregulated.

Arrival (5 min): Look at this week’s string-and-clip DNA model. Point to one detail you notice. Staff: Offer a private, predictable start. Do not require previous knowledge.

Starter (3 min): Recall three ideas from earlier lessons. Show one answer at a time. Staff: All pupils respond; reveal the answers only after attempts. Revisit success criteria.

I Do (5 min): Watch the full model. Point to the step that answers today’s question. Staff: Model one complete example; check every pupil’s response to the show-me question.

We Do (7 min): Complete the missing step in the part-done model. Speak, then match the cards. Staff: Wait five seconds, gesture, then cue if needed. Support the route without supplying the missing answer.

We Do 2 (5 min): Choose one hinge answer. Show it at the same time as the group. Staff: Collect simultaneous responses, apply the decision rule and parallel hinge in the staff note.

Independent (8 min): Two diagrams label a gene differently. Choose the accurate model and explain one reason. Staff: At lesson start choose one adult with supported pupils and one holding the group; swap during the week. Record independence and prompt level.

Review (4 min): Tell a partner what you changed after feedback. Mark a real edit // and read it back when asked ?. Staff: Give a precise VF or NS(+) only when it actually happens. Invite a true self-edit and read-back.

Exit (3 min): Show what you know with a model and explain one reason. Staff: Hear the answer privately in a valid mode and use it to change the next example or prompt.

Planned adaptation — cognition learning: Scaffolding: Place one completed enlarged string model beside an unlabelled model; fade only the gene marker.

Planned adaptation — communication interaction: Flexible grouping: Rehearse “part of” with a partner and allow point/sign/AAC for a labelled explanation.

Planned adaptation — semh: Cognitive/metacognitive strategies: Give a private heads-up before critique of a faulty model and a pause choice; return to one label.

Planned adaptation — sensory physical: Technology: Provide large high-contrast string and an easy-grip clip, or a digital labelled image with speech-to-text.

Planned adaptation — eal: Explicit instruction: Use the same four term cards with definition and sentence; allow any-language planning and M/H explanation frames. Keep M and H scientific demand available.

Planned adaptation — eef strategy: cognition learning: scaffolding; communication interaction: flexible grouping; semh: cognitive/metacognitive strategies; sensory physical: technology; eal: explicit instruction

Staff: confirm the planned adaptations actually used and record any changes below.

If behind — must teach core: Two diagrams label a gene differently. Choose the accurate model and explain one reason. Preserve the individual evidence required above.

If behind — shorten or merge: After a secure hinge, shorten repeated oral rehearsal. If needed, merge the exit reflection into the Arrival of Autumn 2 W05. Keep the core evidence and any safety teaching; record the change and coverage impact.

If behind — next lesson: Autumn 2 W05: Complete a genetic cross (digital Punnett square).

If behind — dependency: Pass on today’s checked understanding and unresolved hinge errors for the next starter. Do not replace incomplete individual work with a group product.

## Individual target opportunities

Cognition & learning: We do: Label the short marked DNA section “gene” and explain it codes for a specific protein. Record the pupil’s own sequencing, attention or independence target, including the prompt level needed.

Communication & interaction: Check: respond to “A new diagram marks a short DNA region carrying instructions for one protein. Which label belongs to that region?” in the pupil’s agreed mode. Evidence an individual communication/turn-taking target without requiring speech.

SEMH: Arrival: Look at this week’s string-and-clip DNA model. Point to one detail you notice. Use the agreed predictable start or pause/re-entry plan; evidence the pupil’s own regulation or engagement target privately.

Sensory/physical: Independent: Two diagrams label a gene differently. Choose the accurate model and explain one reason. Offer the agreed accessible input, enlarged material or adapted equipment; record the pupil’s target-related access/independence, not a diagnosis.

EAL: Model/shared rehearsal: use DNA, gene, chromosome, genome with the actual example; allow first-language planning and oral rehearsal before the independent response. Record the agreed language target; EAL is not SEN.

Capture E against the pupil's own IEDP target on the school's evidence app; the IEDP carries their EHCP-linked and Boxall targets, so one record serves every plan. Follow the pupil's IBSP where it differs from these routines.
