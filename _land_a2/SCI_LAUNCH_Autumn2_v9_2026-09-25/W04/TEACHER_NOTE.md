## AfL hinge and misconceptions

**Question at We do 2:** A new diagram marks a short DNA region carrying instructions for one protein. Which label belongs to that region?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| A. A genome containing the whole cell. | no | Treats a short DNA region as the whole genome. |
| B. A gene within the DNA molecule. | yes | Uses the new case accurately. |
| C. A complete cell containing no DNA. | no | Labels a DNA segment as an entire cell and overlooks its DNA. |
| D. A chromosome containing the whole genome. | no | Uses chromosome for the marked gene and confuses one chromosome with the whole genome. |

**Decision:** About 3 in 4 or more correct: proceed; otherwise re-model a coloured section on one long string and ask the parallel hinge. No auto-grade. **Parallel hinge:** A second diagram outlines an organism’s complete DNA set. Which label fits: gene or genome? Answer: genome; it includes all its DNA.

| Misconception | How to spot | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| A chromosome and a gene are the same whole structure. | Pupil chooses D, calling the marked segment a whole chromosome. | Uncoil the string and point to one marked section for a gene, then the full packaged DNA for a chromosome. |
| A gene is a protein or a separate cell. | Pupil chooses C, calling the DNA region a complete cell. | The clip is a marker; the short section underneath it is DNA and codes for a protein. |

## Prepared model handling · practical safety

**Kit:** Short DNA cord, large blunt clips, genome and chromosome frames, tray

**Method:** Mark one gene with a blunt clip and nest the cord model in its frames.

**Pupil Risk:** A clip could pinch a finger, and a loose cord could catch.

**Pupil Safe:** Use large blunt clips and short cord on a tray. Keep the cord away from necks.
