# SCI_LAUNCH_A2_W01 · teacher note

**Objective (verbatim):** Describe mitosis & the cell cycle (animation).  
**Scheme of work:** `LAUNCH scheme of work, Autumn 2 week 1`.  
**Pupil words:** I am learning to order the cell cycle and explain why a cell makes two matching cells.  
**Reading gate:** LAUNCH 11+; ≈14.7 years (rough visible-body ARI grade + 6; 895 pupil-visible words, excluding data-mbm-guide). This is a rough text-screening proxy, not a pupil's measured reading age. Technical terms and short task directions make different formulas disagree.  
**Specification:** Shared Pearson Edexcel GCSE Biology 1BI0 Foundation / Combined Science 1SC0 Foundation Topic 2 points 1.1 and 2.1–2.3. The large-stage diagram plus full and faded phase sequence name interphase, prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase and cytokinesis from 2.1. Growth/repair and matching body cells address 2.2–2.3. This one lesson does not teach asexual reproduction, full molecular detail or the term diploid. The drawn symbols are simplified; use the official [Biology specification](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/GCSE/Science/2016/Specification/gcse-biology-spec.pdf), [Combined Science specification](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/GCSE/Science/2016/Specification/gcse-combinedscience-spec.pdf) and [Pearson mark scheme](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/GCSE/Science/2016/Exam-materials/1bi0-1h-rms-20240822.pdf) for more detailed phase descriptions.

**Faded worked example:** I do full model: Interphase: copy DNA. Mitosis: prophase (condense), metaphase (line up), anaphase (the two copies of each chromosome are pulled to opposite ends), telophase (two nuclei). Cytokinesis: split the cell. We do part-done: Interphase: copy DNA. Mitosis: prophase (condense), metaphase (line up), ________, telophase (two nuclei). Cytokinesis: split the cell. The removed step is **Anaphase: the two copies of each chromosome are pulled to opposite ends.** Independent task uses the model without a filled answer. **Fade next time:** Next lesson remove the metaphase cue as well; ask pupils to rebuild the sequence from the model before checking.  
**Check for understanding after I do:** “Show me: is DNA copied before, during or after mitosis?” Expected: Before mitosis, in interphase. Pupils show a whiteboard answer or point to the first panel; re-model the paper strips if the response is insecure.

## Challenge and stretch

**We do support:** Use the full model beside the faded one. Point to the two nuclei before choosing the missing step.  
**We do stretch:** Explain why swapping mitosis and DNA copying would break the model.  
**Independent F:** Order the three cards and show the same chromosome colours in each new cell. Stem: First …; then …; last …  
**Independent M:** Explain why each new cell has a matching chromosome set. Stem: Each new cell matches because …  
**Independent H:** Predict what would happen to the sets if copied chromosomes did not separate evenly. Stem: If the copies did not separate evenly, then … because …  
**Go deeper:** How do copy, separate and split work together when new cells are needed for repair?  
**Named early-finisher stretch — Fault finder:** Fault finder: compare two faulty models. One skips DNA copying; the other puts both copies of one chromosome in one nucleus. Find each first error and propose a repair. It compares two distinct faults and proposes repairs, beyond the H prediction of a single unequal separation.

## Hinge and misconceptions

**We do 2 question:** A new body-cell model starts with six chromosomes. After DNA copying, mitosis and cell division, what should each daughter cell contain?  
**Correct C:** C. The same six-chromosome set as the original body cell.  
**Three wrong-answer diagnoses:** A — Confuses body-cell mitosis with chromosome halving in meiosis. B — Counts the copied chromosomes before separation as the number retained by each daughter cell. D — Assumes copied chromosomes stay in the parent cell instead of separating into two cells.  
**Decision:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on; otherwise re-model copying and splitting with two blue and two orange paper strips, then ask the parallel hinge. No automatic grade or saved response.  
**Parallel hinge:** A different body-cell model starts with eight chromosomes. After ordinary mitosis and cell division, how many are in each daughter? Answer: eight, with a matching set.  
**Two anticipated misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot it | Correct it |
|---|---|---|
| Mitosis halves the chromosome number to make gametes. | Pupil chooses A, the halved three-chromosome set. | Contrast mitosis with meiosis: for this body-cell model, copy and separate so both cells keep the same chromosome set. |
| Mitosis alone is the whole cell split. | Pupil chooses D, leaving copies in one cell. | Point to the final cytokinesis arrow; move the two paper circles apart to represent two cells. |

**Two on-screen interactions:** three-step sequence with keyboard-native selects, an explicit check and polite live feedback; four-option all-pupil hinge, answered simultaneously by whiteboard, symbol or point and discussed, without an automatic grade. JS holds only transient form state; reset clears it.

## TA Focus, prompting and independent stage

**At lesson start:** choose the teacher or TA to work with supported pupils at Independent; the other holds the group. Swap roles across the week. The adult with the group can still ask the cold-call bank; no public demand on an individual pupil to disclose a need.  
**TA line at I do:** “Watch the DNA copy. Point to where the two matching sets go.”  
**TA line at We do:** “You have the first and last steps. What might happen between them?”  
**TA line at Independent:** “Show me the last step you know. What does the model tell you next?”  
**Prompt ladder, least first; no physical prompting:**

1. Wait: count to five and watch for an independent move.
2. Point or gesture to the last complete card or model step.
3. Verbal cue: “What happened to the copied chromosomes?”
4. Model one move with one colour; pupil repeats with the other colour.
5. Finish one step together; then ask the pupil to try a fresh step alone.

**Cold-call bank (adult use, two per tier):**

| Tier | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| F | Which stage copies DNA? | Interphase. |
| F | Which step splits the whole cell? | Cytokinesis. |
| M | Why do the daughter cells have matching sets? | DNA was copied and the matching chromosome sets separated into two nuclei. |
| M | How does mitosis help growth? | It produces more body cells with matching chromosome sets. |
| H | What happens to the two copies of each chromosome during anaphase? | The two copies of each chromosome are pulled to opposite ends. |
| H | Why is the order copy, separate, split important? | The copies must exist before separation, and the cell divides after each nucleus has a set. |

**Response modes at Independent:** point, sign, speak, draw, type, write, scribe or staff-captured photo. Record the actual scientific step and prompt level, not the access aid itself. **Handoff at Exit:** tell the other adult the prompt level reached, what was independent, the feedback given and the next step; the teacher uses the exit answer to choose the following model, question or prompt.
