Arrival (5 min): Point to the first and last picture in the cell strip. What changed? Staff: Offer the same strip as large print or tactile paper shapes; accept pointing before speaking.

Starter (3 min): Recall three things from earlier lessons. Show one answer at a time. Staff: Ask the three retrieval questions in order, reveal answers after all pupils respond, then point to the three success criteria.

I Do (5 min): Watch the full model: copy the DNA, separate the matching chromosomes, then split the cell. Staff: Show the paper-strip model; pause for the show-me check before the next stage.

We Do (7 min): Complete the missing step in the part-done model. Then place the paper strips in both new cells. Staff: Run paired talk before the card or select task; offer the support route without giving the missing step.

We Do 2 (5 min): Choose one hinge answer. Show it at the same time as the group. Staff: Use simultaneous whiteboards, point or symbol cards. Apply the decision rule and parallel hinge in the note.

Independent (8 min): Use the model to explain how cell division helps replace damaged body cells. Staff: One adult supports pupils; the other holds the group, swapping roles across the week. Record actual independence and prompt level.

Review (4 min): Tell your partner what you changed after feedback. Mark a real self-edit // and read it back when asked ?. Staff: Give one specific piece of VF, allow a true self-edit and record only feedback that occurred.

Exit (3 min): Show how you know: order the three stages and explain why the new cells match. Staff: Hear every response through speech, sign, point, drawing or writing; use it to change the next example or prompt.

Planned adaptation — cognition learning: Use three large cards with a four-phase mini-strip inside mitosis; remove the anaphase cue, then fade metaphase next lesson.

Planned adaptation — communication interaction: Give two quiet partner turns and the same speaking frame before any writing; accept point, sign or AAC for the explanation.

Planned adaptation — semh: Check in before the top rung and hinge; offer an agreed pause and a one-step return without public correction.

Planned adaptation — sensory physical: Offer enlarged, high-contrast, easy-grip chromosome strips and a seated or standing choice; accept speech-to-text.

Planned adaptation — eal: Pre-teach the science terms with diagram and example sentences; allow planning in any language and the same M/H scientific task with a speaking frame.

Planned adaptation — eef strategy: cognition learning: scaffolding; communication interaction: flexible grouping; semh: cognitive/metacognitive strategies; sensory physical: technology; eal: explicit instruction

## Individual target opportunities

Cognition & learning: We do: Anaphase: the two copies of each chromosome are pulled to opposite ends. Record the pupil’s own sequencing, attention or independence target, including the prompt level needed.

Communication & interaction: Check: respond to “A new body-cell model starts with six chromosomes. After DNA copying, mitosis and cell division, what should each daughter cell contain?” in the pupil’s agreed mode. Evidence an individual communication/turn-taking target without requiring speech.

SEMH: Arrival: Point to the first and last picture in the cell strip. What changed? Use the agreed predictable start or pause/re-entry plan; evidence the pupil’s own regulation or engagement target privately.

Sensory/physical: Independent: Use the model to explain how cell division helps replace damaged body cells. Offer the agreed accessible input, enlarged material or adapted equipment; record the pupil’s target-related access/independence, not a diagnosis.

EAL: Model/shared rehearsal: use cell cycle, interphase, mitosis, chromosome, cytokinesis, prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase with the actual example; allow first-language planning and oral rehearsal before the independent response. Record the agreed language target; EAL is not SEN.
