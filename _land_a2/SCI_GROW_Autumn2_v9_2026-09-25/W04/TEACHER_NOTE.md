# GROW_SCI_A2_W04 · GROW Science teacher note

**Staff-facing; review only. Verbatim SoW objective:** Describe causes and effects of global warming and how to reduce our impact.  
**Source:** `GROW scheme of work, Autumn 2 week 4`. **Pupil goal:** explain a cause and an effect of global warming, then compare two kinds of action.

## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “Burn fossil fuel → add carbon dioxide → more heat stays → heat risks. Cut fuel use to cut emissions; plan shade to help with heat.” We do part-done: “Burn fuel → add carbon dioxide → ____ → heat risks.” **Removed step:** Explain that greenhouse gases slow heat leaving Earth. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Next time omit the gas card and ask pupils to choose both middle links from a mixed set.

We do F: Lay the first and last cards; point to each missing space before choosing a card. M: Explain two chain links. H: Explain why a flood defence does not remove carbon dioxide already added by burning fuel. Independent F: Order one cause and effect. M: Explain two chain links. H: Compare an emissions cut with an adaptation. **Go deeper:** Why might a council cut fuel use and plan flood defences? Name a limit of each. **Named harder stretch — Two kinds of action:** Choose a council goal, justify the first action and explain the problem still left.

The model, source or practical route is: warming-chain cards, ice and card observation trays. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and Which action helps with an impact rather than cutting emissions?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. The diagram is original and simplified, not to scale. Its labels are checked in the facts ledger.

**Practical:** Predict first: Will ice on dark and light card melt alike? Kit: Two equal ice pieces, Two trays, Dark card, Light card, Staff clock. Steps: Put equal ice pieces on cards in trays; Place both trays in the same light; Observe both at one set time; Record what you see. Say why this is not a climate model. Dry spills; use no heat source. This surface observation is not proof of the greenhouse effect.

## Hinge and decision

**Question:** A bus depot burns more diesel. Which immediate link belongs in its emissions chain?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| More carbon dioxide is released. | yes | Uses the new case accurately. |
| The extra carbon dioxide appears only when the depot has less daylight. | no | Makes the emissions depend on daylight instead of fuel burnt. |
| The depot warms first; that warmth makes its engines release carbon dioxide. | no | Reverses the chain: warming follows added greenhouse gases, rather than causing this exhaust. |
| Only local air pollution changes; the amount of carbon dioxide stays the same. | no | Confuses a local air-quality effect with the absence of carbon-dioxide emissions. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on. Otherwise re-model with the named I-do example, then ask the parallel hinge. **Parallel:** Third case: a building burns less gas for heat. What changes first in this simplified chain? Answer: less carbon dioxide from burning that gas, all else equal.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Less daylight controls whether diesel exhaust releases carbon dioxide. | Pupil chooses B, making emissions depend on daylight. | Trace the immediate fuel-burning step regardless of daylight. |
| Diesel exhaust only changes local air quality and adds no carbon dioxide. | Pupil chooses D, treating local pollution as the only diesel effect. | Separate local air-quality effects from the added greenhouse gas. |


**AQA Entry Level Science style bridge (classroom planning):** Order and explain two links in an environmental cause-and-effect chain. This is a planning bridge, not a claimed unit outcome or code.

## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the lesson’s diagram, kit or source. → Ask: What is done, and what is the next step? → Model one step, then return the next step to the pupil. → Finish one example together, then fade the help. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Watch me place cause before effect.”; We do “Which link is in place? What might go between gas and impact?”; Independent “Point to your first link. Which part can you explain on your own?”

At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Point to the cause card. → Burning fossil fuels.; Point to a possible effect. → More heat or a greater flood risk, in some places.
- M: What is added to the air when fuel burns? → Carbon dioxide.; What does a greenhouse gas do? → It slows heat loss from Earth.
- H: How do emissions cuts and flood defences differ? → One limits the cause; the other helps with an impact.; Why does a hot day alone not prove a climate trend? → Climate is a pattern over many years.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause signal and supervised place. Check in before hinge and Independent; point to the last completed step on re-entry. No evidence while a pupil is distressed.
