## Model, challenge and stretch

I do full model: Torch still → dot faces the torch: day → turn the ball → dot faces away: night. Earth’s rotation makes the Sun seem to move.
We do part-done: Torch still → dot faces the torch: day → turn the ball → dot faces away: ____.
**Removed step:** Name night when the dot faces away from the torch.
**Fade next time:** Next time omit the day label too; ask pupils to name both positions before word cards.
Independent has no worked steps; adults can help handle kit without supplying the missing reasoning.

We do F support route: Point to the torch and dot before naming the missing step.
We do M route: Fill the blank and explain why.
We do H stretch route: Explain why the torch need not move to change the view at the dot.

| Tier | Task | Speaking stem |
|---|---|---|
| F | Point to the lit side and the dark side. | I point to ___ for day and ___ for night. |
| M | Explain what the dot sees as Earth turns. | The dot has night when ___ because ___. |
| H | Explain why the Sun seems to move across our sky while the model torch stays still. | The Sun seems to move because ___. The model shows ___. |

Go deeper: Mark two dots on opposite sides. Explain why one has day while the other has night.
**Named early-finisher stretch — Two dots, one turn:** Two dots, one turn: test the claim that the Sun goes round Earth each day. Show the motion that explains both dots. Name one limit of the torch model.

## Hinge at We do 2

Ask: On a new ball model, the red dot has just turned from light into shade. What does the shade represent? All pupils respond together by whiteboard, finger-vote, symbol card, thumbs or point. Do not auto-grade or save.

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Day at the red dot. | no | Keeps the old day label after the dot has rotated into shade. |
| The Sun turning off now. | no | Treats darkness at one place as the Sun switching off. |
| The whole Earth going dark. | no | Extends one dot’s night to every place on Earth. |
| Night at the red dot. | yes | Uses the new case accurately. |

Decision: About 3 in 4 or more right → move on. Otherwise re-model with the dot on the far side of the ball, then ask the parallel hinge.
Parallel hinge: A blue dot turns from shade into torchlight. Which part of a day does this represent? Answer: daytime at that dot.

Two likely misconceptions:

| Misconception | Spot | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Night at one dot means night everywhere on Earth. | Pupil chooses C, “The whole Earth going dark.” | Turn the ball: one dot can be in shade while another still faces the light. |
| The Sun switches off when our place has night. | Pupil chooses B, “The Sun turning off now.” | Keep the torch on and turn the ball; the dot moves into shade. |

## Practical and safety

Prediction: Predict: if the dot turns away from the torch, will it have day or night?
Kit: Torch, Ball or globe, Removable dot, Stable stand if needed
Method:
1. Place the dot on the ball.
2. Keep the torch still and aim it at the ball, away from eyes.
3. Turn the dot to face the torch; record day or night.
4. Turn the dot away; record day or night.

Results table: dot faces torch / day or night; dot faces away / day or night.
Risk: Check against the school risk assessment and CLEAPSS guidance. Do not shine the torch into eyes.
