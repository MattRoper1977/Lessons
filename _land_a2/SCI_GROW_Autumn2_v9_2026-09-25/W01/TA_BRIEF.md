Arrival (5 min): Place a dot on the ball. Point to the side lit by the torch. Staff: Offer a seat or standing spot. End with a one-minute reset.

Starter (3 min): Think back. Say, point or write one answer for each question. Staff: Ask three low-stakes questions without a public test.

I Do (5 min): Watch: the torch stays still. I turn the ball. The dot has day when it faces the light. It has night when it faces away. Staff: Model the full sequence. Keep light out of eyes. Ask the show-me check.

We Do (7 min): Now try the same model. Two steps are done. Complete the last step: turn the dot away and name what comes next. Staff: Wait for the missing step. End with a one-minute reset.

We Do 2 (5 min): Turn the ball. Choose where the dot is when it is night. Show your choice with the group. Staff: Use an all-pupil hinge response and the decision rule in the note.

Independent (8 min): Show day and night at the dot. Explain why the Sun seems to move, even when our torch stays still. Staff: One adult supports agreed pupils, the other holds the group; swap across the week. One-minute reset at end.

Review (4 min): Tell a partner what your model shows. Hear one comment. Change one part and say what you changed. Staff: Give one specific comment, invite a self-edit (//) or read-back (?).

Exit (3 min): Show how you know. Tell us your feedback and next step. Say, point, sign, draw or write. Staff: Hear answer, choose next model or help; hand over highest prompt and independent part.

Planned adaptation — cognition learning: Use a three-step dot and torch card. Omit its last word, then fade the card.

Planned adaptation — communication interaction: Point to the model before a partner rehearsal with the M or H speaking frame.

Planned adaptation — semh: Offer a known kit role, a quiet pause cue and one small re-entry step.

Planned adaptation — sensory physical: Use a stable stand or a large tactile dot; a photo can capture a genuine demonstration.

Planned adaptation — eal: Pre-teach science words with the diagram. Plan in any language; use M or H speaking frame before writing. EAL is language access, not a reason to move down to F.

Planned adaptation — eef strategy: cognition learning: scaffolding; communication interaction: scaffolding; semh: flexible grouping; sensory physical: technology; eal: explicit instruction

## Individual target opportunities

Cognition & learning: We do: Name night when the dot faces away from the torch. Record the pupil’s own sequencing, attention or independence target, including the prompt level needed.

Communication & interaction: Check: respond to “On a new ball model, the red dot has just turned from light into shade. What does the shade represent?” in the pupil’s agreed mode. Evidence an individual communication/turn-taking target without requiring speech.

SEMH: Arrival: Place a dot on the ball. Point to the side lit by the torch. Use the agreed predictable start or pause/re-entry plan; evidence the pupil’s own regulation or engagement target privately.

Sensory/physical: Independent: Show day and night at the dot. Explain why the Sun seems to move, even when our torch stays still. Offer the agreed accessible input, enlarged material or adapted equipment; record the pupil’s target-related access/independence, not a diagnosis.

EAL: Model/shared rehearsal: use rotation, axis, shadow, orbit with the actual example; allow first-language planning and oral rehearsal before the independent response. Record the agreed language target; EAL is not SEN.
