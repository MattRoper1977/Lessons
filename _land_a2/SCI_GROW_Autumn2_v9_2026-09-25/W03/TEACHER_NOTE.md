## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “Claim: Earth goes round the Sun → card B says the same → cite card B.” We do part-done: “Claim: The Moon goes round Earth → card C says the same → cite card ____.” **Removed step:** Choose and name the source card for the Moon claim. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Next time omit the ready claim and ask the pupil to choose a claim from two cards.

We do F: Point to the sentence on card B and read or hear it before choosing its label. M: Share two facts and name each matching card. H: Explain why card A supports the Sun claim but does not support the Moon claim. Independent F: Point to one fact and its source card. M: Share two facts and name each matching card. H: Use two cards to explain Earth’s place, then repair a false label. **Go deeper:** A poster calls the Moon a planet. Use two class cards to repair the label and name both sources. **Named harder stretch — Source check:** Two cards disagree about the Moon. Choose the supported claim, then name a reliable source for a further check.

The model, source or practical route is: three offline source cards, index cards, pencils. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and Which claim can you support from card C?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. The diagram is original and simplified, not to scale. Its labels are checked in the facts ledger.

## Hinge and decision

**Question:** Which claim is backed by our class cards?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Earth makes its own light. | no | Confuses a planet with a star. |
| The Sun is a star. | yes | Correct use of card A. |
| The Moon is a planet. | no | Confuses a natural satellite with a planet. |
| Card C says Earth goes round the Sun. | no | Chooses the wrong source label even for a true fact. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on. Otherwise re-model with the named I-do example, then ask the parallel hinge. **Parallel:** Which card backs ‘The Moon goes round Earth’: A, B or C? Answer: C.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Earth makes its own light like a star. | Pupil chooses A, “Earth makes its own light.” | Use card A for the Sun as a star; Earth is a planet that receives light. |
| Any card can support a true claim. | Pupil chooses D, which names card C for an orbit claim not on that card. | Match the actual wording of the claim with card B. |


**AQA Entry Level Science style bridge (classroom planning):** Present one fact and name its matching source. This is a planning bridge, not a claimed unit outcome or code.

**Feedback codes:** VF · WS · I · NS(+) · E · R · // · ?. At We do 2, record WS or I and actual prompt level; at Review, VF and specific NS(+); at Exit, one self-edit // or read back ?. R means a response to the next step heard by an adult in the next lesson, never auto-awarded. E: evidence captured on the school's evidence app only if an observable curriculum action occurred, never while distressed. The page stores no response. Pathways: high support with VF/WS; Build: mix of WS/I; Grow: mostly I and self-edit; Launch: independent self-assessment.

## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the lesson’s diagram, kit or source. → Ask: What is done, and what is the next step? → Model one step, then return the next step to the pupil. → Finish one example together, then fade the help. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Listen to my claim, then watch me point to card B.”; We do “Which part is done? Where is the card label still missing?”; Independent “Which card gives your fact? Show that part without my help.”

At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Which card says the Sun is a star? → A.; Which card says Earth goes round the Sun? → B.
- M: How do you show a source? → Name the matching card.; Why must the card match the claim? → A different card might not support it.
- H: Why is ‘Moon is a planet’ wrong? → It is a natural satellite of Earth.; What would you check next if two cards disagree? → A reliable astronomy source, such as NASA.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause signal and supervised place. Check in before hinge and Independent; point to the last completed step on re-entry. No evidence while a pupil is distressed.
