**Staff-facing; review only. Verbatim SoW objective:** Describe the Sun, Earth and Moon as approximately spherical bodies.  
**Source:** `GROW scheme of work, Autumn 2 week 2`. **Pupil goal:** show how a ball models the Sun, Earth and Moon.

Arrival 5 · Starter 3 · I do 5 · We do 7 · We do 2 5 · Independent 8 · Review 4 · Exit 3 = 40. One-minute resets are inside We do 2, Review and Exit. Success: I can choose a ball to model a round body., I can turn it to show more than one side., I can name one limit of a model.

## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “Ball → turn it → see another curved side → useful for an approximately spherical body; ball sizes and gaps are not to scale.” We do part-done: “Ball → turn it → see ____ → useful because ____.” **Removed step:** Name the new curved side and explain why it helps. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Next time remove the ball cue and ask for a model choice before showing kit.

We do F: Point to the ball and the paper edge before you turn the model. M: Turn the ball and explain what new view it gives. H: Explain why one photo can look circular but cannot show the full solid shape. Independent F: Choose a ball rather than a flat circle. M: Turn the ball and explain what new view it gives. H: Explain what a ball model reveals and how its scale may mislead. **Go deeper:** Compare a ball and a photo. What can each show, and what does each hide? **Named harder stretch — Model limits:** A photo shows Earth as a circle. Choose another observation or model to test its full shape. Explain why one photo is not enough.

The model, source or practical route is: safe balls, paper circles, torch. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and What can show another curved side when turned?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. The diagram is original and simplified, not to scale. **Practical:** Predict first: Predict which object can turn to show a different curved side: ball or paper circle? Kit: Three safe balls or modelling spheres, Paper circles, Torch with adult-controlled beam, Tray or stable surface. Steps: Compare a ball with a flat circle; Turn the ball once and record what new side you see; Turn the paper circle and record what it shows; Put the torch on the ball; do not point it into eyes. ## Hinge and decision

**Question:** A class models the Moon’s whole shape. Which object is the closest simple shape?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| A thin flat disc. | no | Uses a flat circle for a three-dimensional Moon. |
| A long straight stick. | no | Mistakes a long one-dimensional shape for a round body. |
| A round solid ball. | yes | Uses the new case accurately. |
| A flat square sheet. | no | Chooses a flat square because the diagram is on paper. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on. Otherwise re-model with the named I-do example, then ask the parallel hinge. **Parallel:** Which simple shape could model the Sun’s whole shape: ball or flat ring? Answer: ball; neither size nor gaps are to scale.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| A circle on paper is a sphere. | Pupil chooses A, “A thin flat disc.” | Turn a ball to reveal a new side; a flat circle cannot do that. |
| Spherical means perfectly smooth and exactly round. | Pupil describes C as perfectly smooth and exactly round. | Say approximately spherical; the real bodies are not exact toy balls. |


**AQA Entry Level Science style bridge (classroom planning):** Describe what you see when you turn a shape model. This is a planning bridge, not a claimed unit outcome or code.

## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the lesson’s diagram, kit or source. → Ask: What is done, and what is the next step? → Model one step, then return the next step to the pupil. → Finish one example together, then fade the help. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Watch the ball, then point to the side you can see.”; We do “What is done? What does turning show next?”; Independent “Show your model choice. What can you explain yourself?”

At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Point to the solid shape. → The ball.; Point to the flat shape. → The paper circle.
- M: What changes when you turn the ball? → The side we can see.; Why is the ball a useful shape model? → It has a curved surface on more than one side.
- H: What is one limit of the toy-ball model? → Its size, texture or distance may not match the real body.; Why is one flat photo not enough? → It only shows one view of a solid body.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause signal and supervised place. Check in before hinge and Independent; point to the last completed step on re-entry. No evidence while a pupil is distressed.
