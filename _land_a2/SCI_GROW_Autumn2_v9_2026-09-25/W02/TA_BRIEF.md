Arrival (5 min): Sort a coin, paper circle and ball: flat or solid? Staff: Offer a predictable seat or standing spot and the first visual cue. No public test.

Starter (3 min): Think back. Say, point or write one answer for each question. Staff: Ask three low-stakes retrieval questions; prior coverage must be checked.

I Do (5 min): Watch me turn a ball. I can see another curved side. A paper circle is flat. A ball helps show the shape of the Sun, Earth and Moon, but its size is not exact. Staff: Model the full reasoning step, pre-teach four words and ask the show-me question.

We Do (7 min): Use a ball and a paper circle. The model and first label are ready. Turn the ball and complete the missing label: new side or the same flat face? Staff: Show the part-done example, then allow paired talk before an answer. One-minute reset inside this stage.

We Do 2 (5 min): Show everyone which shape model lets us turn to see another side. Staff: All pupils answer the four-option hinge; use the decision rule, not a score.

Independent (8 min): Turn a shape model. Explain one way it helps and one limit. You can show, say or draw. Staff: One adult supports agreed pupils, the other holds the group; swap across the week. One-minute reset inside this stage.

Review (4 min): Hear a comment on your model. Change one label or spoken reason and say what changed. Staff: Give a specific verbal comment (VF) and invite one self-edit (//) or read-back (?).

Exit (3 min): Show which shape model helps. Say one way it helps and one thing it cannot show exactly. Staff: Hear the answer and name how it changes the next example or support. One-minute reset and handoff.

Planned adaptation — cognition learning: Give a tactile ball and a two-step turn card, then remove the turn cue.

Planned adaptation — communication interaction: Let pupils rehearse the M or H model explanation with a partner and pointing cue.

Planned adaptation — semh: Offer a safe kit role and quiet pause cue before the model comparison.

Planned adaptation — sensory physical: Use a large stable ball or textured ball; allow a photo of the pupil’s real demonstration.

Planned adaptation — eal: Teach spherical with a ball and gesture. Keep M/H open with the speaking frame and home-language planning.

Planned adaptation — eef strategy: cognition learning: scaffolding; communication interaction: cognitive/metacognitive strategies; semh: flexible grouping; sensory physical: technology; eal: explicit instruction

## Individual target opportunities

Cognition & learning: We do: Name the new curved side and explain why it helps. Record the pupil’s own sequencing, attention or independence target, including the prompt level needed.

Communication & interaction: Check: respond to “A class models the Moon’s whole shape. Which object is the closest simple shape?” in the pupil’s agreed mode. Evidence an individual communication/turn-taking target without requiring speech.

SEMH: Arrival: Sort a coin, paper circle and ball: flat or solid? Use the agreed predictable start or pause/re-entry plan; evidence the pupil’s own regulation or engagement target privately.

Sensory/physical: Independent: Turn a shape model. Explain one way it helps and one limit. You can show, say or draw. Offer the agreed accessible input, enlarged material or adapted equipment; record the pupil’s target-related access/independence, not a diagnosis.

EAL: Model/shared rehearsal: use spherical, curve, shadow, model with the actual example; allow first-language planning and oral rehearsal before the independent response. Record the agreed language target; EAL is not SEN.
