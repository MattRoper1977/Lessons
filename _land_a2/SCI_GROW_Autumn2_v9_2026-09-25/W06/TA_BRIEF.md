Arrival (5 min): Choose the question card: does surface change how far this toy goes? Point to what we will change. Staff: Offer a predictable seat or standing spot and the first visual cue. No public test.

Starter (3 min): Think back. Say, point or write one answer for each question. Staff: Ask three low-stakes retrieval questions; prior coverage must be checked.

I Do (5 min): I mark a start line. I keep the toy and launch the same. I change the surface, measure distance and repeat. Runs may still vary. Staff: Model the full reasoning step, pre-teach four words and ask the show-me question.

We Do (7 min): The plan has a fixed toy and start line. Add the missing launch control. Name the surface to change. Staff: Show the part-done example, then allow paired talk before an answer. One-minute reset inside this stage.

We Do 2 (5 min): Everyone chooses the fair plan for comparing two surfaces. Staff: All pupils answer the four-option hinge; use the decision rule, not a score.

Independent (8 min): Test two runs per surface or observe. Record distance. Name what changed, stayed the same and could limit the test. Staff: One adult supports agreed pupils, the other holds the group; swap across the week. One-minute reset inside this stage.

Review (4 min): Check the controls and result with a partner. Fix one label before you conclude. Staff: Give a specific verbal comment (VF) and invite one self-edit (//) or read-back (?).

Exit (3 min): Name the change, one control, one result and a next step. Staff: Hear the answer and name how it changes the next example or support. One-minute reset and handoff.

## Individual target opportunities

Cognition & learning: We do: Name surface as the changed factor and a consistent launch as a control. Record the pupil’s own sequencing, attention or independence target, including the prompt level needed.

Communication & interaction: Check: respond to “A team compares carpet and vinyl using one trolley. Which plan lets them test the surface fairly?” in the pupil’s agreed mode. Evidence an individual communication/turn-taking target without requiring speech.

SEMH: Arrival: Choose the question card: does surface change how far this toy goes? Point to what we will change. Use the agreed predictable start or pause/re-entry plan; evidence the pupil’s own regulation or engagement target privately.

Sensory/physical: Independent: Test two runs per surface or observe. Record distance. Name what changed, stayed the same and could limit the test. Offer the agreed accessible input, enlarged material or adapted equipment; record the pupil’s target-related access/independence, not a diagnosis.

EAL: Model/shared rehearsal: use variable, fair test, result, repeat with the actual example; allow first-language planning and oral rehearsal before the independent response. Record the agreed language target; EAL is not SEN.

Capture E against the pupil's own IEDP target on the school's evidence app; the IEDP carries their EHCP-linked and Boxall targets, so one record serves every plan. Follow the pupil's IBSP where it differs from these routines.
