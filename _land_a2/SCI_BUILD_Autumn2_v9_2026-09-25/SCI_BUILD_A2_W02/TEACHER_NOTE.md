## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “This rock feels rough. I put it with the rough rocks.” We do part-done: “This rock feels smooth. I put it with the ____ rocks.” **Removed step:** Name the group from an observed property. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Show two unlabelled samples; pupil names both groups.

We do F: Feel one safe rock. Point to rough or smooth. M: Sort two real rocks. Say your rule. H: Can one rock fit two groups? Name each rule. Independent F: Look at two rocks. Point to one change. M: Sort two rocks by one thing you can check. H: Sort the same rocks in two ways. Tell why. **Go deeper:** Could one rock fit two groups? Show both rules. **Named harder stretch — Rule test:** Swap the rule. Which rocks move group, and why?

The kit route is hands-on or sensory: clean blunt rocks, optional hand lens, smooth and rough cards. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and Which step comes after look and feel?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. The diagram is original and simplified, not to scale. ## Hinge and decision

**Question:** Two rocks look alike. How can you sort them?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Check one thing you can see or feel | yes | Uses an observed property. |
| Guess from the name | no | Uses a label instead of checking. |
| Call them the same | no | Misses a possible difference. |
| Use only a photo of colour | no | Assumes one cue tells all properties. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on; otherwise re-model This rock feels rough. I put it with the rough rocks. Ask the parallel hinge. No scores or answers saved. **Parallel:** Two rocks look different. Can they both fit the smooth group? Feel them. Yes, if both are smooth.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Rocks of the same colour must have the same feel. | Uses colour only, without touching. | Use safe samples to check rough or smooth as well. |
| A rock can belong to only one group. | Rejects the same rock from a new valid sort. | Show one sample can be smooth and dark; name the rule each time. |


**Feedback codes:** VF · WS · I · NS(+) · E · R · // · ? At We do 2, record WS or I and actual prompt level; at Review, VF and specific NS(+); at Exit, one self-edit // or read back ? R means a response to the next step heard by an adult in the next lesson, never auto-awarded. E: evidence captured on the school's evidence app only if an observable curriculum action occurred, never while distressed. The page stores no response. Pathways: high support with VF/WS; Build: mix of WS/I; Grow: mostly I and self-edit; Launch: independent self-assessment.

## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the object or model. → Ask: What will you check next? → Model one step and give the next turn back. → Finish one example together, then let the pupil try. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Watch my first step. What do you see?”; We do “What step is missing? Show it with the kit.”; Independent “What can you do first on your own?”. At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Point to a rough sample. → The checked rough rock in the kit; What is a rock? → A solid thing from the ground.
- M: How do you sort two rocks? → Check a feature such as rough or smooth; What is a property? → A thing you can check.
- H: Can one rock fit two groups? → Yes. It may be smooth and dark under two different rules; What does our diagram leave out? → Real rocks must be checked by sight or safe touch.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause and supervised place. Give one short cue. Re-enter at the last completed step. No evidence while distressed.

**Pupil-facing safety:** Use clean blunt rocks. Keep them on the tray. Do not taste; wash hands after.
