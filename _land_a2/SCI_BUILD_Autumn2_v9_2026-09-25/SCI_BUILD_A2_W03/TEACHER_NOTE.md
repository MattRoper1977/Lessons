## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “Adult uses one blunt tool. A gets a mark. Three drops stay on top.” We do part-done: “Mark B. Add three drops. The drops ____.” **Removed step:** State the observed result at rock B, rather than guess it. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Pupil picks the test step and result from the unlabelled kit.

We do F: Watch one drop. Point to the top or tray. M: Try the safe test. Say what happened. H: Why must each rock get the same drops? Independent F: Point to a mark and a wet rock. M: Show both tests. Tell what you saw. H: Why use the same tool and drops? **Go deeper:** Could a hard rock also let water through? Use each test result. **Named harder stretch — Run-off check:** A drop slides off. How will you fix the test?

The kit route is hands-on or sensory: safe rocks, adult-held blunt tool, dropper, tray and towel. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and What must you check to say water passed through?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. ## Hinge and decision

**Question:** The tray under a new rock C is wet before the water test. What should the class do first?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Call C porous from the wet tray. | no | Uses water present before the test as a result. |
| Call C hard from the wet tray. | no | Confuses a water clue with hardness. |
| Skip the test and guess from colour. | no | Uses appearance instead of the planned check. |
| Dry the tray, then repeat the test. | yes | Removes the old water before observing a new result. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on; otherwise re-model Adult uses one blunt tool. A gets a mark. Three drops stay on top. Ask the parallel hinge. No scores or answers saved. **Parallel hinge:** A drop from new rock D runs around its edge into a dry tray. Does that prove water passed through D? No; check the route and repeat safely.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| A wet surface proves water went through a rock. | Points to a wet top as the result. | Check the underside and tray; state only what was observed. |
| The scratch test and water test measure the same thing. | Uses scratch result to answer a water question. | Compare two separate result columns, mark and water through. |

## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the object or model. → Ask: What will you check next? → Model one step and give the next turn back. → Finish one example together, then let the pupil try. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Watch my first step. What do you see?”; We do “What step is missing? Show it with the kit.”; Independent “What can you do first on your own?”. At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Point to the tray below the rock. → The tray that catches drops beneath it; What is a test? → A check to find out.
- M: Does a wet top prove water went through? → No. Check beneath the rock and tray; What does absorb mean? → Take in a liquid.
- H: Why use the same tool and drops? → Then the rock is the planned change in the check; What does a drawing leave out? → It cannot show whether drops truly passed through a sample.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause and supervised place. Give one short cue. Re-enter at the last completed step. No evidence while distressed.

**lesson record:** BUILD_SCI_A2_W03 via staff device or projector only. **Gatsby 4:** A lab technician compares rock samples with the same checks.

**Reading gate:** BUILD below age 8; rough ARI grade 0.61 on 648 pupil-visible words (adult guidance and navigation excluded); heuristic age guide 6.61 years, not a formal reading-age test. This is a rough screen, not an individual assessment.

**Apparatus staff to confirm:** The USGS source describes water moving through connected spaces in rock. It does not validate our open classroom tray. Inspect whether drops travel through the actual sample or around its edge; record the observed route without claiming permeability from tray drops alone.

**Pupil-facing risk:** Tools or rough rocks may hurt skin; spills may be slippery.

**Pupil-facing safety:** Let staff use the tool. Keep drops in the tray. Do not taste; wash hands after.
