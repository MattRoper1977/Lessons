## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “A gets three drops. I wait one count. B gets the same.” We do part-done: “A gets three drops. B gets ____ drops. I wait the same time.” **Removed step:** Fill three as the water amount for rock B. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Remove the amount and the time prompt; pupil plans both controls.

We do F: Place three dot counters by each rock. M: Plan what stays the same and what changes. H: Why could different sample sizes spoil this check? Independent F: Point to what stays the same. M: Plan one fair water check. H: One rock is much larger. How could you compare? **Go deeper:** Drops run off an edge. Did water pass through? Fix it. **Named harder stretch — Fairness repair:** Find two unfair steps. Fix them.

The kit route is hands-on or sensory: two safe rocks, dropper, tray and towel, dot counters. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and What should we put in each sample?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. The diagram is original and simplified, not to scale. Its labels are checked in the facts ledger.

**Practical:** Predict first: Will the drops act the same? Kit: Two like-size safe rocks, Dropper, Tray. Steps: Guess first; Add three drops to A. Wait one count; Do the same on B; Record and compare. Results headings: Rock, Drops, What happened? Check against the school risk assessment and CLEAPSS guidance. Use clean blunt samples; control the water and dry any spill.

## Hinge and decision

**Question:** New samples C and D get four and nine drops. Is the water amount controlled?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| No. Give each four drops. | yes | Keeps the water amount equal across samples. |
| Yes. Both got some water. | no | Ignores the different amounts. |
| No. Use the same rock. | no | Removes the changed sample from the comparison. |
| Yes. D could be bigger. | no | Adds an unsupported reason for unequal amounts. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on; otherwise re-model A gets three drops. I wait one count. B gets the same. Ask the parallel hinge. No scores or answers saved. **Parallel hinge:** New rocks E and F each get four drops, but E waits ten counts and F waits two. Is the wait fair? No; keep the wait the same.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Any water on both samples makes the test fair. | Accepts three drops versus ten. | Use the same amount for both while the sample changes. |
| The two tests must use the same rock to be fair. | Refuses to change sample. | Change the rock you compare; keep water amount and time the same. |


**Feedback codes:** VF · WS · I · NS(+) · E · R · // · ? At We do 2, record WS or I and actual prompt level; at Review, VF and specific NS(+); at Exit, one self-edit // or read back ? ## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the object or model. → Ask: What will you check next? → Model one step and give the next turn back. → Finish one example together, then let the pupil try. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Watch my first step. What do you see?”; We do “What step is missing? Show it with the kit.”; Independent “What can you do first on your own?”. At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Point to the dropper. → The tool used to add drops; What stays the same? → The drop count and wait time.
- M: Are three drops and ten drops fair? → No. Give each rock the same drop count; What is a result? → What the check showed.
- H: One sample is much larger. What will you do? → Use samples of similar size or state that size also changed; How could run-off fool you? → A drop in the tray may have run off the side, not through the rock.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause and supervised place. Give one short cue. Re-enter at the last completed step. No evidence while distressed.

**Pupil-facing safety:** Use blunt rocks in a tray. Wipe spills. Do not taste; wash hands after.
