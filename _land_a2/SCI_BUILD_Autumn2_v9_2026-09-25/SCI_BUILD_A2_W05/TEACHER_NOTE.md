## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “This raw rock is from nature. I write raw rock in the Natural row.” We do part-done: “People shape and fire clay to make a brick. I write brick in the ____ row.” **Removed step:** Choose Human-made as the group for the finished brick. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Give the raw and finished item with a blank table; pupil fills both rows.

We do F: Hold a raw rock. Point to its group. M: Sort four safe items. Tell your rule. H: Where do raw clay and finished brick go? Why? Independent F: Put one raw and one made item in groups. Fill one row. M: Sort four items. Add two rows to a table. H: Why is clay natural but a brick made? **Go deeper:** An object has two materials. How can your table show both? **Named harder stretch — Mixed item judge:** Choose a made item with two raw materials. Plan a fair row.

The kit route is hands-on or sensory: clean raw rock, brick card, leaf card, safe bottle picture. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and Which table row is right?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. The diagram is original and simplified, not to scale. Its labels are checked in the facts ledger.

## Hinge and decision

**Question:** People shape sand into a glass bottle. What is the finished bottle?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| A raw, natural sand sample. | no | Confuses the source material with the finished object. |
| A raw and finished rock. | no | Calls a finished item raw at the same time. |
| A human-made glass item. | yes | Names the finished object as made by people. |
| A thing outside all groups. | no | Misses the natural or human-made grouping. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on; otherwise re-model This raw rock is from nature. I write raw rock in the Natural row. Ask the parallel hinge. No scores or answers saved. **Parallel hinge:** People cut wood and assemble a chair. Is the finished chair raw wood or a human-made item? A human-made item.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| A made thing is natural whenever its raw material is natural. | Calls finished brick raw clay. | Put source clay and finished brick in separate rows. |
| A table is only a picture of groups. | Cannot name row and column information. | Point to the item column and group column, then make one row. |


**Feedback codes:** VF · WS · I · NS(+) · E · R · // · ? At We do 2, record WS or I and actual prompt level; at Review, VF and specific NS(+); at Exit, one self-edit // or read back ? ## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the object or model. → Ask: What will you check next? → Model one step and give the next turn back. → Finish one example together, then let the pupil try. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Watch my first step. What do you see?”; We do “What step is missing? Show it with the kit.”; Independent “What can you do first on your own?”. At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Point to the raw rock. → The unmade rock sample; What is a table? → Rows and columns for facts.
- M: Is a finished brick made by people? → Yes. People make it from raw clay; What is a material? → What a thing is made of.
- H: How can clay be natural but brick made? → Clay is raw material; people shape and fire the brick; How can a table show two raw materials? → Add two material entries for one made item.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause and supervised place. Give one short cue. Re-enter at the last completed step. No evidence while distressed.

## Prepared model handling · practical safety

**Kit:** Clean blunt raw rock, picture cards of made items, tray

**Method:** Hold the checked rock, sort it with the made-item cards, then fill a table row.

**Pupil Risk:** A raw rock could have a rough edge or loose dust.

**Pupil Safe:** Use only a clean blunt checked rock on a tray. Do not taste or scrape it; wash hands after.
