## Faded example, tiered task and stretch

I do full: “This slate sample is thin. Slate can make a roof tile. I match it to a roof.” We do part-done: “This sample leaves a mark on the small board. I match it to ____.” **Removed step:** Name the job based on the observed mark. Independent: no filled step. **Next fade:** Show an unlabelled safe sample and job cards; pupil tests and gives a reason.

We do F: Point to one job card. Feel one safe sample. M: Match a sample. Say which feature helps. H: Could one rock be good for one job but poor for another? Independent F: Match one sample to one job. M: Choose a rock and say what feature helps. H: Why could a soft rock fail on a busy path? **Go deeper:** Can one rock suit two jobs? Show the need for each job. **Named harder stretch — Job switch:** Choose a sample for a roof and a mark. Test each reason.

The kit route is hands-on or sensory: safe slate or other samples, small test board, job cards. Two on-screen interactions are card sort and What should come before the job match?; native keyboard controls, live feedback, no storage. The diagram is original and simplified, not to scale. Its labels are checked in the facts ledger.

## Hinge and decision

**Question:** A new sample stays firm in a safe pressure test. Which possible job fits that clue?

| Option | Correct? | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| A writing stick if it leaves a mark. | no | A mark was not observed in this test. |
| A path stone if it stays firm. | yes | Uses the observed firmness for a possible job, subject to more checks. |
| A sponge if it soaks up water. | no | Water uptake was not observed in this test. |
| A window if it lets through light. | no | Light transmission was not observed in this test. |

**Rule:** About 3 in 4 or more right → move on; otherwise re-model This slate sample is thin. Slate can make a roof tile. I match it to a roof. Ask the parallel hinge. No scores or answers saved. **Parallel hinge:** A different sample D splits into broad thin sheets. Which job could be checked next: a roof covering or a soft marking stick? A roof covering, after further checks.

**Misconceptions:**

| Misconception | Spot | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| A named rock is good for every job. | Matches one sample to all jobs. | Check a feature needed for each job. |
| Colour alone tells whether a rock will make a mark. | Chooses a colour without testing. | Try the teacher-approved marking test on a small board. |

## Adults, regulation and prompt ladder

Wait and count to 5. → Point to the object or model. → Ask: What will you check next? → Model one step and give the next turn back. → Finish one example together, then let the pupil try. No physical prompt. Stage scripts: I do “Watch my first step. What do you see?”; We do “What step is missing? Show it with the kit.”; Independent “What can you do first on your own?”. At Independent, one adult works with supported pupils, the other holds the group; choose roles at the start and swap across the week. If only one adult attends, use the actual staffing plan. At Exit, hand off the deepest prompt level and what was independent.

**Cold-call / invite bank, answers:**
- F: Point to the slate card. → The slate sample or picture; What does hard mean here? → Not easy to mark or change.
- M: Why does the slate fit the roof example? → A suitable slate can form a thin roof tile; What is a property? → A thing you can check.
- H: Can a sample suit one job but not another? → Yes. Jobs require different tested features; What does our picture leave out? → Whether this real sample meets all the needs of the job.

**Regulation:** Agree a quiet pause and supervised place. Give one short cue. Re-enter at the last completed step. No evidence while distressed.

## Prepared model handling · practical safety

**Kit:** Clean blunt adult-checked rock samples, job cards, tray

**Method:** Feel one safe face, compare a feature with a job card, then give a reason.

**Pupil Risk:** A rough or sharp sample edge could scratch skin.

**Pupil Safe:** Use clean blunt samples on a tray. Do not taste or make dust; wash hands after.
