Make The Change The Feedback Asked For

BUILD · Art · Week 3. Trinity Arts Award Bronze. Make The Change The Feedback Asked For.

Objective: apply the feedback you took, once, on one named part of your piece, and record what the change did.

Success evidence: a before state kept, one change made on one named part, and one sentence saying what it did.

Your feedback line came off an approved feedback sheet, in the words of a named person. You talk about the part, the mark and the tool. You are never asked about your home, your family, your body, how you look or what you believe. Nobody in this room is asked what they believe. Identity here means what your own artwork keeps choosing, and today you read it off the piece in front of you.

40 minutes. Offline.

This deck evidences Bronze Part A, Explore the arts as a participant. The register says Part A requires active participation in any art form, improvement, and a summary of what they learnt. Today's change and the record of what it did are the improvement evidence; the summary of what was learnt is the next Part A deck, not this one. Bronze attempted rule, in the register's words: ONE Attempted allowed overall. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass. The evidence file cap is 10 files for the whole Bronze level, so put the before state and the after state on one photo sheet rather than opening a second file. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication.
Delivery is through a validated centre; confirm local validation and an adviser trained at Bronze before assessment. Neither is asserted as completed here. Core, Extend and Challenge are support routes inside Bronze, not different awards. The prompts and sample quantities are teaching scaffolds, not extra assessment requirements. Scribe only the pupil’s own words. Store evidence by part; one cumulative file per part is a suggested four-file plan within the ten-file cap.

Starter

Starter · create the need

Two makers, one line of feedback

Two makers got the same feedback line.

The line said: the sky is too flat.

Maker one went back over the whole sheet.

They changed the sky, the hill and the tree.

The piece did look different at the end.

But nobody could say which change did it.

Maker two changed the sky and nothing else.

They pressed harder in one corner of it.

The dark corner was the only new thing.

One change can be proved. Ten changes cannot.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

I turn my feedback into one change

My feedback line says: the leaf edge is lost.

The name of the person who said it is on my sheet.

First I read the line out loud.

Reading it out stops me guessing at it.

Then I turn the line into one change.

A change needs a verb, a place and an amount.

My verb is press. My place is the leaf edge.

My amount is one pass with a 4B pencil.

Before I touch the piece I keep the before.

With no before, what it did cannot be shown.

Then I make the change once and stop.

Last I record what the change did.

My before word was lost. My after word is sharp.

My sentence says: the dark edge pushed the leaf forward.

Model the stop. Put the pencil down flat on the desk after one pass and let the pause be seen before you speak again.

The record is not a last job you rush.

3 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the lines, then match the tool

Six feedback cards are on your table.

Each card holds a line a person said.

Sort them into two piles: a change I can make, and not a change yet.

Three cards say: press harder in the dark corner. Add one line along the top edge. Mix one darker green for the leaf.

Three cards say: make it better. It needs more. I like it.

A change card has a verb, a place and an amount.

Now take the change pile and the three tool cards.

Match each change to the tool it needs.

The tool cards say: the 4B pencil. The ruler and the fineliner. The green paint and the black paint.

Every card gets a match, even when you are not sure.

A guess I can see beats a blank card.

Then hold up the card your table argued about most.

I Do 2 · connect

What it did needs a before

Now watch one change recorded two ways.

In the first I keep the before, then press the line.

I hold the before next to the piece.

The leaf edge is pale in one and dark in the other.

So what it did can be seen by anybody.

In the second I press the line first.

Then I write: it got better.

Better is my word, and nobody can check it.

So your record needs the before word from last week.

It needs the after word you use today.

It needs one sentence saying what the change did.

A change can also fail, and that is still evidence.

If the edge went muddy, write muddy.

You write what it did, not what you hoped.

  • 1 · keep the before as a photo, copy or unchanged sample.
  • 2 · make the one change.
  • 3 · write the before word and the after word.
  • 4 · write one sentence saying what it did.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Say your change out loud first

Work in twos, with your own piece and your own line.

Partner one reads their feedback line out.

Then they say their change in one sentence.

The sentence needs a verb, a place and an amount.

Partner two counts the three parts on their fingers.

If one part is missing, they ask for it.

They ask which part, or how much, or which tool.

They do not say if the change is a good idea.

Then swap over, with two minutes each way.

Say it out loud before you touch the piece.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Make the change, then record what it did

Now do it on your own piece.

Keep the before first, as a photo, a copy or an unchanged test piece.

Then make your one change, once.

Put the tool down when the change is done.

Do not tidy the rest of the piece today.

Now write the record on your sheet.

Write the before word, then the after word.

Then write one sentence starting: the change made it.

Name the part in that sentence, not the whole piece.

Then write if you keep the change or undo it.

Both answers are allowed, and both are evidence.

Last write the name of the person who gave the line.

  • Core · Make the change. Point at before and after. Say what it did and have both words and your reason recorded.
  • Extend · make the change, write the before and after words, and one sentence naming the part.
  • Challenge · as Extend, and write what you would change next if you had one more line.
