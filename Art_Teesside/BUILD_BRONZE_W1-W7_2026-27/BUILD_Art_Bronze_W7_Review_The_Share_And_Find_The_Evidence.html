Review The Share And Find The Evidence

BUILD · Art · Trinity Arts Award Bronze. Review The Share And Find The Evidence.

Objective: Review how well your skills share went, then find the evidence for all four Bronze parts.

Success evidence: Your own Part D review linked to your plan and real delivery, a next step, and a four-part evidence map that names any gaps.

Theme: My Creative Identity. Choose what your art does with line, shape, colour or form. You do not need to share private facts about your life.

40 minutes. Use the work and tools that staff have ready in the room.

This deck evidences Bronze Part D, Arts skills share: plan, then deliver a short workshop or presentation passing on an arts skill, then review how well it went. ONE Attempted allowed overall. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass. The whole Bronze evidence portfolio has a 10-file cap. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication. Results are Pass or Below Pass only. Core, Extend and Challenge are support choices within Bronze, not award levels. Advisers must not write plans, reviews or evidence for pupils. Scribe or record the pupil's own words without improving them. The digital portfolio is organised by part. Every locator names the file plus page, slide or timecode. Only an adviser trained at Bronze may assess, and delivery is through a validated centre; this deck does not assert that either condition is confirmed. Source: tools/artsaward/SPEC.json, Bronze and universal rules. Today evidences the review step of Part D. It also checks existing A, B and C evidence; it does not teach or fabricate those parts anew. D review must refer to real delivery, not rehearsal alone. If a share or other required activity is missing, record the gap and arrange completion; never replace participation with a written claim. Four cumulative files called Bronze-A, Bronze-B, Bronze-C and Bronze-D are an optional organisation suggestion, not an award requirement. Other digital layouts are acceptable within the 10-file cap and part organisation rule. Count actual evidence files, not pages; preserve originals and locators if staff help consolidate. The taught review and short portfolio check are not a promise that the qualification is complete or passed.

Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Look at what took place

Put your skills share plan beside the delivery record. Add the learner feedback.

Check what the record says. Did you pass on the skill, complete part of the share, or rehearse? Use the right words.

If delivery is still to do, tell staff. You can review the practice, but keep it labelled as practice. It does not stand in for the real share.

If you delivered, find the goal on your plan. What did you want the learner to be able to do?

Now find a moment that helps you judge that goal. It may be a sample, a reply or a staff note about what happened.

Keep both in view.

Starter

Starter · create the need

A view needs a reason

Listen to two sample reviews of a made-up fold lesson. These are teaching examples, not anyone's evidence.

The first says, 'It went well because it was fun.'

The second says, 'I wanted the edges to meet. The learner's last fold shows that they did. My slow demo helped.'

Which review links to a goal and something we could check? Point to those words.

Enjoyment can matter too. But it does not tell the whole story of how well a skill was passed on.

Your review will use your own goal, your learner's response and your own view.

I Do

I Do · model

Read the evidence, then judge

Watch the adult review the fold lesson. They start with the plan, not with a claim that all went well.

The plan says the learner will line up two edges. The record shows a first try with a gap and a later try with the edges together.

The adult reads the learner feedback. It says the close view helped, but the first steps were too quick.

They can now say what helped and what needs work. Both can be true in the same share.

They point to the later fold when they explain the outcome. They point to the feedback when they choose a slower start next time.

They keep the learner's words apart from their own view. A reply from someone else is useful evidence, not the whole review.

Your learner may have made partial progress. Say what the work shows. A clear review need not claim a perfect result.

Look at your own plan and record. What did you mean to teach? What really happened? What does that tell you about your teaching?

I Do

I Do 2 · connect

Four parts, four different jobs

After the review, you will check the work you have already kept for Bronze. There are four parts.

Part A is arts participation. Find your making, the improvement and your own summary of what you learnt.

Part B is being an audience member. Find the real arts event or experience, your view of its quality and creative impact, and evidence that you shared your views with someone.

Part C is arts inspiration. Find research into a real artist or craftsperson's practice, career, life and work. Find why you chose them.

Part D is the skills share. Find your plan, your delivery and your own review of how well it went.

A heading alone is not evidence. Open the work and check what it shows. A plan for an experience does not show that it took place.

Mark a gap as a job to do. Do not fill it with a claim you cannot support.

Independent work

Independent · evidence

Write the review that your work supports

Now make your own Part D review. Use speech, writing or a recording that you can check.

Start with what you meant to teach. Compare that plan with the actual share. Say how well it went and what helped you judge.

Use the learner response and your record. Explain what you would keep and what you would change next time.

Point to the evidence with a file plus page, slide or timecode. An adult may write your words down, then read them back for you to check.

Finish the four-part map. List the evidence you can find and the gaps that still need work. Count the files with staff if you need help.

Save the review in Part D. Keep its location on your map.

  • Core · use prompts to say, point or write your review. Keep your own view, a link to the plan, evidence of the learner response and a next step. With support, check all four parts and name the file plus page, slide or timecode.
  • Extend · write or record the same full review in your own layout. Compare the plan with what took place. Explain a change you would make and check all four parts with exact evidence locations.
  • Challenge · keep the same full review and four-part check. Weigh two pieces of evidence that suggest different things. Explain which next step they support and why.
