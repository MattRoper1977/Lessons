BUILD · Art · Week 3. Trinity Arts Award Bronze. What Improved, In My Own Words.

Objective: summarise what you learnt across Part A in your own words, checked against the measure you set in week one.

Success evidence: your Part A pages in week order, one ring on met, part met or not met, three sentences in your own words, and a locator that names the file, the page and the box.

Everything you write today comes off your own pages. You write about marks, tools, boxes and numbers. You are never asked about your home, your family, your body, how you look or what you believe. Nobody in this room is asked what they believe.

Theme: My Creative Identity. Identity here means what your own artwork keeps choosing.

40 minutes. Offline.

This deck evidences Bronze Part A, Explore the arts as a participant. The register says Part A requires active participation in any art form, improvement, and a summary of what they learnt. This lesson IS that summary; the participation and the improvement were evidenced in the earlier Part A lessons. Bronze attempted rule, quoted from the register: "ONE Attempted allowed overall. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass." The evidence file cap for the whole Bronze level is 10 files, so the summary sheet joins the Part A section rather than opening a new file. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication. Bronze is the Level 1 Award in the Arts, qualification number 501/0081/6, and results are Pass or Below Pass only: no grades, no marks language. The register also states that advisers must not write plans, reviews or evidence for pupils, so you may scribe a pupil's words and you may never improve them. The portfolio is digital and organised by part, and an evidence locator names the file plus the page, slide or timecode; "in arts log" is refused as a locator. Claim no number here that is not in the register.
Delivery is through a validated centre; confirm local validation and an adviser trained at Bronze before assessment. Neither is asserted as completed here. Core, Extend and Challenge are support routes inside Bronze, not different awards. The prompts and sample quantities are teaching scaffolds, not extra assessment requirements. Scribe only the pupil’s own words. Store evidence by part; one cumulative file per part is a suggested four-file plan within the ten-file cap.

I check my work against my measure

My skill card names one skill: shade a flat tone.

My measure says a box with no white gaps.

First I lay my pages out in week order.

Baseline piece, skill card, skill strip, then the change.

I do not write one word yet.

Next I read the measure out loud, word for word off the card.

Then I hold box eight against the measure.

I look for white gaps, because that is what it asks.

Box eight has two small gaps left in it.

So my measure is part met.

Part met is a real answer here.

It is not a fail, and I write it down.

Last I write three short sentences.

Three sentences: what I set out to do, what my pages show, and what I learnt.

Model the part met verdict on purpose. A room that only ever hears met will write met, and Part A wants the summary honest, not flattering.

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the summaries, then match the missing part

Six summary cards are on your table.

Each card is somebody's summary of Part A.

Sort them into two piles: checked, and not checked.

A checked card names a measure, shows proof and says what was learnt.

  • A · I aimed for no white gaps. Box 8 has no gaps. I learnt to move each line closer. Bronze-A, page 3.
  • B · My work got a lot better this term.
  • C · I aimed for six lines of the same width. Box 7 has six. I learnt to keep a light grip. Bronze-A, page 4.
  • D · I can hold a line for ten counts. I think I did it.
  • E · My measure was ten clean marks. Box 8 has ten. Log page 2.
  • F · I tried hard every week and I never gave up.

Now take the pile that is not checked.

Match each card to the part it is missing.

It is the measure, the proof, or what you learnt.

Every card gets a match, even a guess.

Answer key: A and C are checked. B and F are missing the measure. D is missing the proof, because no page or box is named. E is missing what was learnt. Take the argued card first when you go round. B, D and F also lack a learning statement; a card may have more than one gap. Accept all well-evidenced missing parts.

Name the page, not the whole folder

Now watch the same summary go wrong.

I write that my proof is in the arts log.

An adult opens the folder and starts hunting.

There are eleven pages in there.

My proof is real, but nobody can find it.

Proof that cannot be found does not count.

So name three things in order: the file, the page, then the box.

Do the same for a photo: name it, and name the part it shows.

  • 1 · name the file, such as skill strip.
  • 2 · name the page number written on it.
  • 3 · name the box or the ring on that page.
  • 4 · write today's date beside it.

A locator is not extra work. It is the proof.

Write your summary, in your own words

Now write your Part A summary on the sheet.

Start by copying your measure into the first box.

Copy it word for word off your skill card.

Do not write it from memory.

Then ring one word of three: met, part met, not met.

Hold your last box against the measure first.

Then write three short sentences under the ring.

Sentence one says what you set out to do.

Sentence two says what your pages show.

Sentence three starts with the words I learnt.

Name a mark, a tool or a number in it.

A word like better is too soft to check.

Then write your locator on the last line.

File, page, box, and today's date.

Nobody writes this summary for you.

An adult may write your words down, and only your words.
