BUILD · Art · Week 4. Trinity Arts Award Bronze. The Review Frame, Before The Experience.

Objective: prepare your review frame before the experience, so every row holds the moment, the craft choice, the effect, and who it was for.

Success evidence: one ruled frame sheet with four columns and four rows, row one filled from the approved pack, and a record of who received it and how and today's date beside it.

Everything you write comes from the approved pack or from work put in front of you. You write about the work: the moment, the choice, the effect. You are never asked about your home, your family, your body, how you look or what you believe. Nobody in this room is asked what they believe. Identity here means what you as a maker keep choosing, read off artwork and never off a life.

Nothing is booked. No event, venue or date is named in this deck. The experience may be a visit. It may be a visitor coming in to us. It may be a live link on screen. An online exhibition counts too. Your frame is the same for all of them.

40 minutes. Offline.

This deck evidences Bronze Part B, Explore the arts as an audience member. The register says Part B requires attending at least one arts event or experience, reflecting on quality and creative impact, and views shared with at least one other person, evidenced. The register also notes that an online exhibition is legitimate. This deck builds the instrument for that part and asserts no attendance: nothing is booked, no event, venue or date appears, and it runs word for word the same whether we go to them, they come in to us, or we meet live on screen. Bronze attempted rule, in the register's words: ONE Attempted allowed overall. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass. The evidence file cap is 10 evidence files for the whole Bronze level, so this frame sheet joins the Part B file rather than opening a new one. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication.
Delivery is through a validated centre; confirm local validation and an adviser trained at Bronze before assessment. Neither is asserted as completed here. Core, Extend and Challenge are support routes inside Bronze, not different awards. The prompts and sample quantities are teaching scaffolds, not extra assessment requirements. Scribe only the pupil’s own words. Store evidence by part; one cumulative file per part is a suggested four-file plan within the ten-file cap.

From a notice to a row

Last lesson you wrote notices.

A notice names a choice you can point at.

Today each notice gets a home.

That home is a row on a review frame.

A frame is a sheet with four columns.

The columns are the moment, the craft choice, the effect, and who it was for.

You rule the frame first, before the experience.

Then all you have to do is fill it in.

You need three things today. Your frame sheet, a ruler and a pencil.

Starter · create the need

Two reviews of one work

Here are two reviews of the same work.

The first was written from memory, a week late.

It says the work was good and the room was big.

You cannot tell what the maker did.

The second was written on a frame.

It names a moment, a choice, an effect and who for.

You can picture the work from it.

The work did not change. One writer had rows ready.

  • 1 · rule the frame first.
  • 2 · fill one row at each moment.
  • 3 · check the row before you move on.

Memory fades fast. A blank row waits.

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

I fill one row

A print from the approved pack is on my desk.

I will fill one row in front of you.

Column one is the moment.

The moment is the bit I stopped at.

I write it so you could find it too.

I write: the dark strip along the bottom.

Column two is the craft choice.

That is what the maker did in that spot.

I write: black ink, laid on thick.

Column three is the effect.

The effect is what that choice did.

I write: my eye drops to the bottom first.

Column four is who it was for.

I read that off the label and the pack.

I write: made for a school hall wall.

Watch my order. The moment goes first, every time.

A choice makes no sense without a moment.

Fill the row on the board in this order, left to right. Do not fill a second row; the second row is the pupil's. If a pupil offers a strong line, park it on the board for their own sheet rather than writing it into yours.

There is one rule for column four.

You never guess about a person.

You read who it was for off the work.

The label, the size and the pack all tell you.

A checked label can name who a work was made for. Low labels alone do not tell us that.

If a source does not name the audience, write not stated. You may offer a guess, but label it as a guess and say why. Do not turn a low label or a large shape into a fact about the maker’s aim.

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Sort the cards, then match the column

Six cards are on your table. Each card holds one line.

  • The room goes dark for ten seconds.
  • It was a really good hour.
  • The maker left the joins showing.
  • Your eye goes to the red first.
  • The practice label says this work was made for young children.
  • I liked all of it.

Sort them into two piles: frame lines and empty lines.

A frame line names something in the work.

An empty line only names how you felt.

Now take the frame pile on its own.

Match each card to the column it belongs in.

The four columns are moment, craft choice, effect, and who it was for.

Every card gets a match, even when you are not sure.

A guess I can see beats a blank card.

Adjudication key. Frame lines: the dark room line, the joins line, the red line, the audience label line. Empty lines: it was a really good hour, and I liked all of it. Matches: dark room is the moment, joins are the craft choice, red is the effect, the audience label states who it was for.

I Do 2 · connect

Frame first, or three words later

Now watch the same job done the wrong way.

I take no frame with me at all.

I tell myself that I will remember it.

A week later I write three words: it was good.

Three words is not a review.

It shows no craft choice, so it shows no quality.

The frame does two jobs for you.

It tells you what to look for.

It gives you a place to put it.

Now here is the test for a finished row.

Read the effect, then read the choice.

Ask if that choice caused that effect.

If any choice would give the same effect, the row is weak.

So change the effect until it follows the choice.

My eye drops down passes the test.

It was lovely fails it. Thick black ink did not make it lovely.

  • 1 · rule four columns and four rows.
  • 2 · fill the moment before you move on.
  • 3 · name one choice, not three.
  • 4 · make the effect follow the choice.

One moment per row. Two moments in a row is really two rows.

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Test a row with a partner

Use the model row shown earlier. Your own piece comes next.

Work in twos with your frame sheets.

Partner one reads the model row out loud.

Partner two asks the two test questions.

Question one: could I find that moment?

Question two: does the effect come from the choice?

If either answer is no, the row gets changed.

Change it now, in pencil, not later.

Then swap over, with two minutes each way.

You may not tell your partner the row is nice.

Nice gives the writer nothing to fix.

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Rule the frame, fill row one

Now build your own frame sheet.

Rule four columns down the page.

Head them moment, craft choice, effect, and who it was for.

Rule four rows under the heads.

One work from the approved pack is on your desk.

Fill row one from that work now.

Leave rows two, three and four blank.

Those three rows wait for the experience.

Label your choice: colour, scale, material or sound.

Then ring the one word in the effect box that does the work.

Write your name and today's date at the top.

Write the file name this sheet will be saved as.

The register refuses "in arts log" as an evidence locator: the locator names the file plus the page, slide or timecode. Get the file name onto the sheet today, while the sheet is in front of them. Advisers must not write plans, reviews or evidence for pupils; scribing a pupil's own words is not the same thing as writing the row.

  • Extend · rule the frame and fill row one in all four columns.

A frame with three blank rows is finished work today.

The blank rows are the point of it.

Read one row to an adult

Read row one to the adult at your table.

They will ask you the two test questions.

Answer them, then fix the row if you need to.

Record who checked your work and today's date.

They write them next to row one, on your sheet.

That is the record that the row was checked.

Your sheet then goes into the Part B file.

It does not go in your bag.

You are the audience member here.

Your frame decides what you will notice.

Pick one craft choice you want to look for, whatever the route is.

Write that pick at the foot of the sheet.

Add this work to your Part B file. Write the file name and page number. For a sound or video clip, give the file name and the time where your evidence starts.

This is a practice share from the pack. Label it preparation. Part B is completed after the real arts experience, its review and its share.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
