What An Audience Member Actually Does

BUILD · Art · Week 4. Trinity Arts Award Bronze. What An Audience Member Actually Does.

Objective: name what an audience member notices, and say why noticing is the evidence a review is built on.

Success evidence: four notices on your frame sheet, each naming one choice and what it did, with one ringed.

Everything you notice comes from the approved pack or from work put in front of you. You talk about the work: colour, scale, material, sound. You are never asked about your home, your family, your body, how you look or what you believe. Nobody in this room is asked what they believe. Identity here means what you as a maker keep choosing, read off artwork and never off a life.

Nothing is booked. The event is not named yet. We may go to them. They may come to us. We may meet live on screen. An online exhibition counts too. The job in this deck is the same either way.

40 minutes. Offline.

This deck evidences Bronze Part B, Explore the arts as an audience member. The register says Part B requires attending at least one arts event or experience, reflecting on quality and creative impact, and views shared with at least one other person, evidenced. The register also notes that an online exhibition is legitimate. This deck is the preparation for that part and asserts no attendance: no event, venue or date is named anywhere in it, and it runs unchanged whether we go to them, they come to us, or we meet live on screen. Bronze attempted rule, in the register's words: ONE Attempted allowed overall. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass. The evidence file cap is 10 files for the whole Bronze level, so today's frame sheet joins the Part B file rather than opening a new one. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication.
Delivery is through a validated centre; confirm local validation and an adviser trained at Bronze before assessment. Neither is asserted as completed here. Core, Extend and Challenge are support routes inside Bronze, not different awards. The prompts and sample quantities are teaching scaffolds, not extra assessment requirements. Scribe only the pupil’s own words. Store evidence by part; one cumulative file per part is a suggested four-file plan within the ten-file cap.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Two visitors, one room

Two people go to the same room of work.

The first walks the whole room in four minutes.

They pass thirty works and name none.

Later they write three words: it was good.

The second stays with one work for four minutes.

They write that the blue is darker at the bottom.

Then they write what it did: my eye sank.

Now think about the review each one owes.

The first has nothing to build on.

The second has a choice and an effect.

  • Seeing is passing your eyes over a work.
  • Noticing is naming one choice you can point at.
  • Evidence is that notice, written down at the time.

I Do

I Do · model

I notice one work, out loud

Watch me notice, using one card from the approved pack.

Step one is stop. I stay with one work.

I count to twenty before I write.

Step two is name. I name one choice I can see.

I say that a thick dark border fills the edge of the card.

That is a choice about line, tone and composition.

Step three is effect. I say what the choice did.

I say that the dark border draws my eye to the pale centre.

Step four is write. I write both parts now.

A quick note can help you recall the detail later.

Notice the words I did not use.

I did not say nice, boring or amazing.

These views need a detail from the work to explain them.

Say the four step names out loud as you do them: stop, name, effect, write. The steps are the thing being taught here, not the card you happen to hold.

We Do · everyone commits

Name the choice you noticed

Match each practice notice to a craft choice. Then add what that choice did for you.

Model explanations

  1. The blue is darker at the bottom. — Colour. This names a change of colour or tone. Say where it draws your eye.
  2. The frame is taller than me. — Scale. This compares its size with the viewer. Say how that size affects your view.
  3. The wood is left rough on the back. — Material. This notices the surface of a material. Do not touch the work unless permitted.
  4. The hum gets louder as you walk in. — Sound. This notices a change you hear. Use a description, visual cue or recording if that gives better access.

This model supports discussion. A well-explained different connection can be useful too.

Give your own view with a detail: “I found it … because …”. An opinion matters; the detail helps explain it.

I Do 2 · connect

One work close beats thirty works fast

Now watch the same room done the wrong way.

I walk fast and I look at everything.

At the end I write it was good.

Those three words cannot be reviewed.

Part B asks you about quality and creative impact.

Quality needs a choice to hang on.

Impact needs an effect you felt.

So the rule is fixed: one work at a time.

Four notices fill a sheet. Thirty glances fill none.

Write at the time, not on the way home.

Use a pencil, a phone note, or an adult who writes for you.

  • 1 · pick one work and stay with it.
  • 2 · name one choice you can point at.
  • 3 · say what that choice did to you.
  • 4 · write both parts before you move on.

We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Try one notice with a partner

Work in twos, with one pack card between you.

Partner one gives a notice out loud.

It must have a choice and an effect.

Partner two checks it with one question.

The question is: what can I point at?

If nothing can be pointed at, it is an opinion.

Then partner one says it again with a choice in it.

Swap over, with two minutes each way.

You may give your honest view. Add a detail to explain it.

If you get stuck, name a colour first.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Four notices on your frame sheet

Now work on your own.

Take one card from the approved pack.

Write four notices on your frame sheet.

Each notice fills two boxes.

Box one is the choice you can point at.

Box two is what that choice did.

Label each choice: colour, scale, material or sound.

Use the labels that fit the work. Do not invent a sound or material you cannot check.

Then ring the notice you would put in a review.

A good ring names a choice and an effect.

A weak ring names only a feeling.

Nothing is booked yet, so these are practice notices.

The same frame works whatever the route turns out to be.

  • Core · Use the labels to notice a craft choice and its effect. Say your view to an adult and record whom, how and where the record is saved.
  • Extend · write four notices, label each choice, and ring the strongest.
  • Challenge · as Extend, and write why that one is the strongest.

If you finish early, write one question for the maker.
