Arrival · retrieve

A skill you can show

Bring a piece from your Part A work. Find a small skill in it that you can now use.

You might make a pale tone, join two shapes, fold a neat edge or print a clear mark. Pick from your own work. These are ideas, not a set list.

Point to the part that shows your skill. Can you show how you made it? Choose a step you can explain with the tools here.

Think of someone who could learn that step from you. It may be a peer, a small group or a staff member.

Your choice of marks and tools is enough.

Keep your work where you can see it. It will help you plan.

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Seeing a skill is a start

Watch a short demo. First the adult makes a mark but hides their hand. What could a learner miss?

Now the adult turns the page so you can see. They show the grip, make the mark and leave space for you to try.

Which version helped you know what to do? Point, tell a partner or note a reason.

A share needs more than a finished piece. Your learner needs to see the skill and have a way to use it.

Today you will plan that chance. Then you will test whether your steps make sense.

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Build a plan that leaves room to learn

Watch the adult build a test plan for a simple paper fold. This is their example, not your plan to copy.

They choose one skill: line up two edges before they press the fold. They place an open sheet beside a folded one.

They name the learner and ask if the learner wants to try. They check that both people can see and reach the paper.

First they show the edges. Then they bring them together, hold them still and press. They slow down at the hard step.

Next the learner tries with a new sheet. The adult waits. They do not grab the sheet and finish it.

For the check, the learner points to the two edges. Do they meet? The next try depends on what that check shows.

The plan needs time to show, time to try and time to check. The adult sets a short time for each and tries it with a clock.

They add spare paper and a clear space to work. If folding is hard to grip, a larger sheet may help.

Your skill may need a different tool or check. Choose those from the work you know.

Which note helps a learner?

Sort these pairs. In each pair, pick the note that would help someone take part.

You may point to a choice before you explain it. Work with a partner or with the adult.

Then pick one pair to improve. Keep the skill simple. Add a learner action, a clear step or a way to check.

Use your own skill in the plan.

  • A · Be good at drawing. / Show the angle of the pencil.
  • B · Watch me do it all. / Try a mark on your own scrap.
  • C · Ask if it was nice. / Check if the two edges meet.

I Do 2 · connect

Test the part that may go wrong

Look back at the fold plan. The adult asks a learner to try just the first step.

The learner puts the wrong edges together. That gives the adult useful news about the plan.

The adult adds two small dots to show which edges meet. They try the step again. They keep the first note so the change is clear.

A test helps you find a gap before the share. It is not the full share.

Choose the step in your own plan that may need a closer view, more time or a new cue.

Check the tools with staff. Keep sharp or hot tools out unless staff have agreed a safe way to use them. A paper or drawing skill may need less kit.

Give the learner a way to ask you to pause. They may watch a step again or use a different grip.

Plan how you will know what they learnt. A sample, a short reply or an observed try can help.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Try your plan with someone

Tell a willing partner or staff member which step you want to test. Ask them to act as a new learner.

Show that step, then let them try. Ask what they could see and what they still need you to explain.

Listen before you change the plan. Their answer may show that one step needs to come sooner.

Write, draw or record the change you choose. Say why that change will help your learner.

Swap roles if both of you are ready. You may choose staff support for the test.

If there is no learner ready, mark the test as still to do. Ask staff to help you set it up. Do not claim a test that did not take place.

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Make your plan your own

Now set out the plan you will use. Keep it easy to read while you teach.

Name your art skill and who will learn it. Say what you want the learner to be able to do.

Set out the steps you will show. Add what the learner will do at each point. Leave time for their try.

List your tools and where they will sit. Add how much time you need and how you will check learning.

Add a way to pause or change the task if a learner needs help. Keep the skill in that change.

Use your test note to make one useful edit, if the test showed a need. If it worked, say what showed that.

Keep the plan and test in your Part D section. Name the file and page, slide or timecode. Choose the support that helps you keep your own ideas.

  • Core · choose your skill and learner. Speak, point or write each part of your plan with the sheet prompts. Keep your own choices, the learner task, a check and the test change. Name the file and page, slide or timecode.
  • Extend · make the same full plan in your own layout. Use your test to change a step, tool, time or check. Name where the plan and test are saved.
  • Challenge · keep the same plan and evidence. Test a second way to explain a hard step. Say which way you will use and why.

Ready for a real share

Show your plan to the person who helped you test it. Ask them to find the learner task and the check.

Can they find both without a long search? Add a clear label if needed.

Tell staff what you still need for the share. A missing tool, learner or test is a job to arrange.

Keep the plan ready for next time. You will use it, pass on the skill, then review what took place.

Your plan records what you intend. It does not yet prove that you taught the skill.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
