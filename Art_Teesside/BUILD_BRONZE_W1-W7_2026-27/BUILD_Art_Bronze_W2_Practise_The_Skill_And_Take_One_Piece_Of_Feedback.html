Practise The Skill, And Take One Piece Of Feedback

BUILD · Art · Week 2. Trinity Arts Award Bronze. Practise The Skill, And Take One Piece Of Feedback.

Objective: work on your chosen skill again, take one piece of feedback from a named person, and write that feedback down in their own words.

Success evidence: four more boxes on your skill strip, one line of feedback in quote marks, and the name of the person who said it.

The feedback is about your artwork. You ask about a mark, a gap, a line or a colour. You never ask about a person's home, family, beliefs, body or looks. Nobody in this room is asked about theirs.

40 minutes. Offline.

This deck evidences Bronze Part A, Explore the arts as a participant. The register says Part A requires active participation in any art form, improvement, and a summary of what they learnt. Today's strip is the participation and the improvement; the quoted line feeds the summary later in the part. Bronze is the Level 1 Award in the Arts, qualification number 501/0081/6. Attempted rule, quoted from the register: ONE Attempted allowed overall. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass. The evidence file cap for the whole Bronze level is 10 files, so today's strip and quote belong on an existing Part A file, not a new one. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication. Results are Pass or Below Pass only; no grades and no marks language anywhere.
Delivery is through a validated centre; confirm local validation and an adviser trained at Bronze before assessment. Neither is asserted as completed here. Core, Extend and Challenge are support routes inside Bronze, not different awards. The prompts and sample quantities are teaching scaffolds, not extra assessment requirements. Scribe only the pupil’s own words. Store evidence by part; one cumulative file per part is a suggested four-file plan within the ten-file cap.

3 min
Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Feedback is one thing you can use

Last session you worked your skill six times.

You wrote what changed from box one to box six.

Find that strip and that sentence now.

Today you work the same skill again.

Then you take one piece of feedback.

Feedback is what one other person tells you about your work.

It is not praise. Specific praise can show what to keep. A clear next step shows what to try.

Feedback you can use names one part and one change.

You take one piece only, from one named person.

A named person is somebody in this room today.

It can be your partner or the adult at your table.

Say the one-piece rule out loud and leave it on the board. Choosing one next step helps pupils use feedback without having to act on every comment at once, and the record then shows a heap of talk rather than a change.

You need your strip, your measure and one pencil.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Two asks, one strip

Watch me ask for feedback twice.

Both times I hold up the same strip.

First I ask if my strip is good.

The answer comes back: yes, it is good.

It does not tell me what to change.

Then I point at box four and ask again.

This time I ask which gap in it is too wide.

The answer comes back: the ones at the top.

Now I have one part and one job.

The strip did not change. The ask did.

  • 1 · name the part you want looked at.
  • 2 · ask for one change, not a yes.
  • 3 · write the answer in their words.

A sharp ask gets you something to do.

I Do

I Do · model

I practise, then I take one piece of feedback

My skill is cross hatching in pencil.

Today I do four more boxes first.

One minute in each box, same pencil all the way.

That is step one, and it is the work.

Now I pick one box to ask about.

I pick box four, because the gaps look wide.

I ring that box so my partner can see it.

Then I ask him one sharp question about it.

He says my gaps at the top are too wide.

I write that line down word for word.

I put quote marks round it, so it stays his.

Then I write his name and today's date under it.

The name matters. Feedback with no name cannot be checked.

Model the ring before the ask. Without a ringed part the responder answers about the whole strip, and the reply comes back too broad to act on.

I do not write down what I wish he had said.

3 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the asks, then match the fix

Six ask cards are on your table.

Sort them into two piles, sharp and blunt.

A sharp ask names one part and asks for one change.

  • Card one says which gap in box four is too wide.
  • Card two says is my shading better.
  • Card three says what one thing would you change about my blending.
  • Card four says tell me about my whole strip.
  • Card five says where do my lines go wrong in box six.
  • Card six says what one thing would you change.

Now take the blunt pile on its own.

Match each blunt card to the fix it needs.

The three fix cards say: name the part, ask for one change, ask for a word and not a yes.

Every card gets a match, even when you are not sure.

A guess I can see beats a blank card.

Then hold up the card your table argued about most.

Adjudication for the cover teacher. Sharp pile: cards one, three and five. Blunt pile: card two is a yes or no, so it needs ask for a word and not a yes; card four asks about everything, so it needs ask for one change; card six names no part, so it needs name the part. Accept any match a pupil can give a reason for, then read the key out.

7 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

Their words, not your tidy version

You have the answer, and there are two ways to write it.

The first way keeps their words.

I write that my gaps at the top are too wide.

The second way tidies it up.

I write that he said it was quite good.

That second line has lost the job.

It holds no part and no change.

So write while they talk, not after.

Write it fast, even if the words come out rough.

Rough words you can use beat neat words you cannot.

If you miss a word, ask them to say it again.

You may ask twice. You may not guess.

  • 1 · write the answer as they say it.
  • 2 · put quote marks at both ends.
  • 3 · write the name of the person.
  • 4 · write today's date under the line.

That line can now be checked, so it counts.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Ask, write, then let them check it

Work in twos with your own strip.

Partner one rings one box and asks one sharp question.

Partner two answers with one thing to change.

You may not answer with good, nice or fine.

Those words give the maker no job to do.

Partner one writes the answer word for word.

Then partner two reads that written line back.

They tick it if those are their words.

They cross it out if they are not.

A crossed line gets fixed now, not later.

Then swap over, with two minutes each way.

The person who said it is the one who checks it.

Independent work

Independent · evidence

Four more boxes, then one piece of feedback

Work on your own now.

Use the skill you chose in week one.

Number four new boxes on your strip first.

Work one minute in each box, then stop.

Keep the same chosen tool and size to the last trial.

Then pick one box to ask about.

Ring it, so the person can see the part.

Now ask one named person one sharp question.

Write their answer down in their own words.

Put quote marks at both ends of it.

Write their name and today's date under the line.

Then write one line back: what you will change first.

  • Core · Make the practice boxes. Use a question stem to ask about one part. Record the reply in their words, with whom and when.
  • Extend · four boxes worked, one sharp ask, and the answer in quote marks with a name and a date.
  • Challenge · as Extend, and one line saying what you will change first.

If you finish early, ring the part you will change.

The register is firm here: advisers must not write plans, reviews or evidence for pupils. Scribing a pupil's own words is access. Supplying the sentence, or improving it, is not, and it makes the line unusable as evidence.

Read it back to the person

Find the person who gave you the feedback.

Read their line back to them out loud.

Then ask them one thing: are those your words?

If they say no, change it there and then.

They were your audience for one minute today.

Their words are about to change what you do next.

Now show the line to the adult at your table.

They check three things: the quote marks, the name and the date.

Then write the sheet name and the page number.

Now pick one thing that happens next. Keep this feedback, ask the same person again, or ask a different named person.
