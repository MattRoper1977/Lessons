Practise The Skill, Session One

BUILD · Art · Week 2. Trinity Arts Award Bronze. Practise The Skill, Session One.

Objective: work on the skill you chose, keep the tool and the box the same, and record what changed between your first attempt and your last.

Success evidence: a strip with every box numbered, box one and your last box ringed, and one sentence that names the change.

Your skill came off a card in the approved skill pack. You talk about the mark, the tool and the box. You are never asked about your home, your family, your body or what you believe. Nobody in this room is asked what they believe. Identity here means what your own artwork keeps choosing, and it is read off the strip in front of you.

40 minutes. Offline.

This deck evidences Bronze Part A, Explore the arts as a participant. The register says Part A requires active participation in any art form, improvement, and a summary of what they learnt. Today's strip is the participation and the improvement record; the summary comes later in Part A. Bronze attempted rule, in the register's words: ONE Attempted allowed overall. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass. The evidence file cap is 10 files for the whole Bronze level, so append the strip to the cumulative Part A file and label its page; do not open a new file for each session. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication.
Delivery is through a validated centre; confirm local validation and an adviser trained at Bronze before assessment. Neither is asserted as completed here. Core, Extend and Challenge are support routes inside Bronze, not different awards. The prompts and sample quantities are teaching scaffolds, not extra assessment requirements. Scribe only the pupil’s own words. Store evidence by part; one cumulative file per part is a suggested four-file plan within the ten-file cap.

Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

One attempt shows nothing

Last week you chose one art form skill.

You also wrote a measure for it.

A measure is the thing you can see or count.

Find that measure now and read it out.

Today you work on that same skill six times.

Each go is called an attempt.

One attempt on its own shows nothing.

Two attempts can show a change.

They only show it if you can tell them apart.

So every attempt gets its own number today.

Measures out before the starter. A pupil practising without last week's measure will write a feeling at the end, and the record then has nothing to answer to.

You need your measure, a trial sheet and the safe tool for your chosen skill.

Starter

Starter · create the need

Two strips, one skill

Watch two strips of practice.

The first strip has six boxes and no numbers.

I cannot tell you which box came first.

So I cannot say that anything changed.

The second strip has six numbered boxes.

I can hold box one next to box six.

Now the change is there to see.

The practice was the same. The record was not.

  • 1 · number every box before you start.
  • 2 · keep the same tool all the way.
  • 3 · keep every box the same size.
  • 4 · stop and compare box one with your last.

A record you cannot read is not evidence.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

I practise, and I keep the box the same

My skill is cross hatching in pencil.

My measure is that the lines stay even.

For cross hatching, draw a set of light lines with even gaps. Add a second set across them at an angle. Keep each set steady. More close lines make a darker patch; wider gaps leave it pale.

I number all six boxes before I draw.

I start in box one and work for one minute.

Then I stop, even if the box is not full.

Stopping keeps every box worth the same.

I move to box two and do it again.

Same pencil. Same box size. Same one minute.

Those three things stay locked all the way along.

At the end I ring box one and box six.

I put those two side by side and look.

In box one my lines cross at odd angles.

In box six two sets of lines cross, with even gaps in each set.

The gaps between them are the same size.

That is my measure, so the change is real.

I write: by box six my lines are more even.

That names the mark and answers my measure.

Model the stop, not the drawing. Pupils copy the timing. If you let box one run long, every strip in the room becomes uncomparable and the whole record is lost.

We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the record cards, then match the fault

Six record cards are on your table.

Each one describes a practice strip. They say this.

  • Card 1 · six boxes, numbered one to six, one pencil.
  • Card 2 · six boxes, and no numbers anywhere.
  • Card 3 · box one in pencil, box six in marker pen.
  • Card 4 · six boxes, all the same size, all numbered.
  • Card 5 · box one is small, box six fills the page.
  • Card 6 · four boxes, numbered, one minute in each.

Sort them into two piles: can compare, and cannot compare.

A card can compare if the boxes are numbered and nothing else changed.

Now take the cannot compare pile.

Match each card to its fault.

The faults are no numbers, tool changed and size changed.

Every card gets a match, even when you are not sure.

A guess I can see beats a blank card.

Then hold up the card your table argued about most.

Adjudication for the cover teacher. Cards 1, 4 and 6 go in the can compare pile. Card 2 is no numbers, card 3 is tool changed, card 5 is size changed. Card 6 has fewer boxes, which is fine: the number of boxes is not the fault, changing the conditions is.

7 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

Change one thing, not three

Now watch me practise the wrong way.

I start box one in pencil, as before.

By box four I want a stronger line.

So I swap to a black pen.

I also make the last box twice as big.

Then I give myself three minutes, not one.

Box six does look stronger than box one.

But I cannot say what made it stronger.

It could be the pen, the space or the extra time.

So lock your tool, your box and your minute.

Then the only thing left changing is you.

That is why the strip counts as evidence.

Your measure works in the same way.

Read the measure before you compare the boxes.

Then your words answer the measure, not your mood.

Better is not a change. It is a feeling.

Even, straighter, darker and closer are changes.

Each one names a mark somebody else can find.

  • 1 · same tool in every box.
  • 2 · same box size all the way.
  • 3 · same time in every box.
  • 4 · compare box one with your last box.

We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Check the strip, then check the words

Use the model practice strip shown earlier. Your own piece comes next.

Work in twos with the model strip.

Do not swap the tools used for the trials.

There are three checks and they go in order.

Check one is a number in every box.

If a box has no number, write miss beside it.

Check two is the same tool all the way.

Check three is boxes of the same size.

Put a tick on the strip if all three hold.

A tick means the strip can be compared.

Now one person names the change in the model out loud.

They must name the mark, not the mood.

If they say better, ask them which mark.

Then point at the two boxes they mean.

Then swap roles, with two minutes each way.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Six boxes, then one sentence

Practise the skill on your own card. For paint, repeat a small colour mix. For cutting, repeat the same edge with safe tools. For drawing, repeat the chosen marks. Label each test so you can compare the first and last.

Work on your own now.

Number your boxes one to six first.

Then work one minute in each box.

Stop when I call time, every single time.

Keep the same chosen tool and size to the last trial.

When the strip is full, ring box one.

Then ring your last box as well.

Hold the two ringed boxes side by side.

Read your measure again before you write.

Then write one sentence under the strip.

Start it with the words by box six.

Name the mark that changed, then say how.

  • Core · Make and number the practice boxes. Ring the first and last. Say what changed and have your words recorded.
  • Extend · as Core, and write one sentence starting by box six that names the mark that changed.
  • Challenge · as Extend, and name the box where the change first shows.

If you finish early, write the one thing you will keep the same next session.

No clear change yet is a real result too. Point at both trials and say what stayed the same. Never invent a change to fill the line.

Exit · Audience and Influence

VOICE: the evidence statement remains in the learner’s chosen communication mode.

AUDIENCE: a familiar adult receives and names back exactly what was heard or seen.

INFLUENCE: one real next action is recorded: retain, add method detail, or leave open.

Say it once to an adult

Read your sentence to the adult at your table.

They read it back to you word for word.

Then they look at box one and your last box.

If no clear change shows, record no clear change yet.

One sentence is enough. Do not write more.

Now write today's date on the strip.

Write the sheet name and the page number too.

That is how anybody finds this strip again.

Now pick one thing that happens next. Keep the same measure, change nothing but the day, or add two more boxes.

Write your pick under your sentence.
