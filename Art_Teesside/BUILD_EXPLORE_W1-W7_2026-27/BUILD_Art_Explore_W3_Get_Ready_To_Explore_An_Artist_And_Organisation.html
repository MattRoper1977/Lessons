Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

From making to finding out

Bring a drawing or paper piece from your recent work. Find a choice you made in it.

You might point to a line, a fold, a join or a shape. What did your hand do to make that part?

Now think about someone else who makes art. What would you like to find out about how they work?

An artist makes art. An arts organisation is a group that helps art happen. We will call it the arts group on these pages.

It may help people make, show or learn about art. Its work is more than the name on a door.

Part B needs a real way to explore both. Today we get ready.

Starter

Starter · create the need

The work and the way to see it

Look at a paper shape the adult has made. First it faces the wall. Then the adult turns it so the class can see.

The folds belong to the making. The space and the way it is shown affect how people can see it.

Which would you ask the maker about? Which might you ask the group that shows the work?

These roles can link. A person may make art and also help run a group. We still need to find out about both jobs.

This classroom model helps us form questions. It is not a record of the Part B experience.

I Do

I Do · model

Rehearsal model: a question from a detail

Watch the adult turn a detail into a question. They point to a fold that makes the paper stand.

They first ask, 'Is it good?' That could bring a yes or no, but it tells us little about the work.

They try, 'How did you make that part stand?' Now the question points to a clear part and asks about a choice.

The adult can show that fold on their own paper. Watch the action as well as the words.

For the arts group, a different question might be, 'How do you help people see work like this?'

That asks about the group's work, not the maker's fold. Both questions have a job.

A useful question can start with how, what or why. It should help you find out something you do not know yet.

You may ask through speech, a written card, a picture or an adult who reads your words. Keep the question yours.

After a reply, you could point to the detail again or ask for a step to be shown. Practise that here; the real reply comes later.

I Do

I Do 2 · connect

Preparation is a step, not the whole job

A page, photo or video can help you think of a question. It cannot answer every question about what a person or group does.

A printed answer does not let you ask for a closer view. A recorded film cannot respond to your own try.

In a live or active experience, you can take part in what is really offered. You might ask a question, try a process, explore a display with a guide or respond to a live demonstration.

The exact activity depends on the real artist and arts group. Staff will check what is arranged and what you can use.

If the slot cards are blank, keep your questions. Leave names and dates open. Do not turn a guess into a fact.

Your preparation is useful. It gets you ready to explore both the artist and the group when the experience is arranged.

Test a question, not a made-up answer

Choose a question about the adult's classroom paper shape. Ask it in the way you prefer.

Now test a question for the arts group. Ask a partner or adult to say what they think the question means. They do not need to pretend to run that group.

If the question is not clear, change a word or add a sketch. Try it once more.

Label this work rehearsal. Keep the question you want to use later. Do not copy the classroom answer into the place for the real artist's reply.
