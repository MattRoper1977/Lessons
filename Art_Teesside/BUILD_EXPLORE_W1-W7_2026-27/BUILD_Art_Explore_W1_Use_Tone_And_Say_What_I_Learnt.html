3 min

Arrival · retrieve

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Bring back your line drawing if it is here. Find a line choice you tried. If the drawing is elsewhere, you can start a simple shape on fresh paper today. The new work will still show how you take part.

Today you use tone. Tone means how light or dark an area looks. With one pencil, you can make pale grey, dark grey and shades between them. The white paper can be a light area too.

This continues the same arts activity: drawing. You are adding another way to use the tool. You will keep a result and explain what you learnt from making it.

Put your paper on a firm surface. Test the pencil in the practice space. Ask for a different grip or tool if you cannot use it with comfort.

3 min

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Look at the three boxes marked A, B and C. A is white paper. B is pale grey. C is darker grey. The boxes are the same size, so look at the tone rather than their shape.

Point to the darkest box. Now find the lightest. Where would you put B between them? You can show the order with the letters instead of saying it aloud.

Tone can help separate one area from another. When a dark shape sits beside a light area, its edge may be easier to see. When their tones are close, the edge may blend in.

Neither effect is always best. A maker might want a clear sign or a soft shape. Think about which effect could help the small drawing you want to make.

6 min

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

I make a small tone patch with light strokes. I hold the pencil at a comfortable angle. I move back and forth across the patch without digging the point into the page.

After the first layer, I lift the pencil and look. Some paper shows between the marks. I add another light layer where I want a darker tone. A darker area does not need a painful grip or a torn page.

I leave a small area white beside the patch. That gives me a light tone to compare it with. Now I add more layers to the other side. I can see a change from white through grey to dark.

I stop when the tone suits my purpose. More layers are not always better. If I want a pale cloud, making it very dark may change the effect I wanted.

My model learning note says: light layers let me choose when to stop. I point at the pale and dark ends to show what I mean.

4 min

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Sort these visible cards under two headings: a tone choice, and something else. Use the letters to show your answers.

  • A: Leave part of the paper white.
  • B: Make the sheet twice as wide.
  • C: Add another gentle pencil layer.
  • D: Turn the paper so your arm has room.

A and C change a tone choice. B changes the size of the paper. D changes how you can reach it. Size and reach can matter, but they are not the same as light and dark.

Choose A or C and say when you might use it. You could keep white for a light area or add a layer for a dark one. There may be more than one good reason.

Now point to the part of the model that shows your choice. Linking the action to its result will help your learning note later.

5 min

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

There is another way to make tone. I draw a set of short lines that run alongside each other. This is hatching. I leave gaps so the light paper shows through.

When I put similar lines closer together, less white shows between them. The area often looks darker. I try it on a spare patch before using it in the drawing.

I can add a second set across the first. That is cross-hatching. Both sets remain visible and cross each other. It is a different action from tracing one line again and again.

I do not need to use every method in one drawing. I choose layers or hatching for the effect I want. My learning can name the method and what I noticed it did.

A neat patch is not the only useful result. A rough patch can show a rough surface. Describe what the marks suggest instead of calling all rough marks wrong.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Create your own practice patches now. Make a pale patch and a darker one. Use gentle layers, or try changing the gaps between hatch lines. Keep your hand comfortable.

Pause and compare the patches you have just made. Which action gave you a tone you can use? Point to that action’s result while you explain it to a partner or an adult.

The checker asks: what did you do here? You might say that you added a layer or left wider gaps. If the patches look alike, check what actually changed before you claim a difference.

Keep the practice on your sheet. Add a short note about something you found. You may speak your note for a scribe. They read it back so you can check the meaning.

11 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Make a small drawing that uses tone. You can redraw a simple shape from last time or choose a fresh shape. Decide where you want a light area and where a darker area could help.

Use layers or hatch lines to build the tone. Look up from the page between stages. If two areas merge when you wanted a clear edge, try a different tone on spare paper first.

Choose a result to keep. Point to an area where the technique did something you noticed. Say what you tried and what you learnt. Include a line choice from the earlier lesson if it helps explain the drawing activity.

  • Core: use a large simple outline and the pale/dark prompts. Make your own tone marks. Point and explain a learning point for a verbatim scribe.
  • Extend: choose and apply a tone method, keep the drawing and record your own learning about the action and result.
  • Challenge: keep the same participation and learning evidence, then compare layers with hatching in two small areas.

Your account need not say you improved by a set amount. Explore asks what you learnt through taking part. A note about what you would try differently can be honest and useful.
