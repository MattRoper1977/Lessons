This is Part D: Share. Look back at Part A: Take part, Part B: Explore and Part C: Create. Choose one thing you enjoyed or one thing you achieved. Then find a way to tell someone about it.

Your choice can be small and clear. You might value a line you learnt to make or a choice in your own art. You do not have to call the whole course fun.

Today you will try your message with a listener. Keep this short practice as a rehearsal. Next time you will use your chosen way to share.

3 min

Arrival · retrieve

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Bring your own work into view

Open your real work from A, B and C. Lay it out in three groups, or open the three part files. Keep the labels beside the work so you can tell where each piece came from.

In A, find the two arts tasks and your learning. In B, find your real artist and arts group experience. In C, find your own art and the steps you took to make it.

Touch, point to or name one thing that draws your eye. You can use a photo or a sound note if the work is too large to bring.

If a record is missing, mark that gap. Ask for help to find it. A blank space must stay honest.

3 min

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

What would you want a listener to know?

An image can show your work, but it may not show what it means to you. Your words, signs or chosen labels can help a listener hear your view.

Read this example message: “I liked making soft and dark lines. The dark line helped my shape stand out.” It names a personal enjoyment and gives a reason tied to art.

Now read: “This is a sheet of lines.” That tells us what we can see. It does not yet say what the maker enjoyed or achieved.

Point to the words that give a personal view in the first example. Keep the idea, but do not copy its claim into your record. Your message must fit your own work.

6 min

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Look back, choose, show, tell

Look at the four steps below. First, scan all three parts. You can choose from any of them, but take time to look across the whole set.

Next, choose a true point. Enjoyment means something you liked in the arts work. Achievement means something you managed or made. You may choose either one.

Here is an example sentence frame: “I chose this from Part __. I enjoyed __ because __.” A second frame is: “I chose this from Part __. I achieved __. This part shows it.”

A blank frame is only a prompt. Use your own words to fill it. You can point while you speak, sign, or ask for your exact words to be written.

Last, let a listener hear or read the message. A short message with a clear link is enough to start. It need not be a speech to the whole room.

4 min

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Sort the four example cards

Use three headings: Enjoyment, Achievement, and Needs a personal view. Point to a heading or write the card numbers beneath it. All four cards are here.

  • Card 1: “I liked the sound of the paper as I folded it.”
  • Card 2: “I made a base that held my shape up.”
  • Card 3: “There is a shape on this page.”
  • Card 4: “I enjoyed choosing where the pale patch went.”

Make your own sort first. Then compare one choice with a partner or adult. Which words helped you decide?

Check key: Enjoyment has 1 and 4. Achievement has 2. Needs a personal view has 3. Card 3 could gain a true personal view, but we cannot choose that view for its maker.

5 min

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Choose a way that lets your view through

You can speak with your work beside you. You can make a page with an image and your own words. You can sign, point to chosen words, or make a short sound clip.

Choose a way you can use with a real listener. A page needs someone to read it. A clip needs someone to hear it. The person must receive your personal view.

For example, a page could have the chosen image at the top. Under it, place “I enjoyed…” and your own reason. A listener can read that while you point to the part you mean.

You may keep your art choice private from the whole class and share with one agreed person. You only need to talk about the arts work.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Try the message with one person

Choose one real item after your look back. Tell your partner or adult what you enjoyed or achieved. Point to the item as you share, or let them read your chosen words.

The listener says what they heard: “You chose __. Your view was __.” They use your words as far as they can. They do not add praise that changes your meaning.

Check that reply. Did your view reach them? If not, try one small change. Move the image beside the words, point to a clear part, or leave a longer pause.

If you have no real record yet, use a labelled example to test a format. Mark this as practice and name the missing work. Do not claim the example as your own.

11 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Make your own sharing plan

Set out your chosen item and a small plan. Name its part, your enjoyment or achievement, your way to share, and the person who can receive it. Keep your words short if that suits you.

Use most of this time to try the message. Your listener checks the meaning with you. You can change the format while keeping the choice your own.

  • Core · Look across A–C and choose a true enjoyment or achievement. Point, sign or speak it to a listener in rehearsal. Ask an adult to record your exact message and chosen way to share.
  • Extend · Look across A–C, choose your own enjoyment or achievement, and make a page or short spoken message. Rehearse it with a listener, using the real work to show what you mean.
  • Challenge · Make the same personal choice after looking across A–C. Share it in rehearsal, check what the listener understood, and change one feature so your meaning is clearer.

Save the plan and rehearsal note in your Part D file. Label them as preparation. Give the actual file name and page, slide or timecode so you can find them next time.

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Leave with a choice you can use

Show your listener the plan. Confirm what you want them to know and how you will share it. Ask them to say what they understood from your rehearsal.

You decide whether to keep the message or change it. Note the choice in your own words. A listener can help you make it clear, but they cannot choose your enjoyment for you.

Check that your chosen work is saved with its part label. If something is missing, keep that gap on your plan. Next time starts from this real point, even if you still need help to find a record.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
