Develop The Work And Record A Choice

Explore · Part C · Create. Develop the work and record a choice.

Today you will look closely at your work, try a change, and choose what to do. Save the work as it is before you move, cover or add a piece.

We are making a new print and collage. A print is a mark pressed from one surface to another. A collage joins pieces to make one image.

Your Part C record needs the making steps and the final artwork. Keep the earlier states even when you prefer the new one. They show how your work grew.

You can point, speak, type or write to show your choices. You may make a pattern or a made-up place. Your art does not have to tell a story about your life.

This deck supports Explore Part C, Create. Part C requires an artwork, a record of the PROCESS, and the final work. The new print-and-collage activity is distinct from the drawing and paper sculpture in Part A and the live or active exploration in Part B. Do not relabel a finished A or B work as C. Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Place your current collage beside its first sketch or saved image. Find a shape that is still there. Find a step you have made since the start.

What do you notice first when you look at the work? You can point to a print, a paper shape or a space. Look for what catches your eye, not what you think an adult will choose.

Keep the work as it is for a moment. Before you add anything, save a clear record of this state. You will need to look back at it after your next choice.

4 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Look at the same grey shape on two scraps. One scrap is pale and one is dark. The shape may stand out more clearly on one of them.

A strong difference between light and dark can help an edge stand out. This is called contrast. You can use it to draw the eye to part of an image.

You may want one clear focus, or you may want the marks to blend. Both can suit an idea. First, look at what is there. Then decide what effect you want.

Would it help to try a loose pale strip beside a dark print before gluing it? Tell a partner what the loose trial would let you check.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

Watch me develop my collage. I want the path of prints to be easy to follow. At this point, one dark print blends into the dark paper beside it.

I save a clear image of the work before I change it. I check that the whole piece is in view. I can still see the dark print and its edge in this record.

I place a pale strip beside the dark print without glue. Its edge now stands out. Then I try a smaller strip. The small strip leaves more of the old paper in view.

I compare the trials with my saved start. I choose the small pale strip because it shows the edge without hiding as much of the print. I fix the strip and keep a new record.

My choice is about the effect I want. If a pupil wants a soft edge, their choice may be different. The trial helps them decide for their own image.

5 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort these cards into KEEPS A BEFORE STATE or DOES NOT KEEP ONE. Think about what will still be there after a paper shape is covered.

  • 1. A clear photo shows the whole work before a new strip is glued.
  • 2. A ring is drawn around an edge, then that whole area is painted over.
  • 3. A separate sketch shows the layout before it changes, with the maker’s note.
  • 4. Someone says, I will remember what it looked like.
  • 5. The old test strip is kept beside the new strip.

The key is KEEPS A BEFORE STATE: 1, 3 and 5. DOES NOT KEEP ONE: 2 and 4. A memory can help you talk, but it does not keep the earlier work.

Which kept record could you use today? Choose a method that shows your real step clearly. You do not have to use a camera.

5 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

The before and after records help me name a change. I do not just write that my work got better. I name what I changed and the effect I can see.

In my model, I say: I added a small pale strip. Now I can see the dark print edge. These words match the two records.

I might test a change and decide to leave it out. Then I keep the trial and say why I kept the work as it was. Trying and choosing are still part of making.

A helper can record my exact words. They can ask which edge I mean. They should not turn my choice into a new reason that I did not give.

4 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Try a light piece and a dark piece beside the same print on a scrap. Keep both loose while you compare them. Which makes the edge easiest to see?

Now try a wider strip, then a narrow one. Look at how much of the print remains in view. Tell a partner or adult which effect you would choose for this small trial.

Keep a sample or a record of the trial. It gives you a method to use on your main work. You can choose where it fits, or choose a different way to develop the image.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Look at the before-state record you just saved. Choose a part of your work to develop. Try a loose piece, a gap or a change of size before you fix anything.

Use what the trial shows you to make your own choice. Add or move a piece if it suits your idea. Then keep the new state and name what you did.

  • Core: use large trial pieces and a helper to steady the work. Choose the effect yourself. Keep the before state, the trial and your own words; keep the final artwork when ready.
  • Extend: try a change, keep the before and after states, and explain the effect in your own words. Retain the process trail and the final artwork.
  • Challenge: compare two changes to contrast or space. Choose one or keep the old layout for a reason. Save the trials, the process trail and the final artwork.

Use a note, speech, pointing with labels, or a short recording to show your choice. Make sure the record points to your work and to a step that really happened.
