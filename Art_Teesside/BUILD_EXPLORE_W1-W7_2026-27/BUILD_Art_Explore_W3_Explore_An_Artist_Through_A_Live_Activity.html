Explore An Artist Through A Live Activity

BUILD · Art · Trinity Arts Award Explore. Explore An Artist Through A Live Activity.

Objective: Take part in a real artist session and keep a clear record of what you explored.

Success evidence: After the real session: the real artist’s identity, the pupil’s own action and finding, and an exact evidence location. Rehearsal stays labelled preparation.

You can explore through looking, listening, asking and trying. Choose a way to take part that works for you. You do not need to share private facts about your life.

We may go to them, they may come in, or we may meet live on screen. The route comes from the current slot card. No meeting is promised by this deck.

40-minute lesson frame. Staff may extend or split the real experience across sessions.

This deck serves Explore Part B, Explore. Part B requires at least one artist AND at least one arts organisation, through live or active experience. Advisers must not write plans, reviews or evidence for pupils. Scribe or record the pupil's own words and check them back unchanged. 3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Rehearsal: a closer look

Watch the adult make a loop on scrap paper. Look at where it starts and where it ends.

Now they make a second loop, with a small gap left open. What changed in the line? Point to the place.

A closer look helps you find something worth asking about. It may be a tool, a shape, a sound or a way to move.

You do not need to decide whether the work is best or worst. Start with what you can notice.

This is a classroom practice. It helps you look closely; it is not a record of contact with the artist in the slot.

I Do

I Do · model

Rehearsal model: look, ask and respond

The adult models how a question can grow from what is there. They point to the open loop and ask why a maker might leave a gap.

Next they try a loop of their own. They leave a gap in a new place. They look at what it does to the shape.

The adult names that try as their own response. They do not claim it tells them what an absent artist thinks.

In a real session, use the artist's work or actions as your starting point. Look or listen for a choice you want to explore.

Ask your question in an agreed way. An adult can read your own words. Or you can point to the part you mean.

Give the artist time to respond. Notice a word, a gesture or a step they show. If a try is offered, take part with the safe tools provided.

Keep what you learnt close to that moment. It may differ from what you expected.

If a reply is not clear, ask for a closer view or a simpler step. Finding out can take more than one look.

I Do

I Do 2 · connect

A clear record shows your part

A picture of some tools does not show what you explored. Add the action that belongs to you.

After the real session, record who the artist was and what they did. Then say what you asked, tried, noticed or found out.

Keep those two roles clear. You may have tried a process the artist showed. That does not make their work your own.

Use the real name from the confirmed card or source. Staff can help check spelling. Do not guess that the artist belongs to a group just because both names appear nearby.

Choose evidence that shows the moment. A work sample with a note may help. So may a short clip or a staff note.

Your own finding can be spoken, shown or written. Ask an adult to read back any words they write for you.

Label a practice as practice. An unanswered question stays a question.

Take part when the real session is ready

Staff will say whether the real session is ready or this is practice time. Use the right label.

Independent work

Independent · evidence

Keep what you actually found

After a real session, make a record. The details are still fresh. Name the artist and the session accurately.

Show your own action and one thing you found out. Point to a sample or moment that helps explain it.

If only preparation took place, keep that label. Improve your question or note about a detail. Label it clearly and leave the experience record open.

Find the arts group part of your sheet too. Was that group actively explored in this same experience? Check with staff. A name alone does not show that.

Save the record in Part B. Write the filename and page, slide or timecode. Keep preparation and real experience easy to tell apart.

  • Core · keep your question and prompt strip close. Use them to take part in the real artist session. Point, ask through an adult or try with support. Keep who the artist was, what you explored and the file location. If only rehearsed, label it preparation.
  • Extend · take part in the same real session and make your own record. Link a detail in the artist’s work to your question, try or response. Keep the artist element separate from the arts group element.
  • Challenge · keep the same real experience and record. Use the artist’s reply to form a follow-up, or try a change they suggest. Explain what that helped you find out. Do not invent an answer.
