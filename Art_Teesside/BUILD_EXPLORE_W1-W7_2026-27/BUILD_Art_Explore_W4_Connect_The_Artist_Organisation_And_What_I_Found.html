Connect The Artist, Organisation And What I Found

BUILD · Art · Trinity Arts Award Explore. Connect The Artist, Organisation And What I Found.

Objective: Use real experience records to explain what you found about an artist and an arts organisation, and check both are evidenced.

Success evidence: Own findings about both real roles, a supported connection or labelled comparison, exact record locations and an honest next action for any gap.

You can explore through looking, listening, asking and trying. Choose a way to take part that works for you. You do not need to share private facts about your life.

We may go to them, they may come in, or we may meet live on screen. The route comes from the current slot card. No meeting is promised by this deck.

40-minute lesson frame. Staff may extend or split the real experience across sessions.

This deck serves Explore Part B, Explore. Part B requires at least one artist AND at least one arts organisation, through live or active experience. Desk research alone fails; archives and prerecorded video are preparation only. Explore has four parts, each a distinct activity. Overall Pass allows ONE Attempted. Any Not Attempted = Below Pass. The whole Explore portfolio has a 10-file cap, organised digitally by part. The three assessment areas are art form knowledge and understanding, creativity, and communication. Results are Pass or Below Pass only. Only an adviser trained at Explore may assess; delivery is through a validated centre. Neither local training nor validation is asserted as confirmed here. Advisers must not write plans, reviews or evidence for pupils. Scribe or record the pupil's own words and check them back unchanged. Core, Extend and Challenge are support routes within Explore and retain the live or active requirement. An evidence locator names the filename plus page, slide or timecode. R1 pupils visit, R2 the artist or arts organisation comes in, R3 live remote exchange: all remain unconfirmed until actually arranged. Do not treat a recorded video as a live exchange. A single genuine experience can serve both if the pupil’s exploration of each is clear. If two experiences involved unrelated people and groups, allow a comparison of their arts roles without inventing a working relationship. Missing actual participation must be completed when arranged, with time beyond this lesson if needed. The pupil’s explanation is theirs; do not author it for them. I Do

I Do · model

Use the record before you make a link

Start with the artist record. Find the real name and the action you took part in. Look at the detail you chose to keep.

What did you find out about the person's art or way of working? Point to the part that supports your answer.

Now do the same for the arts group. Find its real name. Look for your action and what you found about its work.

Do the records show that the person and group worked together? Perhaps the source names a workshop the group ran with that artist. Check the source before you say so.

If the records do not show a working link, do not make one up. You can still compare what you explored.

The artist may have helped you notice a making choice. The group may have helped you notice how people can use or see art. Use only the details in your own records.

A link should help explain the work. It should not add a new fact just to make a neat story.

Keep a question mark where you need to find out more.

7 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

A working link or your comparison?

There are two clear ways to connect the findings. Choose the one your records support.

A working link means the real artist and group did some work together. The source must show that link. Name where it can be found.

A comparison means you have noticed a shared idea or a difference. It is your thought about the two experiences.

You might compare the tools or the space. Or look at how people take part. That does not mean the artist belongs to the group.

Use words that keep the difference clear. Does the record show a real link? Or did you notice a shared idea?

Make a small sketch, use arrows or explain aloud. Keep a detail from each real record beside the link.

If you are unsure, keep it as a question. You do not need to force a connection that the work does not support.

Independent work

Independent · evidence

Make your own account of what you found

Use your real records to show what you found about both roles. You may write, draw, speak or use a short recording.

Name the artist and arts group accurately. Keep your own action in each experience clear.

Explain a detail from each. Then add your supported link, your comparison or the question you still have.

Keep each filename and page, slide or timecode beside the finding. The reader should be able to follow it back to the real moment.

If preparation is all you have for one element, say so. Keep that gap as a next action. Complete the real session when arranged.

Save this account with Part B. Keep the original records too.
