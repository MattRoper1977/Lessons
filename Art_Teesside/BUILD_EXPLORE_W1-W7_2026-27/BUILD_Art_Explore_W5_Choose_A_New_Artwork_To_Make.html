Choose A New Artwork To Make

Explore · Part C · Create. Choose a new artwork to make.

Today you will try a print tool, choose an idea, and keep a record of your first steps. Your choices will guide a new work over four lessons.

We are making a new print and collage. A print is a mark pressed from one surface to another. A collage joins pieces to make one image.

Keep your test marks and first layout. Part C needs both the steps you took and the final artwork. Today begins that trail; the final piece comes later.

You can point, speak, type or write to show your choices. You may make a pattern or a made-up place. Your art does not have to tell a story about your life.

This deck supports Explore Part C, Create. Part C requires an artwork, a record of the PROCESS, and the final work. The new print-and-collage activity is distinct from the drawing and paper sculpture in Part A and the live or active exploration in Part B. Do not relabel a finished A or B work as C. Think about how you used a tool in Part A. You may look at your own drawing or paper form to help you recall a skill. Which action helped you control a line, fold or join?

4 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Look at a strip with one print and a strip with the same print repeated. A repeat can lead your eye across a page. A single mark can draw your eye to one spot.

A print may look different from its tool. Paint can fill a small gap. A light press may miss an edge. We need to try the tool before we choose how to use it.

Which would help you choose: a guess about the tool, or a test on scrap paper? Explain by pointing to the mark you would inspect.

Our task is to make an image from prints and paper pieces. You can choose a pattern, a path through shapes, or a made-up skyline. These are starting points, not answers to copy.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

Watch the test before you try your own. I put a thin layer of paint on the flat face of a sponge. I press it straight down on scrap paper, hold it still, then lift it straight up.

I can see a shape with a clear edge. I try again with more paint. This mark has a thick blob at one side. For my idea, the thin coat gives the edge I want.

I let the print dry. Then I tear a dry paper strip, place it beside the print, and move it across the edge. Part of the print is now hidden. That is overlap.

I want my marks to look like stepping stones. I leave gaps to make a path. I do not glue yet. I keep both tests and make a quick sketch of the loose layout.

This is my model, not your plan. You may want a soft edge or a dense patch. A different choice can work if it gives the effect you want.

5 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

I can learn from an old skill without turning an old work into my new piece. I used firm pressure for a fold in Part A. Here I use steady pressure to make a print.

I choose a small card so I can reach the whole surface. I choose a sponge that is easy to hold. These choices help me make the work myself.

On a blank page I put a rough sketch, a test print and my own words: I want a path with wide gaps. A sketch need not be neat. It needs to help me remember the start.

If I speak my idea, an adult can write my exact words and read them back. The adult does not choose the image or write a plan for me.

4 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

On scrap paper, try a small print with a thin coat. Lift the tool without sliding it. Try another mark beside it. Keep both so you can look from one to the other.

Let the tests dry in the agreed place. While they dry, place dry paper pieces on your fresh backing. Try a gap, then an overlap. Which helps your idea?

Tell a partner or adult which test you want to use. Point to the part of the mark that helped you decide. You may choose a different tool and try again.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Choose your new image and start a loose layout. Use your tests to choose shapes, marks and spaces. You can work with a few large pieces or several small ones.

Keep a sketch or image of the layout before you move it. Add a test sample and a short note or recording of your idea. Say which tool or paper you chose.

  • Core: use large ready-cut pieces, a chosen print tool and spoken words or pointing. Make your own layout. Keep the tests and start record, then keep the final artwork when it is made.
  • Extend: make and label your own tests, choose a layout and record your reason. Keep the process trail and the final artwork as the work grows.
  • Challenge: test two ways to space the same marks. Choose one for a reason. Keep both trials, your process trail and the final artwork.

Your route changes the help you use. It does not remove the making or its record. Check that your words say what you chose, not what someone else wanted.
