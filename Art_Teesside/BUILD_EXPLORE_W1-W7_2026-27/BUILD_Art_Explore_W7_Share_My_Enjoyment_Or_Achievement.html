Share My Enjoyment Or Achievement

This is Part D: Share. Today a real person will receive your chosen enjoyment or achievement. You can speak, sign, show a page, or play a short clip that carries your own view.

Use the choice you made when you looked back at Part A: Take part, Part B: Explore and Part C: Create. You may change that choice if a different true point now matters more to you.

Keep a clear record of what you shared and how. Then check all four parts so the work is easy to find and any gaps are plain.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

What counts as getting your view across?

Read these example messages. First: “Here is my art.” Second: “I am pleased that my folded shape stands up. I chose a wide base to help it stay still.”

The second message lets the listener know what the maker values. It links an achievement to a choice in the art. A photo could help show the wide base.

Your view may sound quite different. You might enjoy trying a tool, even if the result was hard to control. You can share that honest view without claiming that all of the work went well.

Tell a partner which words make the second example personal. The examples teach the task. They are not evidence of anything you have done.

I Do · model

Share first, then keep a true trace

Set the chosen work where your listener can see it, if seeing it helps. Give your message at a pace that works for you. Let them read, watch or hear it fully.

You can pause, point, use your usual aid or ask for more time. A listener should wait for your meaning. They must not finish your view with words you did not choose.

Here is a record frame: “I shared __ with __. I used __. My message was __.” Fill it after the sharing has happened. Include the real date and keep the chosen page or clip.

If an adult writes while you sign or speak, check their record. Ask them to change any word that alters your meaning. A short true record is useful.

We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Check words without adding a new claim

Use this example message: “I liked how the pale patch stood out.” Its example record says: “I liked all the colours and found the task easy.”

Find what changed. The message named one pale patch. The record added all the colours and an easy task. Those claims were not in the message.

Say or show the words that the record should keep. The clear correction is: “I liked how the pale patch stood out.” The maker’s own view now stays intact.

Before your turn, agree how your listener will receive the message. Let them know if you need quiet, a pause, a closer view or time to point. Check that the arrangement works for both of you.

Independent work

Independent · evidence

Share your chosen view now

Use your chosen way to share with your listener. Keep your real work in reach. Say what you enjoyed or achieved, and show what it links to. Let the listener hear or read the whole message.

  • Core · Look across A–C and choose something you enjoyed or achieved. Share it with a real person. You can speak, sign, point or show a page. Check that an adult note keeps your exact meaning.
  • Extend · Look across A–C and choose something you enjoyed or achieved. Share it with a real person in your chosen way. Keep your words or signs, how you shared and where the record is saved.
  • Challenge · Look across A–C and choose something you enjoyed or achieved. Share it with a real person. Use their reply to check your meaning reached them. Make it clear if needed, then keep a true record.

After you share, note what took place. Name who heard or read your view and how you shared. Give the real date. Add your words or signs and the file location. Check the saved note. Open that place. Check it holds your shared words, image or clip.

Use the last few minutes to open all four part files. Note missing work. A gap does not become complete just because you checked a folder.
