Explore What An Arts Organisation Does

BUILD · Art · Trinity Arts Award Explore. Explore What An Arts Organisation Does.

Objective: Explore an arts organisation through a real session and show what its work helps people do.

Success evidence: The real arts group’s identity, the pupil’s active exploration of its work, a finding and an exact record location. Preparation is labelled separately.

You can explore through looking, listening, asking and trying. Choose a way to take part that works for you. You do not need to share private facts about your life.

We may go to them, they may come in, or we may meet live on screen. The route comes from the current slot card. No meeting is promised by this deck.

40-minute lesson frame. Staff may extend or split the real experience across sessions.

This deck serves Explore Part B, Explore. Part B requires at least one artist AND at least one arts organisation, through live or active experience. A group can help art happen

Find your question for the arts group. What did you want to learn about its work?

Read the current arts group card with staff. It comes from the organisation slot. If the name or session is still open, keep it open on your sheet.

An arts group may help people make art, see art or learn about art. It can work in a building, outside or through a live link.

A logo tells you a name. It does not tell you all the work that group does.

Today you will look for that work in a real session, if one is ready. If not, the classroom practice stays preparation.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Rehearsal: making space for art

The adult draws two small paper pictures. They place one flat behind a pile of books and one where everyone can see.

Which can you explore from your seat? Point to what helps or gets in the way.

Now the adult moves the hidden picture into view. The art itself has not changed. The way people can reach it has.

Showing work is one way an arts group can help. It may also provide tools, run a workshop or help people talk about art.

This is a classroom model. It gives us ideas to test against what a real group actually does.

I Do

I Do · model

Rehearsal model: a job and its purpose

Watch the adult set up a tiny paper display. First they put both pictures close together, with one covering the other.

They try to look at each picture. Then they move them apart and check the view again.

The job was to place the work. The purpose was to help people see it. Those two things link.

The adult asks, 'What changed for the person looking?' You may answer by pointing to the space they made.

Use that same link in the real arts group session. Notice a job that the group does. Ask how that job helps people make, see or learn about art.

You might be offered a workshop task, a guided look at work or a live question exchange. Take your part in the real session.

Do not assume the group runs every kind of art or does every job. Find out from the people and work involved.

If you can, check a finding in a second way. A reply and a visible action may help explain each other.

Keep the finding about this real group. A general fact about galleries is not the same thing.

Visible cards: match the job and purpose

Match each job card to a purpose card. Use the words below, not a guessed story about the eventual group.

Could a job help in more than one way? Point to your best match and explain what makes it useful.

Keep these as possible roles. The real group may do different work. Find out through the experience.

  • A · Arrange pictures with space between them.
  • B · Set out safe tools for a workshop.
  • C · Invite questions about a piece of art.
  • 1 · Help people think and talk about the work.
  • 2 · Help people see each piece clearly.
  • 3 · Help people try a way to make art.

Independent · evidence

Keep your group finding

After the real session, put the group name on your record. Use the checked name from the source.

Show what you did and what you found about the group's work. Link a role to what it helps people do.

You could use a sample or short note. An agreed clip or staff account of your action may also help. Your own finding can be spoken or shown.

If the work is only preparation, keep that label. Save the useful question and tell staff which real element is still to do.

Check the artist record too. Do not mark it complete just because the group record has a name.

Save the work in Part B. Name the filename and page, slide or timecode.
