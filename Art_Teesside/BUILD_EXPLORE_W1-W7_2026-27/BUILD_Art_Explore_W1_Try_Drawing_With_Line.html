BUILD · Art · Week 1. Trinity Arts Award Explore. Try Drawing With Line.

Objective: Take part in drawing, try controlled line choices and say what you learnt.

Success evidence: your own line drawing, a record of how you took part, and a learning point linked to your marks.

Theme: Making and Sharing. Make choices about the art. You may keep your private life private.

Prepare plain paper, pencils, spare paper strips and optional thicker grips. Each pupil needs a practice area and a drawing area; these can share a sheet. On a large sheet or board, make the labelled examples described in the starter: A continuous curve, B a broken line following the same curve, C short overlapping strokes. These are teacher-made examples, not works attributed to an artist. Keep them visible. Project or write the exact sorting cards from We Do; pupils can sort by letter without printed cards. Use an empty cup or a folded paper shape as an optional still-life subject, or let pupils create an invented shape. No unprovided picture pack is needed. Offer wider paper and an accessible drawing tool. Agree verbal or visual pause cues and avoid physically moving a pupil’s hand.
3 min

Arrival · retrieve

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Today you begin the drawing activity. You will make marks, try a choice and say what you learnt. Your drawing does not need to look like anyone else’s.

Place a sheet where your arm can rest with room to move. Keep the pencil point away from your face. Test its grip on a spare strip. If it feels hard to hold, ask for a wider grip or another tool.

A line can lead the eye round a shape. It can be long or short, smooth or broken. These are choices a maker can try. A broken line is not always a mistake.

For now, look at the blank drawing space. Think of a simple shape you could draw there. Keep the pencil on the table while we look at the model.

3 min

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Look at the three labelled lines on the board. Line A is one curve with no break. Line B follows a curve too, but has gaps. Line C is built from short strokes that overlap.

Trace each route in the air with your finger. Which one lets your eye glide along? Which makes your eye pause? You may notice a different effect from the person beside you.

These lines could show the same edge in different ways. The choice depends on what the maker wants to show. A clear edge might suit a simple sign. A broken edge might suggest a shape that is only partly seen.

Point to a line you want to try. Then find one feature of it to watch while the teacher draws. You are choosing a way to make a mark, not the best person at drawing.

6 min

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

I want a long curved edge. I mark a small start dot and an end dot. They give my line a place to go. I move the pencil above the paper first to try the route.

Now I lower the point and use a light touch. I move my hand and forearm together. I watch where the line is going, not just the point under my hand. I stop near the end dot and lift.

The curve has a small bend I did not plan. I can keep it, or make a fresh attempt beside it. I do not need to grind over the same patch until the paper wears thin.

For a broken curve, I follow the same route but lift the point at gaps. I choose where to stop and start. Those lifts make breaks on purpose.

My learning note could say: planning the route helped me keep the curve long. That is a model of a note. Your note must come from what you actually try.

4 min

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Use two headings: helps a planned line, and needs a change. Sort these cards by letter. The cards stay visible while you decide.

  • A: I try the route above the page before I draw.
  • B: I press harder each time I miss the edge.
  • C: I pause and choose where my line should end.
  • D: I keep drawing when the pencil grip hurts.

A and C help a planned line. B needs a change: pressing hard may make the mark darker, but it will not choose the route. D needs a change too. Stop and ask for a grip or tool that works for you.

Choose one card from the second heading. Say a useful next action. You might try a lighter touch, make space for your arm or ask for support. We change the action, not judge the person.

5 min

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

I can use light touch or firm touch. On a spare strip, I draw one line each way with the same pencil. A firmer touch often leaves a darker mark. The result also depends on the pencil and paper.

Watch me build a dark edge in small steps. First I make a light guide. Then I follow the part I want to keep with another pass. I leave the spare guide visible while I choose.

Inside my shape, I add short strokes. These do a different job from the outside edge. Close strokes may suggest a rough surface. More space between them leaves more paper showing.

I point to the edge and then the texture. This lets a reader see which choice my note describes. “I drew an edge first” is clearer than “I did some stuff”. Both the work and my words help show how I took part.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Now use the practice space on your sheet. Choose a start and an end. Try a curve in the air, then draw it. Make a broken version beside it by lifting the point on purpose.

Look at the two lines you have just made. Point to a place where the movement of your hand changed the mark. Tell a partner or an adult what you did there.

The checker asks: where should I look? Show the exact place. They can help read your note or hold the paper still if you ask. They do not draw the line for you.

Add a short note or have your own words recorded. It may name a lift, a turn or a change in touch. If the two attempts look alike, say that honestly. You have still tried the drawing action.

11 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Use the drawing area for your own simple image. You can draw the object on the table or invent a shape. Choose a long line, a broken line or a set of short strokes to begin.

Build an edge, then decide whether to add marks inside it. Look between each choice. Ask what the new marks help the drawing show. You may leave space empty if that suits your idea.

Keep your practice marks with this drawing. Choose a part that shows something you learnt. Say what you tried and what you found out. A learning point can be about using the tool, making a mark or choosing an effect.

  • Core: use a larger shape and the start-and-end prompts. Make your own marks, then point and say what you learnt. An adult can record your exact words.
  • Extend: choose your lines and make the drawing. Add your own note about an action and what it helped you discover.
  • Challenge: keep the drawing and learning note, then show how two line choices give different effects.

These are support choices. Each keeps your participation and learning. There is no required number of perfect lines. Keep the result that shows what you actually did today.

Place your drawing and practice work together. Read or say your learning point while you point to the part it names. If a scribe wrote it, check that the words are yours.

This is the start of activity one: drawing. The next lesson adds tone to this same activity. Later you will also take part in paper sculpture. A folder full of drawing pages alone does not show two different activities.

Add today’s record to the Explore-A file. Write its actual file name and page or slide. For a sound clip, give the file name and timecode. Keep your work where it can be found for the next lesson.

Choose a line idea you would like to use again. You can also name something you want more help to try.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
