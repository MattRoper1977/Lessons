Explore · Part C · Create. Finish the artwork and show its making.

Today you will finish your new collage and show how you made it. You will keep the final work with real records of the steps that led to it.

We are making a new print and collage. A print is a mark pressed from one surface to another. A collage joins pieces to make one image.

Part C needs an artwork, its making process and the final work. A plan alone does not show the making. A final image alone does not show the steps you took.

You can point, speak, type or write to show your choices. You may make a pattern or a made-up place. Your art does not have to tell a story about your life.

This deck supports Explore Part C, Create. Part C requires an artwork, a record of the PROCESS, and the final work. The new print-and-collage activity is distinct from the drawing and paper sculpture in Part A and the live or active exploration in Part B. Do not relabel a finished A or B work as C. Arrival · retrieve

Set out your current artwork and the records you kept while making it. Find the start idea, a test, and a later state. You may have a sample, sketch, photo or short account.

Look back at the effect you wanted. Does your work now show it? Point to a part that is ready and a part that still needs care.

Choose a useful last step. You may need to fix a loose edge, finish a planned section, or clear spare scraps from the backing. You do not have to fill every space.

4 min

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Imagine two records of an artwork. The first has only a finished image. The second has only a sketch of what the maker hoped to do. What can each record show? What is missing?

The finished image shows what was made, but not how it grew. The sketch shows a start idea, but not that the maker went on to make the work.

Our record will join real making steps to the final piece. It helps someone follow your choices from the first test to the artwork you have now.

You can keep a short, clear trail. You do not need a long story. Each part should show or name something you actually did with your own work.

7 min

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Watch me finish my model. I look at the whole work before I touch it. The path is clear, but one paper edge lifts from the backing.

I keep a view of this state. Then I raise only the loose corner, add a little glue underneath, and press it flat with a clean scrap over the top. I check it again when it is ready.

I stop adding pieces because the open space is part of my idea. More paper would cover the gaps I chose. Finishing can mean knowing when to stop.

To keep the final view, I lay the dry work flat in even light. I include all four edges and keep my hand out of the frame. I check the image is sharp enough to see the prints and joins.

I keep the actual artwork too. If a scan gives a clearer view of this flat piece, I can use that. I name who captured the image so the record is clear.

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Sort these record cards into PROCESS, FINAL WORK, or NEXT ACTION. Say or point to the group for each number before we check the key.

  • 1. A test strip shows the marks made with two paint coats.
  • 2. A clear scan shows the whole finished collage.
  • 3. A saved image shows the loose layout before its pieces were fixed.
  • 4. I need to finish the last section before I record it as done.
  • 5. My note says why I chose a small pale strip during making.

The key is PROCESS: 1, 3 and 5; FINAL WORK: 2; NEXT ACTION: 4. A next action is useful, but it is not proof that the action has happened.

Which two groups must be present for the complete Part C record? We need process and final work. Keep any next action honest until it is done.

5 min

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

I put my first test and loose layout before the later state. I place the final image after them. I check that these records all belong to the same new artwork.

Under the layout I write: I moved the strip to leave a wider gap. Under the final image I write: This is my finished path collage. My words fit what the records show.

I open the saved file and note the page for each record. The words in my arts log do not tell a reader where to look. I need the real file name and a page, slide or timecode.

I do not remove an early test just because it looks less neat. It shows a part of the process that the final work cannot show by itself.

4 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

With a partner or adult, check your work from edge to edge. Look for loose pieces and any last step that belongs to your idea. Ask before touching someone else’s artwork.

Try a view of the whole work. Can you see the corners and the print marks? Move the camera or scan setting if an edge is cut off. Credit the person who helps capture it.

Now find a process record that matches part of the work. Point to the link between them. If you cannot find one, tell an adult what is missing so the gap can be checked.

9 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Carry out your last chosen making step. Preserve a state before a change. When the work is ready, keep a clear view of the whole final piece and store the artwork safely.

Add the final record beside your real tests, stages and choice notes. Put them in an order that helps a reader follow the making. Check that your own words match your work.

  • Core: use access tools and a helper to steady or capture the work. Make your own final choice. Point or speak to link your kept process records to the whole final artwork.
  • Extend: complete your last step, then order and label your real process records and final artwork. Explain a choice in your own words.
  • Challenge: complete the same process and final record. Trace how an early trial shaped a later choice, using your actual work to show the link.

If the work needs more time, say which step is left. Keep the part you have made and arrange time to finish. Do not label an unfinished image as the final piece.
