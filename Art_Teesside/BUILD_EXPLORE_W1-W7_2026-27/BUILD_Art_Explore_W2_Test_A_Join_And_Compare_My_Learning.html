Test A Join And Compare My Learning

BUILD · Art · Week 2. Trinity Arts Award Explore. Test A Join And Compare My Learning.

Objective: Continue paper sculpture by testing a join, then identify what you learnt in drawing and paper sculpture.

Success evidence: your own joining trial, an accurate paper-sculpture record, and learning from both distinct arts activities with locations for the evidence.

Theme: Making and Sharing. Make choices about the art. You may keep your private life private.

Arrival · retrieve

Bring your paper form to the clear space on the table. If it could not be stored, make a fresh tent from a plain rectangle. Fold it in half, open it part way and rest the open edges on the table.

Today you try a join. A join connects one part to another. You will use a folded tab and tape to connect a paper form to a flat base.

You will also look back at drawing and paper sculpture. Explore Part A asks you to take part in at least two arts activities and say what you learnt. These are our two different activities.

Keep the drawing record nearby, but leave it clear of the tape. There is no need to stick the work into a new folder for each lesson.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Look at a loose paper form on a base. When the teacher gently moves the base a little, the form may slide. Now watch the form connected by a tab. Try the same small movement.

The join can help the parts move together. It does not make the whole form impossible to knock over. A tall narrow shape may still lean, even when its join stays attached.

We need to check two things: whether the parts stay joined, and whether the whole form stands. Those are different questions. Adding more tape to a sound join may not fix a base that is too narrow.

Point to the join and then the base. Say which one you would inspect if a tab peeled away. Then say which one you would inspect if the joined form tipped as a whole.

6 min
I Do

I Do · model

I start with a wide paper strip. I fold it near one end and press a crease. I open it to make a corner, like a small letter L. This is my tab.

The short part lies flat on the base. The other part rests against the lower side of my paper form. I hold the tab in place while I check that both sections touch their surfaces.

Next I put a small tape piece across the foot of the tab and onto the base. It bridges the edge, so it touches both. I use another piece to bridge the upright tab section onto the form.

I press the tape down gently. I let go and move the base a little. If the tape lifts, I check whether it reached both surfaces. Tape stuck only to the tab will not attach it to the base.

I can try another tab on a different side if needed. I first look for the cause, rather than wrapping the whole form in tape.

4 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort these cards under two headings: check the join, and check the whole form. Match each letter to the problem it names.

  • A: The tape has peeled off the base.
  • B: The tab stayed attached, but the whole form tipped.
  • C: The tape touches the tab but not the form.
  • D: The base is narrow and the tall form leans.

A and C ask us to check the join. B and D ask us to check the whole form and its support. More tape is not the answer to every problem.

Choose a card and say a useful next check. For C, we could look for tape that bridges the two surfaces. For D, we could try a wider base position. The paper may behave differently, so test the idea before calling it a fix.

5 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

I test the tab join, then look back at what I learnt. My note names an action and something I found out. It does not need a score or a claim that everything improved.

Here are two model learning notes. For drawing: leaving white gaps between lines gave me a pale tone. For paper sculpture: the tape needed to touch both the tab and the base.

These are teacher examples, not words to copy as your own experience. You may have learnt a different lesson. Your paper might show that a wider base helped, or that a fold made a new space.

I can compare the activities without saying one is better. Drawing makes marks on a surface. Folding changes a sheet’s form in space. Both let me make choices, but the actions are different.

If I have no record of an art task, I leave that gap clear. A model cannot prove that I took part.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Make one trial join with your own folded paper and base. Place the tab, bridge its edges with tape and let go. Move the base gently, then stop and look.

Tell a partner or an adult what stayed attached and what moved. Point to the part you mean. A checker can help hold the base still when you ask, but what you saw stays yours.

If the join fails, look at where it came apart. Choose a small change to try. You may use a fresh tab or reposition a tape piece. Keep a note of the trial before taking it apart.

Say a learning point from this test. It can explain a problem as well as a working join. Have your words recorded if writing would stop you from taking part.

11 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Now use the join in your paper sculpture. Decide where a tab could connect the form to the base. Make and test the join. You can change the position if the real test gives you a reason.

Keep an clear, true record of what you did. It may be the form itself, a clear work-only photo with a note, or a located recording of your action and explanation.

Then look at your drawing record. Under Drawing, say what you tried and what you learnt from it. Under Paper sculpture, do the same for folding, standing or joining. Point to your own evidence for each.

  • Core: use a prepared wide tab and help with the tape backing. Place and test your join. Use the two activity prompts to say your own learning from both art tasks for a verbatim scribe.
  • Extend: make and test the join, then record learning from drawing and paper sculpture and say where the work is saved.
  • Challenge: retain both art tasks and their learning, then explain a useful difference between making a mark and building a form.

If an art task or learning point is missing, label it to do. Arrange a real chance to complete it with the adult. Do not write that an activity happened just to fill the box.
