GROW · Art · Week 5. Trinity Arts Award Silver. Lead The Project In Action.

Objective: Lead a real phase of your arts project, work with its participants and keep evidence of your own decisions and contribution.

Success evidence: actual project delivery, the pupil’s own leadership actions, real responses from others and precise Unit 2 evidence locations.

Unit 2 is your arts leadership project. Keep it distinct from your personal arts challenge in Unit 1. These lessons support your real project at its planned scale.

40-minute lesson frame. Real activity may continue in recorded programme time. No event, attendance or booking is asserted here.

3 min
Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Open your agreed role and current practical plan. Which part of the real project will you lead today? Name the people who need to understand what you are doing.

Check that the materials, space and responsible adult are ready. Look again at the risks and the agreed way to pause. A missing safety check is a reason to stop and resolve it, not to hurry past it.

If the real project is not ready, say what is missing. You can rehearse a method, but label that as preparation. Today’s date on a worksheet does not prove that delivery happened.

Keep your own role visible. Leading does not mean doing every job. It means taking responsibility for your agreed part and helping other people work towards the arts purpose.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Compare two short teaching examples. In the first, a leader takes the pencil and finishes another person’s pattern. In the second, the leader shows a mark on spare paper and gives the pencil back.

Which example helps the other person take part? Explain what the leader did, rather than simply calling them good or bad.

A leader may need to demonstrate, arrange resources, explain a choice or invite a response. These actions support the project. Taking over every task can hide what participants wanted to make.

A photograph of the finished group work will not show all these actions. Today you will keep a record of your own leadership as well as what the project produced. Both can matter, but they tell different parts of the story.

6 min
I Do

I Do · model

Watch the adult lead a tiny mark-making task with a willing partner. They explain the purpose: try how a repeated mark can make a pattern. They place paper and a pencil within reach.

The adult shows a dot, a short line and a curve on a separate scrap. They name each action while making it. Then they invite the partner to choose a mark and repeat it in their own way.

They ask the partner to show where they will start. That checks the explanation without demanding a long spoken reply. The adult gives time to try before offering more help.

If the partner is unsure, the adult asks which step needs showing again. They repeat that step slowly. They do not draw the partner’s pattern or decide which mark the partner must choose.

The adult checks the result of the help: can the person now begin, or is a different tool or explanation needed? They use the real response rather than assuming their support worked.

This small model teaches a leadership method. Your project may use different art and a different role. Keep its purpose, participants and agreed limits at the centre of your decisions.

4 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort these visible cards into LEADERSHIP ACTION or NEEDS A CHANGE. Point to a letter and give a reason before checking the key.

A: I show the process on scrap paper, then let the participant try.

B: I say everyone joined in because their names were on my plan.

C: I ask what was unclear and change my explanation.

D: I finish their work without asking because time is short.

The leadership actions are A and C. B confuses planned participation with real participation. D takes away the other person’s action and choice.

Choose a useful replacement for B or D. You could record who actually took part, offer a smaller safe task, or explain which part needs more time. Keep the record honest when plans change.

6 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

Look back at the live model. A finished pattern shows the participant’s marks. It does not, by itself, show how the adult explained the task or responded to a question.

A short factual note can fill that gap: the adult showed a curve on spare paper, then repeated the first step when asked. The note must describe what really happened in the model.

For your project, identify your own actions and decisions. Keep another person’s response separate from your thoughts about it. They may have found a step hard even when you felt your explanation was clear.

An observer can record what they saw or heard. They cannot know your reason unless you explain it. Add your own words or confirmed signs, and check any scribed account before saving it.

This delivery record supports 2C. Your actual exchanges and response to others also support 2D. Mark both parts clearly without copying the same image into several new files.

A quiet, useful intervention can show leadership. You do not need a loud speech, a public audience or a dramatic problem to make your contribution count.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Practise explaining one safe art step from your own project to a willing collaborator. Agree that this is rehearsal. Show the action using suitable materials before asking them to try.

Ask them to show or say what comes next. Listen for one thing that needs a clearer explanation, a closer view or more time. Change that support and check whether it helps.

Let the collaborator do their own part. Ask before handling their materials or moving their work. Respect a request to pause, and involve the responsible adult for any safety concern.

Keep a brief rehearsal note if it will help you deliver. Do not label this practice as the project itself unless it genuinely is an agreed delivery phase with real participants and a real arts purpose.

10 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Lead the agreed part of your real arts project now, if it is ready. Make the purpose and next step clear. Use the art method, resources and access support you prepared.

Work with the other people in their real roles. Give them space to make choices. Check what they understand, notice what actually happens and respond within the limits you agreed.

Core: use a short cue card and access help. Carry out your own real leadership role, communicate with participants and respond to them. Point, speak or dictate an accurate record of your actions and contribution.

Extend: lead your agreed phase and record decisions, participant responses and how you worked with others. Link a real example to your own role.

Challenge: keep the same actual delivery and role evidence. Notice how a leadership choice affects participation, make a safe adjustment if needed and check its effect with the people involved.

Use the evidence method agreed in your plan. A factual observer note, work sample, short clip or annotated image may help. Add your own account; do not let the evidence task prevent you leading safely.

Save the actual file name and page, slide or timecode under Unit 2, Parts 2C and 2D. If delivery could not occur, name the gap and arrange a genuine opportunity. Keep rehearsal labelled as rehearsal.

3 min
Exit ticket

Exit · identify your contribution

Pause with the team when the project phase allows. Name one action you actually led and one response you received. Check a factual detail with a collaborator if you are unsure.

Find the saved evidence for that moment. Can a reader tell which action was yours, which came from another person and what happened next? Add a clear label if the roles are hard to follow.

Record what is finished and what still needs attention. The next delivery lesson can support the same project. It does not mean you must invent a second event or claim that this phase completed every aim.
