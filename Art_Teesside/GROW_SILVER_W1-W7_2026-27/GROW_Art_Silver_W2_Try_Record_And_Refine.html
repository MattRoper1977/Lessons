Starter

Starter · create the need

A change should teach you something

Artists often try, look and adjust. The useful part is noticing what a change does to the work.

You could alter the tool, pressure, paper and spacing together. The result may be interesting. It is then harder to tell which choice caused an effect.

To test one choice, keep other features steady. Try it on a small sample first. Then you can compare more clearly.

This is a practical method, not a rule that all art must be made one way. Your intention decides what is worth checking.

Choose a detail where a careful trial could help you understand your next step.

I Do

I Do · model

Adult model: build tone with hatching

The adult draws parallel pencil lines with a light grip. Hatching builds tone with rows of lines; gaps leave paper visible.

On a fresh patch, they bring the lines closer while keeping similar pressure. Compare the tone beside the first patch.

On another patch, they add light lines at a different angle. This is cross-hatching. The overlapping lines make a darker tone.

Their aim is a dark area with visible line texture. Hard pressure is not the only way to darken it.

They choose a patch to develop and record the change, keeping the earlier samples. For paper form, try a tab or support before attaching it.

Examine the effect and choose what to keep. Your art and preference may differ from the adult’s.

Model explanations

  1. The pencil, paper and light pressure stay similar. What might closer line spacing do? — Create a darker tone while retaining line texture.. Closer lines leave less white paper between them and can create a darker tone. Check your real samples from the same distance.
  2. Paper, pencil, pressure and spacing all change. Can the result isolate spacing alone? — No; several features changed at once.. The art may be useful, but several changes prevent that single-cause conclusion.
  3. Your actual patch differs from your prediction. What belongs in the process record? — Both real patches and what you noticed.. The real difference helps you decide a next trial. A prediction must not replace the observed work.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Practise and inspect the result

Begin the step you selected from your own plan. Use the technique staff have demonstrated for your materials.

For hatching, compare line spacing or direction without losing the earlier patch. For a folded tab, crease the joining edge clearly. Test the fit on scrap before fixing it in place.

For a tab join, fold the joining strip so it lies flat against the second piece. Align the edges before using the agreed adhesive. Support the join while it sets. Test the scrap gently and look for twisting or a gap before deciding to use it on the main form.

Pause where the result can tell you something. Look from the relevant angle or distance rather than inspecting only the easiest part.

Ask a partner or staff member what they notice. Focus their attention on the detail you are checking. Point to the work so the question is clear.

Record the response accurately if one is given. You decide whether and how it should affect your next action.

Independent work

Independent · evidence

Refine the work and keep the reason

Use what you noticed to choose a next step. You may repeat the method, adjust one feature or try a different safe approach.

Keep the earlier state before acting. Then practise the change and inspect its effect against your own intention.

Record what you did and why. Use a short note or spoken account of the detail. Simply saying the result is better does not explain the change.

Keep any outside observation clearly attributed. Add your own response rather than presenting the other person's view as yours.

Update the next step on your plan if needed. Continue the real practice for the time the challenge requires.

Save the evidence in Unit 1, Part 1B with the filename and page, slide or timecode.
