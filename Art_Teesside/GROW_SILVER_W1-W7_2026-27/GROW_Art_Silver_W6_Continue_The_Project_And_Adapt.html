Arrival · retrieve

Open the record from your real project delivery. Find your own role and a response from someone who took part. What does that record actually show, rather than what you hoped it would show?

Check what remains in the project. You might need to finish a planned phase, support another group within the same activity or complete a task already agreed. Use the real plan and people.

If all delivery is complete, do not invent a second event for this lesson. Keep the earlier delivery evidence. Staff will help identify whether any real follow-through is needed, and label a review or rehearsal honestly.

Today’s method is to notice, check, adapt and look again. An adjustment should help the arts purpose and the people taking part, not merely make your record longer.

3 min

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Consider this teaching case: a person keeps asking where to begin the art task. A leader decides that the whole group needs a harder task instead.

What evidence supports that decision? The question may show that the first step is unclear. It does not prove that the task is too easy.

An observation says what happened. An interpretation is what you think it might mean. Keep them separate until you check with the person involved.

Ask a question such as, Which part do you need to see again? A useful adjustment might be a clearer demonstration, a different position or more time. It might also be no change if you have misunderstood the issue.

Start from the real response. You do not need to invent a problem to show leadership.

6 min

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Watch the adult test an explanation with a willing partner. The card says, Choose a mark and repeat it across the paper. First the writing is small and the card is placed at a distance.

The adult asks what the partner can see and what they need. They use the actual reply. If the writing is clear already, they say so; they do not manufacture a difficulty.

Now they show a larger-lettered version at the same position. The partner stays in the same viewing seat. Which version gives a clearer starting point? The partner’s actual answer supplies the comparison evidence.

The adult changes only the letter size in this trial. The wording, art task, card position and viewing seat stay the same. This makes it easier to judge whether the letter-size change helped.

They check the effect by inviting the partner to point to the first action or begin the marks. A yes alone may not show whether the next step is understood.

The adult records what was tried, the real response and the decision. If neither version helps, they keep that result and test another explanation. An unsuccessful trial can guide a useful next step.

4 min

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Match each visible card to OBSERVATION, POSSIBLE MEANING, or ACTION TO CHECK. Read the whole statement before choosing.

A: The person asked me to repeat the first step.

B: Perhaps they could not see where I pointed.

C: I ask which part they want shown again.

D: They paused after I moved the tool.

E: I demonstrate the move slowly and ask them to show the next step.

The key is observation: A and D; possible meaning: B; action to check: C and E. A pause can have more than one cause. We must not turn a guess into a fact about another person.

Choose one observation and suggest a respectful check. Keep your question about the task or access, without asking the person to disclose private information.

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

A change needs an owner and an agreement. If I move an instruction card within the agreed space, I may be able to do that within my role. If I change the room or use a new tool, other checks may be needed.

Look back at the practical plan. Ask whether the adjustment affects resources, risk, access, timing or another person’s job. Discuss the change with the people responsible before using it.

Keep the original instruction or state beside the updated one. Record what led to the change and what happened afterwards. Do not replace the first record so that the project looks as if it worked perfectly from the start.

For 2C, show the leadership decision and its place in actual delivery. For 2D, show how you listened, agreed the action and worked with others to carry it out.

A team may reasonably choose to keep the current method. Record that decision and the evidence behind it. Leadership includes checking a concern carefully; it does not mean changing something every time you hear a comment.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Choose a small explanation or resource from your real project that could use a check. Keep the original version. Ask a collaborator to try it or review it from the position it will be used.

Listen to the actual response. Agree one safe adjustment to trial, or a reason to keep the method. Do not force a complaint when the step works.

Try the adjustment with suitable spare materials before changing the main activity. Check the result with the person who raised the issue. They may still need something different.

This lab is a practical trial. Label it as rehearsal unless it genuinely takes place within the agreed project delivery. Keep the distinction clear even when the same people are involved.

10 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Continue the real delivery phase that remains in your plan. Use your agreed role to support its arts purpose. Notice what people actually do, ask or show while taking part.

When a response calls for adjustment, check its meaning. Agree a safe change with the relevant people, try it and look for its effect. Keep enough of the earlier state to explain what changed.

Core: use a cue strip and access support while carrying out your own real delivery role. Check a response with a collaborator, contribute to the decision and record your action and the actual result.

Extend: lead your phase, make or retain a method for a reason and check the effect with others. Keep the original state, your decision and the true response.

Challenge: retain the same real delivery and collaboration evidence. Compare possible adjustments, explain the choice within your role and check whether it helped the intended arts outcome.

If no further delivery is needed, work from the actual existing evidence and label any review or preparation as such. Do not stage a pretend second session. Name any genuine delivery gap for staff to arrange.

Save the record under Unit 2, Parts 2C and 2D, with the actual file name and page, slide or timecode. Keep the pupil account separate from an observer’s factual note and from another person’s words.

3 min

Exit · check the effect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Point to the response you checked and the decision that followed. What evidence shows the effect? It is acceptable to say that the result is still unclear or the change did not help.

Confirm the record with the people involved where needed. Make it clear who suggested the change, who agreed it and what you personally did. Shared work does not mean everyone did the same thing.

Keep unfinished practical matters visible for the team. Save the precise location of the record so you can use it in your later review. A truthful limit is more useful than a success claim with nothing behind it.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
