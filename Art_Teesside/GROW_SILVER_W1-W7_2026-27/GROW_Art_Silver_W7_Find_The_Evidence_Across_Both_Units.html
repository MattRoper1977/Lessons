3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Check whether the conclusion reaches too far

Read this authored example conclusion: “The session finished, so our team worked well together.” Finishing tells us about the end of the session. It does not show how the team worked.

Here is an example record: “I asked which task needed help. We agreed to change the order, and I moved the paper to the next table.” The record gives a contribution that a review can discuss.

A more useful conclusion is: “I helped the team change the order after checking what was needed. Our agreement gave me a clear task.” This claim is linked to the example record.

Tell a listener what became clearer. These are teaching examples only. Your conclusion must grow from your own project and records.

6 min
I Do

I Do · model

Make a reasoned change to your review

Use a simple sequence: read the claim, check the record, adjust the judgement, then explain the next step. The review should become more accurate, not merely longer.

In the example, the leader could add: “Next time I would check task order with the team before we start. That may reduce the need to move tools during the task.” This follows from the problem in the example.

The leader cannot claim that the new order would always work. A next step is a plan with a reason until it has been tried. You can explain why it seems useful while naming that limit.

Read back a change before saving it. If a scribe helped, check every statement about what you thought or learnt. Keep your own view when someone offers a different interpretation.
