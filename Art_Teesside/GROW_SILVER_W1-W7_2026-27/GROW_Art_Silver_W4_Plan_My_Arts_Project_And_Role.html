Plan My Arts Project And Role

Silver · Unit 2 · Part 2A · Plan my arts project and role.

Unit 2 is Arts leadership. You will plan an arts project and your own role in leading it. Your Unit 1 work can inform your choice, but the two units keep separate evidence.

Leadership means helping people work towards an arts purpose. It can involve explaining, listening, organising choices and responding when someone needs support. It does not require being the loudest person.

Today you choose a project and a role that you can describe clearly. The practical plan, real delivery, work with others and review follow in later stages of the project.

3 min
Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Look at art you have made or a method you have explored. Which skill could you explain clearly, or which art idea could you help a group develop? Use your actual experience to choose a starting point.

Think about the people who might take part. They could be willing peers, a small group or staff learners. What might help them enjoy or understand the art activity? Do not assume they have already agreed.

List an interest you bring and a responsibility you might manage. You can write, speak or use labelled pictures. Your plan should show your own thinking rather than a role chosen for you.

4 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Compare two project statements. First: We will do some art and I will help. Second: I will lead a print activity so learners can explore repeated shapes. I will demonstrate a print and guide their choices.

The second statement names an arts purpose and a leadership responsibility. We can begin to picture what the leader will do. The first leaves both the art and the help unclear.

A project aim describes what you want the activity to achieve. Your role describes what you personally will lead. A group may share an aim while each person holds a different responsibility.

You do not need to run a large event. A manageable project can give you real decisions to make, people to work with and useful evidence to review later.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

Watch me plan an example before you make your own choice. My model project is a print activity for a small willing group. I want the learners to explore how a repeated shape can create a pattern.

I consider what I could lead. I could explain the task, model a safe print, ask learners about their choices and help them compare results. These actions connect my role to the arts purpose.

I also name what belongs to others. An adult checks the room and materials. Learners choose their own shapes and spaces. I do not take over their artwork just because I lead the activity.

I picture an outcome: learners can show a pattern and explain a choice. I will later plan how to find out whether that happened. At this point it is an intention, not a result.

This example shows how to connect ideas. It is not a plan for you to copy. Your arts purpose, people and responsibility should suit the project you choose.

5 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

A group project can make responsibility hard to see. I separate the team’s shared work from my own decisions so a reader can understand my contribution.

For my model, the group may arrange a small display. My role could be to guide the choice of labels. I would ask what each maker wants viewers to notice and help agree a clear order.

Someone else could lead the physical layout. I would listen to that person and agree how our tasks connect. Naming my role does not mean I work alone.

I also check whether I know enough to lead the task. If I need to practise the method or ask for access support, I include that as preparation. I do not claim a skill I have not tried.

4 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Consider two feasible project ideas. You might lead a print activity, guide a group collage or help a group select and explain work for a display. You may suggest another arts project.

For each idea, name the arts purpose and a role you could lead. Check whether the materials, people and your current skills make it a sensible choice.

Talk with a partner or adult about the trade-off. Which option gives you a clear responsibility and suits the people who may take part? Keep your reasons; the final choice is yours.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Choose your arts project and record its purpose. Identify the people who may take part and the outcome you hope to support. State what still needs agreement.

Define your own leadership role through concrete actions. Explain how those actions serve the arts purpose. If you are working in a team, distinguish your role from the responsibilities of others.

  • Core: use prompts, labelled pictures or an exact scribe to record your own chosen arts project and personal leadership role. Include the purpose, intended outcome and any needed preparation.
  • Extend: make the same pupil-chosen project and role plan in your own format, connecting your responsibilities to the purpose and intended outcome.
  • Challenge: retain the same project and personal role evidence. Compare a second feasible option and explain why your chosen responsibility offers the best fit.

Keep the first version before you revise it after feedback. Your plan can change as you learn more. The record should show which choices were yours and why you made them.
