3 min
Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Open your project purpose, your own role and the practical plan. Put a finger beside one step you are responsible for. Say what other people need from you to carry it out.

Your plan covers promotion, recruitment, resources, risk and feedback. These words describe real jobs: how people hear about the activity, who joins, what is needed, what could cause harm, and how you hear responses.

Today you test a part of that plan with other people. You are finding out whether it works before calling it ready. A test can reveal a useful problem.

Choose a safe step that can be tried with the time and people available. Keep an untouched copy of the current plan so your later change can be seen.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Read this teacher example: the instruction says, Make a pattern with three types of mark. The pencil and paper are ready, but the group cannot see an example of a mark.

What would you check before deciding the group needs more pencils? The missing help may be a clear explanation, not another resource.

A plan can look complete on paper yet leave a person unsure what to do. Testing lets a real person try the step and describe what happened. Guessing what they might think does not do that job.

Each collaborator may notice something different. One may need the instruction closer. Another may need more time. A useful test makes room for their actual response rather than asking them to agree that your plan is good.

6 min
I Do

I Do · model

Watch the adult model a small test with a willing partner. They show paper and a pencil and ask the partner to make a pattern using three types of mark.

The partner says what is clear and what needs explaining. The adult listens without finishing the reply. They repeat the point back: You want to see what counts as a type of mark. Is that right?

The adult then draws a dot, a short line and a curve on scrap paper. They name the actions while making them. They ask whether this makes the instruction clearer, rather than claiming it must help.

They agree a change together: keep a large mark example beside the task. The adult records the real response and the change to this model test. Your test may need a different change.

Notice the sequence. We tried the instruction first, heard a response, checked its meaning, then agreed what to change. The adult did not invent a complaint or write the partner’s answer before asking.

If the test reveals a safety issue, stop that part and involve the responsible adult. Agreement between pupils does not make unsafe equipment safe.

4 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort these visible cards into USEFUL TEST or NEEDS A CHANGE. Use the letter to show your choice before checking the key.

A: I ask a partner to try reading the task from the place they will sit.

B: I write that everyone understood before anyone tries it.

C: I ask what the partner found difficult and check I understood the reply.

D: I keep testing a damaged tool because the group voted to continue.

The key is useful test: A and C. B invents a result. D ignores a safety concern; stop and ask the responsible adult.

Choose B or D and explain a better next action. Keep the action specific. Saying communicate better does not tell someone whether to ask, listen, change the instruction or stop using a tool.

6 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

A group decision does not show everyone’s contribution by itself. Compare these two teacher-example notes. We sorted it is too broad. I showed the instruction; my partner tested it; I checked their reply and enlarged the example identifies roles.

The second note also needs to be true. A detailed invented note is not better evidence than a short honest one. Name what you actually did and what came from someone else.

An updated plan shows the practical change for 2B. A note of the exchange shows how you worked with others for 2D. They may be kept together if both parts can be found.

Recheck the wider plan after a change. A larger display may need a different space. A new material may change resources or risk. A different group size may affect recruitment and the way you gather feedback.

For example, a shorter art task may suit people who can join only briefly. That affects the invitation and the time you offer. Check the change with those people; do not write that they accepted it before asking.

Keep the first plan as well as the update. Label what is still untested. One successful trial does not prove that every practical issue has been resolved.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Now choose one step from your own plan to test with a real collaborator. Tell them what you want to find out and invite them to try it. Use agreed safe materials.

Watch what happens before offering an answer. Ask what helped or got in the way. Check their meaning by repeating or showing the point back.

Agree one action to keep, change or check further. You may disagree at first. Give a reason tied to the task, and ask for the other person’s reason before deciding.

Record the trial while the details are fresh. Label it preparation or rehearsal. This actual team test supports planning and working together; it is not a claim that your whole project has been delivered.

10 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Use the test result to update your practical plan. Show what changed and why, or explain why you kept a step. Check promotion, recruitment, resources, risk and feedback for any linked change.

Agree who will carry out the next action. State your own part clearly, alongside any part another person has accepted. Do not assign a job to someone who has not agreed to it.

Keep the other person’s response accurately, then record your own contribution and how you responded. Ask them to check a shared factual detail if needed; your explanation remains yours.

Core: use the five practical headings and help with materials. Take your own part in the real test, listen and agree an action. Dictate or show your update and contribution for an accurate record.

Extend: conduct the test, agree the practical action and update your plan. Link your own contribution and the response from others to the decision you made.

Challenge: retain the same real test and records. Check a consequence of the change elsewhere in the plan and agree how the team will handle it.

Save the records under Unit 2, Parts 2B and 2D. Name the actual file and page, slide or timecode for each. If a test could not happen, name what remains to be done rather than reporting an imagined outcome.

Exit ticket

Exit · check the next action

Show your collaborator the updated step and your own next job. Ask whether the record matches the action you agreed. Correct a factual error without changing someone’s words to make them sound more helpful.

Use the file location to find the first plan, the trial and the update. Can a reader tell what you did, what another person contributed and why the decision changed?

The next session supports real delivery when the project is ready. Keep any missing permission, resource or agreement visible. A plan being saved does not mean those jobs are complete.
