Meet A Practitioner And Trace A Path

Silver · Unit 1 · Part 1D · Meet a practitioner and trace a path.

Part 1D connects a real encounter with research. Once a route is arranged, meet arts practitioners and gain first-hand experience. Explore arts organisations and investigate routes into learning and work.

3 min
Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Look at a question from your arts challenge. What do you want to understand about how an artist works? Choose something you could learn by watching, asking or trying a process.

Turn a broad question into a focused one. Instead of How do you make art?, try How do you choose which print to keep? A focused question can reveal a decision you may test in your own work.

Keep the question in your research record. Check what is known about the actual artist and organisation, then mark what still needs arranging. A prepared question shows intent, not completed contact.

Choose an access method that lets you take part and keep a record. You may need a reader, a tool grip, time to process an answer, or a short note after the activity.

4 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

A biography can tell you about an artist’s work, but it cannot respond to your next question. During a live exchange, you can ask about a choice, watch a process and respond to what happens.

First-hand experience means being involved in the actual workshop or event. Follow the agreed activity and make a record of what you explored. Merely copying a fact about the person does not show that experience.

An arts organisation can support artists, present work or help people take part. Research the role of the actual organisation you explore. Do not assume its role from its name.

You will also examine education and career pathways. One asks how skills can be learnt; the other asks how people may use those skills in arts work.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

Watch how I prepare to learn from a process. I try tearing a paper strip quickly, then tear another while turning the paper slowly. I notice how the edges differ. This classroom model practises observation; it is not evidence of your practitioner meeting.

I form a question from what I saw: What helps you choose a rough or smooth edge? If the artist explains a choice, I can ask a further question linked to their answer.

During a real workshop, I follow the agreed activity and keep a sample or a located record of my action. I note who led it and what I tried. I record only what happened.

Afterwards, I separate their words from my own observations. A direct quote needs an accurate record. If I am unsure of the wording, I use a faithful summary and identify it as a summary.

I might want to change a method I use, or decide it does not suit my aim. Either influence needs a reason connected to something I genuinely learnt.

5 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Match each card to ENCOUNTER, ORGANISATION, EDUCATION, CAREER, or INFLUENCE. These are example record types, not facts about a named person.

  • 1. My workshop sample and note name the artist, task and what I tried.
  • 2. A checked page explains what an arts centre does and who can take part.
  • 3. A provider’s course page explains the skills taught and entry requirements.
  • 4. An artist explains a work route and the skills it involves.
  • 5. I explain how a process I explored changes my next arts choice.

The key is ENCOUNTER: 1; ORGANISATION: 2; EDUCATION: 3; CAREER: 4; INFLUENCE: 5. Notice that education and career are separate questions, even where one route connects them.

At the real arranged meeting, meet the artist and join the first-hand workshop or event. Use your question when appropriate and keep the resulting evidence. Respect a request not to record.

5 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

A pathway is a possible route, not a promise. I can trace learning into work while checking what each step needs. I need evidence for both sides of the route.

I show how to read the course page beside the artist or organisation source. I find what can be learnt, who the route is for and any stated entry steps. I keep the source title and exact location.

Then I find what an arts role involves. I separate a source fact from a guess about pay, access or success. If the page does not answer a question, I mark that gap for further checking.

Finally, I connect a finding to my interest: this route includes a skill I want to develop. The learner chooses that link; an adult cannot write the influence for them.

We Do

We Do 2 · lab

With a partner or adult, check a real finding from your encounter against its record. Does it identify who you met, what the activity was and what you personally explored?

Now check the organisation and pathway sources. Can you find an organisation role, an education route and a career route? Keep the publisher, page title and source location beside each finding.

Ask which claim is least clear. Decide whether to check the source, ask a further question or keep the gap open. A bold guess is still a guess.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Build your own account from the real meeting, first-hand activity and checked research. Show what you found out about the artist and organisation, then name both an education route and a career route.

Summarise the influence in your own words. What has changed in your understanding, interest or next arts choice? Point to a real finding that explains the link.

  • Core: use selected source passages, prompts and exact scribing. Keep the real meeting and first-hand activity, organisation research, education and career routes, and your own influence summary.
  • Extend: organise the same encounter, organisation, both pathway areas and own influence summary with clear source and evidence locations.
  • Challenge: keep the same full evidence and compare two possible routes or findings. Explain which better fits your present arts interest and why.

Retain your original notes when you revise the account. Check any scribed words with the adult. Name any contact or research still to do; do not present practice as a real meeting.
