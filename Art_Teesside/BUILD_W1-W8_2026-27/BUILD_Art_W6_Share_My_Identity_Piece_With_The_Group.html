BUILD · Art · Week 6. Trinity Arts Award Discover. Show One Part, Say Why You Chose It.

Objective: share your identity piece with the group by pointing at one part, naming the mark or colour you used, and saying in one sentence why you chose it.

Success evidence: one share to your table group, one sentence in your own words, and one word the group gave back.

Your identity piece is built from cards in the approved pack. You talk about the card, the mark and the colour. You do not talk about your home, your family or what you believe. Nobody in this room is asked about theirs.

40 minutes. Offline.

3 min

Arrival · retrieve

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

A share has three parts

To share is to show one thing and say why.

It is not a speech, it is three short parts.

Part one is the point, where you point at one part of your piece.

Part two is the name, where you name the mark or the colour.

Part three is the why, where you say why you chose it.

Read the three parts once and leave them on the board. Do not invite talk about home; the card pack is the guard this lesson rests on.

Last week your photo went in the log. Today the group hears the words.

You need three things: your piece, your log sheet and one pencil.

3 min

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Two shares, one piece

Watch two shares of the same piece.

In the first I hold the whole sheet up and say it is done.

You look at all of it, and you do not know where to look.

In the second I point at one corner, name the mark and say why.

Now you know where to look, and you heard one reason.

The piece did not change. The share did.

  • 1 · point at one part.
  • 2 · name the mark or the colour.
  • 3 · say why you chose it.

A short share with one reason beats a long one with none.

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

I share my piece

My piece is built from two cards, a key and a leaf.

I point at the top corner, and that is part one.

I name what is there. It is a hard mark in grey. That is part two.

Then comes the why: I chose a hard mark because the key is metal.

That is part three, and I stop there.

Then I take one word back from the group.

One person says one word about what they saw, such as sharp, heavy or cold.

I write that word on my log sheet, because it is their word.

Say each part name out loud as you do it. Point. Name. Why. The words are the thing being taught, not the piece.

Watch my hand, not only my mouth. My finger stays on one part.

Watch my speed too. I count to three before I speak. That pause gives the group time to look.

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Sort the shares, then match the missing part

Six share cards are on your table. Each card holds one share, written out.

Sort them into two piles: ready to say, and not ready.

A ready card has a point, a name and a why.

Now take the pile that is not ready.

Match each card to the part it is missing. It is the point, the name or the why.

Every card gets a match, even when you are not sure.

A guess I can see beats a blank card.

Then hold up the card your table argued about most.

7 min

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Point first, then talk

Now watch the same share done the wrong way.

I hold the whole sheet up and say lots of words.

Nobody knows which part I mean, so the words are wasted.

So the order is fixed: point first, then talk.

Your finger does the first job, which is to pick the part.

Your voice does the second job, which is to name the mark.

If you talk first, the group hunts for the part. By then your reason has gone past.

One more rule: choose one part only. Two parts in one share is really two shares.

  • 1 · put the piece flat on the desk.
  • 2 · point, and hold your finger there.
  • 3 · name the mark or the colour.
  • 4 · say why you chose it.
5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Try it once with a partner

Work in twos and take your own piece.

Partner one shares, using point, name and why.

Partner two listens and says one word back.

The word must be about the mark or the colour.

Good words are sharp, faint, warm, rough, bright or worn.

You may not say nice, because it gives the maker nothing to use.

Then swap over, with two minutes each way.

Say your why out loud, even if it feels short. A short why is still a why.

If you cannot find a word, point at the part you mean.

9 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Your share, and the word you got back

Now you share with your table group.

Put your piece flat, then point at one part.

Name the mark or the colour you used.

Then say one sentence, starting with the words I chose this because.

Your why joins the mark to the card. A hard mark goes with a metal key.

Your group gives you one word back. Write it in the log box.

Then ring the part you pointed at, on your piece or on your photo.

  • Supported · point at one part and name the mark.
  • Standard · point, name the mark, and give one reason in a sentence.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say what the word you got back tells you.

Work on your own now, with one share each in table order.

If you finish early, write the part you would point at next time.

Say it once to an adult

Read your sentence to the adult at your table.

They read it back to you word for word.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they write the new one.

That adult is your audience today, and your words changed what they looked at.

Now pick one thing that happens next. Keep the sheet, share to the whole class next week, or change one word.

Write your pick on the sheet with today's date.

Your sheet goes in the arts log folder, not in your bag.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
