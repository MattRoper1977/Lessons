The Marks You Chose More Than Once

BUILD · Art · Week 7. Trinity Arts Award Discover. The Marks You Chose More Than Once.

Objective: look back at your log pages, find one mark and one colour you chose more than once, and write one sentence saying what those repeats show about your identity in your artwork.

Success evidence: two rings on your own log pages, both week numbers written, and one sentence in your own words.

You look at your own pages only. You talk about marks and colours. You do not talk about your home, your family or what you believe.

40 minutes. Offline.

3 min
Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

A repeat is a choice you made twice

Last week you said why you chose one part.

Today you need every page you have made.

Today you look at all your pages at once.

Put them in week order, left to right.

A repeat is a mark or a colour you used more than once.

One use can be an accident. Two is a choice.

Your repeats are your identity in your artwork.

Identity here means what your own work keeps choosing.

Say the one-is-an-accident line once and leave it on the board. Without it pupils ring every mark on the page.

You need your log pages in week order, one pencil and two rings to draw.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Two sets of pages

Here are two sets of log pages.

In set A every page uses a new mark.

You cannot say what that maker likes.

In set B a thick black line comes back three times.

Now you can say one true thing about that maker.

Set B is not better art. It is easier to read.

Look across the row, not down at one page.

  • 1 · lay your pages out in week order.
  • 2 · look for a mark that comes back.
  • 3 · look for a colour that comes back.
  • 4 · ring one of each.

You are looking for the same thing twice, not for your best page.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

I read my own pages

I put my pages in week order on the desk.

I look across them, not down at one.

The same short line shows up in week two and in week five.

That is a repeat, so I ring it on both pages.

One mark can change size and still be a repeat.

My short line is small in week two and long in week five.

It is the same kind of mark, so it counts.

I write the week numbers next to each ring.

Now the colour. Grey comes back three times.

I ring the grey once, on the page where it is clearest.

Last comes the sentence, and it is one line.

I write: I keep choosing hard grey lines.

Then I add the why in the same line, because I like edges that show.

Write the sentence where the group can see both halves. What I keep choosing, then why. The halves are the thing being taught, not the art.

That sentence is about my art. It is not about my week.

3 min
We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the cards, then match the repeat

Six log cards are on your table.

Each card shows two small pages by one maker.

Sort them into two piles: a repeat, and no repeat.

A repeat card shows the same mark or colour twice.

Now take the repeat pile.

Match each card to the word for its repeat. It is a line, a shape or a colour.

Every card gets a match, even when you are not sure.

A guess I can see beats a blank card.

Then hold up the card your table argued about most.

7 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

A sentence that points at a page

Here are two sentences about the same pages.

The first one says my art is good.

Nobody can pick up a page and check that.

The second one says I keep choosing thick lines.

You can look at week two and week five and check it.

So your sentence names the mark or the colour first.

Then it says one thing that repeat shows.

A colour works the same way. I keep using grey.

That names a colour, so it can be checked too.

Good, nice and bad are not repeats, so they stay out.

  • 1 · name the mark or the colour.
  • 2 · say which weeks it is on.
  • 3 · say what it shows about your art.
  • 4 · keep it to one line.

5 min
We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Check the ring, then check the words

Work in twos and swap pages.

Find the ring your partner drew.

Then find the second page that mark is on.

If you can find it, put a tick by the ring.

A tick means the ring is a real repeat.

If you cannot, write the word once by the ring.

Once means it is not a repeat yet, so they ring a different mark.

Now your partner reads their sentence out to you.

It has to name the mark or the colour you ticked.

If it only says good or nice, ask them to name the mark instead.

Then swap back, with two minutes each way.

You are not marking the art. You are checking the ring and the words.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

Two rings and one sentence

Work on your own now.

Use a pencil, so a ring can be moved.

Ring the mark first, then the colour.

Ring one mark that comes back on two pages.

Ring one colour that comes back on two pages.

Write the week numbers next to each ring.

Then write one sentence under your last page.

Start it with the words I keep choosing.

  • Supported · ring one repeat and write the two week numbers.
  • Standard · ring a mark and a colour, and write one sentence starting I keep choosing.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say what you would keep doing next time.

If you finish early, ring a third page where the same mark shows.

Exit ticket

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE: the learner may pause or choose a private response.

VOICE: the evidence statement remains in the learner’s chosen communication mode.

AUDIENCE: a familiar adult receives and names back exactly what was heard or seen.

INFLUENCE: one real next action is recorded: retain, add method detail, or leave open.

Say it once to an adult

Read your sentence to the adult at your table.

They read it back to you word for word.

Then they point at one page that shows it.

If they cannot find one, change the mark you named.

One sentence is enough. Do not write more.

Now pick one thing that happens next. Keep the sentence, use the mark again next week, or try a new mark.

Write your pick on the page with today's date.

Your pages go in the arts log folder, not in your bag.
