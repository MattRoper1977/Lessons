One Photo, One Comment, In The Log

BUILD · Art · Week 5. Trinity Arts Award Discover. One Photo, One Comment, In The Log.

Objective: photograph one finished piece of your own work so it fills the frame, then write one comment that names the step you took and one thing you would change.

Success evidence: one clear photo in the log box, one comment in your own words, and today's date.

You photograph your own work and nothing else. You do not photograph a person. No faces, no names, no rooms at home. The piece on the desk is the whole shot.

40 minutes. Offline.

Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

A log is proof that you did it

Your arts log holds what you made.

Each page holds two things. A photo, and a comment.

The photo shows the work. The comment says what you did to it.

A photo with no words tells nobody what you chose.

A comment with no photo shows nobody the work.

Read the two-part rule once and leave it on the board. Every pupil should be able to say photo and comment before the starter runs.

You need three things today. Your finished piece, the log sheet, and the grey card.

Three words matter today. Frame. Glare. Comment.

The frame is the part the photo holds.

Glare is white shine sitting on top of your work.

The comment is the line you write under the photo.

3 min
Starter

Starter · create the need

Two photos of one piece

Here are two photos. Both show the same drawing.

In photo one the drawing fills the frame. You can see every mark on it.

In photo two the drawing sits far away, beside a pencil case.

Both photos are real photos. Only one of them is evidence.

Evidence means an adult can see what you did.

Nobody has written a word yet. The frame did all the work.

  • 1 · fill the frame with the work.
  • 2 · take the other stuff out of the shot.
  • 3 · check that the marks still show.

Now look at the top of photo two.

A white patch of glare hides a mark you spent ten minutes on.

7 min
I Do

I Do · model

Four steps to a log photo

First I place my piece flat on the grey card.

The card is plain, so the edge of my work shows.

Second I turn the card to face the window.

Light from the side leaves a shadow. Light from the front does not.

So turn the card, not the light.

Third I hold the camera straight over the work.

Straight on keeps the shape true. A slant makes a square look bent.

I move in close until the work fills the frame.

Fourth I look at the photo, not at the work.

I check three things. The marks, the glare, and the edges.

My first shot has glare. So I turn the card and shoot again.

Say each step name out loud as you do it. Place, light, shoot, check. The four words are the thing being taught, not the photograph.

One shot is rarely the right shot. Two or three shots is normal.

If two shots both fail, move the card and not the camera.

We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the photo cards, then match the faults

There are six photo cards on your table.

Sort them into two piles. Log ready, and not ready yet.

Now take the pile that is not ready.

Match each card to one fault card. Glare, blur, too far, or a person in the shot.

Every card gets a fault, even when you are not sure.

A guess I can see beats a blank card.

Then hold up the one card your table argued about.

If two tables disagree, both give one reason. We do not vote.

7 min
I Do

I Do 2 · connect

The comment that goes under the photo

One comment goes under each photo.

A good comment names the step you took.

Then it names one thing you would change next time.

Here is my comment. I drew the key with a hard mark.

Next time I would press more lightly at the top.

That tells an adult what I did, and what I saw.

Nice is not a comment. It tells nobody what you did.

Use the frame on your sheet. It has two lines.

Line one starts with the words: I made this by.

Line two starts with the words: next time I would.

  • 1 · name the tool or the mark you used.
  • 2 · name the step you took with it.
  • 3 · name one change for next time.
  • 4 · read it back and check it is true.

We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Take your shot, then check a partner's

Take one shot of your own piece now. Use the four steps.

Then work in twos. Cameras stay in the hands that hold them.

Hold your screen up. Look at your partner's shot for ten seconds.

Then say one fault word. Glare, blur, too far, or clear.

Clear means the photo is ready for the log.

Point at the part of the photo you mean.

Your partner takes one more shot if they need it.

You may not say that it is fine.

Fine gives the other person nothing to change.

Your partner faults your shot the same way.

You may keep a photo your partner faulted. Say why.

9 min
Independent work

Independent · evidence

One photo, one comment, one date

Work on your own now. Take the shot that goes in the log.

You may keep the shot you took with your partner.

Place, light, shoot, check. Say them under your breath.

Put the photo in the box on your log sheet.

Write your comment on the two lines under it.

Write today's date in the corner of the sheet.

A log page with no date proves nothing later.

  • Supported · take the photo and write line one of the frame.
  • Standard · take the photo, write both lines, and add the date.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name which of the four steps you had to fix.

Log a piece that you made yourself, and not somebody else's.

If you finish early, take one more photo up close.

Exit ticket

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE: the learner may pause or choose a private response.

VOICE: the evidence statement remains in the learner’s chosen communication mode.

AUDIENCE: a familiar adult receives and names back exactly what was heard or seen.

INFLUENCE: one real next action is recorded: retain, add method detail, or leave open.

Read your comment to one adult

Read your comment to the adult at your table.

They read it back exactly as you wrote it.

If that is not what you meant, say it again.

They write the new line underneath the old one.

Then pick the one thing that happens next.

Keep this photo, retake it, or add a second line.

Write your choice on the sheet before you go.

The sheet goes in the arts log folder, not in your bag.
