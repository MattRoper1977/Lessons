Making the Piece: One Symbol, One Medium

BUILD · Art · Week 2. Trinity Arts Award Discover. Making the Piece: One Symbol, One Medium.

Objective: choose one medium from the approved tray, make one finished piece of the symbol you drew last week, and say why that medium suited the mark you chose.

Success evidence: one finished piece, the medium named on the label, and one sentence saying why that medium was the right one.

The symbol is the one from your card. You do not draw your home. You do not draw your family. You make the card.

40 minutes. Offline.

Retrieval

Arrival · retrieve

Back to the box you ringed

Last week you drew one symbol three times. You ringed one box.

That ringed box is your plan. Today you make it bigger and finish it.

A medium is what you make it with. Pencil is a medium. Chalk is a medium. Cut paper is a medium.

Each medium has a mark it is good at. Chalk is good at soft. Cut paper is good at sharp.

Do not let a pupil restart with a new symbol. The point of the week is finishing the thing already chosen.

You need your sheet from last week, one tray, and one sheet of grey card.

A finished piece is one you decided to stop. It is not one that ran out of time.

So watch the clock and stop yourself, before the clock stops you.

Starter

Starter · create the need

The wrong tool for the mark

Here is a sharp key drawn in soft chalk. The edges have gone fuzzy.

Here is the same sharp key in cut paper. The edges are clean.

Nobody worked harder on one than the other.

One tool just fits the mark and the other one fights it.

So pick the tool after you know the mark, never before.

  • 1 · look at your ringed box.
  • 2 · name the mark in it.
  • 3 · pick the tool that does that mark.

Look at the tray list on your table. Every tool on it does one job well.

None of them does every job. That is why there is a list and not one pencil.

I Do

I Do · model

Making one piece, start to finish

My ringed box was the worn key. Worn means soft edges and a rubbed surface.

So I pick chalk, because chalk rubs. Cut paper would fight me.

First I draw the shape big, in light pencil, filling most of the card.

Then I lay the chalk on its side and rub across the shape once.

I stop. One rub is a mark. Ten rubs is mud.

Last I write the medium on the label. Chalk on grey card.

Say "stop" out loud when you stop. Knowing when a piece is done is the hardest part of this unit.

Watch the size. My shape fills most of the card, with a small gap all round.

A tiny shape in the middle of a big card looks unfinished, however good the mark is.

So draw the gap first if it helps, then fill what is left.

We Do

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the marks, match the tools

Six mark cards are on your table. Sort them into soft marks and sharp marks.

Then match each card to a tool from the tray list.

Some marks take two tools. Put both against those.

When your table is done, hold up the card you found hardest.

Hold one up even if your table did not agree. A guess we can see is worth more than a blank.

Two of the six cards fit more than one tool. If your table found only one for those, look again.

I Do

I Do 2 · connect

The ground counts too

The ground is what you work on. White paper is a ground. Grey card is a ground.

Chalk on white paper hardly shows. Chalk on grey card shows straight away.

Pencil is the other way round. It reads on white and disappears on grey.

So choose the ground after the tool, and hold them next to each other before you start.

If you cannot see your mark from two steps back, change the ground and not the mark.

Hold the tool against the ground before you commit. One test mark in the corner is enough.

That corner mark is not a waste. It is the fastest way to find out you picked wrong.

If the test mark reads, carry on. If it does not, change one thing and test again.

We Do

We Do 2 · lab

Two steps back

Work in twos. Put your card on the desk and both stand two steps back.

Your partner says whether the shape still reads from there.

If it does not, they say which part disappeared first.

Fix that part only. Do not restart the piece.

Then swap and do the same for theirs.

Say what disappeared, not whether you liked it. Liking it is not information.

If nothing disappeared, say that too, and both of you stop looking.

Independent work

Independent · evidence

Finish the piece and label it

Work on your own. Make your piece big, filling most of the card.

Use one medium. Two media at once is a different lesson.

Stop when the mark is there. Then write the medium on the label.

Underneath, write one sentence starting: I used this because.

  • Supported · make the piece and name the medium on the label.
  • Standard · make the piece, label the medium, and give a reason.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say what you would change about the ground.

Keep your card from last week next to you. It is your plan, not decoration.

If you finish early, do the two steps back test again and write what you saw.

Show it to one adult

Hold your piece up for the adult at your table.

Read your sentence. They read it back exactly as you said it.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they write the new one.

Then pick one thing that happens next: keep it, add one more mark, or start a second piece.

Your choice goes on the label before you leave.

The piece goes in the arts log folder with the sheet from last week clipped behind it.
