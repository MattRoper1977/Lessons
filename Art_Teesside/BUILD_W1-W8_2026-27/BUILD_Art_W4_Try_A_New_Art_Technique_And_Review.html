BUILD · Art · Week 4. Trinity Arts Award Discover. Try It Once, Then Say What It Did.

Objective: try one new technique from the approved list, use it three times on scrap, and say one thing it does that your usual way does not.

Success evidence: three scrap tries of one new technique, and one sentence saying what it does differently.

Techniques come from the approved list. You are trying a tool, not showing anything about yourself.

40 minutes. Offline.

3 min

Arrival · retrieve

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

New means new to you

A new technique is one you have not used before. It does not have to be hard.

Rubbing a texture through paper is a technique. So is tearing instead of cutting.

You try it on scrap, never on your good piece.

Scrap is where you are allowed to be bad at something.

Three tries, not one. One try tells you nothing except whether you were lucky.

Scrap paper out before anything else, and plenty of it. A pupil who runs out will start using their good sheet.

You need the technique list, a pile of scrap, and one strip of good paper you will not touch today.

Your good strip stays face down until the end. Turning it over is a decision for next week.

Everything today happens on scrap, and scrap is not a lesser kind of work.

Write the technique name at the top before your first try, not after your third.

3 min

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

One try and three tries

Here is one try at rubbing a texture. It came out patchy.

Here are three tries at the same thing. The third one is even.

Nothing changed except doing it twice more.

If we had stopped at one, we would have said the technique does not work.

It did work. We were just new at it.

So three tries, every time, before you decide anything.

  • 1 · pick one new technique.
  • 2 · try it three times on scrap.
  • 3 · say what it does that your usual way does not.

Look at the patchy try again. Nothing about it is wrong; it is a first try.

First tries look like that for everyone, including people who are very good.

A technique that works first time was probably one you already knew.

7 min

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Three tries at one technique

I pick rubbing. I put a leaf under the paper and rub the side of a pencil over it.

Try one: I press too hard and the paper tears a little.

Try two: I press lighter. The shape comes through but only in the middle.

Try three: lighter still, and I move all the way across in one direction.

Now the whole leaf shows, edges and all.

What does it do that drawing does not? It gives me the real texture of the leaf.

I could not have drawn every one of those little lines.

Keep all three tries side by side. The comparison is the lesson; a single good result is not.

Watch my hand press lighter each time. That is the one thing I changed.

Change one thing between tries. If you change three, you will not know which one did it.

We Do · everyone commits

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Sort the tools by what they do

Six technique cards are on your table. Sort them into two groups.

One group makes texture. The other group makes edges.

Rubbing. Tearing. Cutting. Sponging. Scratching. Folding.

Then match each card to a job it would be good for.

When your table is done, hold up the card you were least sure about.

Hold one up even if your table did not agree.

Two cards could go in either group. Say which two, and pick a group for each anyway.

7 min

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

What a technique cannot do

Every technique is good at one thing and bad at another.

Rubbing gives you texture and takes away sharp edges.

Tearing gives you a soft edge and takes away straight lines.

Knowing what a tool cannot do is as useful as knowing what it can.

So say both. What it does, and what it costs.

Then you will know when to use it and when not to.

Say the cost out loud before you write it. It is harder to say than the good part.

A technique with no cost has not been tried properly yet.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Guess the technique

Work in twos. Put your best try in front of your partner without saying anything.

They guess which technique made it.

If they cannot tell, the try is not clear enough yet, so do a fourth.

If they get it straight away, ask them what gave it away.

Then swap and do the same for theirs.

If they guess right, that is the try to keep on top of the pile.

9 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Three tries and one sentence

Work on your own. Pick one technique from the list and keep it.

Do three tries on scrap. Number them one, two and three.

Keep all three, including the bad one.

Then write one sentence starting: this does something my usual way does not, which is.

If you finish early, write a second sentence saying what it costs.

  • Supported · two tries, and the sentence said aloud for an adult to write.
  • Standard · three numbered tries and a written sentence.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and a second sentence saying what the technique costs.

Write the technique name at the top of your scrap so the three tries stay together.

Do not throw the first try away. It is the one that shows what changed.

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Show the three to an adult

Lay your three tries out in order for the adult at your table.

Read your sentence. They read it back exactly as you said it.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they write the new one.

Then choose what happens next: use this technique in your piece, try it once more, or leave it.

Write that choice on the top try before you leave.

All three tries go in the arts log folder, in order, with the number showing.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
