Objective: choose one card from the approved symbol pack, draw it three times using three different marks, and say which mark says it best.

Arrival · retrieve

A mark is a choice

A mark is the line you leave on the page.

A thin line is one mark. A thick line is another. A smudge is another.

The same shape can be drawn with a soft mark or a hard mark.

The shape stays the same. The feeling changes.

Today you pick one card. You keep that card all lesson.

Read the three lines once. Do not invite examples from home; the card pack is the guard the whole lesson rests on.

You need three things. The symbol card. One pencil. One sheet with three boxes.

Three words matter today. Mark. Shape. Meaning.

The mark is how you draw. The shape is what you draw. The meaning is what it tells someone.

You can change the mark and keep the shape. That is the whole lesson.

Starter · create the need

Same shape, two marks

Here are two drawings of one leaf.

One is drawn with a hard, sharp line. One is drawn with a soft, smudged line.

Look at the edge of each leaf. Ask yourself one thing.

Which one looks fresh, and which one looks old?

Nobody wrote a word. The mark did the work.

  • 1 · look at the edge.
  • 2 · name the mark.
  • 3 · say what it makes you think.

Nobody is wrong here. Two people can read the same mark in two ways.

What matters is that you can say which mark you saw.

I Do · model

Three marks, one symbol

I take one card from the pack. My card is a key.

In box one I draw the key with a hard line. I press down. The edge is sharp.

In box two I draw the same key with a soft line. I press lightly. The edge is faint.

In box three I draw the same key again. This time I rub the pencil sideways. The key looks worn.

The shape did not change. The key is a key in all three boxes.

What changed is how it feels to look at.

Say the mark name out loud each time you change it. Hard. Soft. Worn. The words are the thing being taught, not the drawing.

Watch my hand, not just the page. A hard mark needs a slow, firm hand.

A soft mark needs a light, quick hand. The pencil is the same pencil.

If a box goes wrong, leave it. A crossed out box still shows a mark you tried.

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the marks

You have three small mark cards. Hard. Soft. Worn.

I hold up a drawing. You hold up the card that matches the mark.

Hold it up even if you are not sure. A guess you can see is better than a blank.

We do four drawings. Then we count how many the room agreed on.

If two people hold up different cards, both say one reason. We do not vote.

Then I say which mark I drew, and you keep your reason either way.

I Do 2 · connect

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Colour on top of the mark

Now I add one colour to my key.

I use one colour only. One colour is a choice. Six colours is a mess.

A warm colour is red, orange or yellow. A cool colour is blue, green or grey.

I put a cool grey on the worn key. Now it looks like old metal.

Try the same grey on the sharp key. It looks like new metal instead.

Same colour. Different mark. Different answer.

One more rule. Colour goes on last, never first.

If you colour first, the pencil marks stop showing. The shape goes soft and you lose the edge.

So the order is always the same. Shape, then mark, then one colour.

  • 1 · draw the shape from the card.
  • 2 · choose a mark and press to match it.
  • 3 · add one colour on top.
  • 4 · check the edge still shows.

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Test one mark on your card

Work in twos. Take your own card.

Draw your symbol once with a hard mark. Show your partner.

Your partner says one word for how it looks. Sharp. Old. Heavy. Light.

Write that word next to the box. It is their word, not yours.

Then swap. You give them a word for theirs.

You may not use the word nice. It tells the drawer nothing they can act on.

Use a word about the mark instead. Sharp. Faint. Rough. Heavy.

If you cannot think of one, point at the part of the drawing you mean.

Independent · evidence

Three boxes, one choice

Fill all three boxes with your own card.

Box one is a hard mark. Box two is a soft mark. Box three is a worn mark.

Add one colour to one box only. Say why you chose that box.

Then ring the box that says your symbol best.

Write one sentence under it. Start with the words: I chose this one because.

  • Supported · fill two boxes and ring one.
  • Standard · fill three boxes, add one colour, ring one and give a reason.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say what the colour changed.

Work on your own now. The card stays on your desk the whole time.

Do not copy the key I drew. Your card is your card.

If you finish early, draw one more box with a mark you have not used yet.

Say it to one adult

Read your sentence to the adult at your table.

They read it back to you exactly as you said it.

If it is not what you meant, say it again. They write the new one.

One thing happens next. Keep the sheet, add a second colour, or leave it as it is.

Whatever you pick is written on the sheet before you go.

Your sheet goes in the arts log folder, not in your bag.
