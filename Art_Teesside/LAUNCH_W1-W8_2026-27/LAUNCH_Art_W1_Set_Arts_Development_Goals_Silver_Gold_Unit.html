LAUNCH · Art · Week 1. Trinity Arts Award Silver, Unit 1. Three Goals, One Unit, Evidence You Can Point At.

Objective: write three arts-development goals for Silver Unit 1, each naming the skill, the evidence that will show it, and the date it will be reviewed.

Success evidence: three goals on the unit sheet, each with a named skill, a named piece of evidence, and a review date inside this term.

Goals come from the approved skills framework for this unit. They describe your arts practice, not your circumstances.

40 minutes. Offline.

3 min

Arrival · retrieve

What a goal has to carry

A goal for this unit has three parts, and all three are assessed.

It names a skill from the framework. It names the evidence that will show the skill. It names a date the evidence is reviewed.

A goal with no evidence is a wish. A goal with no date never gets checked.

You will write three. Three is the number Unit 1 asks for, so three is what we do.

Read the three parts once and leave them on the board. Every later stage is a test of whether all three are present.

You need the skills framework, the unit goal sheet, and the review calendar for this term.

Unit 1 is the part of Silver you plan rather than make. It is assessed on the planning, not on the finished work.

That is why a vague goal costs marks even when the artwork that follows is strong.

3 min

Starter · create the need

Two goals, one of them unassessable

Goal one says: get better at printmaking this term.

Goal two says: cut and print a two-layer lino block, keep the three test prints as evidence, and review it in week six.

Both are honest. Only one of them can be marked by somebody else.

The first names no evidence and no date, so nothing about it can ever be checked.

An assessor is not being difficult. They simply have nothing to look at.

  • 1 · name the skill from the framework.
  • 2 · name the evidence it will leave.
  • 3 · name the review date.
  • 4 · check all three are present.

Read both goals again and decide which one you would rather be marked on.

Most people pick the second, and the reason is always the same: it tells the assessor where to look.

7 min

I Do · model

Writing one goal, then repairing it

I start with a weak goal: improve my drawing.

I go to the framework and find the row that fits. The row says observational drawing from a set object.

Now my skill is named, so I ask what it would leave behind.

It would leave three timed studies of the same object, so those are my evidence.

Last I pick a date. Week six gives me four working sessions, which is enough for three studies.

The finished goal reads: make three timed observational studies of one set object, keep all three, review in week six.

Say which part you are adding as you add it. The sequence is the transferable thing, not the example.

Notice the order I worked in. Framework row first, then evidence, then date.

Working the other way round produces a date attached to a goal nobody can size.

3 min

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the goals, then match the missing part

Six goal cards are on your table. Sort them into two groups: assessable and not yet assessable.

Then match each card in the second group to the part it is missing: skill, evidence or date.

Some cards are missing two parts. Put both labels against those.

When your table has finished, hold up the card you argued about most.

Hold one up even if your table did not agree. A disagreement you can see is worth more than silence.

Two of the six cards are already assessable. If your table finds three, check the third again.

7 min

I Do 2 · connect

Why the date is not the last detail

The review date does more work than it looks like it does.

It decides how big the goal can be, because a goal has to fit the sessions before it.

Three studies in two weeks is possible. A finished two-layer print in two weeks is not.

So set the date first if you are unsure, and let it cut the goal down to size.

Spread your three review dates out. Three goals reviewed on the same day is one review, not three.

A goal you finish early is not wasted. You write the next one and date that.

Write the date on the calendar as well as on the sheet, so it exists in two places.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

Three-part check on a partner's goal

Work in twos. Read your partner one of your goals.

They say back the three parts in order: skill, evidence, date.

If they cannot find one of the three, that part is missing, whatever you meant to say.

Add it now, out loud, and read the whole goal again.

Then swap over and check theirs the same way.

Check the goal, never the person. The only question is whether the three parts are there.

If your partner's goal has all three, say so and stop. There is no mark for finding a fault.

9 min

Independent · evidence

Three goals on the unit sheet

Work on your own. Write three goals on the unit sheet.

Each one names a framework row, the evidence it will leave, and a review date this term.

Use different framework rows. Three goals from one row is really one goal written three ways.

Underline the evidence in each goal, so an assessor can find it without reading the whole sheet.

  • Supported · two goals, each with a framework row and a review date, evidence named aloud.
  • Standard · three goals from three different rows, each with named evidence and a review date.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say which goal is most at risk of slipping, and why.

Write the framework row number next to each goal, so the link is on the page and not in your memory.

If you finish early, go back to your first goal and check its date still fits the sessions you have.

3 min

Exit · Audience and Influence

Read one goal to an adult

Read one of your three goals to the adult at your table.

They read it back exactly, and name the three parts as they hear them.

If a part is missing, say the goal again with that part in and they write the new version.

Then choose what happens to the sheet: keep all three, drop one, or move a date.

Your choice is written on the sheet before you leave, with today's date next to it.

The unit sheet goes into your Silver folder, not into your bag.
