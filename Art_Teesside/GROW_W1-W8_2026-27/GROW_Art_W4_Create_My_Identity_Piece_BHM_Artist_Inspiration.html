GROW · Art · Week 4. Trinity Arts Award Explore. One Borrowed Technique, Credited.

Objective: make the identity piece you planned, borrow exactly one technique from an artist on the approved card pack, and credit it in writing on the label.

Success evidence: the planned piece made, one borrowed technique named on the label, and the artist credited by name.

Symbols and artists both come from the approved packs. The piece is about the symbol you chose, never about your home or family.

Arrival · retrieve

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Borrowing is not copying, and the label is the difference

Every artist has borrowed from another artist. That is how the whole subject works.

Copying is taking the work. Borrowing is taking one technique and using it on your own thing.

What separates them, in this unit, is whether you say so.

An uncredited borrow reads as a copy even when it was not one.

A credited borrow reads as research, which is what it is.

Say the difference once, plainly, and leave the credit line on the board all lesson.

You need your planner, your symbol card, an artist card, and the label sheet.

The artist cards in this pack are all people whose work is in public collections you could go and see.

Crediting them is normal practice, not a special rule for school.

Starter · create the need

Two labels for the same piece

Label A says: cut paper on grey card.

Label B says: cut paper on grey card, with the overlapping shapes borrowed from Sonia Boyce.

The piece is identical. Only the label is different.

Label B tells a viewer where to look and tells an assessor you read something.

It also protects you, because now nobody can say you took it without saying.

  • 1 · make the piece you planned.
  • 2 · borrow exactly one technique.
  • 3 · name it on the label.
  • 4 · credit the artist by name.

Read label A once more. Nothing in it is untrue, and it still hides where the idea came from.

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Making the plan, then adding one borrow

My planner says the bird card, in cut paper, on grey, with no added background.

So I cut the bird first, exactly as planned, and I stop there.

Then I look at my artist card. Their technique is overlapping the same shape twice.

That is one technique. I do not also take their colours, or their scale, or their subject.

I cut a second bird and lay it slightly over the first.

The label reads: cut paper on grey card, overlapping borrowed from the artist on card four.

The whole borrow is one sentence long, and it is the sentence that gets assessed.

Refuse a second borrow if a pupil asks. Two borrows and the piece stops being theirs.

Notice the order. The plan is made first and the borrow goes on top of it.

Borrowing first and planning around it produces their piece with your symbol in it.

We Do · everyone commits

Sort them: borrow or copy?

Six cards are on your table. Sort them into borrowing and copying.

Used their way of overlapping shapes. Made the same picture. Used their exact colours and subject.

Used one of their marks on my own symbol. Traced their painting. Worked at their scale.

Then match each borrowing card to the one line you would write on a label for it.

When your table is done, hold up the card you argued about most.

Two of the six are close to the line. Decide anyway, and be ready to say why.

I Do 2 · connect

Why one borrow and not three

Take three techniques from one artist and you have made their work, not yours.

Take one and put it on your own symbol, and the borrow shows up clearly against everything else.

A viewer can see exactly what came from where, which is what a credit is for.

So choose your one borrow before you start, and write it on the planner.

If you find a second one you want, save it for next term rather than adding it now.

Write the borrow on the planner before you cut anything, so it is a decision and not a drift.

5 min

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Find the borrow

Work in twos. Show your partner your piece and your artist card together.

They say which part of your piece came from the card.

If they cannot find it, the borrow is not visible enough yet.

Say what you meant to borrow, then decide together whether to make it clearer or change the label.

Then swap over and do the same for theirs.

If they find it straight away, say so and stop. There is no mark for making it harder.

9 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Make it, label it, credit it

Work on your own. Make the piece your planner describes.

Keep the limit you wrote in box four. It still applies today.

Borrow exactly one technique from your artist card.

Write the label: medium, ground, the technique you borrowed, and the artist's name.

If you finish early, write one line saying what the borrow changed about the piece.

  • Supported · make the piece and name the artist on the label.
  • Standard · make the piece, name the borrowed technique and credit the artist.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say what the borrow changed about the piece.

Keep the artist card next to the piece the whole time you work, face up.

If you change your mind about the borrow, cross the old label line out rather than rubbing it out.

3 min

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Read the credit to an adult

Read your label to the adult at your table, with the artist card next to it.

They read it back and point at the part of your piece they think you borrowed.

If they point at the wrong part, add one word to the label and read it again.

Then choose what happens next: keep it, make the borrow clearer, or take a second technique next term.

Write that choice on the label with today's date before you leave.

The piece, the planner and the artist card go into the folder together.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
