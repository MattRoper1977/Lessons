GROW · Art · Week 2. Trinity Arts Award Explore. One Skill, Three Tries, One Log Entry.

Objective: choose one skill from the approved technique list, practise it three times, and write one arts-log entry saying what changed between the first try and the third.

Success evidence: three dated attempts at one named skill, and a log entry that names one thing that changed and one thing that stayed the same.

Every skill comes from the approved technique list. The log records what your hand did, not what happened at home.

40 minutes. Offline.

Arrival · retrieve

A skill is something you can repeat

A skill is a thing your hand can do the same way twice.

Hatching is a skill. Blending is a skill. Cutting a clean edge is a skill.

Having a good idea is not a skill, because you cannot repeat it to order.

An arts log is the record of a skill getting better. It is not a diary.

A log entry only counts if someone else could read it and see what you did.

Say the distinction between a skill and an idea once, plainly. Every later stage depends on pupils holding it.

You need the technique list, a practice sheet with three strips, and your log page.

Your log is read by other people. That is what makes it different from a sketchbook.

An assessor reads it, and so does the person who takes the next lesson.

Starter · create the need

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Two log entries about the same afternoon

Entry one says: I did some shading and it went well.

Entry two says: I hatched at forty five degrees three times, and by the third strip my lines stopped crossing.

Both are true. Only one of them can be checked by the person reading it.

The second entry names the skill, the number of tries, and what changed.

That is the shape every entry in your log will take today.

  • 1 · name the skill.
  • 2 · say how many tries.
  • 3 · say what changed.
  • 4 · say what stayed the same.

I Do · model

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Three strips, one skill

I pick hatching from the list. Hatching is drawing lines that run the same way.

On strip one I hatch quickly. My lines cross each other and the tone looks patchy.

I do not rub it out. A weak first strip is the evidence that something changed later.

On strip two I slow down and keep my wrist still. Fewer lines cross.

On strip three I turn the paper instead of turning my hand. Now almost none cross.

My log entry writes itself now, because I can point at what is different.

Name the change out loud before you write it: turning the paper, not the hand. The naming is what is being taught.

Notice what I did not change. Same pencil, same paper, same skill on all three strips.

If you change the skill halfway, the three strips stop being comparable and prove nothing.

We Do · everyone commits

Sort them: skill or idea?

Six cards are on your table. Sort them into two groups.

One group is things a hand can repeat. The other group is things it cannot.

Blending two pencil tones. Wanting to draw a city. Cutting a straight edge with a knife.

Then match each card in the first group to a tool on the technique list.

When your table has sorted all six, hold up the card you were least sure about.

Hold one up even if you disagree with your table. A visible guess can be talked about; a blank cannot.

Where the room splits, both sides give one reason and we move on without a vote.

What makes an entry checkable

A checkable entry uses words that point at the work.

Words like lines, edge, tone, pressure and angle point at something on the page.

Words like good, nice, better and rubbish point at a feeling instead.

Feelings are allowed in your log, but they never replace the thing that changed.

So write what a reader could find if you handed them the sheet.

One sentence is enough, as long as it survives that test.

Try the test on your own draft before your partner does.

Read your sentence, then look at your strips and try to put a finger on it.

If your finger has nowhere to land, the sentence is about a feeling and needs a detail.

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Read it back and point

Work in twos. Read your draft entry to your partner.

Your partner then points at the place on your strip where your sentence is true.

If they cannot find it, the sentence needs one more detail, not more feeling.

Add the detail they were looking for and read it again.

Then swap over and do the same for theirs.

Keep it to the strip in front of you. Do not comment on anyone's ability.

Two minutes each way, and the timer is read out loud.

9 min

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Three strips and one entry

Work on your own. Choose one skill and keep it for all three strips.

Date each strip. Keep the weak one; it is half of your evidence.

Then write one log entry naming the skill, the number of tries, and what changed.

Finish with one thing that stayed the same, because that is what you work on next.

  • Supported · two strips, and say the change aloud for an adult to scribe.
  • Standard · three dated strips and a written entry naming the skill and the change.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name the thing that stayed the same and what you will try next.

Date every strip as you finish it, not at the end.

If you run out of time, three dated strips and no entry is still better evidence than one perfect strip.

3 min

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Read it to one adult

Read your log entry to the adult at your table.

They read it back exactly as you wrote it.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they will write the new version.

Then choose one thing that happens next: keep the skill, change the skill, or add a fourth strip.

Your choice is written on the log page before you leave.

The log page goes into the folder, not into your bag.

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
