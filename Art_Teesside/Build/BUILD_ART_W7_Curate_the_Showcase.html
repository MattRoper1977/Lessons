Enquiry: How does display change the meaning and accessibility of artwork?

Success looks like

  • I can choose how my work is displayed
  • I can write or say a caption in my own words
  • I can help curate someone else's piece respectfully
Aspire · GROW reach: sequence the showcase so the pieces talk to each other.
ARRIVAL GRID · 3–5 MINUTES

Settle, notice and choose

1. NOTICE

Retrieve the artwork’s intended effect and audience.

2. CHOOSE

Choose a curation role.

3. CONNECT

Which display choice could strengthen or weaken meaning?

4. QUESTION

Select one access check the exhibition must pass.

Access: pointing, circling, selecting, speaking, typing or accurate scribing all count. Place knowledge is optional; visual attention is the task.

CHEESE LAYER · VISUAL EXPLAINER
Not an art specialist? Say this: Say: “Where and how you DISPLAY art changes its meaning. Choose the layout, the label, the access route, and what stays private. Curating is an artistic decision — you control what the audience receives.”

See the studio thinking

Think aloud: What changed? What stayed? Which decision had the strongest effect?
I DO · MODEL ONE DECISION CHAIN

Watch the thinking, not just the hand

Model two display options and compare height, spacing, label, lighting, digital access, privacy and audience movement.

Intention

What effect is the artist trying to create?

Alternatives

What could be kept, changed, combined or rejected?

Evidence

What result justifies the selection?

WE DO · CLASS-CONTROLLED TEST

Every voice needs a route

Create a miniature exhibition plan. Pupils use tokens/cards/voice to change order, spacing and access features.
Receipt check: “The decision we followed was ____. It changed ____.”
YOU DO · STUDIO ROUTE

Choose a genuinely usable route

Take an authentic role: curator, installer, label writer, audio describer, photographer, access checker, digital builder or welcome host.
LUNDY LOOP · FOUR MISSIONS

Four zones, one loop — same everywhere. Colour marks the zone; the loop always closes, even if only with the ticket below.

AUDIENCE PROTOCOL

Gallery walk with evidence, not “good”

I notice…

Name a line, shape, material, surface, composition or curatorial decision.

I think the intention may be…

Use visible evidence and allow uncertainty.

One question is…

Ask without demanding private disclosure.

Maker response: The audience noticed what I intended / something unexpected / something I may test next.
