Explore — Festival light patterns

Learning goal

I can plan a light pattern.

Today I can

  • I can find light shapes.
  • I can find bright colours on dark paper.
  • I can choose shapes and colours for my art.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write.

You can ask an adult to move your shapes.

You may pass.

TA Brief

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Current stage

1. Set out the adult-made examples, the word-and-symbol cards and the shape/colour cards; show the silent director role before making marks.

2. Offer one tier and one prompt at a time; accept point, speech, sign, symbols, drawing or writing and allow a pass.

3. In We do, give each pupil time to decide before the shared cards move; record their own shape and repeat choices.

4. Help pupils choose shapes and bright colours for their art; never ask for belief, family or loss.

5. Keep a brief record of the shape, colours, repeat and actual support; give a small review step and upload the evidence to the evidence app the same day, labelled ART-BUILD-A2-W01-P1.

Shared preparation, access and safety

Prepare the word-and-symbol cards, shape cards and colour cards described here before the lesson; no external image, named resource or screen interaction is needed. Read pupil wording aloud, one short prompt at a time. All response choices apply to EVERY question, demonstration response, shared task, route, review and Exit: pupils may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. A pupil may direct an adult to place a shape or move a card. Offer a silent director role throughout; do not require making marks, touching materials or speaking to the group. Never cold call. Any pupil may pass. A pass needs no book entry.

Use one Arrival tier and one Exit tier per pupil. Keep Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until the response. The three Exit checks are brief choices or short responses, not three extended written answers. Allow private thinking before any shared reveal. An adult may record the pupil's own choice, gesture or exact words. No board copying, cutting or gluing is needed in the timed stages.

Festival context: light and colour are ways some people celebrate. People within a tradition may celebrate in different ways. These are original, made-up classroom pictures of light shapes, not copies of any tradition's sacred images. Never ask about a pupil's belief, family, worship or loss. Discuss the shapes and colours in these pictures, not personal disclosure.

Materials safety and access: use pencils, pastels or chalk, coloured and dark paper, and optional glue. Any scissors are used with adult care; prepare cut shapes in advance so cutting is never required. Use no flames. Keep chalk on the paper and do not blow dust. Announce each short task, use a predictable short count-in with a matching hand gesture, and show a flat hand for STOP. At STOP, pause marks or card moves and rest tools on the table. A pupil can choose the silent director role, a quiet place or a pause without explaining why. Offer large, clearly spaced shapes and describe their colour and position; do not infer lack of art understanding from a visual or motor access need.

Kit and stimulus: Make the following simple examples yourself with the permitted materials. Use no named artists, artworks or sacred images. Prepare DOT / RING / RAY / LAMP cards: dot = a filled round spot; ring = a round line with an empty middle; ray = one short straight line; lamp = a plain oval outline with three short rays above it, with no flame. A lamp here is only a made-up drawn sign for a light. Prepare matching bright yellow, pink and light blue colour cards, dark paper and pale yellow paper. Say the colour names aloud when offering them. Each pupil needs shape cards and a small space to arrange a trial pattern, or may direct an adult.

Adult-made Example A: On a dark rectangle, place six large, evenly spaced marks in one straight row: yellow dot / pink ring / yellow dot / pink ring / yellow dot / pink ring. Keep every dot the same size and every ring the same size. The two-mark unit is a yellow dot followed by a pink ring, repeated three times.

Adult-made Example B: On a dark rectangle, place four identical lamp signs in one straight row. Each has a yellow oval outline and three light blue rays above it. Leave clear space between the signs. One whole lamp sign repeats four times; there are two colours. These lamps are drawn marks, not real lights.

Adult-made Example C: Put the same large yellow dot on two equal small rectangles, one dark and one pale yellow. Use the same yellow for each dot and keep their size the same. The yellow dot should be easier to see against the dark paper. Check that this is clear in the room before use; describe the difference and offer close viewing or a large version if needed. This comparison concerns the yellow dot, not all possible colours.

Adult-only EHCP note: Possible outcome areas are communication and interaction; cognition and learning; sensory and physical access; social, emotional and mental health. Link observations only to an actual individual target; do not infer an outcome from participation alone. Regulation strategy: use the announced count-in and visible STOP cue, with an agreed silent return to the next small task if the pupil needs a pause.

Same-day evidence — ART-BUILD-A2-W01-P1: The assigned adult records one chosen book item or a brief observation of the pupil's own response, response mode and actual prompt used, and uploads it to the evidence app the same day, labelled ART-BUILD-A2-W01-P1. Take a photo of the trial pattern with a scribed line or the pupil's own comment for the pupil's Discover log; a photo alone does not show understanding. Do not require a public display.

I do · method

Some people use lights and art at a festival.

We will plan a made-up light picture.

1. A dot is a round spot. A ring is a round line.

2. A ray is a short line. These rays go out from our lamp.

3. In A, I see a dot, then a ring. I see that pair again.

In B, I see a row of lamps. The same lamp is used again.

These bright marks stand out on dark paper.

I will use a yellow dot, then a pink ring.

I can use this pair again to make my pattern.

You may choose new shapes and colours.

Must-know words — four

shape — the form of a mark, like a dot or a ring.

bright — strong and full of light, like our yellow. Bright marks stand out on dark paper.

pattern — marks put in an order.

repeat — use the same bit again.

Show the pair that I will use again.

In your book: One shape pair, in marks, words or pictures.
