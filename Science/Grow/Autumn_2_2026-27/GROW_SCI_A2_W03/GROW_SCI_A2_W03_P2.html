Knowledge organiser

Claim: A claim is an answer that we check with facts.

Evidence: Evidence can help us check if a claim is sound.

Audience: The audience receives your work and can ask a question.

1. Open with the question.

2. State your answer claim.

3. Give two or three facts. Show or say each source.

4. Show a diagram only if it helps.

5. Pause for an audience question.

6. Record what changed, or why it stayed. Choose one next use.

A short talk cannot use every fact. A neat slide does not prove a fact. You still need to check sources when someone asks a question.

TA Brief

Current stage

Priority: settle, wait and protect the learning goal. Use the least prompt that gives access, then fade it.

Prompt ladder: 1. Point to the relevant place / instruction. 2. Ask one short question: "What is it asking you to find?" 3. Rephrase the instruction in simpler language. 4. Provide a scaffold or limited choice. 5. Model the first step only, then return the task to the pupil.

Allow processing time first. Use the least of these five prompts that gives access. Fade it as soon as the pupil continues.

Read the current task exactly while it stays visible. Accept pointing, signs, speech, demonstrations, local typing and exact adult scribing. Keep the science goal and the chosen route.

Record adult prompt level separately from the pupil work. Pupil mark box: I · S · C. Staff prompting record: level used, reason and fading step. Do not put staff prompting instructions on pupil sheets.

Before: check research notes and source labels are ready. Offer a quiet paper route and one named audience. Check devices, sound and movement routines.

During: ask what the fact shows and where it came from. If recall is inaccessible, point to the supplied retrieval box and read it without supplying today’s answer.

Audience: genuinely receive the pupil response. Say back the science heard or seen. If feedback confirms the work, receive a clear reason and a new-use step instead of forcing an unnecessary edit.

Next move: secure understanding transfers to a new use; mixed understanding gets a contrast; a misconception gets a counterexample and a fresh question. An access barrier changes the mode or adult role. A method barrier returns to the source or evidence record.

Feedback and evidence: use VF, WS, I, NS+, //, ?, R and E in the school’s recorded sense. R requires pupil Voice, real Audience, one NS+ prompt and the pupil response in their own colour. Record evidence with SCI-GROW-A2-W03-P2.

Guidance

Prepare paper and school-device routes. Name a real audience for each pupil. Keep the notes from the research lesson with their source labels. Supply question prompts about meaning, evidence or sources.

Check content for flashing or distressing effects. Use school accounts and keep personal information private. Offer signed, spoken, pointed or exact-scribed routes and a quiet audience. Keep the same science goal.

Use the pupil research record as the evidence source. Do not invent missing data or promote an unverified fact to certainty. The source Mars example attributes the red surface to iron minerals reacting with oxygen. A diagram not to scale must say so.

Model order: question, answer claim, 2–3 sourced facts, helpful diagram, audience question, recorded response and next use. Source button order remains deliberately mixed for the pupil sequence task.

Independent evidence: a saved slide, paper panel or witness record; source labels; a genuine received question; and either a visible before/after change or a reasoned confirmation plus one transfer step.

Exact pupil meaning counts. Adult scribing must not upgrade an answer. No cold calling; a pupil may pass. Offer one familiar audience before widening the group.
