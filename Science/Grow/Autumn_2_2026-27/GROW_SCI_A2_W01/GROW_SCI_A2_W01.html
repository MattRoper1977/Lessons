Arrival · 5 min

Place a dot on the ball. Point to the side lit by the torch.

Offer a seat or standing spot. End with a one-minute reset.

Starter · 3 min

Think back. Say, point or write one answer for each question.

  1. What goes around Earth?
  2. Name one mechanism from our forces work.
  3. Name one force from our first science week.

How I know

  • I can point to the day and night sides.
  • I can turn the model and explain when night comes.
  • I can explain why the Sun seems to move.

Ask three low-stakes questions without a public test.

I do · 5 min

Watch: the torch stays still. I turn the ball. The dot has day when it faces the light. It has night when it faces away.

Words
rotation: one turn around an axis
Earth’s rotation brings day and night.
axis: an imagined line a planet spins around
Earth turns around its axis.
shadow: an area where an object blocks light
The ball makes a shadow on the desk.
orbit: a curved path around another object
Earth’s orbit takes it around the Sun.
Full model: Torch still → dot faces the torch: day → turn the ball → dot faces away: night. Earth’s rotation makes the Sun seem to move.
Show me: Show me: where is the dot when that place has day? Point or use a card.

Model the full sequence. Keep light out of eyes. Ask the show-me check.

We do · 7 min

Now try the same model. Two steps are done. Complete the last step: turn the dot away and name what comes next.

Try the model

Predict: if the dot turns away from the torch, will it have day or night?

Kit: torch, ball or globe, removable dot. Aim light at the ball, away from faces.

  1. Place the dot on the ball.
  2. Keep the torch still.
  3. Turn the dot to face the torch. Record day or night.
  4. Turn the dot away. Record day or night.

Risk: Torch beams can dazzle eyes; a loose ball may roll away.

Safe: Aim the torch at the ball, never at eyes. Keep the ball on the tray or stand.

Check against the school risk assessment and CLEAPSS guidance. Do not shine the torch into eyes.

We do 2 · 5 min

All answer: finger-vote, board, card, thumbs or point. Your teacher chooses the next step.

Use an all-pupil hinge response and the decision rule in the note.

Independent · 8 min

Show day and night at the dot. Explain why the Sun seems to move, even when our torch stays still.

Show it your way: Point, sign, speak, draw, type or write. A scribe can help. Staff can take a photo of what you show.

You can plan in any language. Say your idea first, then write or draw it if you choose.

F · First step

Point to the lit side and the dark side.

Say: I point to ___ for day and ___ for night.

M · Make the link

Explain what the dot sees as Earth turns.

Say: The dot has night when ___ because ___.

H · Harder thinking

Explain why the Sun seems to move across our sky while the model torch stays still.

Say: The Sun seems to move because ___. The model shows ___.

Go deeper: Mark two dots on opposite sides. Explain why one has day while the other has night.

Stretch · Two dots, one turn: Two dots, one turn: test the claim that the Sun goes round Earth each day. Show the motion that explains both dots. Name one limit of the torch model.

One adult supports agreed pupils, the other holds the group; swap across the week. One-minute reset at end.
