Verbatim SoW objective: Describe the Sun, Earth and Moon as approximately spherical bodies.

Arrival · 5 min

Sort a coin, paper circle and ball: flat or solid?

Offer a predictable seat or standing spot and the first visual cue. No public test.

Starter · 3 min

Think back. Say, point or write one answer for each question.

  1. What makes day turn to night at one place?
  2. What goes around the Sun?
  3. Name one force you have explored.

Today I will check:

  • I can choose a ball to model a round body.
  • I can turn it to show more than one side.
  • I can name one limit of a model.

Ask three low-stakes retrieval questions; prior coverage must be checked.

I do · 5 min

Watch me turn a ball. I can see another curved side. A paper circle is flat. A ball helps show the shape of the Sun, Earth and Moon, but its size is not exact.

Words: spherical · curve · shadow · model

WordIt meansExample
sphericalshaped roughly like a ballEarth is approximately spherical.
curvea line or surface that bendsThe ball has a curved surface.
shadowa dark area where an object blocks lightThe ball makes a shadow on the card.
modela simple way to show an object or ideaA ball is a model of Earth’s shape.

Watch: Ball → turn it → see another curved side → useful for an approximately spherical body; ball sizes and gaps are not to scale.

Show me: Point: which object can I turn to see another curved side?

Model the full reasoning step, pre-teach four words and ask the show-me question.

Try it with kit

Guess first: Predict which object can turn to show a different curved side: ball or paper circle?

Kit: Three safe balls or modelling spheres · Paper circles · Torch with adult-controlled beam · Tray or stable surface

  1. Compare a ball with a flat circle.
  2. Turn the ball once and record what new side you see.
  3. Turn the paper circle and record what it shows.
  4. Put the torch on the ball; do not point it into eyes.

Independent · 8 min

Turn a shape model. Explain one way it helps and one limit. You can show, say or draw.

Talk first. You can plan in any language.

Point, sign, speak, draw, type, write, use a scribe or show a staff-captured photo.

Go deeper: Compare a ball and a photo. What can each show, and what does each hide?

Model limits: A photo shows Earth as a circle. Choose another observation or model to test its full shape. Explain why one photo is not enough.
