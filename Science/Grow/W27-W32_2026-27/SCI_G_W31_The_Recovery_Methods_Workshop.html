1 • Shared evidence: recovery briefs

All activities are process models and paper investigations. No heating, tasting or apparatus building is needed.

A • Sand + water

Target: collect the sand. Sand is insoluble in water and its grains can be trapped by ordinary filter paper.

B • Salt + water

Target: collect the dissolved salt. Salt solution passes through ordinary filter paper. Removing water can leave salt behind.

C • Salt + water

Target: collect the water. Letting it escape into room air does not collect it. Capture its vapour and cool it to liquid.

D • Sand + salt + water

Target: collect sand and salt separately. Sand is insoluble; salt is dissolved. Different components need a sequence of processes.

Process choices

Filter: retain insoluble solid. Evaporate: leave dissolved solid. Capture vapour and condense: collect solvent as liquid. Methods are models, not instructions to improvise heating.

Limits

No tasting or drinking. A method’s name does not prove complete recovery or safe drinking water; other contaminants and losses may be present.

TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Choose and justify filtration, evaporation or evaporation with condensation for a stated recovery target.

Preparation

  • Default lesson is a process simulation and paper investigation. No heated separation apparatus is used.
  • Name the target before choosing a method. The model follows sand, salt and water; it does not certify drinking-water purity.

Method precautions

  • Do not heat, boil, distil or taste any classroom mixture as part of this pack.
  • The evaporation/condensation diagram is a schematic of processes, not an apparatus construction guide.
  • Optional physical filtration would require a separately prepared school method; all necessary evidence is already supplied.

Practical alternative

Use the four recovery briefs and process cards. Screen process choices and paper sequencing both provide the complete investigation.

Access and regulation

Supported: Match mixture/target pairs to process cards.

Standard: Explain four routes and account for components.

Stretch: Evaluate multistep recovery, loss and limits of purity claims.

Regulation: Offer private writing, pointing, drawing or adult scribing. Allow pass-and-return for public questions and desk-based participation. Choose support from current learning evidence, not fixed ability labels.

Teacher answers

R1–R3

Dissolved salt remains present. Evaporation changes liquid to gas at its surface; it can occur below boiling. Condensation changes gas to liquid.

W1–W3

A filtration gives sand residue; B evaporation leaves salt; C capture vapour and condense to collect water. D filter first, then evaporate saltwater filtrate; sand stays as residue. W3 dissolved substances may pass through and other contaminants may remain; clarity is not purity or drinking-water safety.

Hinge A–C

A loses the water target. B correct. C ordinary filtration does not trap dissolved salt.

S1–S6

Filter; evaporate water; capture and condense; sand residue remains while saltwater passes; filter then evaporate filtrate; no, clarity alone is insufficient.

N1–N4

A filter/sand; B evaporate/salt; C evaporate with capture and condensation/water; D filter then evaporate filtrate/sand and salt separately. N2 dissolved salt passes with the water through ordinary filter paper. N3 vapour escapes unless captured. N4 sand residue and saltwater filtrate; remove water from filtrate to leave salt.

T1–T4

T1 salt target: water leaves and salt remains; water target: capture and condense water vapour while salt remains at source. T2 filter sand before removing water from dissolved salt; evaporating first leaves mixed sand and salt rather than separate products. T3 sample may remain dissolved in unrecovered liquid, stick to apparatus/filter, spill, or be affected by measurement uncertainty. Missing measured mass is not proof of destruction. T4 filtration establishes separation of suitable insoluble particles, not all dissolved substances or microbes; no safety claim is justified.

E1–E4 / assessment

Insoluble sand; remove water by evaporation; capture and condense vapour; dissolved contaminants can remain and appearance is insufficient. Secure: target-dependent route and component accounting. Re-teach method matching by asking where the desired component finishes. Reflection accepts a reasoned target comparison.

condensation
