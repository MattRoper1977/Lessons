Build a model that explains a direction

Learning intention

Explain the insulin response to raised blood glucose using a qualitative, controllable model.

What could reduce a rise in blood glucose?

We will model a mechanism, without medical numbers.

Success looks like

  • Sequence raised glucose → insulin release → target response → movement towards a suitable range.
  • Distinguish glucose, glycogen and insulin.
  • Explain why a qualitative model cannot predict a person’s readings or treatment.

TA Brief

Current stage

Objective

Explain the insulin response to raised blood glucose using a qualitative, controllable model.

Preparation

  • Print the shared page, one selected response route and the exit page. Routes are alternatives, not extra tasks.
  • Read the worked answers and note any constructed data before teaching.
  • Use the core interaction first. Keep the Higher glucagon panel closed unless that extension is appropriate.
  • All displayed levels are qualitative labels. Do not substitute pupil readings, medicine amounts or clinical thresholds into this classroom model.

Method precautions

  • Paper and screen lesson; no human sampling, exercise test, breath holding, medicine handling or personal health disclosure.

Practical alternative

All evidence is supplied. Use paper diagrams and cards, pointing, annotation or dictation if a device is unavailable.

Access and regulation

Supported: Same core concepts with chunked prompts and read-aloud access. Record support used; a pathway is not a diagnosis or fixed attainment label.

Standard: Explain the supplied evidence independently using accurate scientific terms and units.

Stretch: Optional deeper transfer or evaluation. Higher-tier-only material is labelled explicitly and is not required for the core outcome.

Regulation: Offer solo or partner work, a quiet response, a pass and brief reset with clear re-entry. No forced disclosure or public performance.

Teacher answers

Retrieval and We do

R1: blood; R2: respiration and material use/storage; R3: the original departure. W1: A→B→C→D. W2: glucose units, not insulin, are stored as glycogen; insulin signals the processes. W3: no exact concentrations, times or individual parameters are supplied.

Hinge

Correct: raised glucose stimulates more insulin release and uptake/storage responses. Insulin is not converted into glucose. The model supplies no dosing information.

S1 / M1

After absorption raises blood glucose, pancreatic beta cells release more insulin into blood. Insulin-responsive tissues alter glucose handling; some body cells increase uptake and liver/muscle glycogen storage is promoted.

S2 / M2

Insulin=chemical messenger hormone; glucose=sugar/material; glycogen=store of joined glucose units. A hormone signal changes cell processes; it is not the bulk material being stored.

S3

Uptake by insulin-responsive tissues such as muscle and storage as glycogen in liver/muscle help remove glucose from blood, tending to reduce the raised concentration.

S4 / M3

As glucose moves towards the suitable range, the stimulus for extra insulin release reduces. The response counteracts the original rise rather than amplifying it indefinitely.

M4 / T2

No. The model has no calibrated concentrations, real time scale, individual physiology, absorption details or measured response parameters. Similar qualitative patterns need not have identical numerical values or timing.

T1

Some tissues take up glucose through insulin-independent routes. Better: insulin promotes uptake/storage responses in particular tissues, including increased uptake in muscle cells; it does not control every uptake route in every cell.

T3 optional Higher

Lower glucose stimulates pancreatic alpha cells to release glucagon. Glucagon promotes liver glucose release, including glycogen breakdown, tending to raise blood glucose towards usual levels. Insulin responds to raised glucose and promotes uptake/storage; both reduce their original departure. Core success does not require glucagon.

T4

A time/concentration axis with defined units could support comparing measurements. The relationships and parameters would still need testing against independent measured data; adding an axis alone does not show the model predicts accurately for different individuals.

Exit and assessment

E1: pancreas. E2: glucose units. E3: no calibrated concentration scale, real time scale or individual response parameters. L1: specific mechanism correction or justified confirmation. Secure core: correct insulin sequence, substance distinction and model limit.

Word help

blood glucose concentration
The amount of glucose in a stated volume of blood.
insulin
A hormone released by pancreatic beta cells that helps lower raised blood glucose through target-tissue responses.
glycogen
A stored carbohydrate made from joined glucose units, especially in liver and muscle.
pancreas
An organ with endocrine cells that release hormones including insulin.
negative feedback
A response that reduces the original departure from suitable conditions.
qualitative model
A model showing the kind or direction of a change rather than calibrated numerical predictions.
