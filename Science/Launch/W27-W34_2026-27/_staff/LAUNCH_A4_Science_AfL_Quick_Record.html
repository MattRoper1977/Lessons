W1 SCI_LAUNCH_SU1_W01

  1. Q1. What is a stimulus in a control pathway? ✓ A change away from the regulated range.↳ The final response.Reveals: Reverses stimulus and response.
  2. Q2. What detects a change before coordination? ✓ A receptor or monitoring system.↳ A hormone always appearing first.Reveals: Omits detection.
  3. Q3. How do hormones travel in the body? ✓ As chemical messengers in blood.↳ Only along nerve fibres.Reveals: Confuses endocrine and nervous signalling.
  4. Q4. What does negative feedback tend to do? ✓ Counter a change and move toward a regulated range.↳ Amplify every change indefinitely.Reveals: Misreads feedback direction.
  5. Q5. A model releases a hormone before detecting any change. What step is missing? ✓ Detection of the changed condition.↳ Another final response.Reveals: Treats the pathway as an unordered list.
  6. Q6. Are all homeostatic pathways hormonal? ✓ No; this is one type of coordination.↳ Yes; all control is hormonal.Reveals: Overgeneralises one model.

W2 SCI_LAUNCH_SU1_W02

  1. Q1. Blood glucose rises after a meal. Which hormone helps lower it? ✓ Insulin.↳ Glucagon.Reveals: Reverses the hormone response.
  2. Q2. Blood glucose falls below the usual range. Which hormone helps raise it? ✓ Glucagon.↳ Insulin.Reveals: Reverses the low-glucose response.
  3. Q3. Which organ releases insulin and glucagon? ✓ Pancreas.↳ Lungs.Reveals: Mislocates control organ.
  4. Q4. What happens when cells take up more glucose after insulin? ✓ Raised blood glucose tends to fall toward range.↳ It rises because insulin makes glucose.Reveals: Misstates direction of negative feedback.
  5. Q5. A simulation uses a single smooth line. Does it predict one person exactly? ✓ No; it simplifies real physiology.↳ Yes; everyone has the same curve.Reveals: Overinterprets a model.
  6. Q6. If software is unavailable, can a labelled offline sequence still model the two routes? ✓ Yes, if labelled as a model.↳ No; no learning is possible without a live simulation.Reveals: Confuses tool availability with the concept.

W3 SCI_LAUNCH_SU1_W03

  1. Q1. Where does blood returning from the body enter? ✓ The right side of the heart.↳ The left side from the lungs.Reveals: Confuses the two return routes.
  2. Q2. Where does the right side pump blood? ✓ To the lungs.↳ Directly to all body tissues.Reveals: Reverses pulmonary and systemic routes.
  3. Q3. What returns from lungs to the heart? ✓ Oxygenated blood to the left side.↳ Blood directly to body without heart.Reveals: Omits the heart in double circulation.
  4. Q4. Where does the left side pump blood? ✓ To the body.↳ Only to the lungs.Reveals: Confuses left and right outputs.
  5. Q5. Can blood go body → lungs → body without passing through the heart? ✓ No; both circuits pass through the heart.↳ Yes; the lungs pump it.Reveals: Removes the heart from the circuit.
  6. Q6. Can vessel names be decided from red or blue colour alone? ✓ No; trace route and labels.↳ Yes; colour alone fixes every vessel.Reveals: Uses colour as the sole cue.

W4 SCI_LAUNCH_SU1_W04

  1. Q1. Why do many alveoli help gas exchange? ✓ They provide a large surface area.↳ They make the barrier thick.Reveals: Confuses area with thickness.
  2. Q2. Why is a thin alveolar wall helpful? ✓ It shortens diffusion distance.↳ It stops diffusion.Reveals: Reverses structure-function link.
  3. Q3. What does blood flow at the exchange surface help maintain? ✓ A concentration gradient by carrying gases.↳ A sealed surface with no exchange.Reveals: Misses the role of supply and removal.
  4. Q4. What is ventilation? ✓ Movement of air in and out of lungs.↳ Diffusion through the wall only.Reveals: Conflates air movement and gas exchange.
  5. Q5. If a wall becomes thicker but airflow remains, why can transfer slow? ✓ The diffusion distance is longer.↳ Normal airflow removes all need for diffusion.Reveals: Treats ventilation as a substitute for diffusion.
  6. Q6. Would stopped airflow leave the same fresh-air gradient indefinitely? ✓ No; the gradient would be harder to maintain.↳ Yes; the air never changes.Reveals: Ignores gradient maintenance.

W5 SCI_LAUNCH_SU1_W05

  1. Q1. A glucose graph rises then returns toward range. Which principle helps explain it? ✓ Hormonal negative feedback.↳ Seed dispersal.Reveals: Fails to select the taught principle.
  2. Q2. Blood goes through lungs and body via the heart. Which model? ✓ Double circulation.↳ A one-way route without return.Reveals: Omits the two circuits.
  3. Q3. Thick alveolar walls can slow transfer because…? ✓ Diffusion distance increases.↳ The heart stops all airflow.Reveals: Confuses exchange-surface mechanism.
  4. Q4. A report gives one number without units or comparison. Can you infer a trend? ✓ No; context and units are needed.↳ Yes; any number proves a trend.Reveals: Overclaims from insufficient data.
  5. Q5. Faster breathing alone proves oxygen reached every cell. Is that justified? ✓ No; ventilation rose but exchange and circulation evidence is needed.↳ Yes; breathing rate proves cellular delivery.Reveals: Extends an observation beyond its evidence.
  6. Q6. What would strengthen an unfamiliar-context claim? ✓ A relevant measure, source and limitation.↳ More unrelated facts.Reveals: Adds volume without evidential support.

W6 SCI_LAUNCH_SU1_W06

  1. Q1. A practice item says “explain”. What must the response do? ✓ Give a causal link using the relevant concept.↳ List unrelated labels only.Reveals: Confuses explanation with description.
  2. Q2. Before interpreting a graph in practice, what do you read? ✓ Axes, units and labels.↳ Only the line colour.Reveals: Ignores source information.
  3. Q3. A practice item names a thick alveolar wall. Which concept applies? ✓ Longer diffusion distance slows exchange.↳ More seed dispersal.Reveals: Does not select the relevant topic.
  4. Q4. What should a practice response be checked against? ✓ The command, evidence and stated concept.↳ Its length only.Reveals: Checks quantity instead of relevance.
  5. Q5. Does a practice rehearsal supply answers in the live Paper 2 assessment? ✓ No; live assessment follows permitted access arrangements.↳ Yes; use the model answer in the test.Reveals: Transfers rehearsal support into an assessed response.
  6. Q6. Why is “alveoli are in lungs” incomplete for “why does a thick wall slow transfer”? ✓ It does not link wall thickness to diffusion distance.↳ It already explains the cause fully.Reveals: Accepts a location fact as a causal explanation.
