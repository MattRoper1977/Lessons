Stage 1 · Opening

Diffusion: net movement down a concentration gradient

Learning intention: Define diffusion as the net passive movement of particles down a concentration gradient and explain how gradient, temperature, surface area and distance affect its rate.

Today I can

  • I can state the definition of diffusion using net movement, high to low concentration, and passive (no cellular ATP needed).
  • I can explain why particles still move randomly in all directions at equilibrium even when net movement is zero.
  • I can explain how concentration gradient, temperature, surface area, and diffusion distance change the rate of diffusion.

Work in your book first. Point, speak, sign, draw, type, write or direct an adult. You may pass.

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

A drop of purple food dye spreads through a beaker of still water until the water is evenly pale purple. Which statement is true once it looks evenly purple?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: Why does a spray of perfume in one corner of a room eventually reach the back of the room without anyone fanning the air?

Clue

High concentration to low concentration.

Answer: Perfume gas particles move randomly and collide with air particles, diffusing down the concentration gradient from high (near the bottle) to low.

Retrieval 2: Does diffusion happen in solids, or only in liquids and gases? Explain why.

Clue

Particles in solids only vibrate in fixed positions.

Answer: In liquids and gases, because their particles are free to move past one another randomly.

Retrieval 3: Why does food colouring spread out much faster in hot water (60 °C) than in iced water (5 °C)?

Clue

Higher temperature -> more kinetic energy -> faster diffusion.

Answer: At higher temperature, particles have more kinetic energy and move faster, colliding and spreading more rapidly.

Stage 4 · I do 1

I do 1 · Defining diffusion and dynamic equilibrium

I do 1 · Defining diffusion and dynamic equilibrium

Step 1: Identify the concentration difference: more dissolved or gaseous particles in a given volume = higher concentration.

  1. Step 1: Identify the concentration difference: more dissolved or gaseous particles in a given volume = higher concentration.
  2. Step 2: State the direction: net movement is from higher concentration to lower concentration (down the concentration gradient).
  3. Step 3: State the energy source: diffusion is passive — it relies on the random kinetic energy of particles, not metabolic energy (ATP).
  4. Step 4: Describe equilibrium: when concentrations are equal on both sides, random motion continues in both directions at equal rates, so net movement is zero.

Worked example 1

Worked example 1: Why oxygen diffuses into a respiring animal cell. Inside the cell, respiration continuously uses up oxygen, keeping internal oxygen concentration low. Outside in the tissue fluid, oxygen concentration is high. Because particles move randomly, more oxygen molecules cross the cell membrane from outside (high) to inside (low) than the other way. Therefore, there is a net passive movement of oxygen into the cell down its concentration gradient.

Stage 6 · I do 2

I do 2 · Four factors that control the rate of diffusion

I do 2 · Four factors that control the rate of diffusion

Step 1: Concentration gradient: a steeper difference between high and low concentration increases the net rate of diffusion.

  1. Step 1: Concentration gradient: a steeper difference between high and low concentration increases the net rate of diffusion.
  2. Step 2: Temperature: higher temperature gives particles more kinetic energy so they move and collide faster, increasing the rate.
  3. Step 3: Surface area: a larger membrane surface area (e.g. folded microvilli or many alveoli) gives more space for particles to cross at once.
  4. Step 4: Diffusion distance: a thinner barrier (e.g. a one-cell-thick capillary wall) shortens the path, increasing the rate of diffusion.

Worked example 2

Worked example 2 (Comparing two cells): Cell A has a smooth membrane with surface area 100 µm² and membrane thickness 2 µm at 20 °C. Cell B has a folded membrane with surface area 600 µm² and thickness 0.5 µm at 37 °C. Cell B absorbs glucose by diffusion much faster because: (1) its surface area is 6× larger so more particles cross simultaneously, (2) its diffusion path is 4× shorter, and (3) at 37 °C particles have more kinetic energy.

Stage 7 · We do 2

We do 2 · method order and data table

Order the scientific method or reasoning steps

  1. State that particles move randomly in all directions due to their kinetic energy.
  2. Compare the concentration of particles in Region A (high) and Region B (low) to identify the concentration gradient.
  3. State that net passive movement occurs from higher concentration to lower concentration down the gradient.
  4. Link rate changes to gradient steepness, temperature, surface area, or diffusion distance.

Expected point

Steeper concentration gradient, higher temperature, larger surface area (or shorter diffusion distance).

2. Explain why particles still cross a membrane at equilibrium even though concentrations do not change.

Expected point

Particles move randomly in both directions at equal rates, so there is zero net change.

Aspire exit ticket

1. Explain using kinetic theory why heating a liquid from 20 °C to 50 °C increases the rate of diffusion.

Expected point

Particles gain kinetic energy and move at higher average speeds, colliding and spreading down the concentration gradient more rapidly.

2. Why must a gas exchange membrane be thin as well as having a large surface area?

Expected point

A thin membrane minimises the diffusion distance so each particle crosses in less time, maximising the net rate.
