Stage 1 · Opening

Explaining diffusion in the exam: causal chains and command words

Learning intention: Answer exam-style diffusion questions independently by matching the command word (describe vs explain) and building a three-part causal chain (direction, gradient, adaptation).

Today I can

  • I can tell "describe" (what happens / pattern) and "explain" (why it happens using because/so) apart.
  • I can build a 3-mark diffusion explanation linking concentration comparison, random crossings both ways, and net direction.
  • I can audit and repair a weak exam answer by adding the missing causal link.

Work in your book first. Point, speak, sign, draw, type, write or direct an adult. You may pass.

Model clue: Three-panel exam explanation model: Describe vs Explain command words, the 3-step causal chain (WHAT -> BECAUSE -> SO), and model patch surface-area data.

Stage 3 · Starter

Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · Read the command word: Describe = WHAT happens; Explain = WHY (because/so).
Beat 2: Beat 2 · Check the question focus: is it asking about net DIRECTION or about RATE?
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Build the 3-link chain: WHAT moves -> BECAUSE of the gradient -> SO adaptation speeds it up.
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Audit and edit: replace vague words ("wants to", "goes in") with particle-level causes.

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

An exam question asks: "Explain why oxygen enters the blood quickly in the lungs." Why is the answer "Because it diffuses" not enough for full marks?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: What turns a description ("Oxygen moves into the blood") into an explanation?

Clue

Link WHAT to BECAUSE.

Answer: Adding the causal link ("because oxygen concentration is higher in the alveolus than in the arriving blood").

Retrieval 2: Why is the statement "The oxygen spreads out because it wants to reach the blood" scientifically wrong?

Clue

Never give particles human feelings.

Answer: Particles cannot think or "want" anything; they move randomly, and net movement happens because more particles start on the high-concentration side.

Retrieval 3: Look at Panel 3. When model patch area doubles from 10 cm² to 20 cm², what happens to the transfer rate?

Clue

Doubling surface area doubles the rate.

Answer: It doubles from 4 units per minute to 8 units per minute.

Stage 4 · I do 1

I do 1 · Answering a 3-mark "Explain net direction" question

I do 1 · Answering a 3-mark "Explain net direction" question

Step 1: Read the exact question: "Explain the initial net movement of oxygen from alveolar air into arriving blood. [3 marks]"

  1. Step 1: Read the exact question: "Explain the initial net movement of oxygen from alveolar air into arriving blood. [3 marks]"
  2. Step 2: Point 1 (Concentration comparison): Alveolar air has a higher oxygen concentration than the arriving deoxygenated blood (1 mark).
  3. Step 3: Point 2 (Random particle motion): Oxygen molecules move randomly and cross the membrane in both directions (1 mark).
  4. Step 4: Point 3 (Net imbalance): More oxygen molecules cross from air to blood than from blood to air initially, so net movement is into the blood down the concentration gradient (1 mark).

Worked example 1

Worked example 1: Question — "Explain the initial net movement of oxygen from alveolar air into arriving blood. [3 marks]" Model Answer: (1) Alveolar air has a higher concentration of oxygen than the arriving blood. (2) Oxygen molecules move randomly in all directions and cross the walls both ways. (3) Because more oxygen molecules start on the alveolar side, more cross from air to blood than back each second, giving a net movement into the blood down the concentration gradient.

Worked example 2 (Repairing & Transferring): Weak answer to "Explain why a thick membrane slows diffusion [2]": "Because it is thick." Stage 7 · We do 2

We do 2 · method order and data table

Order the scientific method or reasoning steps

  1. State the WHAT sentence (direction of movement or pattern in the data table).
  2. Read the command word (Describe vs Explain) and check whether the question asks about net direction or diffusion rate.
  3. Add the BECAUSE sentence (comparing higher and lower concentration across the barrier).
  4. Add the SO sentence (linking the structural adaptation or factor to diffusion distance, surface area, or gradient steepness).

Use move up and Move down to place the steps in order, then check.

Aspire exit ticket

1. Explain the effect of reduced alveolar surface area on a person's ability to exercise.

Expected point

Less surface area means fewer oxygen molecules can diffuse into the blood per second, reducing oxygen delivery to respiring muscle cells and causing breathlessness/fatigue.

2. Why is it important to check whether an Explain question asks about net DIRECTION or about RATE before writing your answer?

Expected point

Direction requires comparing concentrations across the barrier; rate requires linking adaptations (area, distance, blood flow, temperature) to crossing speed.
