Stage 1 · Opening

Magnification exam questions and scale bar lab

Learning intention: Answer exam-style magnification and scale-bar questions independently, matching the command word, showing four-line working, and checking units and plausibility.

Today I can

  • I can read the command word (calculate, describe, explain, estimate) and give what it asks for.
  • I can show four-line working (equation, unit check, substitution, final answer) on every calculation.
  • I can audit an answer to check it has the right unit (or × with no unit for magnification) and is scientifically plausible.

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · Read the command word: circle Calculate, Describe, Explain or Estimate.
Beat 2: Beat 2 · Audit the units: check if lengths are in mm and µm, and convert mm × 1000 -> µm.

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

A 2-mark question asks: "Image = 40 mm, Actual = 80 µm. Calculate the magnification." Which pupil answer earns full marks?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: What does the command word "Describe" ask you to do on a cell micrograph question?

Clue

Describe = what you see.

Answer: State what is visible (shape, structures or pattern) without needing to explain why.

Retrieval 2: What does the command word "Estimate" ask you to do with a scale bar?

Clue

E.g. if the cell is about 3 scale bars wide, multiply the bar value by 3.

Answer: Compare the size of the cell with the length of the scale bar to give a sensible approximate value.

Retrieval 3: If a student writes only "400" for a 2-mark Calculate magnification question, what are they risking?

Clue

Always show the 4-line method.

Answer: They miss the × symbol and if they made any slip they lose all marks because no working was shown.

  1. Step 1: Read the command word and mark tariff: "A cell image is 60 mm wide. The real cell is 0.15 mm. Calculate the magnification. [2]"
  2. Step 2: Line 1 (Equation) & Line 2 (Units): Magnification = Image size ÷ Actual size. Both are in mm (60 mm and 0.15 mm).

Stage 6 · I do 2

I do 2 · Using a scale bar and auditing flawed exam answers

I do 2 · Using a scale bar and auditing flawed exam answers

Step 1: Locate the scale bar on the micrograph and read its actual length label (e.g. 10 µm).

  1. Step 1: Locate the scale bar on the micrograph and read its actual length label (e.g. 10 µm).
  2. Step 2: Measure the length of the drawn scale bar in millimetres using a ruler (e.g. 20 mm) and convert to µm (20,000 µm).
  3. Step 3: Calculate magnification of the scale bar: M = I ÷ A = 20,000 µm ÷ 10 µm = ×2,000 (or estimate cell width by counting how many scale bars fit across the cell).
  4. Step 4: Audit your final answer: check whether a real cell size (10–100 µm) or organelle size (0.5–10 µm) is biologically plausible.

Worked example 2

Worked example 2 (Scale bar & estimate): On a micrograph, a scale bar labelled 20 µm measures 15 mm long. A plant cell next to it measures 45 mm long. Method A (Estimate/ratio): The cell image (45 mm) is 3 times longer than the scale bar (15 mm), so Actual cell length = 3 × 20 µm = 60 µm. Method B (Equation): Magnification = 15,000 µm ÷ 20 µm = ×750; Actual cell length = 45,000 µm ÷ 750 = 60 µm.

Stage 7 · We do 2

We do 2 · method order and data table

Order the scientific method or reasoning steps

  1. Write the magnification equation or rearrange it for the required unknown (I, A or M).
  2. Circle the command word (Calculate, Describe, Explain or Estimate) and underline the given numbers and units.
  3. Convert millimetres to micrometres (×1000) so both lengths share the same unit, then substitute.
  4. Write the final answer, check the unit (× for magnification, µm or mm for size), and check biological plausibility.

Use move up and Move down to place the steps in order, then check.

Expected point

Check that both measurements are in the same unit (e.g. both mm or both µm).

Independent exit ticket

1. An image is 50 mm wide and the real cell is 0.1 mm wide. Calculate the magnification.

Expected point

50 ÷ 0.1 = ×500.

2. Explain why ×400 is right for magnification but 400 mm is wrong.

Expected point

Magnification is a ratio of two lengths so the units cancel out; mm is a unit of length, not magnification.

Aspire exit ticket

1. An image is 36 mm wide at ×600 magnification. Calculate the actual size in µm, showing your working.

Expected point

36 mm = 36,000 µm; Actual = 36,000 ÷ 600 = 60 µm.

2. How can you check whether a calculated actual cell size is biologically plausible?

Expected point

Compare it with typical cell dimensions (animal/plant cells ~10–100 µm, bacteria ~1–2 µm); an answer in metres or centimetres shows a rearrangement or unit error.
