Stage 1 of 10: Opening

Stage 1 · Opening

Active transport: moving particles against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration

Learning intention: Define active transport as the movement of particles from a lower concentration to a higher concentration (against the concentration gradient) using energy transferred from cellular respiration, and explain how carrier proteins and mitochondria make it work.

Today I can

  • I can state the direction of active transport (low to high concentration — against the concentration gradient).
  • I can explain why active transport needs energy from cellular respiration (in mitochondria) and carrier proteins in the cell membrane.
  • I can explain why "active transport is just faster diffusion" is wrong by contrasting uphill active transport with downhill passive diffusion.

Work in your book first. Point, speak, sign, draw, type, write or direct an adult. You may pass.

Model clue: Three-panel active transport studio: Downhill passive diffusion vs uphill active transport, carrier protein pump powered by energy from mitochondria, and a root hair cell absorbing dilute soil nitrate ions.

Aspire route

1. Compare the energy source of passive diffusion with the energy source of active transport.

Help with this question

Distinguish kinetic motion from metabolic energy.

Check: Passive diffusion relies on the random thermal kinetic energy of particles moving down a gradient; active transport requires metabolic energy transferred from cellular respiration (ATP) in mitochondria.

2. Why must active transport use specific carrier proteins embedded in the cell membrane rather than simple gaps between membrane lipids?

Help with this question

Structural role of carrier proteins.

Check: Because simple gaps would let particles leak back down their gradient; carrier proteins bind a specific ion or molecule and change shape using energy to pump it one way against the gradient.

3. If a metabolic poison stops cellular respiration in a root cell, predict what happens to (a) active transport of nitrate ions and (b) diffusion of oxygen.

Help with this question

Experimental evidence for energy requirement.

Check: (a) Active transport of nitrate stops because no energy is released by respiration; (b) diffusion of oxygen continues because it is passive.

4. Why is the statement "Active transport is just faster diffusion" a serious biological misconception?

Help with this question

Target misconception.

Check: Because diffusion goes DOWN the gradient (high -> low) passively, whereas active transport goes AGAINST the gradient (low -> high) using energy — the defining difference is direction relative to the gradient and energy use, not speed.

Stage 3 · Starter

Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · Direction: Active transport moves particles from LOW concentration to HIGH concentration — AGAINST (up) the concentration gradient.
Beat 2: Beat 2 · Energy & machinery: It requires energy transferred from cellular respiration (in mitochondria) and carrier proteins in the cell membrane.
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Myth-buster: Active transport is NOT "faster diffusion" — diffusion goes downhill (high -> low, passive); active transport goes uphill (low -> high, active).
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Cell adaptation clue: Cells that do lots of active transport (like root hair cells) are packed with mitochondria to supply the energy.

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

A student writes: "Active transport is when particles diffuse really fast using energy." Why would an examiner mark this wrong?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: In the hill analogy (Panel 1), what does rolling downhill represent, and what does pushing uphill represent?

Clue

Downhill vs uphill.

Answer: Rolling downhill = passive diffusion/osmosis (down the gradient, no cellular energy); pushing uphill = active transport (against the gradient, requires energy from respiration).

Retrieval 2: What two things inside a cell are needed to carry out active transport across the membrane?

Clue

Machinery + fuel.

Answer: Carrier proteins in the cell membrane and energy released by cellular respiration (in mitochondria).

Retrieval 3: Why do root hair cells die or fail to absorb minerals in waterlogged soil that has no air pockets?

Clue

Real-world respiration link.

Answer: Without oxygen in the soil, aerobic respiration in the root hair mitochondria slows or stops, so there is not enough energy to power active transport of mineral ions.

Stage 6 · I do 2

I do 2 · How the carrier protein pump works & why active transport is NOT "faster diffusion"

I do 2 · How the carrier protein pump works & why active transport is NOT "faster diffusion"

Step 1: Step 1 (Bind): A specific ion or molecule on the low-concentration side binds to a matching carrier protein in the cell membrane.

  1. Step 1: Step 1 (Bind): A specific ion or molecule on the low-concentration side binds to a matching carrier protein in the cell membrane.
  2. Step 2: Step 2 (Pump with energy): Energy from respiration in mitochondria makes the carrier protein change shape, pushing the particle through to the high-concentration side.
  3. Step 3: Step 3 (Reset): The carrier protein releases the particle inside and returns to its original shape, ready for the next ion.
  4. Step 4: Contrast with diffusion: Diffusion goes High -> Low (down gradient, passive, no carrier energy); Active transport goes Low -> High (against gradient, active, uses respiration energy).

Worked example 2

Worked example 2 (Waterlogged soil Question): A farmer's field floods, filling all air spaces in the soil with water. The crop plants turn yellow from nitrate deficiency even though nitrate ions are present in the soil water. Why? (1) Soil nitrate is more dilute than inside the root hair cells, so nitrate must be absorbed by active transport against the concentration gradient. (2) Waterlogged soil lacks oxygen, so aerobic respiration in the root cells' mitochondria slows down. (3) Less energy is released, so carrier proteins cannot pump nitrate ions into the root hair cells.
