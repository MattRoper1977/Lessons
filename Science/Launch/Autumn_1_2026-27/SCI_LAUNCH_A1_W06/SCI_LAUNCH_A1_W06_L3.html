Stage 1 · Opening

Comparing diffusion, osmosis, and active transport: decision lab and compare answers

Learning intention: Compare diffusion, osmosis, and active transport by stating both similarities and differences across direction, substance, membrane, and energy, and classify unfamiliar transport scenarios from evidence.

Today I can

  • I can state what the command word "compare" requires — both sides named, with similarities AND differences.
  • I can compare any two (or all three) transport processes using direction, substance moved, membrane, and energy from respiration.
  • I can classify unfamiliar biological scenarios into diffusion, osmosis, or active transport using a 3-question decision rule.

Work in your book first. Point, speak, sign, draw, type, write or direct an adult. You may pass.

Model clue: Three-panel Transport decision & Compare studio: 3-column comparison matrix (Diffusion · Osmosis · Active transport), the "Compare = Similarities + Differences" command card, and the 3-step Decision lab.

Stage 3 · Starter

Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · Command word COMPARE = Similarities + Differences (both processes named on every contrast).
Beat 2: Beat 2 · Four discriminators: (1) What moves? (2) Which direction? (3) Membrane / carrier proteins? (4) Energy from respiration?
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Decision lab rule: Water + membrane + down = Osmosis; Against gradient + energy = Active transport; Solute/gas + down + no energy = Diffusion.
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Glitch-buster: "If a substance crosses a membrane, that is osmosis" is FALSE — CO₂ and O₂ cross membranes by diffusion, and ions cross by active transport!

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

A 4-mark question asks: "Compare diffusion and active transport." A student writes four accurate sentences all about diffusion and never mentions active transport. Why does this score low marks?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: Why does writing "They are both types of movement" often fail to score a similarity mark in a GCSE compare question?

Clue

Be specific in similarities.

Answer: It is too vague (simply repeating the question stem); a mark-earning similarity names a specific biological feature they share, such as "Both move substances across a cell membrane" or "Both diffusion and osmosis are passive processes that move particles down a gradient."

Retrieval 2: Which single question separates active transport from BOTH diffusion and osmosis?

Clue

Key separator.

Answer: "Does it move particles against the concentration gradient using energy from cellular respiration?" (Yes = Active transport; No = Diffusion and Osmosis).

Retrieval 3: Why is "If a substance crosses a membrane, it must be osmosis" a dangerous exam trap?

Clue

Science Glitch repaired.

Answer: Because all three processes cross cell membranes! Only the net movement of WATER down a water gradient across a partially permeable membrane is osmosis.

Worked example 1

Worked example 1 (4-Mark compare WAGOLL): "Compare diffusion and active transport. [4 marks]" -> Both processes move substances across a cell membrane (1). Diffusion moves particles down the concentration gradient (from high to low concentration), whereas active transport moves particles against the concentration gradient (from low to high concentration) (1). Diffusion is a passive process that needs no energy supplied by cellular respiration (1), whereas active transport requires energy transferred from cellular respiration (and uses membrane carrier proteins) (1).

Step 1: Scenario 1 (Seaweed & Iodide): Seaweed cells pump iodide ions in from seawater where iodide is 1,000× more dilute, using energy from respiration -> Against gradient + Energy = ACTIVE TRANSPORT.

  1. Step 1: Scenario 1 (Seaweed & Iodide): Seaweed cells pump iodide ions in from seawater where iodide is 1,000× more dilute, using energy from respiration -> Against gradient + Energy = ACTIVE TRANSPORT.

Worked example 2

Worked example 2 (Comparing osmosis and Active transport [4 marks]): (1) Similarity: Both osmosis and active transport involve the movement of substances across a selectively/partially permeable cell membrane (1). (2) Difference in substance: Osmosis moves water molecules only, whereas active transport moves dissolved mineral ions or molecules such as glucose (1). (3) Difference in direction: Osmosis moves water down its water gradient (from dilute to concentrated), whereas active transport moves solutes against their concentration gradient (low to high) (1). (4) Difference in energy: Osmosis is passive (no energy from respiration), whereas active transport requires energy from cellular respiration (1).

Stage 7 · We do 2

We do 2 · method order and data table

Order the scientific method or reasoning steps

  1. Check the direction and energy: if particles move from LOW to HIGH concentration (against the gradient) using energy from respiration, classify as ACTIVE TRANSPORT.
  2. Check what substance is moving: if it is WATER crossing a partially permeable membrane down its water gradient, classify as OSMOSIS.
  3. If a solute or gas moves from HIGH to LOW concentration (down the gradient) with no respiratory energy spent, classify as DIFFUSION.
  4. When writing a "Compare" answer, use "Both..." for a similarity and "...whereas..." to contrast both processes on direction, substance, and energy.

Use move up and Move down to place the steps in order, then check.
