Stage 3 · Starter

Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · Exam-set routine: Circle command word -> Check [marks] -> Write one distinct scientific point per mark.
Beat 2: Beat 2 · The 4 classic weak answers: "400" (missing working/×), "It spreads out" (missing net + gradient + direction), "Water moves in" (missing gradient + membrane), "Active is different" (missing both sides).
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Feedback repair Rule: Never hide or scribble out your first attempt — keep it visible and write your improved scientific repair underneath.
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Glitch-buster: "Writing 'I will try harder' is good feedback improvement" is FALSE — an improvement must add the missing scientific words!

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

A question asks: "Explain why there is an initial net movement of oxygen from alveolar air into arriving blood. [3 marks]" A student writes: "It spreads out." What is the best green-pen repair?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: Why does "400" on its own lose credit on a 2-mark magnification calculation?

Clue

Calculate audit.

Answer: It omits the working (conversion + substitution into I ÷ A) and omits the magnification symbol (×400).

Retrieval 2: Why does "Water moves in" score 0 or 1 out of 3 on an osmosis Explain question?

Clue

Explain audit.

Answer: It omits the water concentration comparison (more dilute outside than inside) and omits the partially permeable membrane.

Retrieval 3: Why must a feedback repair add actual biology words rather than a general comment like "add more detail"?

Clue

Repair rule.

Answer: Only specific scientific points (such as "down the concentration gradient" or "partially permeable membrane") earn GCSE marks.

Stage 4 · I do 1

I do 1 · Tackling a mixed Topic 1 exam set (Calculate, Explain & Compare)

I do 1 · Tackling a mixed Topic 1 exam set (Calculate, Explain & Compare)

Step 1: Item 1 — CALCULATE [2 marks]: "An image of a palisade cell measures 12 mm. Its actual length is 0.03 mm. Calculate the magnification." -> Units match (mm); M = I ÷ A = 12 ÷ 0.03 = ×400.

  1. Step 1: Item 1 — CALCULATE [2 marks]: "An image of a palisade cell measures 12 mm. Its actual length is 0.03 mm. Calculate the magnification." -> Units match (mm); M = I ÷ A = 12 ÷ 0.03 = ×400.
  2. Step 2: Item 2 — EXPLAIN [3 marks]: "Explain why there is an initial net movement of oxygen from alveolar air into arriving blood." -> Higher O₂ concentration in alveoli than arriving blood (1) -> net movement by diffusion down the concentration gradient (1) -> across thin walls into the blood (1).
  3. Step 3: Item 3 — EXPLAIN [3 marks]: "Explain why a potato cylinder gains mass in pure water." -> Pure water is more dilute than cytoplasm (1) -> net water enters cells by osmosis (1) -> across the partially permeable membrane (1).
  4. Step 4: Item 4 — COMPARE [4 marks]: "Compare diffusion, osmosis, and active transport." -> Similarity: all cross the cell membrane (1). Differences: substance (solute/gas vs water vs ions) (1), gradient (down vs down vs against) (1), and energy (passive vs passive vs ATP from respiration) (1).

Worked example 1

Worked example 1 (Full topic 1 Practice set): Q1 Calculate: 12 mm ÷ 0.03 mm = ×400 (2). Q2 Explain diffusion: Oxygen concentration is higher in alveolar air than in arriving blood (1), so there is a net movement of oxygen down its concentration gradient (1) into the blood (1). Q3 Compare: Diffusion and osmosis are both passive down a gradient (1), whereas active transport moves ions against the gradient using energy from respiration (1).

Practical apparatus and safety

Classroom exam-practice and feedback-repair session — no practical hazard. Emphasise that keeping first attempts visible is a celebratory learning routine, not a high-stakes test.

P17 · Planned adaptations

  • Cognition and learning: Rehearse one worked step at a time and fade one cue before the independent check.
  • Communication and interaction: Accept pointing, signing, choice selection or adult scribing.
  • Regulation: Use the quiet pause option and return to the last secure step.
  • Sensory and physical: Offer high-contrast print cards and seated or adult-directed apparatus handling.
