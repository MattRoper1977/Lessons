Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · One thread (The cell): Magnification & resolution let us see the cell; diffusion, osmosis, and active transport move substances across its membrane.
Beat 2: Beat 2 · Microscopy anchor: Magnification = Image ÷ Actual (no units; 1 mm = 1,000 µm); Resolution = smallest gap seen as two separate points.
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Transport anchor: Diffusion (High -> Low, passive) · Osmosis (water, membrane, passive, % mass change) · Active transport (Low -> High, respiration energy).
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Connection lab: Spotting a gap today is diagnostic information so you can repair it in green pen before the assessment.

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

A student says: "Osmosis is just diffusion, so if an exam asks me to define osmosis I can just write 'particles moving from high to low concentration'." Why will this lose marks?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: How are magnification and resolution different when viewing a cell?

Clue

Prior topic retrieval.

Answer: Magnification is how many times bigger the image is than the real object (I ÷ A); resolution is the smallest distance between two points that can still be seen as separate (clarity of detail).

Retrieval 2: How does the potato core practical connect to osmosis?

Clue

Prior topic retrieval.

Answer: Percentage change in mass ((final − initial) ÷ initial × 100) measures net osmosis: positive (+%) means net water entered in a dilute solution; negative (−%) means net water left in a concentrated solution; 0% estimates the tissue concentration.

Retrieval 3: How do mitochondria connect cell structure, electron microscopy, and active transport?

Clue

Cross-topic link.

Answer: Mitochondria are sub-cellular structures (cells) whose fine internal detail is resolved by electron microscopes (microscopy) and which release energy by aerobic respiration to power active transport (active transport).

Stage 4 · I do 1

I do 1 · Building the 4-branch Topic 1 Knowledge Map around the cell

I do 1 · Building the 4-branch Topic 1 Knowledge Map around the cell

Step 1: Branch 1 (Seeing the Cell · Microscopy): Magnification = Image size ÷ Actual size (no units; convert mm × 1,000 -> µm first). Resolution = smallest gap seen as two separate points.

  1. Step 1: Branch 1 (Seeing the Cell · Microscopy): Magnification = Image size ÷ Actual size (no units; convert mm × 1,000 -> µm first). Resolution = smallest gap seen as two separate points.
  2. Step 2: Branch 2 (Diffusion): Net movement of any gas or dissolved solute from higher to lower concentration down a gradient; passive (no respiratory energy); faster with large surface area, thin wall, steep gradient.
  3. Step 3: Branch 3 (Osmosis): Net movement of WATER only from a dilute to a concentrated solution across a partially permeable membrane; passive; measured via % change in mass.
  4. Step 4: Branch 4 (Active transport): Movement of ions/molecules from lower to higher concentration (against the gradient) using membrane carrier proteins and energy from respiration in mitochondria.

Worked example 1

Worked example 1 (Topic 1 Thread WAGOLL): "Magnification is how many times bigger a cell image is than its actual size (Image ÷ Actual, after converting mm to µm). Across the cell's membrane, diffusion and osmosis move substances down a gradient passively without energy from respiration — with osmosis moving water only across a partially permeable membrane. Active transport pumps substances against the concentration gradient (low to high) using carrier proteins and energy released by respiration in mitochondria."
