Stage 1 · Opening

Osmosis: net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane

Learning intention: Define osmosis using all four key terms (water, dilute, concentrated, partially permeable membrane) and predict the direction of net water movement between solutions.

Today I can

  • I can state that a dilute solution has MORE water (and less solute) than a concentrated solution.
  • I can define osmosis using four key terms: water, dilute, concentrated, and partially permeable membrane.
  • I can predict and explain the net direction of water movement across a membrane using crossing counts.

Work in your book first. Point, speak, sign, draw, type, write or direct an adult. You may pass.

Model clue: Three-panel osmosis model: Dilute vs Concentrated reframed by water concentration, the partially permeable membrane gate (water passes, large sugar blocked), and net crossing counts.

Stage 3 · Starter

Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · The cordial test: weak cordial = DILUTE (more water, less sugar); strong cordial = CONCENTRATED (less water, more sugar).
Beat 2: Beat 2 · The size gate: a partially permeable membrane has tiny pores — small water passes both ways, large sugar is blocked.
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Count the crossings: more water molecules start on the dilute side, so more cross from dilute -> concentrated each second.
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Lock the 4-phrase definition: WATER · DILUTE to CONCENTRATED · PARTIALLY PERMEABLE MEMBRANE.

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

Two glasses are the same size. Glass A holds weak, dilute squash. Glass B holds strong, concentrated squash. Which glass has a HIGHER concentration of WATER molecules?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: Why is the word "concentrated" a trap when you first learn osmosis?

Clue

Flip your thinking to count the water molecules.

Answer: Because "concentrated solution" means lots of solute (sugar/salt), which actually means LESS water.

Retrieval 2: What does "partially permeable" mean?

Clue

"Partially" = only some things can pass.

Answer: It lets some small particles (like water) pass through its pores, but blocks larger particles (like dissolved sugar).

Retrieval 3: Does osmosis require energy from respiration (ATP), or is it passive?

Clue

Osmosis is a special case of diffusion.

Answer: It is passive — it relies on the random kinetic energy of water molecules, just like diffusion.

Stage 4 · I do 1

I do 1 · Reframing dilute vs concentrated and locking the 4-phrase definition

I do 1 · Reframing dilute vs concentrated and locking the 4-phrase definition

Step 1: Reframe solutions by WATER: Dilute solution = MORE water (high water concentration), less solute. Concentrated solution = LESS water (low water concentration), more solute.

  1. Step 1: Reframe solutions by WATER: Dilute solution = MORE water (high water concentration), less solute. Concentrated solution = LESS water (low water concentration), more solute.
  2. Step 2: Examine the barrier: a partially permeable membrane (like a cell membrane or Visking tubing) has tiny pores. Water fits through; large dissolved sugar molecules are blocked.
  3. Step 3: Assemble the 4-phrase GCSE definition: "Osmosis is the net movement of WATER from a DILUTE solution to a CONCENTRATED solution through a PARTIALLY PERMEABLE membrane."
  4. Step 4: Check why all 4 phrases matter: leaving out "water" or "partially permeable membrane" confuses osmosis with general diffusion.

Worked example 1

Worked example 1: Why does water move from a dilute solution (Side A) to a concentrated solution (Side B) across a partially permeable membrane? Step 1: Side A is dilute, so it has a higher concentration of water molecules than Side B. Step 2: Large sugar molecules are blocked by the partially permeable membrane, while small water molecules cross randomly in both directions. Step 3: More water molecules cross from Side A to Side B than from B to A, giving a net movement of water from dilute to concentrated.

Stage 6 · I do 2

I do 2 · Predicting net water movement in three cell scenarios

I do 2 · Predicting net water movement in three cell scenarios

Step 1: Scenario 1 (Pure water / very dilute outside): Water concentration is higher outside than inside the cell. Crossings: 9 in vs 3 out -> Net water movement IN; cell swells.

  1. Step 1: Scenario 1 (Pure water / very dilute outside): Water concentration is higher outside than inside the cell. Crossings: 9 in vs 3 out -> Net water movement IN; cell swells.
  2. Step 2: Scenario 2 (Matching concentration outside and inside): Water concentration is equal on both sides. Crossings: 6 in vs 6 out -> No net movement; cell stays the same mass.
  3. Step 3: Scenario 3 (Strong concentrated sugar solution outside): Water concentration is higher inside the cell than outside. Crossings: 3 in vs 9 out -> Net water movement OUT; cell shrinks.
  4. Step 4: Connect to diffusion: Osmosis is simply the diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane down water's own gradient.

Worked example 2

Worked example 2 (Crossing counts): A model cell is placed in a strong sugar solution. In one second, 3 water molecules cross into the cell and 9 water molecules cross out of the cell. (a) What is the net movement? 9 - 3 = 6 water molecules OUT of the cell. (b) Why? The cell cytoplasm is more dilute (contains more water) than the strong external sugar solution, so net water movement is out of the cell through the partially permeable membrane.

Stage 7 · We do 2

We do 2 · method order and data table

Order the scientific method or reasoning steps

  1. State that the partially permeable membrane blocks the large solute particles and allows small water molecules to cross both ways.
  2. Compare the two solutions and label which one is MORE DILUTE (higher water concentration) and which is MORE CONCENTRATED (lower water concentration).
  3. Compare the crossing counts in both directions and state the direction of NET water movement (from dilute to concentrated).
  4. Predict the physical effect on the cell or tubing bag (gains water and swells, stays the same mass, or loses water and shrinks).

Use move up and Move down to place the steps in order, then check.

Aspire exit ticket

Expected point

Because water moves passively down its own concentration gradient (from a dilute solution with high water concentration to a concentrated solution with low water concentration) across a partially permeable membrane.

Expected point

Its mass stays constant because water molecules cross the membrane in both directions at equal rates (no net movement).
