Stage 1 · Opening

Analysing and evaluating the osmosis practical: zero-crossing and method

Learning intention: Interpret a graph of percentage change in mass against sucrose concentration, estimate the zero-crossing concentration, handle anomalies honestly, and write a three-part evaluation (strength, limitation, improvement).

Today I can

  • I can read the concentration where the graph crosses 0% mass change and explain what it estimates about the potato cells.
  • I can spot an anomalous repeat, keep it on the record, and exclude it from the mean with a scientific reason.
  • I can evaluate the osmosis practical by stating one strength, one specific limitation, and one justified improvement.

Work in your book first. Point, speak, sign, draw, type, write or direct an adult. You may pass.

Model clue: Three-panel osmosis evaluation studio: Concentration vs % mass change graph crossing 0% at ~0.35 mol/dm³, flagging an anomalous repeat without erasing it, and the 3-part Evaluate checklist.

Stage 3 · Starter

Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · Plot variables honestly: x-axis = sucrose concentration (mol/dm³); y-axis = signed % change in mass (+ above zero, − below zero).
Beat 2: Beat 2 · Read the zero-crossing: where the line crosses 0% estimates the concentration matching the potato tissue (no net osmosis).
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Handle anomalies honestly: never rub out an odd repeat — circle it, keep it on the record, and exclude it from the mean with a reason.
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Evaluate 1-2-3: 1 Strength + 1 Specific limitation + 1 Concrete improvement (never "it was fun" or "human error").

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

In the table at 0.6 mol/dm³, three repeats give −9.1%, −8.7%, and −0.4%. A student says: "Rub out −0.4% and write −9.0% so the table looks neat." What is the right scientific response?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: Why is the point where the graph crosses 0% biologically interesting?

Clue

Isotonic / zero-change point.

Answer: Because 0% mass change means no net water movement — the external solution concentration matches the water/solute concentration inside the potato cells.

Retrieval 2: What is the difference between "analysing" data and "evaluating" a method?

Clue

Analyse = data; Evaluate = method quality.

Answer: Analysing uses the data to find a pattern or read a value (like the zero crossing); evaluating judges how reliable/valid the method was and suggests specific improvements.

Retrieval 3: Why is "The experiment went well and was fun" NOT a scientific evaluation?

Clue

Judge the method, not your mood.

Answer: It states a personal feeling instead of naming a controlled variable (strength), a source of error/scatter (limitation), and a procedural fix (improvement).

I do 1 · Reading the zero-crossing and handling an anomalous repeat

I do 1 · Reading the zero-crossing and handling an anomalous repeat

Step 1: Check the axes: x-axis = Sucrose concentration (mol/dm³); y-axis = Percentage change in mass (%) with a clear horizontal line at 0%.

  1. Step 1: Check the axes: x-axis = Sucrose concentration (mol/dm³); y-axis = Percentage change in mass (%) with a clear horizontal line at 0%.
  2. Step 2: Trace the trend: from +11.2% (at 0.0) and +5.1% (at 0.2) down to −1.4% (at 0.4). The line crosses 0% between 0.2 and 0.4, at about 0.35 mol/dm³.
  3. Step 3: State what 0.35 mol/dm³ means: it estimates the equivalent solute concentration of the potato cells (where water crosses in and out at equal rates).
  4. Step 4: Check the repeats at 0.6 mol/dm³ (−9.1%, −8.7%, −0.4%): circle −0.4% as an anomaly, keep it in the table, and calculate the mean from the two concordant repeats: (−9.1 + −8.7) ÷ 2 = −8.9%.

Worked example 1

Worked example 1 (Zero crossing & Anomaly): On the sample graph, y = +5.1% at 0.2 mol/dm³ and y = −1.4% at 0.4 mol/dm³. Reading where the line crosses y = 0% gives an estimated tissue concentration of 0.35 mol/dm³. At 0.6 mol/dm³, Repeat 3 (−0.4%) is far from Repeats 1 and 2 (−9.1% and −8.7%), likely because surface water was not blotted off. We keep −0.4% in the table, circle it, and calculate Mean = (−9.1 + −8.7) ÷ 2 = −8.9%.

Stage 6 · I do 2

I do 2 · Building a full 3-part GCSE evaluation (Strength + Limitation + Improvement)

I do 2 · Building a full 3-part GCSE evaluation (Strength + Limitation + Improvement)

Step 1: Part 1 (Strength): Name a variable that was well controlled (e.g. all cylinders were cut to the same length from the same potato and soaked for 30 minutes).

  1. Step 1: Part 1 (Strength): Name a variable that was well controlled (e.g. all cylinders were cut to the same length from the same potato and soaked for 30 minutes).
  2. Step 2: Part 2 (Limitation + Evidence): Name a specific source of variation and point to the evidence (e.g. hand-blotting varied, giving a 2.7% spread between repeats at 0.4 mol/dm³: −1.4%, −2.9%, −0.2%).
  3. Step 3: Part 3 (Justified improvement): State WHAT to change and WHY it reduces that variation (e.g. roll each cylinder 3 times on a fresh dry paper towel so surface water is removed consistently).
  4. Step 4: Audit weak phrases: replace "human error" or "it went well" with specific apparatus or method steps (blotting, evaporation lids, temperature water bath, 3 repeats).

Worked example 2

Worked example 2 (3-Part Evaluation): (1) Strength: Cylinder length and soak time were kept constant, and percentage change was used to account for different starting masses. (2) Limitation: Blotting was done by hand without a fixed routine, which left varying amounts of surface liquid on the cylinders (shown by the spread of repeats at 0.4 mol/dm³ from −0.2% to −2.9%). (3) Improvement: Blot every cylinder by rolling it twice on a fresh sheet of paper towel, and repeat each concentration three times to identify anomalies and calculate a reliable mean.

Practical apparatus and safety

Data analysis, graphing, and method evaluation session — no chemical or sharp-instrument hazard. Emphasise that evaluating a method critiques the procedure, never the person who carried it out, and that honest recording of anomalies is good science.

P17 · Planned adaptations

  • Cognition and learning: Rehearse one worked step at a time and fade one cue before the independent check.
  • Communication and interaction: Accept pointing, signing, choice selection or adult scribing.
  • Regulation: Use the quiet pause option and return to the last secure step.
  • Sensory and physical: Offer high-contrast print cards and seated or adult-directed apparatus handling.
