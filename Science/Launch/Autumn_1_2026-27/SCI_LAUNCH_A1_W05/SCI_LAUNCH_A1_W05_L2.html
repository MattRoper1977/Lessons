Stage 3 · Starter

Starter · 4-beat model and retrieval

Whiteboard 4-beat sequence

Beat 1: Beat 1 · Set the controls: same potato, equal cylinder size, same soak time, same blotting — only sucrose concentration changes.
Beat 2: Beat 2 · Weigh BEFORE soaking: once a dry-blotted cylinder enters the tube, you can never go back and get its initial mass.
Beat 3: Beat 3 · Blot gently and consistently after soaking: surface liquid clinging to the outside is not water that crossed membranes.
Beat 4: Beat 4 · Calculate signed % change: (final − initial) ÷ INITIAL × 100 — keep the + or − sign!

Ready: step 0 of 4

Class fingers or thumbs vote

Before we put any potato cylinder into a test tube, why is it essential to blot and weigh each cylinder FIRST and label its tube?

Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers (or point), then tap to check.

Retrieval 1: Two identical potato cylinders start at 4.00 g. One goes into pure water (becomes 4.40 g); one goes into 0.8 mol/dm³ sucrose (becomes 3.60 g). What convinces you water moved?

Clue

Compare final mass with initial mass.

Answer: The cylinder in pure water gained 0.40 g (+10%) as water entered by osmosis, while the cylinder in strong sucrose lost 0.40 g (−10%) as water left.

Retrieval 2: Why must we blot every cylinder the same way on paper towel before reweighing?

Clue

Surface droplets distort final mass.

Answer: To remove excess surface liquid clinging to the outside without squeezing water out of the cells, so only water inside the tissue is weighed.

Retrieval 3: What does a minus sign (e.g. −10.0%) in your percentage change column mean?

Clue

Keep the + or − sign.

Answer: It means the final mass was lower than the initial mass — the potato cylinder lost mass because net water movement was OUT of the cells.

Stage 4 · I do 1

I do 1 · Setting up the core practical method and control variables

I do 1 · Setting up the core practical method and control variables

Step 1: Step 1 (Cut equal cylinders): Cut potato cylinders of the same diameter and length so surface area and diffusion distance are controlled.

  1. Step 1: Step 1 (Cut equal cylinders): Cut potato cylinders of the same diameter and length so surface area and diffusion distance are controlled.
  2. Step 2: Step 2 (Blot, weigh, label): Gently blot each cylinder, weigh its INITIAL mass in grams on a balance, and place it into a labelled tube (0.0 to 1.0 mol/dm³ sucrose).
  3. Step 3: Step 3 (Control the soak): Leave all cylinders for the same time at the same temperature in the same volume of solution. Only sucrose concentration differs.
  4. Step 4: Step 4 (Blot and reweigh): Remove each cylinder, blot consistently the same way on paper towel to remove surface droplets, and record its FINAL mass.

Worked example 1

Worked example 1 (Variables & Method): Independent variable (what we change) = concentration of sucrose solution (mol/dm³). Dependent variable (what we measure/calculate) = change in mass / percentage change in mass (%). Control variables (kept the same) = cylinder length/surface area, same potato variety, soak time, temperature, and consistent blotting.

Stage 6 · I do 2

I do 2 · Calculating signed percentage change in mass and explaining the trend

I do 2 · Calculating signed percentage change in mass and explaining the trend

Step 1: Write the formula: Percentage change = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ INITIAL mass × 100.

  1. Step 1: Write the formula: Percentage change = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ INITIAL mass × 100.
  2. Step 2: Worked calculation (Gain in pure water): Initial = 4.20 g, Final = 4.62 g. Change = 4.62 − 4.20 = +0.42 g. % change = (+0.42 ÷ 4.20) × 100 = +10.0%.
  3. Step 3: Worked calculation (Loss in 0.8 mol/dm³ sucrose): Initial = 4.00 g, Final = 3.60 g. Change = 3.60 − 4.00 = −0.40 g. % change = (−0.40 ÷ 4.00) × 100 = −10.0%.
  4. Step 4: Link sign to osmosis: + means net water moved IN (outside more dilute); − means net water moved OUT (outside more concentrated); 0% means matching concentration.

Worked example 2

Worked example 2 (Signed % Change): A cylinder in 0.8 mol/dm³ sucrose starts at 4.00 g and finishes at 3.60 g. Step 1: Change = 3.60 − 4.00 = −0.40 g (keep the minus sign!). Step 2: Divide by INITIAL mass (4.00 g): −0.40 ÷ 4.00 = −0.10. Step 3: Multiply by 100 = −10.0%. Biological meaning: the external solution was more concentrated than the cell cytoplasm, so water left the cells by osmosis.

Stage 7 · We do 2

We do 2 · method order and data table

Order the scientific method or reasoning steps

  1. Divide that mass change by the INITIAL mass (never the final mass).
  2. Subtract the initial mass from the final mass (final − initial) and write the + or − sign clearly.
  3. Multiply by 100 to convert to a signed percentage (+% for gain, −% for loss) and round to 1 decimal place.
  4. Explain the result using osmosis: state which side was more dilute and which way net water moved across the partially permeable cell membranes.

Use move up and Move down to place the steps in order, then check.

Supported exit ticket

1. Which mass do you divide by to find percentage change: initial mass or final mass?

Expected point

The initial (starting) mass.

2. What does a negative percentage change (like −10%) mean happened to the potato's mass?

Expected point

It lost mass (water moved out of the potato).

Independent exit ticket

1. A potato cylinder goes from 5.00 g to 4.50 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass, including the sign.

Expected point

(4.50 − 5.00) ÷ 5.00 × 100 = −0.50 ÷ 5.00 × 100 = −10%.

2. Name two control variables that must be kept the same in the osmosis core practical.

Expected point

Any two from: cylinder size/length, same potato, soak time, temperature, consistent blotting.

Aspire exit ticket

1. Explain why a potato cylinder in concentrated sugar solution loses mass.

Expected point

The water concentration outside is lower than inside the potato cells, so net water leaves the cells by osmosis through their partially permeable membranes.

2. Why can inconsistent blotting between cylinders ruin the validity of the percentage change results?

Expected point

Leaving extra surface droplets adds unmeasured liquid mass to the final reading, while squeezing too hard removes intracellular water, distorting the true osmotic mass change.
