I do · 5 min

Watch the full model. Point to the step that answers today’s question.

Words

WordMeaningExample
stem cella cell that can divide and form specialised cell typesStem cells are being studied for possible medical uses.
benefita possible helpful resultA possible benefit would be replacing damaged cells.
riska possible harmful result or uncertain effectA study must consider a risk to volunteers.
evidenceinformation that tests or supports a claimThe trial needs evidence of both benefit and harm.

Full model: A fictional proposal may replace damaged cells. A possible harm is unknown. A careful study must measure both benefit and risk; possible use is not proven treatment.

Show me: Show me: is “it might help” evidence that the treatment already works?

Answer: No. It is a possible benefit; evidence from an appropriate study is needed.

Model one complete example; check every pupil’s response to the show-me question.

We do · 7 min

Complete the missing step in the part-done model. Speak, then match the cards.

One-minute reset: use the agreed move, then return to one step.

Part-done model: Possible benefit: may replace damaged cells. Possible risk: ________. Evidence needed: a fair check of benefit and harm.

Speak first: Speaking: “The proposal may …, but the evidence must show …”

F · support: Give three colour-coded headings and a fictional benefit/risk card; pupil supplies the missing risk and one request for evidence.

M · explain: Explain why a possible benefit is not evidence that a treatment works.

H · stretch: Ask whether “support more research” and “approve a treatment for use” require the same strength of evidence; justify the distinction.

Which panel statement is not based on evidence? Choose it, then state the better question.

  • The trial records how much damaged tissue changes.
  • A treatment may help a damaged tissue.
  • It must work because many people want it.
  • A study could find a harmful effect.

Ready to match.

Wait five seconds, gesture, then cue if needed. Support the route without supplying the missing answer.

Choose one answer

Independent · 8 min

A fictional stem-cell proposal lacks safety results. Decide what to test next.

One-minute reset: use the agreed move, then return to one step.

Talk before write: Speaking: “I would recommend … because …; I would also check …”

You can plan in any language.

F: Sort the fictional proposal statements under possible benefit, possible risk and evidence needed. A possible benefit is …; a possible risk is …

M: Explain why a proposal needs evidence of both effect and harm before a decision. The claim is not yet proven because we need evidence that …

H: Evaluate whether to support further research with a qualified reason, risk and evidence request. I would support/review further research if …; however, before treatment use I would need …

Go deeper: If a careful new study showed no improvement, how should the panel change its recommendation and why?

Early-finisher stretch — Research panel minute: Separate further research from routine treatment; explain the evidence needed for each.

Show it your way: point, sign, speak, draw, type, write, use a scribe or a staff-captured photo.
