DNA, genes and chromosomes

Learning goal

I am learning to show how a gene is part of DNA and how DNA is packed into chromosomes.

Today I can

  • Use the words DNA, gene and chromosome in the right way. Label each one on a simple string model.
  • Say that a gene is a section of DNA. It carries the instruction for one protein. A gene is not a whole cord and not a whole chromosome.
  • Tell one chromosome apart from the genome. A chromosome is one packaged DNA molecule. The genome is all of the DNA of an organism.

40 minutes · take a pause when you need one. You may pass on a personal example.

At Independent, one adult supports access to Supported while the other holds the group; swap across the week. Wait, gesture, cue, model one step, then try a fresh step independently. Give VF, then one precise NS+; wait for a real pupil response R and // edit. Use ? for read-back. WS and I describe actual support; E requires target-matched evidence.

During We do, the lesson teacher is the named audience. Offer an optional request for reading help, fewer pieces, clearer labels, adult-directed handling or thinking time. Try the change and check its effect. Passing and private requests are valid.

Before the lesson · quick read

Before the lesson, glance through the books for a few minutes. Notice feedback from the last lesson, pupil responses and where the learning stopped.

Ask any cover teacher or TA what was actually taught, who was present and what changed for individual pupils.

Check the roll against the books. Pupils new to the class have no prior evidence; start from what every pupil shows now.

An earlier NS+ is history. Do not chase, back-mark or retrospectively close it. Begin fresh feedback from this lesson.

After the check, one read shapes the next lesson. Read each success criterion with its access support, choose the next route, then teach; do not reopen earlier feedback.

Preparation and practical safety

Use the supplied models, cards and drawings. This lesson uses paper and screen models. If a string-and-clip model is used, take a short cord and large blunt clips on a tray, keep the cord away from necks, and offer a picture alternative. No living material or chemicals are handled.

Destination link (Launch): DNA, genes and chromosomes are GCSE Biology ground. They sit behind genetic testing, inherited conditions and genome research, and lead on to Level 3 science, nursing and laboratory apprenticeships.

Choose a route at each task using what the pupil shows. Switching adds no task, record or label.

Use paper models or teacher-authorised apparatus. No tasting, personal genetic disclosure, physical contact or unplanned chemical handling. Offer adult-directed handling and a seated route.

Science staff guidance

Beside the You do, mark I (independent), S (supported) or C (choice): the level of independence, not the effort.

Give time to start → point to the relevant place → rephrase → offer a small prompt or word bank → reduce to a limited choice. Never straight to telling the answer.

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Tell the teacher the barrier: reading, vocabulary, processing, regulation, writing or a science misconception. Avoid prompting through every question or dictating the final answer.

If behind

60-second recap: mitosis gives each new nucleus a matching set of chromosomes, chromosomes are found in the nucleus, and a new treatment claim needs evidence about benefit and risk. Pause and recall one idea you already know; no particular old question is required. In an exam-style response, follow the instruction, use accurate subject terms and connect a reason to the given evidence. If an idea is unclear, use today’s clues and ask for one small prompt.

A pupil who missed last lesson: give the Arrival reminders and today's must-know words, not the missed lesson's work.

Arrival and Starter

Arrival: answer these four short questions in the book.

  1. What is one reason a claim for a new stem-cell treatment needs evidence?
  2. Which structure holds the matching sets of chromosomes after mitosis?
  3. Where are chromosomes found in a eukaryotic animal cell?
  4. Link to today: a DNA cord has one short section marked with a clip. Is the marked section a gene, or is the whole cord a gene?

Starter: Which picture shows a gene: the short marked section or the whole cord? One short answer in the book.

You do routes

Independent is preferred: work in the book without answer-giving support. Supported uses a small prompt beside the same task. Choice uses limited options, matching or selecting and then a short scientific sentence, label or reason in the book. Aspire is the challenge for every route.

Mark scheme · one point per mark

Independent

Explain why a gene is part of DNA and not a separate material. [2 marks]

  1. A gene is a section of a DNA molecule (1 mark)
  2. The section codes for a specific protein, so a gene is DNA and not another material (1 mark)

Compare a gene, a chromosome and the genome. Use DNA, section, protein and set in your answer. Support one point with the string model or Reference A. [6 marks]

  1. DNA is the molecule that stores genetic instructions (1 mark)
  2. A gene is a short section of DNA that codes for a specific protein (1 mark)
  3. A chromosome is a long DNA molecule packaged with proteins (1 mark)
  4. A chromosome holds many genes (1 mark)
  5. The genome is all of an organism’s DNA, including every chromosome (1 mark)
  6. Evidence from the string model or Reference A, with the limit that models are not to scale (1 mark)

Explain why one chromosome is not the whole genome. Use an example. [2 marks]

  1. A chromosome is one long DNA molecule packaged with proteins (1 mark)
  2. The genome is all of the organism’s DNA, so it holds every chromosome (1 mark)

Distinguish DNA from a gene. Say what each is and give an example from the string model. [3 marks]

  1. DNA is the molecule that stores genetic instructions (1 mark)
  2. A gene is a short section of DNA that codes for a protein (1 mark)
  3. A suitable example from the string model, such as the clipped part of the cord (1 mark)

Compare a chromosome with a gene. State what each is and one difference in size. [3 marks]

  1. A chromosome is a long DNA molecule packaged with proteins (1 mark)
  2. A gene is a section of DNA (1 mark)
  3. A chromosome is much bigger and holds many genes (1 mark)

Aspire

Compare a gene, a chromosome and the genome. Explain how each relates to DNA. [4 marks]

  1. A gene is a short section of DNA (1 mark)
  2. A chromosome is a long DNA molecule packaged with proteins (1 mark)
  3. The genome is all of the DNA of the organism (1 mark)
  4. A gene sits inside a chromosome, which sits inside the genome (1 mark)

A model labels one chromosome as the whole genome and the clip as the gene. Correct two labels or claims, justify each edit with evidence about size and role, and compare with the nested picture. [8 marks]

  1. A chromosome is only one part of the genome (1 mark)
  2. Justified: the genome is all of the DNA, so it holds every chromosome (1 mark)
  3. The clip only marks the gene (1 mark)
  4. Justified: the gene is the DNA section beneath the clip (1 mark)
  5. A gene is a short section of DNA that codes for a specific protein (1 mark)
  6. A chromosome holds many genes (1 mark)
  7. The nested picture shows gene in chromosome in genome (1 mark)
  8. The model is not to scale, so size is shown by position and not by measure (1 mark)

A diagram shows a gene as the biggest frame with DNA inside it. Explain why this is wrong and correct it. [3 marks]

  1. DNA is the molecule and a gene is only a section of it (1 mark)
  2. So a gene cannot hold the DNA (1 mark)
  3. Correct: the gene sits inside the DNA (1 mark)

A pupil says a gene is a protein. Use the string model to explain what a gene is and what it codes for. [3 marks]

  1. A gene is a section of DNA (1 mark)
  2. It codes for a specific protein (1 mark)
  3. The gene is not the protein; it holds the instruction for it (1 mark)

Explain what is lost if the whole genome is called one gene. Include the section, the package and the whole set. [4 marks]

  1. A gene is only a short section of DNA (1 mark)
  2. A chromosome is a long package holding many genes (1 mark)
  3. The genome is the whole DNA set holding every chromosome (1 mark)
  4. Calling it all one gene hides differences in size and role (1 mark)
