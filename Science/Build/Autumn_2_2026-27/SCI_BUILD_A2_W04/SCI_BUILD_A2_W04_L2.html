Open the evidence cards and tasks

  • Granite is hard. It will not crumble under boots.
  • Chalk is soft. It crumbles into bits or dust.
  • Slate splits into flat sheets and keeps rain out.

2 A rock that fits

  • A busy step needs a hard rock: granite fits.
  • A roof tile needs flat sheets that keep rain out: slate fits.
  • A board stick needs a soft rock that leaves dust: chalk fits.

3 A rock that fails

  • Chalk fails on a step because it crumbles under feet.
  • Sandstone fails on a roof because it soaks up water.
  • Granite fails as board chalk because it is too hard and scratches.

  • Granite fits a step. It fails as board chalk.
  • Chalk fits a board. It fails on a step.
  • A rock can be good for one job and bad for another.

Method strip · stick in your book
  1. Set out the job cards and rock cards on the desk. Keep any samples on a tray.
  2. Choose one card. With the adult, find the job and the rock feature. STOP AND CHECK with the adult. Then go on.
  3. Look at the card words. Or look at an inspected sample. You can point instead. Both ways count.
  4. Write what the card shows. Give one feature clue for a rock that fits and a rock that fails.

Put out just the kit for this stage. If you cannot take part safely, watch the teacher show it. In your book, write what you saw. Say what you can tell from it.

After the task, write what you saw. Say what it tells you about today's goal. Add the steps or a diagram only if they are part of the task.

Planned photograph point: after the picks are recorded and before the pupil explains why each rock fits or fails. Card evidence is identified as card evidence; it never proves a real test was run.

Planned adaptations

Cognition and learning: Arrival and independent: show one job card at a time; point to the deciding feature before moving on; fade the prompt once the pupil continues.

Communication and interaction: We do and check: read one option aloud, allow silent planning and accept point, sign, drawing or exact scribing; wait before revealing.

SEMH: Arrival and review: use a predictable first–then cue, a private check-in and an agreed pause/re-entry. Give one quiet next step with no public scores.

Sensory and physical: I do and independent: use projected diagrams or large adult-prepared cards; no forced handling; never stand on a sample; staff handle samples when required.

EAL: Starter and model: rehearse fit, fail, crumble and compare with the diagram; permit first-language planning and a because stem. EAL is not SEN.

EEF strategy: explicit instruction; cognitive and metacognitive strategies; scaffolding; flexible grouping; technology used where it improves access.
