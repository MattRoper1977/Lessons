W8A
Sugar Evidence: Read the Label		Four fictional or photocopied nutrition-label cards on the same per-100 g basis; number line; results strip; pencil; optional clean empty packets as visual references.Nothing is eaten, opened or offered as a reward. Use clean empty packages or cards only. Check allergy and safeguarding arrangements even though there is no tasting; keep discussion about labels, not pupils' bodies or food choices.Use the in-page label lab and preserve the ordered values plus the pupil's bounded conclusion on the print route.
Use large-print cards, symbols and a floor number line. A pupil may point or direct an adult to place each value; exact adult scribing is valid.		Four sugar values in correct order with g per 100 g, one evidence sentence and one statement of what the comparison cannot show.
W8B
Body Science Checkpoint		Skeleton or labelled diagram; vertebrate/invertebrate cards; safe card-and-split-pin arm model; nutrition/body-job cards; food-group mats; simple food-chain cards; tiered checkpoint sheet.Teach bodies and food on models, diagrams and supplied cards, never by judging a pupil's body, diet or home. No food is eaten. Check split pins and small parts, offer a no-touch route and avoid public scores or rank order.Use the in-page four-strand checkpoint. The same conceptual evidence is produced through card placement, explanation and next-strand choice.
A pupil may point, sign, operate one switch, direct an adult, use eye gaze or have exact words scribed. Keep the science demand while changing access.		One observable response from each strand, one because connection, the pupil's chosen next strand and an adult witness note naming independence and prompts.
W9A
Rock Evidence Lab: Look Before You Label		Six teacher-pre-screened coded educational rock specimens or macro cards; white trays; hand lenses; feature tags; two hoops or sorting mats; Rock Comparison Ticket.Use only pre-inspected educational samples. Keep them on trays and below shoulder height. No throwing, tasting, smelling, grinding, scratching or deliberate breaking. Wash hands after handling.Use the in-page coded-feature lab and record two specimen codes, two observations and one grouping rule.
Use macro image cards, point while an adult moves a specimen, or direct the whole inspection. Touching rock is not required.		A Rock Comparison Ticket with two codes, two observed properties, one similarity, one difference and one explicit grouping rule.
W9B
Build a Rock Sorting Key		Coded rock specimens or macro cards from W9A; yes/no mats; criterion cards for crystals, grains, bands/planes and holes; Mystery T; Sorting Key Case Card.Keep pre-screened samples on trays. This is observation and card grouping only: no scratch, water, drop or break tests. A no-touch macro-card route is equivalent.Use the in-page fixed-criterion key, then record the mystery placement and a second possible criterion.
Point or say/sign while an adult moves cards. The pupil owns the criterion and every placement decision.		A Sorting Key Case Card with one exact question, all reference placements, defended Mystery T placement and one complete regrouping rule.
W10A
Rock Hardness: Mark or Groove?		Two teacher-pretested coded dry rock samples; stable trays; secured blunt scratch jig or teacher demonstration; fixed guide; wipes; hand lens; result card; eye protection where required by local assessment.Follow the school's current rock-testing risk assessment. No unknown, sharp, dusty or crumbling samples; no hammering, breaking, tasting, smelling or dust blowing. An adult controls the secured tool. Stop immediately on flaking or dust and wash hands.Use the in-page evidence gate and supplied representative mark/wipe/groove observations. Label them as model data, not results from the class samples.
Direct the adult tester, use macro before/after cards or interpret the fixed data. Handling the rock or tool is never required.		A Rock Hardness Method Card with prediction, changed sample, controls, after-wipe observation and a relative conclusion within the stated method.
W10B
Hardness Evidence Lab		Four teacher-pretested coded dry samples; stable individual trays; secured blunt scratch jig or teacher demonstration; fixed guide; wipes; hand lens; four-row result table; eye protection where assessed.Follow the local risk assessment. No hammering, breaking, tasting, smelling or dust blowing. An adult controls the secured tool. Stop on flaking, crumbling or dust; preserve the note and switch to fixed-data parity. Wash hands.Use the in-page four-code matrix with A deep, B shallow, C faint and D no groove after wiping. Identify it as representative model data.
Direct the adult through the method or use the printed fixed observations. Recorder, observer and conclusion roles are complete participation routes.		A complete four-code raw-results table, one preserved repeat/check note, a relative comparison using at least two codes and one method or sample limitation.
W11A
Permeability: Surface or Through?		Cross-section picture cards, water-route arrows, observation cards, three-zone evidence sheet and pencil or communication aid.Core learning uses dry cards. If staff show a tiny water demonstration, use a stable tray and needle-free dropper; pupils do not handle unknown rocks, build rigs, taste, smell, scratch or break samples.Use the embedded evidence sorter and capture the frozen route locally; no account, upload or live website is needed.
Use three large pictures labelled TOP, INSIDE and BELOW. An adult moves arrows exactly as the pupil directs and scribes exact words.		Six classified observations, one labelled through-route, one because explanation and one method-check decision.
W11B
Water Through Rock Lab		Staff-built sealed rigs; pre-inspected prepared sample pieces; stable trays; 5 mL needle-free pipettes or syringes; marked collectors; 120-second timer; wipes; result sheet.Only staff build and approve rigs. No hammering, chipping, scratching, licking, tasting, smelling or dust blowing. Keep samples on trays, contain spills, stop for leaks/damage/flaking/dust and wash hands. A director or fixed-data route is full participation.Use the embedded fixed records: A = 0.5 mL and B = 3.0 mL after the same 5 mL and 120 seconds. Complete the same observation and comparison table.
Use printed method and result cards. A pupil sequences the method and directs an adult scribe; no handling is required.		Method checklist, surface/underside observation, collector values with mL, valid/fault decision, bounded comparison and one method-control question for W13.
W12A
Rock Jobs: Which Property Matters?		Four supplied job briefs, W10 scratch and W11 water evidence icons, property-question grid and pencil or communication aid.Use cards or pre-screened lightweight samples on trays. No real construction, site access, heavy lifting, scratching, wetting, breaking, tasting, smelling or collecting is required.Use the embedded job-property lab and save the frozen question chain locally; no account, upload or live website is needed.
Use one large job picture at a time and fixed property choices. An adult scribes the pupil's exact because link.		Four job-property questions, two because explanations, one needs-another-check decision and one model limit.
W12B
Choose Rock for the Job		Fictional job briefs, candidate evidence matrix, decision sheet, audience question card and pencil or communication aid.Use cards or sealed lightweight display samples only. No real construction, heavy lifting, scraping, wet testing, breaking, climbing or site work is required.Use the embedded matrix and save the before-and-after choice locally; no account, upload or live website is needed.
Use two candidate cards and one enlarged brief at a time. An adult scribes exact pupil reasoning and the audience response.		Brief criteria, two-candidate comparison, chosen candidate, relevant because evidence, one limit, one audience question and visible response.
W13A
Fair Test Planner: Change One Thing		Control cards, diagram of staff-built sealed rig, method planner, blank final results table, pilot checklist and pencil or communication aid.Planning uses dry cards only. Pupils do not cut, seal, build, lift, scratch or prepare rigs or samples. Physical piloting happens only after local risk assessment, staff pretesting and teacher approval.Use the embedded planner and save the local method card. No camera, account, network, upload or pupil identity is used.
Use large CHANGE / MEASURE / SAME / PILOT cards while an adult scribes the pupil's exact plan and selected access route.		W14 question, prediction, changed variable, measure with unit, at least three controls, repeat plan, blank results table, W13B pilot checks and safety stop.
W13B
Method Pilot: Test the Test		One clearly labelled staff-prepared practice rig or fixed pilot log; stable tray; 5 mL needle-free tool; 120-second timer; collector; wipes; method V1/V2 sheet; readiness card.Only staff build and approve a practice rig. No final A/B samples are used. No hammering, chipping, scratching, licking, tasting, smelling or dust blowing. Stop for leaks, instability, damage, flaking, dust or unclear measurement. The fixed pilot-log/director route is full participation.Use fixed pilot P1/P2 records: P1 edge leak and missing unit; V2 adds seal check and mL heading; P2 seal holds. Make the same revision and readiness decision.
Use printed pilot cards and method V1/V2 strips. An adult moves or scribes exactly as directed; no water or rock handling is required.		Pilot checklist, one procedure observation or fault, method V1 and visibly revised V2, safety/access decision, W14 readiness card and a statement of what the pilot cannot conclude.

Shared safeguards

  • No pupil is forced to handle equipment, speak publicly or be recorded.
  • Pointing, directing an adult, signing, drawing, typing and exact adult scribing can preserve the same conceptual evidence.
  • Retries are expected and never scored.
  • In-page state exists only in the current tab and clears on reload.
  • All links are local; no account, upload or live website is required.
