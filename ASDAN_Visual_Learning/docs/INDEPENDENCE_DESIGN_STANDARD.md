# ASDAN independence design standard

## Purpose

Visual scaffolding is successful only when it helps a pupil begin, continue, check and finish the real task with less adult direction than before. Attractive screens, completed clicks and high rehearsal scores are not the outcome.

The standard applies to all BUILD, GROW and LAUNCH ASDAN visual teaching.

## The independence handover

Every visual activity ends with five visible elements.

### 1. The real task

The screen states the physical, spoken, written, practical, project or portfolio task that follows. It labels the interaction as rehearsal so pupils do not mistake clicking for completing the lesson requirement.

### 2. A first step

The first action must be available without rereading the whole slide. It should normally be a verb plus an object:

- choose one real example;
- place the first step;
- collect the approved equipment;
- mark the income;
- identify one user;
- point to one hazard;
- locate one existing item of evidence.

### 3. Stop-and-check conditions

The pupil needs a visible way to know whether to continue, adapt or request support. Checks should be observable and finite:

- all required parts are present;
- the total balances;
- one variable changed;
- the sequence is safe and complete;
- the audience and purpose match;
- the source actually supports the claim;
- the work has a beginning, controlled process and result.

“Do your best” and “make it good” are not checks.

### 4. The least-help ladder

Use help in this order, while keeping reasonable adjustments available:

1. look again at the frozen visual or model;
2. use one cue, icon, sentence stem or checklist item;
3. request one specific prompt;
4. use the teacher, TA, assessor, responsible adult, safety or approved access route.

The ladder is not a test of worth. Some tasks require an adult by design.

### 5. Structured reflection

Reflection should ask about the decision or method, not demand a broad emotional disclosure. Useful prompts include:

- What did you notice that changed your choice?
- Which step prevented an error?
- What did the second run show?
- What support helped access the task?
- What will you do first next time?
- Where is the authentic evidence, or is it not yet available?

## Scaffold fading

### Keep

Keep information that remains part of the real task:

- safety rules;
- approved criteria;
- access tools;
- a formula or reference table;
- allergen information;
- equipment instructions;
- communication or emergency routes.

These are not dependence. They are task infrastructure.

### Fade

Fade temporary teaching support:

- the teacher’s completed example;
- arrows that reveal every next move;
- pre-selected evidence;
- model wording intended only to show structure;
- an animated route once pupils can use the static reference;
- hints that answer the deciding question.

### Do not remove reasonable adjustments

Reading support, symbols, alternative response routes, scribing, communication aids, adapted equipment, extra processing time and adult safety supervision are access. They should not disappear because a pupil has become more independent.

## Pathway calibration

### BUILD

Independence means the pupil can make one clear decision, state or point to the reason and begin the real task. Do not require a long written explanation to prove independence.

### GROW

Independence means the pupil can commit a prediction, control one variable, compare evidence and choose a justified next action.

### LAUNCH

Independence means the pupil can investigate, locate authentic evidence or honestly state that it is not yet available, reason from it and use the authorised next route. It does not mean self-awarding a level or evidence state.

## Adult prompts

Prefer prompts that return responsibility:

- “Which part of the frozen visual is relevant?”
- “What have you already checked?”
- “What is the first unsafe or missing step?”
- “Which one variable are you changing?”
- “What evidence actually exists?”
- “Which authorised route does this decision need?”

Avoid completing the reflection, selecting the evidence or inventing a partner response for the pupil.

## Observable implementation checks

A lesson-level visual passes this standard only when:

- the screen and real task are clearly separated;
- the first independent action is visible;
- completion conditions are specific;
- drag is not the only interaction;
- keyboard and touch routes work;
- static and reduced-motion routes retain all meaning;
- no interaction claims qualification achievement;
- no personal data has to be entered;
- the teacher/access route is visible;
- reset clears all temporary state;
- reloading leaves no pupil data behind.
