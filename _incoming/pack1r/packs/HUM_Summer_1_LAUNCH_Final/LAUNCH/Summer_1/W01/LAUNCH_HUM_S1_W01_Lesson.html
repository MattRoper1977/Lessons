LAUNCH · Sum1·W1 · 4 minutes

Arrival

Choose one route and answer four retrieval questions.

Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

1. Resource

Which is a fuel resource: coal or a chart title?

Help: A fuel can be burned for energy.

Answer: Coal.

2. Emissions

Does emissions mean gases released or distances travelled?

Help: Think about gases entering the atmosphere.

Answer: Gases released.

3. Read values

Which is larger: 384 or 373?

Help: Compare the tens after the shared hundreds.

Answer: 384.

4. Source check

Which helps check online evidence: its author and date or only its colour?

Help: Think about who made it and when.

Answer: Its author and date.

1. Resources

Name one fossil fuel used for energy.

Help: Coal is one example.

Answer: Coal, oil or natural gas.

2. Evidence

Why record the date of an online dataset?

Help: Figures may refer to earlier years or be revised.

Answer: To know the time period and which release is being used.

3. Calculate

Find the difference between 384 and 373.

Help: Subtract 373 from 384.

Answer: 11.

4. Scope

Does a UK figure automatically describe the whole world?

Help: Compare a country with the globe.

Answer: No; the geographical coverage differs.

1. Explain a link

How can burning a fossil fuel affect climate?

Help: Connect combustion, greenhouse gases and warming.

Answer: It releases greenhouse gases, including carbon dioxide, which contribute to warming.

2. Audit

Name two checks before using a chart as evidence.

Help: Consider author, date, units, scope and method.

Answer: Any two of author, date, unit, geographical scope or method.

3. Quantify

A value falls from 384 to 373. Estimate the percentage fall.

Help: Use 11 ÷ 384 × 100.

Answer: About 2.9%, or 3% rounded.

4. Limit a claim

Why does a fall in annual emissions not prove climate change has stopped?

Help: Consider emissions still occurring, accumulated gases and global scope.

Answer: Annual emissions remain positive; greenhouse gases accumulate, and a UK series is not the global climate record.

LAUNCH · Sum1·W1 · 5 minutes

I Do

Watch how I audit the source and describe the figures.

Teacher think-aloud

Who: the UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ). Published: 5 February 2026. Measure: net UK territorial greenhouse gas emissions, in MtCO₂e. The rounded totals fall from 384 in 2023 to 373 in 2024: a fall of 11 MtCO₂e, about 3%. This tells us about emissions inside the UK, not the world total.

Listen and track the evidence. The next stage is your turn to try.

LAUNCH · Sum1·W1 · 4 minutes

I Do 2

Watch how I link resource use, emissions and warming without overclaiming.

Teacher think-aloud

People use fossil fuels for electricity, heat and transport. Burning them releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas that contributes to warming. DESNZ attributes much of the UK fall in 2024 to lower gas and coal use in electricity supply and industry. But this UK dataset alone cannot establish global temperature change or count all emissions linked to imported goods.

Independent

Explain resource use and climate change using two figures and one limitation.

Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

Supported task

Use the frame: “DESNZ reports UK territorial emissions of ___ in 2023 and ___ in 2024, measured in ___. Using less ___ can reduce emissions. Greenhouse gases contribute to ___. This dataset does not show ___.” Say it, write it or direct an exact scribe.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.

Standard task

Write or present a short geographical explanation: identify the source and date; compare two figures; explain a resource-use link to emissions and climate change; end with one limitation of the dataset.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.

Stretch task

Evaluate the claim: “Falling UK emissions show resource choices can matter, but do not prove the climate problem is solved.” Use two figures, a causal explanation and a geographical limitation. Distinguish emissions from temperature.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.
