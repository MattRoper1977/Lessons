My place, the UK and the wider world

Objective: Locate my place the UK and the wider world on maps.

Locate one place you already know on a map.

Today I can

  • Locate Middlesbrough in north-east England.
  • Locate the UK in Europe on a world map.
  • Explain what changes when a map shows a wider area.

A town planner uses maps at different scales to connect a local site with a region. Choose the map view that would help plan a local visit.

GROW · Sum1·W1 · 4 minutes

Arrival

Choose one route and answer four retrieval questions.

Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

1. Map purpose

Does a map help show places or tell the time?

Help: Think about finding a place.

Answer: It helps show places.

2. Country

Choose a country: England or Europe.

Help: Europe is a continent.

Answer: England.

3. Continent

Choose a continent: Europe or Middlesbrough.

Help: Middlesbrough is a town.

Answer: Europe.

4. Local detail

Which view could show a school gate: a local plan or a world map?

Help: Look for the view of a smaller area.

Answer: A local plan.

1. Locate

What does locate mean when reading a map?

Help: Think find and show.

Answer: Find and show where a place is.

2. Country and continent

Is the UK a country or a continent?

Help: Europe is the continent containing the UK.

Answer: A country made up of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

3. Direction

What does north-east combine?

Help: Use the two words in the direction.

Answer: North and east.

4. Scale choice

Why might a world map be unhelpful for finding a school entrance?

Help: Think about how much local detail fits.

Answer: It covers a very large area so that local detail is too small.

1. Different views

Why could two maps of the same place look different?

Help: Consider area shown, purpose and detail.

Answer: Different scales, purposes or selected features.

2. Parts of the UK

Name the four countries of the UK.

Help: England is one; Northern Ireland is another.

Answer: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

3. Misconception

Correct: “Europe is another name for the UK.”

Help: Use country and continent.

Answer: The UK is a country in Europe; Europe includes many countries.

4. Wider view

What happens to local detail when a larger area fits on the same page?

Help: Think of zooming out.

Answer: Local detail becomes smaller and some is omitted.

I Do

Watch how I move from Middlesbrough to the UK and Europe.

Teacher think-aloud

I start with the local label: Middlesbrough. On the UK view its marker is in north-east England. I zoom out: the UK lies off the north-west coast of mainland Europe. Europe is a continent. England is one of the four countries of the UK; the UK is not a continent.

Listen and track the evidence. The next stage is your turn to try.

GROW · Sum1·W1 · 4 minutes

I Do 2

Watch how I explain the change from a local view to a world view.

Teacher think-aloud

A local plan can show school entrances and nearby streets. A world map shows countries, continents and oceans but cannot show our school entrance at this size. When I show a wider area on the same size page, local detail becomes smaller. I choose the view to suit the question.

Independent

Locate your place, the UK and Europe and describe one change of scale.

Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

Supported task

Point to Middlesbrough on the UK map and to the UK on the world map. Identify Europe. Complete aloud: “My place is in ___. The UK is in ___.” Use the labels or ask for an exact scribe.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.

Standard task

Locate Middlesbrough, the UK and Europe on the supplied maps. Draw a small location chain. Write or say two sentences: where your place is and what changes when you zoom out.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.

Stretch task

Locate your place within England, the UK and Europe. Explain why a local plan is more useful than a world map for finding a school entrance. Identify one limitation of these simplified maps.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.
