BUILD · Sum1·W1 · 4 minutes

Arrival

Choose one route and answer four retrieval questions.

Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

1. Find a place

Which place is for learning: school or river?

Help: Think about where lessons happen.

Answer: School.

2. Use a symbol

The key says S = school. What does S mean?

Help: Read or point to the words after the equals sign.

Answer: School.

3. Use direction

Point to the right side of this page.

Help: An adult can model left, then you show the opposite side.

Answer: The right side of the page.

4. Name a feature

Choose a natural feature: river or library.

Help: A river is flowing water; a library is built by people.

Answer: River.

1. Recall a place

Name a place people visit near a school.

Help: Think of a park, library, shop or bus stop.

Answer: Accept a suitable local place; check the actual locality with the adult.

2. Read a key

Why does a map have a key?

Help: Think about an unfamiliar symbol.

Answer: To explain what the symbols mean.

3. Choose direction

A library is on the right of a school on a plan. Which way would you point?

Help: Keep the plan the same way up.

Answer: Right across the page.

4. Sort features

Which is human-made: a river or a bus stop?

Help: Human-made means people built or arranged it.

Answer: Bus stop.

1. Choose evidence

How could you check the name of your school?

Help: Think of a sign, school document or a trusted adult.

Answer: Read the school sign or confirm with a trusted adult.

2. Explain a key

Why can the same square mean different things on different maps?

Help: Read each map’s own key.

Answer: Symbols depend on the key; do not assume their meaning.

3. Describe position

What must stay fixed when we say left or right on a plan?

Help: Imagine turning the page upside down.

Answer: The orientation of the plan, or an agreed viewpoint.

4. Question a plan

Can a classroom plan prove a route is safe outside?

Help: Think about roads, crossings and changes.

Answer: No. An adult must check the actual route and current conditions.

Independent

Name your school and locate two nearby features on your own plan.

Point, speak, sign, draw, write or direct an adult. An adult may read or scribe your exact words. Choose one route; do not complete all three.

Supported task

Name your school to the adult. Draw a school box. Add or point to two nearby features you know. Show one position with above, below, left or right. The adult may scribe your exact words.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.

Standard task

Draw a simple plan of your school and two real nearby features. Add a key. Describe one feature’s position using left, right, near or far. Tell the adult which detail needs checking.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.

Stretch task

Draw and label a school plan with three local features and a key. Explain one position and how you would check it against the real place. Explain why this plan alone cannot confirm a safe walking route.

Before recording: rehearse your idea aloud, sign, point or direct an adult. Check the key words and success criteria.
