LAUNCH Careers Spring 1 Week 4
Following instructions and teamwork
Year A. Spring 1 Week 4, 25–29 January 2027. Friday 29 January 2027, P1. One forty-minute formative lesson for a pair. Six-week term 4 January–12 February; Week 6 assessing. No clock time supplied.

Exact weekly outcome: Following instructions & teamwork.

B91 Spr1·W4 and C91 exact outcome retained. Inherited framework/resource context from row 88 generic. Row 91 gives no minute sequence: authored 5/8/10/12/5 lesson. Same business/order/roles as Week 1; reader and maker enact the checker and preparer roles. Written and spoken instructions, shared meaning check, reset and role swap implement the owner brief. Two six-minute classroom rounds are guides, not a speed target. Supported/Standard/Stretch retained.

Success checks
Use written and spoken instructions in the paired assembly task. Try reader and maker roles with usual access. Review six features of a clear instruction. Record a clarification or check, self-assess five Week 1 expectations and choose a next step.

Same invented workplace
An invented small business prepares simple paper packs for customers. It has a supervisor, a preparer and a checker. It has no name, real employer, location or contact details. This is classroom simulation only.

Prepare
Two large ready-cut cards and two paper strips per pair, plus spares; written steps, role routine, six-item checklist, tier pack, vocabulary, books or blank Type instead record and usual aids. Reset/reuse materials between rounds. Keep the written instructions visible; do not turn the task into a memory or listening test.

Arrival and I Do
0–5: retrieve a Week 1 expectation and Week 3 honest task-record idea; agree roles, access and private help. 5–9: compare the invented unclear instruction “Put stuff there quickly.” with “Put one paper strip beside each large card.” Explain what, how many and where. 9–13: adult models reading one written step aloud; maker says or shows its meaning, does it and checks. Check after model: one strip for each card, beside rather than on top. Keep model and written order visible.

We Do
13–19: rehearse reader/maker routine and the six checks: Goal, Parts, Order, Exact words, Finish check and Help. Apply them to the whole written task, including its header and help line. Record Clear, Needs a change or Not checked yet; accept justified concerns. Each learner chooses one improvement, rather than copying an adult answer. 19–23: private hinge B pause/ask if unclear instead of guessing. Parallel A compare work with the instruction rather than assume it is finished. Re-model and recheck privately.

You Do
23–29 round one: agree reader/maker, read the order and one step aloud, say/show meaning, assemble and compare both packs with the order. 29–35: reset the same paper, swap reader and maker and repeat/check. Each learner records actual roles and help. Pause or reduce repeated output for access, keeping the role swap as an offered opportunity. An adult can partner. Missing roles remain Not seen today; never claim the planned swap happened if it did not.

Review and exit
35–38: short individual reflection “When I didn't understand I…” Or use: Nothing was unclear today; a check that helped was… / I did not try this part yet; the help I need is… Rate the five Week 1 expectations using actual evidence. 38–40: privately check one evidence clue, set one NS, keep the reply and next review time; save each record. Pointing, voice or exact scribe is valid; no public performance or personal history requested.

Supported
Use four numbered steps, a reader/maker role card, model and sentence starts. Hear one step, say or show its meaning, do it and check. Keep the written steps and usual aid. Adult partner or exact scribe valid. Try both roles and keep your own reflection and five ratings.

Standard
Use written and spoken steps to make and check two paper packs, then reset and swap roles. Review the six clear-instruction checks and explain one judgement. Record what you did when a step was unclear, or a check that helped. Rate the five Week 1 expectations.

Stretch
Explain why an instruction could be misunderstood and improve it without changing the order. Compare the planned roles with what happened and explain one teamwork adjustment. Check the result against the order; use the same reflection and five expectations. Extra writing optional.

Model check and fade
After the model ask what goes beside each large card and how many. Key: one paper strip beside each card, not on top. Keep header, steps, model and normal aids; fade answer cues only. Repeat meaning may be a word, selected phrase or demonstration. A usual reading/communication aid is access, not automatically a content prompt.

Paired roles and access
One reads the written step aloud; the maker can also see the steps and use the normal aid. Reading support, agreed communication aid or adult voice can carry the learner’s selected message. Maker checks meaning before acting. Each has a turn in both roles through reset/swap, with adult partner if preferred. No blindfold, hidden instruction, racing or removal of aids.

Adaptations and prompt ladder
Cognition and Learning: four numbered steps, one step at a time, checklist and reduced repetition. Communication and Interaction: rehearsal, wait time, visible order, usual aid and exact scribe/readback. SEMH: predictable roles, private review, pass and pause/return. Sensory and Physical: clear accessible table, seated roles and large print. EAL: plan in any language. 1 Point to place/instruction; 2 ask a short question; 3 rephrase; 4 scaffold/choice; 5 model first step only then return task.

Private help route
Trusted adult or school help route. Point to HELP, write or ask privately. If one adult is busy, use the route to another. Pass, pause and supported return available. No personal employment, family or accident account requested.

Evidence sheet
Evidence Sheet page 1 has the six clear-instruction checks with blank judgements. Page 2 is a blank typed paired-task record, including one instruction improvement and the short clarity reflection. Page 3 has five blank self-ratings/evidence clues and next step/help/reply. Page 4 is the private staff record. Book/usual aid default; all tiers use the same evidence goal.

Evidence and judgement
Keep each learner’s actual reader/maker roles, a task/result check, six checklist judgements and one improvement, a short clarification or check reflection, five Week 1 self-ratings and one next step/reply. Ratings exactly: I did this; I did this with a prompt; I need another try; Not seen today. Normal access differs from supplying thinking. Judge observable instruction use, clarification and teamwork; no personality, reading-fluency or pace score.

Feedback and portfolio
VF, WS, I, NS, //, ?, E and R generic codes. E actual banked work. R after Voice, Audience, NS and reply in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode. One NS might make a question more exact or add a result check. Retain first try and reply. Offer to Communication portfolio; staff confirm any accredited mapping. A classroom simulation does not prove workplace competence or real placement attendance.

Safe delivery
Ready-cut paper only. No tools, machines, money, food, live customers, personal data or deliveries. Keep the area clear. Stop/keep clear/report actual concerns and follow school procedures. Do not create hazards for the task. Listen calmly to concerns, avoid probing or secrecy promises and follow the usual school route; keep private concerns outside shared evidence.

Scope
Only Week 4 authored. Week 5 customer service and Week 6 reflection/assessing follow one per return in the same invented business. Further GROW Careers paused.

Where this leads
