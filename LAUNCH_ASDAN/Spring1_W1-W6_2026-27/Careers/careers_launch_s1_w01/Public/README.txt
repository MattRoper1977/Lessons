LAUNCH Careers Spring 1 Week 1
Workplace expectations and behaviours
Year A. Spring 1 Week 1, 4–8 January 2027. Friday 8 January 2027, P1. One 40-minute lesson for a pair of learners. Formative; six-week term, 4 January–12 February. Week 6 assessing. No clock time supplied.

Exact weekly outcome: Workplace expectations & behaviours.

C88 exact weekly outcome retained. A88 strand shortened to Employability & Careers; D88–F88 identifying framework, resource and evidence labels expressed generically. The row supplies no minute sequence or prescribed five-item list: the 5/8/10/12/5 sequence, invented business, expectations, simulation and self-assessment implement the owner brief. No live digital tasks or personal work records. Tier labels Supported/Standard/Stretch follow this specific brief.

Lesson success checks
Identify the five expectations. Choose an appropriate response in a scenario. Use a short simulation to self-assess all five and select one next step with evidence. These are teaching success checks derived from the weekly outcome.

Our continuing workplace
An invented small business prepares simple paper packs for customers. It has a supervisor, a preparer and a checker. It has no name, real employer, location or contact details. This is classroom simulation only.

Prepare
Two sets of tiered reference packs, order card, five-expectation card, books or offline typed record and normal aids. Two large ready-cut cards and two strips per pair, plus spares. Use safe, accessible surfaces. No live workplace contact or personal records. Confirm the trusted adult and pause/return route.

Arrival and I Do
0–5: welcome privately, show the order and ask what would help someone start it. Offer a neutral example and a pass. 5–9: introduce business, roles and five expectations. 9–13: model A: pause, ask what a step means, read it back. Model a self-check with an actual observed example, not a prefilled learner answer.

We Do
13–19: match A/B to instructions/teamwork, then use additional cases as appropriate. Rehearse preparer/checker roles with the order in view. Respectful communication includes aids and asking for time. 19–23: hinge: unclear task, A guess and rush or B pause and ask. Key B. Parallel: missing strip, A report and correct then check or B hide it; key A. Re-model and recheck privately.

You Do
23–26: use the chosen Supported/Standard/Stretch prompt; do not require every case. 26–31: two short rounds of the paper order, resetting and swapping roles, with an adult taking the partner role if needed. Each learner should have a chance to ask/check and give an update. 31–35: self-assess the five expectations using actual task evidence. Mark unobserved expectations Not seen today. Reduce scenario repetitions to protect access and reflection time.

Review and exit
35–38: privately compare one self-rating with a task clue, keep the first response, give one NS and retain the reply. 38–40: learner names one achievable next step and help, then save their individual self-assessment. E actual banked evidence; R only after Voice, Audience, NS and reply. No public score comparison.

Supported
Match A and B using two labelled choices, a word bank and a model. Keep a short order checklist and sentence stems during simulation. Use voice, pointing, aid, typing or exact scribe. Give one evidence example and rate all five honestly.

Standard
Match four cases to expectations and explain two responses. Agree roles, carry out and check the order, then complete the five-row self-assessment with an evidence example and a next step. Keep normal access aids.

Stretch
Compare competing pressures in D/E and justify a response that protects quality and access. Suggest a practical adjustment, try the same order and evaluate how it helped. Use the same five-row self-assessment; more writing is optional.

Model check and fade
After A, ask privately whether the model checked meaning or guessed. Key: checked meaning. Ask for the action clue. For B, elicit the learner’s own role agreement. Fade answer cues only; retain model, written order and usual aids.

Adaptations and prompt ladder
Cognition and Learning: short checklist, one instruction at a time, model and reduced cases. Communication and Interaction: processing time, rehearsal, written/aided alternatives and exact scribe. SEMH: predictable turns, private feedback, pass and pause/return. Sensory and Physical: accessible layout, seated roles and usual aids. EAL: plan in any language. 1 Point to place/instruction; 2 ask a short question; 3 rephrase; 4 give scaffold/choice; 5 model first step only, then return task.

Private help route
Use a trusted adult or the school’s help route. Point to HELP, write or ask for a private talk. If one adult is busy, seek another through the route. Pass, pause and supported return are available. No personal work history, grades or family information requested.

Evidence
Keep one short individual self-assessment against the five expectations, one observed example or work reference, one next step and reply. Each expectation has a rating; Not seen today is valid. Evidence Sheet page 2 is a blank typed alternative, page 3 a staff observation record. Use books or usual aids by default.

Fair judgement
Judge recognition, response and evidence-based reflection, not personality, eye contact, speed, confidence, accent or a real employment record. A communication aid or normal reading support does not automatically mean a prompt was needed. Record adult-supplied thinking separately. A discrepancy between self-rating and observation starts a private question, not a public challenge. Missing evidence stays visible.

Feedback and marking
VF verbal feedback; WS worked with support; I independent; NS next step; // self-edit; ? read back; E actual evidence banked. R follows Voice, Audience, NS and learner reply in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode. Give one specific NS such as checking both packs or making a clarification request. Offer to the Communication portfolio; staff confirm any accredited mapping.

Boundaries and misconceptions
A workplace expectation does not mean obeying an unsafe or unclear request. Pause, check and seek help. Respect does not require eye contact or public speech. Reliability includes communicating a support need. A mistake can prompt a correction. This classroom record does not prove placement attendance, employment readiness, legal compliance or a qualification.

Cover and continuity
Use the separate one-page Cover Plan for immediate delivery. Keep business and expectation wording for Weeks 2–6, one per return. No later week is authored. Further GROW Careers remains paused. Listen calmly to concerns, avoid probing or secrecy promises and follow usual procedures; keep concerns outside shared evidence.
