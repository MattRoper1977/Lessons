LAUNCH Careers Spring 1 Week 5
Customer service skills
Year A. Spring 1 Week 5, 1–5 February 2027. Friday 5 February 2027, P1. One forty-minute formative lesson for a pair. Six-week term 4 January–12 February; Week 6 assessing. No clock time supplied.

Exact weekly outcome: Customer service skills.

B92 Spr1·W5 and C92 exact outcome retained. Inherited framework/resource context from row 88 generic. Row 92 gives no minute sequence: authored 5/8/10/12/5 lesson. Same business and paper-pack order as Week 1. Customer/service-worker practice roles, three calm invented scenes and five service checks implement the owner brief. Two six-minute classroom rounds are guides, not a speed target. Supported/Standard/Stretch retained.

Success checks
Use a calm invented request and the service brief to greet, listen/read, offer an allowed solution, explain a limit only if needed, and close. Try service worker and customer roles with usual access. Keep a service checklist, short reflection, five Week 1 expectation ratings and a next step.

Same invented workplace
An invented small business prepares simple paper packs for customers. It has a supervisor, a preparer and a checker. It has no name, real employer, location or contact details. This is classroom simulation only.

Prepare
Three calm scene cards, service brief, five-step guide, checklist, tier packs, vocabulary, book or blank Type instead and usual aids. Optional ready-cut paper packs illustrate the order; for B a card or prop can show one missing strip. Reset/reuse paper. No real orders, payment, contact or personal customer details. Confirm private help and adult partner route.

Arrival and I Do
0–5: retrieve a Week 1 expectation and a useful clarification phrase from Week 4; agree access and roles. 5–9: explain five steps, with polite limits conditional. Read the service brief. 9–13: model invented C, a request for a third pack. Check request, explain only two available, offer those or ask supervisor without a result/time promise, confirm and thank. CfU: is a third pack available now? Key no; two or a supervisor check are the allowed next steps. Model is not learner evidence.

We Do
13–19: introduce all three calm scenes: A two packs, B mild missing-strip complaint, C third pack. Use customer lines and replies as written; no acted anger or added pressure. Compare relevant responses and practise the five-item checklist. A/B need no refusal; C uses a polite limit. 19–23: main hinge B honest two-pack limit and allowed next step rather than a third-pack/time promise. Parallel A clarify the request rather than guess. Re-model/recheck privately. Keep scripts and usual aids visible.

You Do
23–29 round one: choose A, B or C and roles. Rehearse one calm exchange, then record actual service clue and checklist. 29–35: reset, swap customer and service worker, choose a scene and repeat. Each learner gets a worker and customer opportunity; an adult can partner. Pause or reduce repeated talk for access; do not claim a planned turn occurred if not tried. Both can use voice, aid, writing or selected lines. No public performance, forced speech or memory test.

Review and exit
35–38: short individual reflection “One thing I did to help the customer was…” Or use: A phrase or aid that helped was… / I did not try this part yet; the help I need is… Rate the five Week 1 expectations from actual evidence. 38–40: privately check one clue, set one NS, keep reply and review time; save each record. Do not force a refusal to complete the checklist or request personal customer histories.

Supported
Choose A, B or C. Keep the calm customer script, service brief, five-step guide and sentence starts visible. Use one phrase at a time, usual aid or exact scribe. Adult can partner. Swap roles and keep your own short service checklist, reflection and five ratings.

Standard
Use the brief to respond to a calm invented customer, then swap roles. Check the request and offer a relevant fix or polite limit. Use the five-item service checklist with an actual example. Record a short reflection and rate the five Week 1 expectations.

Stretch
Explain why a response fits the request and stays within the service brief. Improve one phrase so it gives a useful next step without an unsupported promise. Explain a limit of this short role-play. Swap roles and keep the same service checklist, reflection and five expectations; extra writing optional.

Model check and fade
CfU after C model: no third pack is available in the practice. Offer two or ask supervisor about more without a promise or time. Keep scripts, brief and five-step guide; fade answer cues only. Usual communication/reading aid is access. A selected phrase or written exchange can show service thinking; a model or adult-supplied response is recorded as support.

Paired roles and access
Greet, listen or read, check the request and use the service brief. Offer an allowed solution, say no politely only when needed, then confirm and close. Use one calm invented customer line and the reply. No shouting, pressure, insults or acted anger. You can read, use an aid or select a line. Choose one of the three scenes per round. Reset and swap customer and service worker for round two. Each learner records their actual worker turn and customer turn. Both can see the scripts and service brief. Use voice, usual aid, writing or selected words. An adult can partner. No public performance is required.

Adaptations and prompt ladder
Cognition and Learning: one short line/step, visible brief, checklist and reduced repetition. Communication and Interaction: rehearsal, wait time, usual aid, written or selected replies and exact scribe/readback. SEMH: predictable calm scripts, private review, pass and pause/return. Sensory and Physical: accessible table, seated turns and large print. EAL: plan in any language. 1 Point to place/instruction; 2 ask a short question; 3 rephrase; 4 scaffold/choice; 5 model first step only then return task.

Private help route
Trusted adult or school help route. Point to HELP, write or ask privately. If one adult is busy, use the route to another. Pass, pause and supported return available. No personal customer, employment or family account requested.

Evidence sheet
Evidence Sheet page 1 has five service checks with blank judgement and actual-clue cells. Page 2 is a blank typed service record, including scene, actual roles, response/result, service clue and short reflection. Page 3 has five blank Week 1 ratings and next step/help/reply. Page 4 is the private staff record. Book/usual aid default; all tiers use the same evidence goal.

Evidence and judgement
Keep actual service worker/customer turns, chosen scene, response/result, five service checklist judgements with an actual clue, short reflection, five Week 1 self-ratings and one next step/reply. Service states: Seen; With a prompt; Not needed in this scene; Not seen yet. Polite limit may be Not needed in A/B; an unobserved opportunity is Not seen yet. Introduction/model of C does not prove an independent refusal. Week 1 ratings remain: I did this; I did this with a prompt; I need another try; Not seen today. Judge observable listening/checking, allowed response and clear closing; no eye-contact, smiling, appearance, fluency or personality criteria.

Feedback and portfolio
VF, WS, I, NS, //, ?, E and R generic codes. E actual banked work. R after Voice, Audience, NS and reply in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode. One NS might check the request or replace a promise with a supervisor question. Retain first try and reply. Offer to Communication portfolio; staff confirm accredited mapping. A short classroom role-play does not prove workplace competence or real customer experience.

Safe delivery
Calm invented lines only. No shouting, insults, acted anger or confrontation. No real orders, money, customer details, deliveries or unsafe tools. Optional ready-cut paper only. Keep clear/report actual concerns and follow school procedures. Listen calmly to concerns, avoid probing or secrecy promises and use the school route; keep private concerns outside shared evidence.

Scope
Only Week 5 authored. Week 6 work-experience reflection and half-term assessing follow one per return in the same invented business. Further GROW Careers paused.
