Health and safety at work
Year A. Spring 1 Week 2, 11–15 January 2027. Friday 15 January 2027, P1. One forty-minute lesson for a pair. Formative; six-week term 4 January–12 February, Week 6 assessing. No clock time supplied.

Exact weekly outcome: Health & safety at work.

Row 89 supplies no minute sequence: 5/8/10/12/5 phases, invented scenes, sign teaching examples, risk/control card and report implement the owner brief. Success checks
Identify a hazard and who could be harmed/how. Explain one suitable control and who to tell. Read common sign shapes with symbols and words. Keep one short individual risk-and-control card and a clear report.

Same invented workplace
An invented small business prepares simple paper packs for customers. It has a supervisor, a preparer and a checker. It has no name, real employer, location or contact details. This is classroom simulation only.

Prepare
Print scene cards A–D, safety sign card, tiered packs and vocabulary. Books/usual aids or blank typed card ready. View scenes at a clear size and read neutral descriptions if needed. No real spill, obstruction, high stack, climbing, machinery or hazard demonstration. Confirm private help, pause/return and the actual school safety route.

Arrival and I Do
0–5: retrieve Week 1 safety/help expectation; ask what a worker can do if unsure. 5–9: hazard is something that could cause harm; risk considers how likely and how serious harm could be; control reduces risk. Use A box/route, worker trip/fall, keep clear/report and supervisor-arranged removal/check. 9–13: teach four sign categories with shape, symbol and written meaning. Read the words even when colour is available.

We Do
13–19: compare A with D; identify what changed and what cannot be known from a picture. Match sign type/shape/message. A wet-floor warning helps warn but does not remove the spill. Rehearse a report: what, where, possible harm, keep-clear action and request. Adult plays supervisor and acknowledges the concern. 19–23: hinge B: warning alone does not remove the hazard. Parallel A: unclear instruction, pause/ask rather than guess. Re-model and recheck privately.

You Do
23–27: use Supported/Standard/Stretch prompt with B spill or C stack. 27–32: each learner makes a short card: hazard and scene, who/how harmed, control and responsible role, report, immediate action and check/unknown. 32–35: each gives a brief report to adult/partner through usual mode; adult may partner privately. Each owns a card; do not copy the adult model as independent evidence.

Review and exit
35–38: privately check one card/report for correct hazard-versus-harm and a control that fits. Keep first try, set one NS and retain reply. 38–40: name who to tell and one safe first action; bank actual card/report. Missing response stays unobserved with a follow-up.

Supported
Choose B spill or C stack. Use the scene, two hazard choices, word bank, report frame and model A. Give hazard, who/how harmed, one control, who to tell and a short message. Book, pointing, voice, aid, typing or exact scribe valid.

Standard
Choose B or C. Explain the hazard and possible harm, select one suitable control and who arranges it, then rehearse a clear report. Link a sign type to its meaning. Complete your own short risk-and-control card.

Stretch
Compare a warning sign with a control that deals with the hazard. Explain what risk remains and how a supervisor could check the control. Evaluate D without calling it risk-free. Use the same short individual card; extra writing optional.

Signs and limits
Four teaching categories: warning triangle; prohibition circle with diagonal bar; mandatory circle; safe-condition square/rectangle. Each card includes words and a message. Colour is an extra clue. The diagram shows simplified teaching examples, not approved signs for installation. Actual signs and local instructions must be checked. Eye-protection message explains the sign type; no protected equipment task or PPE trial is part of this paper lesson.

Worked model and fade
A: box is hazard, trip/fall is possible harm. Report to supervisor and stay clear. Supervisor arranges safe removal/check. Ask if “box” or “fall” names the hazard; key box. Keep model, scene and words; fade answer cues for B/C only.

Adaptations and prompt ladder
Cognition and Learning: one scene/field at a time, reduced choices, model and frames. Communication and Interaction: wait, rehearse, use aid, read/write exact words. SEMH: predictable private turns, pass/pause/return. Sensory and Physical: large scenes, verbal description, seated role and no colour-only meaning. EAL: plan in any language. 1 Point to place/instruction; 2 ask a short question; 3 rephrase; 4 scaffold/choice; 5 model first step only then return task.

Private help route
Use a trusted adult or the school’s help route. Point to HELP, write or ask privately. If one adult is busy, seek another through the route. Pass, pause and supported return available. No personal accident account or disclosure requested.

Evidence
Keep one individual risk-and-control card with scene/hazard, who/how harmed, one suitable control and responsible role, report message, immediate action and a check/unknown. Evidence Sheet page 2 blank typed alternative, page 3 staff record. Book/usual aid default. A selected or dictated message is valid; record access separately from answer prompts.

Fair judgement
Judge a plausible hazard/harm link, a relevant control and a clear report to the supervisor/adult. Accept an explained alternative visible in a scene. Risk is not a synonym for the hazard itself. No numeric matrix or real inspection is required. D shows useful controls but cannot prove all risks absent. Do not infer job readiness, a real incident response or compliance from classroom work.

Feedback and help
VF, WS, I, NS, //, ?, E and R generic codes. E actual banked work; R after Voice, Audience, NS and reply in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode. Give one NS such as naming who could be harmed or adding the location to a report. Offer to Communication portfolio; staff confirm mapping. Exact scribe reads back and permits correction. No public questioning or score comparison.

Safety boundaries
Learners observe pictures, keep clear and report. They do not remove obstructions, clean unknown spills, climb/reach/catch stacks or operate equipment. In a real concern follow the school/workplace reporting and emergency procedures; adult supervises immediate action. If the first supervisor is unavailable or the hazard remains, use the agreed route to another responsible adult; do not resume simply because a sign was placed.

Continuity and scope
