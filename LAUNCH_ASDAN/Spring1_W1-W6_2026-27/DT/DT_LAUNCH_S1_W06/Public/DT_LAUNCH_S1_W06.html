Finish and evaluate our card pouch

Thursday 11 February 2027 · P1 · 40 minutes · assessing week

Weekly outcome: TEXTILES: finish and evaluate a textile product.

I can finish my item safely, test its purpose and explain what the evidence shows.

Today I can

  • I can complete or direct an agreed finishing action and record the help used.
  • I can test the pouch against its purpose and give my own evidence-based view.
  • I can check the six weekly outcomes honestly and choose a next step.

Use your actual item, design and earlier work. Record your own results in your book. Rehearse in any language.

Words to use

finish

Complete the agreed details and make the item ready to check.

purpose

What the item needs to do for its user.

test

A check that gives evidence about how the item works.

evaluate

Use evidence to explain what worked and what could improve.

secure

Fasten a loose end so it stays in place.

evidence

A truthful record of what actually happened.

Arrival

Our item and purpose

0–5 minutes

Find your design, item and earlier records.

Choose a safe finishing action with the adult.

Put tools down while you check the plan.

The actual purpose and design

A small reusable pouch for three paper prompt cards, each 60 mm by 80 mm. The top stays open. It should hold the cards upright, have joined sides and bottom, contain no loose sharp parts and be comfortable to handle.

Default model: two fabric pieces, each 100 mm wide by 120 mm high. A seam line is 10 mm inside the left, right and bottom edges. Leave the top open. Default join is hand running stitch. Use each pupil’s actual approved design and any recorded change.

I do

A finishing action shown

8–13 minutes

Watch the agreed securing and trimming routine.

See how the adult counts tools back.

Keep sharp parts away from the test.

1 Check

Put tools down and compare the actual item with the design. The adult checks that the fabric, thread and any bonding route are suitable. Count and control all needles before work.

2 Finish

With the agreed support, secure loose thread ends. For a hand-stitch end, take a small stitch through both layers on the back. Leave a loop before pulling tight. Pass the needle through the loop and pull snug. Repeat once. Trim only the loose tail with agreed scissors. Do not cut the seam or fabric.

3 Clear and test

Return and count all needles and other tools. Check that no sharp parts remain in the item. Test only when it is safe. If adhesive was used, wait for its full labelled cure before testing.

Say and do

Use a safe sample, not the pupil’s evidence. For running stitch, demonstrate a small securing stitch through both layers on the back. Leave a loop before pulling tight, then pass the needle through it, pull snug and repeat once. Keep fingers clear of the needle exit. Demonstrate trimming the loose tail only with agreed scissors. Return the needle to its container and count it back. Show an unfinished edge and explain why it is not ready. Fade the model by asking the pupil to name the next safety action.

Check and response

Ask: Can we test with a needle still in the item? Expect no, stop and get the adult to remove and account for it. If not secure, keep tools with the adult and use directed handling.

A safe test decision

20–25 minutes

A needle is still in the pouch.

Choose: test now, hide it in the seam, or stop for the adult’s removal and count.

Test only after the item is cleared.

Parallel check

A bonded seam has not reached its full labelled cure. Is it ready for the test?

No. Leave testing pending. Wait for the full labelled cure and the adult’s actual check. Do not invent a cure time.

Prompt ladder

Point to the place or instruction. Ask a short question. Rephrase. Offer a scaffold or choice. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Planned adaptations

Cognition and Learning

Show one action and test at a time. Model the first step, then fade support. Keep the design and actual item side by side. Read grid outcomes aloud and chunk them into two groups of three.

Communication and Interaction

Accept pointing, speech, signing or pupil-directed adult handling. Ask one short question and wait. An adult may scribe exact pupil words, read them back and allow correction.

SEMH

Offer a private adult review and a calm pause with tools down. Unfinished work and gaps are recorded without ranking pupils. No public performance, personal disclosure or competition is required.

Sensory and Physical

Use an appropriate agreed tool, prepared threaded needle or adult handling directed by the pupil. Offer visual checks without touch. Record the actual role rather than claiming an unperformed skill.

EAL

Rehearse in any language. Pair the word with the actual action or item. Offer a short sentence start. Confirm meaning and read the pupil’s record back.

Where this leads

Textile makers and quality-checking teams compare a finished item with the brief. They record real test results and use them to choose a useful improvement.

Pause lesson

Put tools down. Keep the work safe. Agree a calm return with the adult. Resolve any safety concern before restarting.
