I do

An honest adult review

8–13 minutes

Watch the review claim being matched to its source.

Three review steps

1 Find the evidence

Find the actual plan, cost record, safe-working notes and any checked food-only photo. Name what is present and what is missing.

2 Check each claim

Use a recorded taste note only for taste. Use a photo or actual plate record for appearance. Recheck the portion cost and food groups.

3 Choose one improvement

Name a real strength or limit, then one useful change and why. Say how you would check whether the change helped.

A claim we can support

20–25 minutes

There is a photo but no recorded taste note.

Choose: taste proved, taste not assessed, or taste copied from a friend.

Use the evidence before making a claim.

Parallel cost check

A £2.80 batch made two actual portions. What is the cost per portion?

£1.40 per portion, because 280p divided by 2 is 140p. This division applies to the whole two-portion batch, not an already per-portion price.

What to say and do

Correct hinge answer is taste not assessed. If any pupil chooses another answer, stop the claim, separate the evidence types and reteach. Parallel check: a £2.80 batch made two portions. Cost per portion is £1.40, not £2.80 or 70p. A quiz answer does not supply missing evidence.

My evaluation

Choose one improvement and give a reason.

One route at a time

Supported route

  1. Read or hear your actual record with an adult. Mark any gap.
  2. Choose taste note, appearance note or not assessed for each claim.
  3. Use pence to check one portion with a calculator or adult.
  4. Say one food-group comment and one improvement with a reason.

Standard route

  1. Use your actual plan and Week 2 record or photo.
  2. Check taste status and appearance separately.
  3. Recalculate ingredient-use cost per portion and review the food groups.
  4. Give one improvement, a reason and a way to check it next time.

Stretch route

  1. Complete the core honest review first.
  2. Compare the actual dish with the Week 1 design and explain a real change.
  3. Cost a second version or show the effect of portion number on a batch cost.
  4. Weigh cost, likely practical demands and food-group balance. Keep predictions separate from findings.

One useful improvement

My actual evidence says or shows ___. I would change ___. This might help because ___. Next time I would check ___.

A prediction is a possible future effect. It is not a result you have already tested.

Prompt ladder

Point to the place or instruction. Ask a short question. Rephrase. Give a scaffold or choice. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Planned adaptations

Cognition and Learning

Show one review heading at a time. Read the actual record aloud. Model the first cost addition, then fade. Keep all amounts in pence before converting to pounds. Use a calculator and a food-group list.

Communication and Interaction

Accept pointing, speech, signing, drawing or pupil-directed scribing. Ask one short question and allow thinking time. Read the review back so the pupil can confirm or correct it.

SEMH

Keep food preferences and evidence private. No compulsory tasting, body or weight talk, home-finance questions or public rankings. Offer a calm pause and an agreed return cue. Keep feedback about the design.

Sensory and Physical

No food contact or tasting is required. Offer a large-print view and verbal description of any actual photo. A pupil who did not taste records taste not assessed and can still review cost, appearance and food groups.

EAL

Plan and explain in any language. Pair each review word with a short example. Read money and quantities aloud. Confirm the meaning of the pupil’s own words before scribing or translation.

Where this leads

Food designers and catering teams compare evidence, cost and nutrition before changing a recipe. An honest review helps the next person make a better version.

Pause lesson

Take a quiet pause and agree a short return cue with the adult. Keep your evidence private.

No food, tasting or personal disclosure is required.
