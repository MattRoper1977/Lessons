Ruler checks

100 mm = 10 cm. 120 mm = 12 cm. 10 mm = 1 cm.

A card is 60 mm by 80 mm. Use actual marked fabric and ruler values, not screen sizes. Any diagram is not a cutting template.

Say and do

Model the zero mark and explain that a ruler’s physical end may sit before zero. Read 100 mm as 10 cm and 120 mm as 12 cm. Keep millimetres as the working unit. Show the three joined edges and the open top.

Check and response

Ask whether 10 mm equals 1 cm or 10 cm. Correct is 1 cm. Revisit the marked scale if units are mixed.

I do

A checked cut and running stitch

8–13 minutes

Watch measure, mark and recheck.

See how the needle goes through both layers.

Watch the flat thread and secure end.

Five steps for the default running stitch

The adult approves and supervises tools. Use this method only if it matches the actual design.

1 Check and mark

Retrieve your actual Week 4 design. Measure from the ruler’s zero mark, not its end. Mark two 100 mm by 120 mm rectangles. Recheck each side and mark 10 mm seam lines on the left, right and bottom.

2 Cut with approval

An adult checks the marks before cutting. Keep fingers outside the blade path. Use agreed fabric scissors under the agreed supervision. Turn the fabric rather than reaching across the blades. Put scissors down when you stop.

3 Align and start

Lay the two pieces together with the edges aligned. The adult selects a suitable needle and thread for the actual fabric. Pass thread through the needle eye and tie a small tail knot, or use a prepared threaded needle. Start between the layers and bring the needle out through the upper layer at the seam guide so the knot stays inside.

4 Make running stitches

Follow the marked line through both layers. Move about 5 mm along the line and put the needle through both layers. Bring it back through both layers about 5 mm further along. Keep fingers clear of the exit point. Pull gently away from faces so the thread lies flat. Repeat small even passes along the left, bottom and right lines. Leave the top open.

5 Secure and return

On the back, take a small stitch through both layers and leave a loop before pulling tight. Pass the needle through the loop and pull snug. Repeat once. An adult demonstrates this and helps as needed. Trim thread only with agreed scissors. Count and return every needle to its container.

Say and do

Use the measuring card and join steps. Demonstrate a checked mark and a short cut with the locally approved scissor method. Then align layers, thread and knot an appropriate needle, start between layers and show small running stitch passes about 5 mm apart along the seam line. Keep fingers clear of the exit point. Pull the thread flat, not tight enough to pucker. Show the securing loop twice and safe tool return. Model one pass, then invite the pupil to explain the next action before handling a tool.

Check and response

Ask: Do we join only one layer or both? Expect both layers on the marked line. If unclear, demonstrate through two prepared layers again before pupil sewing.

The accuracy hinge

20–25 minutes

The plan says 100 mm. A cut piece is 103 mm.

Is it within the 2 mm check range?

Stop and agree a safe correction.

Choose and explain

A seam line is 7 mm from the edge. The 10 mm target check is 8 to 12 mm, so re-mark before joining. Return to the ruler if the check is missed.

Say and do

Correct answer is no: the width range is 98 to 102 mm. A 103 mm piece is 3 mm too wide. Remeasure from zero, then discuss whether a safe trim is suitable before joining. Parallel check: a seam line is 7 mm from the edge; the 10 mm target range is 8 to 12 mm, so re-mark before sewing. If either check is missed, return to the ruler and use paired checks before continuing. These are improvement checks, not pupil grades.

Check and response

All pupils can point to the relevant planned and actual values or explain the difference before proceeding. An adult decides whether trimming is safe and still meets the actual design.

Lesson guidance

Lesson purpose

Make a small reusable pouch to hold three paper prompt cards, each 60 mm by 80 mm. Leave the top open. Join the sides and bottom. It should hold the cards upright, have no loose sharp parts and be comfortable to handle.

Default plan: two pieces, each 100 mm wide by 120 mm high. This is 10 cm by 12 cm. Mark a seam line 10 mm inside the left, right and bottom edges. Keep the top open. Use the pupil’s actual approved Week 4 design if it differs.

Safety and support

  • Adult checks actual design, tools, fabric and access needs before issuing equipment.
  • Use scissors and needles only with agreed adult supervision. Keep fingers clear of blade and needle exit points.
  • Count needles before and after. Keep them in a container. Never put needles or pins in your mouth or leave them in the pouch.
  • Pause with tools down and work safe. Stop for a missing or broken needle and keep the area clear. The adult recovers and accounts for every part and controls disposal. Do not release the pouch until all sharps are accounted for.
  • If bonding was chosen, use only adult-approved fabric adhesive after actual label checks. Follow its full cure time. No heat, irons or solvents are used in this lesson.

Planned adaptations

Cognition and Learning

Show one measure or stitch action at a time. Model from ruler zero, then fade the next check. Use the same units throughout. Offer a marked guide, prepared pieces or a repeated checklist, recording support honestly.

Communication and Interaction

Accept pointing, speech, signing and pupil-directed adult handling. Give one short instruction and thinking time. Read the recorded account back for the pupil to correct.

SEMH

Offer a calm pause with tools down and needles counted or safely contained. Agree a short return cue. Keep the accuracy check about improving the work, not judging the pupil. Use private adult feedback if preferred.

Sensory and Physical

Offer an approved adapted tool, prepared pieces, a threaded needle or pupil-directed adult handling. Check actual fabric sensitivities privately. Use a large clear ruler view. No sharp tool is issued until the adult approves the route.

EAL

Rehearse in any language. Pair the ruler value, unit and action word. Check the meaning of through both layers and top open before tools are issued. Use confirmed scribing where needed.

Prompt ladder

Point to the place or instruction. Ask a short question. Rephrase. Offer a scaffold or choice. Model the first step only, then return the task when safe.

Where this leads

Textile makers use measurements, safe tools and repeat checks to turn a design into a useful item. Accurate records help another person understand a change.

Pause lesson

Tools down and work safe. Count or contain needles. Take a calm pause and agree a short return cue with the adult.
