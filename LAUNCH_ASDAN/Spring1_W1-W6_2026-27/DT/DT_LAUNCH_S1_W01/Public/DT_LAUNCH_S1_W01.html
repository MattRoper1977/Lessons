A balanced meal on a budget

Thursday 7 January 2027 · P1 · 40 minutes

Weekly outcome: FOODWISE: plan a balanced meal on a budget.

I can plan one balanced meal, check its cost and explain my choices.

Today I can

  • I can name the food groups in my meal.
  • I can add the ingredient costs and stay within £2.00.
  • I can choose a safe three-step dish for next week and give a reason.

In your book. You may speak, sign, draw or direct an adult to record your confirmed words. Plan in any language.

Theme: Resilience & Change. Today we design. Next week we prepare the agreed dish. Tasting is optional.

balanced

A variety of foods that helps meet our needs over time.

budget

The most we can spend.

portion

The amount for one person.

ingredient

A food used in a dish.

hygiene

Actions that keep food and equipment clean and safe.

cross-contact

When an allergen gets into another food by contact.

Arrival

The design brief

0–5 minutes

Plan one meal for one person.

Your ingredient-use budget is £2.00.

Today is planning. We will prepare the agreed dish next week.

Design brief: one person, £2.00 ingredient-use budget, a feasible dish for a supervised practical.

No one needs to discuss spending or food at home. Ask for a private adult review if helpful.

What to say and do

Read the brief aloud. Ask what the meal must do: offer variety, cost no more than £2.00 and be possible in a short supervised practical. Explain that the prices are invented and no one needs to discuss home spending. Offer spoken planning or a private adult check. Use the last minute as a quiet settling reset.

Check and response

Ask: Whose meal and what limit? Expect one person and £2.00. Re-read the brief if either is missing.

Materials-free fallback

Without food, equipment, printer or paper, read the price rows aloud. Pupils speak, sign or direct their ingredient choices. Rehearse the three steps with empty hands. An adult records exact choices, total and reason when a recording method is available. Mark transcription pending until it exists. This is planning or simulation, never a claim that the meal was prepared.

A balanced eating pattern

The two largest groups in a balanced eating pattern are vegetables and fruit, and starchy foods. Include protein foods and dairy or suitable alternatives. Use small amounts of oils and spreads. Balance is built across the day or week; every meal does not need exact plate sections. Choose water as a drink. No calorie counting, weight talk or food judgement is needed.

What to say and do

Use the balanced plate recap. Invite food group sorting, with the model meal as an example rather than a rule for every meal. Explain that protein is a nutrient in several foods; beans are counted in the protein group for this model to avoid double counting. Oils are used in small amounts when needed, not added to tick a box.

Check and response

Wrap is starchy food; beans supply protein; carrot is a vegetable. Fruit adds variety and yoghurt adds dairy. Read the recap again if pupils use only colour as a test of balance.

Model one-line reason

My £1.40 meal has starchy food from the wrap, protein from beans, vegetables and fruit, and dairy from yoghurt. It leaves 60p of my £2.00 budget.

Next week’s three-step dish

We rehearse with empty hands today.

1 Prepare

With adult approval, wash the vegetables and fruit. Grate the carrot and finely slice the cabbage using the agreed tools. An adult can prepare these for you.

2 Mix

Use 80 g of ready-to-eat cooked beans, drained by an adult if needed. Mash with a fork. Mix in about 20 g of the plain yoghurt and the vegetables.

3 Assemble

Put the filling on the wrap. Fold it. Serve with the washed fruit and the rest of the yoghurt. Keep it chilled until eaten and follow the adult’s plan.

What to say and do

Point to each amount and price. Add 35p + 30p + 15p + 10p + 20p + 30p = 140p, or £1.40. Then £2.00 minus £1.40 = £0.60. Name the food groups and model the one-line reason. Explain the three recipe steps using empty-hand gestures only. No food or sharp tools are needed today. Fade the model by asking pupils to supply the final addition.

Check and response

Ask: Is 140p £1.40 or £14.00? Expect £1.40. Show 100p plus 40p and repeat the check if needed.

Parallel check

A meal costs £1.55. How much is left from £2.00?

45p, because 200p minus 155p is 45p.

What to say and do

Use the hinge choices. Correct answer is 60p because 200p minus 140p equals 60p. If any learner chooses another answer, rehearse with written pence amounts before independent work. One route at a time

Supported route

  1. Use the model wrap and choose apple or pear.
  2. Read or hear each price. Tick off each item once in your book.
  3. Add in pence with an adult or calculator, then check against 200p.
  4. Say your own reason using: My meal has ___ from ___ and costs ___.

Standard route

  1. Choose the model meal or swap ingredients from the card.
  2. List the exact amounts and add their prices. Work out the money left.
  3. Explain how your choices include the main food groups.
  4. Keep the three-step method and identify two safety actions for next week.

Stretch route

  1. Complete the core plan and one-line reason first.
  2. Cost a second version using two substitutions. Keep it within £2.00.
  3. Compare cost, food groups, likely texture and practical demands.
  4. Justify which version you would prepare. Include a limitation of invented portion prices.
Type instead

Use the meal plan prompts and one route. Pupils may use the model ingredients but make and explain their own choice. An adult can scribe the pupil’s words and read them back. Check food groups, arithmetic and a feasible dish. A different dish needs a checked two- or three-step method, suitable ingredients and adult approval before practical use. Use the last minute to pause, review the list and organise the book.

Kitchen safety and hygiene for next week

  • Before food. Tell the adult about dietary and access needs privately. The adult checks every ingredient label and the agreed allergy plan before ingredients are issued.
  • Clean hands and surfaces. Wash hands with soap and water before food preparation, after the toilet and after touching waste. Dry them well. Tie hair back, cover cuts and clean the work surface and tools.
  • Tools. An adult opens tins and removes sharp lids before the activity. Use knives, graters and open tins only with adult approval and supervision. Keep fingers away from sharp edges. Ask for pre-cut vegetables or adult help.
  • Food choice. Use beans labelled cooked and ready to eat. Dry beans need a different cooking process and are not used here. Keep ready-to-eat food away from raw food.
  • Chill. Keep chilled ingredients at 5°C or below, follow use-by dates and storage labels, and take out only what you need. The adult handles storage and any leftovers.
  • Eating and stopping. Tasting is optional. Never share a tasting spoon. If anything spills, breaks or feels unsafe, stop, put the tool down and tell the adult. Clear and clean using the agreed routine.
Dietary and allergy check

The model contains wheat including gluten and milk. Soya is present in the optional alternative. All ingredients and alternatives need label checks for all relevant allergens and cross-contact. A plant alternative is not automatically suitable. If a safe substitution is unavailable, use the planning or simulation route. Never describe a meal as allergen-free.

The adult checks next-week readiness. Keep unconfirmed details marked pending.

A plant alternative is not automatically suitable. If a safe substitution is unavailable, use the planning or simulation route. Never describe a meal as allergen-free.

Prompt ladder

Point to the place or instruction. Ask a short question. Rephrase. Give a scaffold or choice. Model the first step only, then return the task.

Planned adaptations

Cognition and Learning

Show one step at a time. Read the price card aloud. Model an addition, then leave the next one for the pupil. Use pence throughout before converting to pounds. Offer a calculator and a repeated checklist.

Communication and Interaction

Accept pointing, speech, signing or pupil-directed scribing. Give thinking time and a brief choice before an open question. Read the final plan back so the pupil can correct it.

SEMH

Use invented prices and a fictional design brief. No home spending, body, weight or personal diet disclosures. Avoid good-food or bad-food labels. Offer a private adult review and a calm pause with an agreed return.

Sensory and Physical

No food handling or tasting is required today. Provide a clear large-print view. Offer pre-prepared vegetables and adult tool handling for the later practical. Plan a suitable route before any food is issued.

EAL

Plan in any language. Pair ingredient names with the price row and food group. Read quantities and money aloud. Use the pupil’s own words with confirmed translation or scribing where available.

Where this leads

Food designers and catering teams balance cost, nutrition, access and safe preparation. A clear plan helps another person prepare the intended meal.

Pause lesson

Put tools down. Take a quiet pause. Agree a short return cue with the adult.
