LAUNCH Weekly - Autumn!B96, C96

  • Team working skills: plan a team activity.

Aut2 W2; current calendar absolute W10. This is a classroom teaching and planning lesson. It makes no claim of community impact or qualification completion.

Prior: Build from the three-card display contract. The brief, resource inventory and model roles are repeated so this lesson also works alone.

Next: Next week, use the agreed plan in a classroom activity, recording actual actions and changes rather than treating scheduled time as completed work.

Preparation

  • Print the task/duration table, brief, support cards and blank time strip. Use paper cards or coloured pencil blocks; no digital calendar or real external project is required.

Care and safety

  • Core activities stay on the classroom table using the supplied paper resources. No public contact, sales, spending, photographs or personal disclosures are required.
  • For a real follow-on project, staff confirm the purpose, materials and audience within the school’s existing arrangements.

Teacher response guidance

  • Model: A0–2, B2–8, C2–5, D8–12. Total duration is15 task minutes, but elapsed model time is12 because B/C overlap.
  • Wait plan: A0–2; B4–10; C2–5; D10–14. Finish14.
  • Swap option: coordinator B2–8; checker C2–5; D8–12 with suitable available owners. Maker arrives4 and agrees useful support or final-check tasks. Not every available person must be busy every minute.
  • At review, inspect three drafts, matching symbols and prepared spaces. If incomplete, allocate the missing task and revise final check/time.

Evidence of learning

Check dependencies, owner capacity, honest elapsed time and justified recovery. Scheduled or hypothetical time is not actual assessed plan-use time; consult current ASDAN assessment requirements separately. These tasks are teaching/rehearsal. Use only the learner’s actual contribution; no automatic award sign-off is implied.

Access and independence

Allow spoken comparisons and a partner drawing the pupil-directed timeline. Keep owner capacity and dependency reasoning in every route.

Lundy Loop

Receive the pupil’s adaptation and ask which specific handover could still fail. Carry the revised plan forward for an actual later task if appropriate.

Arrival · What must happen first?

Use the supplied three-card brief if the prior lesson was not taught. Clarify planned minutes versus actual evidence.

Starter · Four tasks, one deadline

Durations are constructed planning assumptions, not measured class performance. Sum is15 but elapsed can be12 due parallel work.

I do · Trace the dependencies

Trace A2 + max(B6,C3) + D4 =12. B and C have different owners. If one person owns both, that overlap is invalid.

We do · Choose start times

Interactive schedule calculates finish times and checks dependencies. It accepts valid later schedules, while explaining when they miss the model deadline.

I do · A review point needs evidence

Avoid a review that is just a timer alarm. The state of the work determines action. Adults retain safety and access support responsibilities.

We do · Choose a proportionate response

Quality and honest timing take precedence over pretending the schedule was met.

Independent · The maker arrives late

Fresh absence constraint. One valid swap: coordinator drafts2–8, checker arranges2–5, maker joins4 and receives a later check/support task, final check8–12. But do not force a role swap without agreement; pupil may justify waiting14min. Team capacity/safety remain local considerations.

Exit · Hand over the changed plan

Adult receives the comparison and highlights the next actual check. No planned time is entered as completed assessed time.

Misconceptions

Adding every duration always gives elapsed time. Different people can work in parallel if dependencies allow.

A detailed schedule proves the work took place. It records intention. Observe and record actual use separately.
