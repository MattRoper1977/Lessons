Team game

Describe the bars

Work in pairs. One partner looks at cards A to D below. The other looks away from the screen. Describe the three item bars, labels and zero scale. The partner sketches the chart in a book. Compare it with the source, then swap roles. The teacher walks round to hear each pair. Use touch cues or direct a scribe if helpful.

Points. Each pair earns 1 point for all three item labels and values (20, 10, 8), and 1 more for a zero scale with no whole-total bar. Every pair can score and retry.

Invite a private regulation check. Green · Ready, Amber · Help, Red · Pause. Return to learning: quiet pause, usual aid, one short task, then rejoin at the current step. Offer a quiet start, usual aid or pass. Use the cue and accept the pupil’s usual communication mode.

Starter · 3 min.

Starter question 1 revisits last week. Take one minute for recall and reveal. Why is =B4*C4 better than typing a fixed number in cell D4? Cue: Input · updates. Expected: If B4 or C4 changes, the formula updates D4 automatically. Then ask: Why should you leave the whole total row out when charting the three items? Expected: Including the whole total adds all items together and makes the item columns harder to compare. Use fictional kit points. Plan your chart in your book first.

Teaching script 2.

I do · 5 min.

Select the items, check zero scale and compare. Select A3:B6 for the headings and three items only. Insert a column chart with a title, labels and zero scale. Change one value and write a checked comparison. In the model table, Paper=6, Card=6 and Fasteners=4. Selecting A3:B6 makes three clear columns starting at 0 without the total row. Leave row 7 (whole total) out of the chart range. A scale starting above 0 exaggerates small differences. Save, close, reopen and check both chart and numbers.

We do · 8 min.

Which cell range makes a fair column chart of the three kit items? Ask for a choice and a reason before revealing. B. A3:B6 includes the headings and three items while leaving out the whole total row. In the task table, Paper=20, Card=10 and Fasteners=8. When B4 changes from 20 to 12, how many more points does Paper have than Card?

Teaching script 3.

You do · 13 min.

Check the book plan before charting. Ensure learners select A3:B6 (excluding the total), add title and axis labels, verify the scale starts at 0, and test changing B4 from 20 to 12. Aspire compares before and after, explains the zero scale and saves version 2. In your book, note the range A3:B6 and the three values. Make your column chart, then change B4 from 20 to 12. Write a comparison with two values, then save and reopen. Supported route. Select A3:B6 for the three items, insert a three-column chart and add labels. Change B4 from 20 to 12, write one comparison and reopen your saved file. Independent route. Chart A3:B6 without the total, add a title and axis labels, and check the scale starts at 0. Change B4 from 20 to 12, compare two items with numbers, then save and reopen. Aspire route. Compare paper and card points before and after B4 changes from 20 to 12. Explain why a scale starting above 0 misleads, keep version 1 and save version 2. Choice route. Agree a route and access mode. Direct an adult if selecting cells, charting or saving needs support.
