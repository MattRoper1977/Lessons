Team game

Human reading order

In teams, make three paper cards: title, chart and comparison. Use the slide 2 case below. Stand in reading order or hold the cards from your seats. A partner reads in that order and describes the chart values. The teacher walks round to check each group. Swap roles.

Points. Each team earns 1 point for title, chart, comparison in order, and 1 more for a description with Paper 12, Card 10 and Fasteners 8. Every team can score and retry.

Invite a private regulation check. Green · Ready, Amber · Help, Red · Pause. Return to learning: quiet pause, usual aid, one short task, then rejoin at the current step. Offer a quiet start, usual aid or pass. Use the cue and accept the pupil’s usual communication mode.

Starter · 3 min.

Starter question 1 revisits last week. Take one minute for recall and reveal. Why should a column chart scale start at zero and leave out the whole total? Cue: Zero · fair bars. Expected: Starting at zero and charting only the items lets you compare the column heights fairly. Then ask: Why does a chart image on a slide need alternative text? Expected: Alternative text states what the chart shows if someone cannot see the image. Use the three-slide fictional kit deck. Plan your fixes in your book first.

Teaching script 2.

I do · 5 min.

Check titles, contrast, description and reading order. Give each slide a specific title and large dark text. Add a chart description and put the title first in order. Explain one access change, then preview and save. On slide 2, replace the vague title Points with Kit points comparison, add chart description (Paper 12, Card 10, Fasteners 8) and put the title first. Do not rely on colour alone without text labels. Check that slide numbers match the table (12, 10 and 8). Preview all three slides, save, close and reopen.

We do · 8 min.

On slide 2, what is the clearest reading order for the slide objects? Ask for a choice and a reason before revealing. B. Putting the slide title first, then the chart, then the comparison helps a reader or screen reader follow the slide. Why is a chart description saying 'Column chart: Paper 12, Card 10, Fasteners 8; Paper is 2 above Card' better than just writing 'chart'?

Teaching script 3.

You do · 13 min.

Review the three practice slides in books before editing. Check that learners replace vague titles, increase pale text to large dark text, add a meaningful chart description, set reading order (title, chart, comparison), and explain one change on slide 3. In your book, list one fix for slide 1, slide 2 and slide 3. Update the three slides, chart description and reading order. Write I changed __ so that __, then preview, save and reopen. Supported route. On slide 1 and slide 2, add clear titles, larger dark text and a chart description. Set reading order (title, chart, comparison), explain one change on slide 3 and save. Independent route. Fix all three slides with clear titles, strong contrast, chart description and reading order. Explain one access change with its reason, then preview, save and reopen your file. Aspire route. Refine the chart description so it states the key comparison without repeating every word. Explain why colour alone is not enough, keep version 1 and save version 2. Choice route. Agree a route and access mode. Direct an adult if editing slides, checking order or saving needs support.

Teaching script 4.

Review · 4 min.

Check all three slides for specific titles, dark text, chart description, reading order and your reason on slide 3. Optional. Respond to feedback with an adult. You may pass. Mark R only after the pupil has responded to feedback with an adult. Keep the first response and any change separate. Edit in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode.

Exit · 3 min.

Ask the two route checks. What helped you most today, or what would you change next time? Point, speak, sign, draw, write or choose. You may pass. Accept the pupil’s own example or a pass. Record their own words and actual support. At check-out, record E for … against the pupil's target.
