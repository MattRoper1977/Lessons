3 minStarter · predict

What would count?

Sort three statements into activity, output and outcome. An activity done is not automatically evidence that a useful change occurred.

Think → choose → explain or point → keep the first response visible.
Activity

A planned or recorded action carried out by the group. An activity log can show that work occurred, but it does not by itself show what changed for anyone.

Output

A thing produced, arranged or shared. A genuine output may be useful evidence of work, yet quality or completion does not automatically demonstrate an outcome or impact.

Outcome

A bounded change supported by evidence from an appropriate perspective. A claim about benefit needs a traceable record and must name whose view is represented or still missing.

Worked comparison — illustrative, not project evidence: A meeting note is an activity record. A finished information sheet is an output. A supported change in access or understanding may be an outcome only where genuine evidence shows it. Confidence and neatness cannot replace that trail.

Intention

Copy the existing intended change accurately. Do not rewrite it after seeing the result. If it was broad, narrow the review question rather than pretending the original plan was precise.

Observed record

Locate one genuine source. State what it records, whose perspective it carries and whether it shows an activity, output or possible outcome. Keep the date and locator attached.

Limit before decision

Name a missing perspective, uncertain source, alternative explanation or difference between contribution and outcome. Only then choose KEEP, CHANGE or STOP / HOLD.

Model sentence frame for the process, not a learner answer: intention → source → observed information → represented perspective → limit → decision → responsible role → report-back. Each arrow needs evidence or an honest gap.

3 minWe Do · choose

Choose, then justify

Choose the decision justified by the evidence, then identify who must receive it and when the learner gets a response.

Select 1, 2 or 3, then explain the evidence clue.
KEEP

The genuine evidence supports continuing the bounded action, and the limits do not overturn that decision. Name what stays, why, who receives the decision and when it will be reviewed again.

CHANGE

The record, learner response or limitation supports a specific revision. Name the part to change without claiming that the revised action has already happened or will be accepted.

STOP / HOLD

Consent, capacity, safety, evidence, authorisation or a responsible decision is unresolved. Preserve the reason. Stopping or holding can be the most accurate outcome of the review.

Hinge check: Point to the evidence clue and the limit before choosing a card. A decision is not stronger because it sounds positive; it is stronger when the trail and boundary can be checked.

Accuracy includes limits

SPACE: allow a private choice and time to inspect the record.
VOICE and AUDIENCE: preserve the learner's own reason and name the receiving role.
INFLUENCE: record the next bounded action and report-back point; never promise that an external partner will agree.
SPACE

Allow enough time and privacy to inspect the record and choose a response mode. Do not require personal disclosure or public explanation as the price of participating.

VOICE + AUDIENCE

Preserve the learner decision and reason in the selected mode. Name the actual receiving school role. An audience exists only when a responsible person genuinely receives the response.

INFLUENCE

Record the bounded teaching, support or project decision that follows. If no action is authorised, state that. Never promise an external reply, agreement, publication or future event.

Boundary test: The loop is complete only when the learner response is received and a genuine next move or honest hold is reported back. A polished form with no receiving role is not influence.

Locate

Find the W13 index row and its original or approved locator. Keep its status. If the record is missing, rehearse with a clearly labelled neutral example but do not insert the example into the real review.

Connect

State whether the record supports an activity, output or bounded outcome. Identify the represented perspective. Separate learner contribution from shared work and adult support.

Limit and decide

Name what the record cannot establish. Choose KEEP, CHANGE or STOP / HOLD, then name the receiving role and a realistic report-back point.

Shared check: Does the reason follow from the source? Is any missing voice visible? Would the decision change if the constraint, capacity or perspective changed? What remains adult-owned?

Prepare the review

Bring the W13 index, genuine records and the existing intended outcome. Choose an accessible response route. Do not search for personal details or make external contact from this lesson.

Build the reasoning trail

Write intention, activity or output, observed information, learner contribution, represented perspective, missing perspective or limit, decision and reason. Keep adult support separately labelled.

Hand the decision forward

Name the responsible school role. Record only an action within the school's authority; otherwise use AWAITING DECISION. Set a report-back point without promising completion, agreement or impact.

Decision

Hand forward KEEP, CHANGE or STOP / HOLD with one genuine evidence locator. State the reason in learner-owned words or the selected response mode; keep adult scribing visible.

Limit

Name what remains uncertain, missing or represented by only one perspective. A review remains honest when it says what cannot yet be concluded.

Report-back

The class teacher receives the decision, names back what was heard and records any adult-owned action. Give a realistic lesson point for reporting back or say plainly that no further action is authorised.

Final check: Do not claim that the project is complete, successful, assessed, verified or accepted. This lesson records a bounded review trail for the authorised centre role.
