Follow the Wash Card, Then Say It Back

LAUNCH ASDAN · Week 3 · Slot 1

Objective: follow the wash card in order, then say your partner's steps back exactly.

Success evidence: the steps ticked in order, one care label line filled in, any step you could not finish marked 'not sure', and one feedback line that names something you saw.

Every item, photograph and card in this lesson comes from the school laundry kit. In this room we only talk about the school kit. Nobody is asked about washing, chores or anything at home.

40 minutes. Offline.

Prepare, all from the laundry kit box in the practical room store: the school wash card on the board plus one paper copy per pupil; photo card 3; three colour-sort trays and one ask tray; the supplied load, which must contain a white towel and a red sock or equivalent; at least one item with a readable tub-40 label; at least one item with a deliberately faded label; two towels to fold; one cloth for wiping the trays; one record sheet per pupil from the cover folder. If photo card 3 is missing, hold up one white towel and one pink towel and tell the story instead. At the end of the lesson collect every sheet, put them in the LAUNCH cover folder, and hand the folder to the class TA.
The school wash card, word for word, five steps. 1 Sort the load by colour. 2 Check the care label. 3 Fold the clean items and stack them. 4 Wipe the trays and put the kit back on the shelf. 5 Tell the next person what you did. If your copy is worded differently, read your copy aloud and use its words all lesson. No machine is switched on and no detergent is handled today: pupils sort, read labels, fold and wipe only. Model at the station, not from the front, and slow right down on the 'not sure' step and on step 4. In I Do 2, use another adult as your say-back partner, not a pupil.

3 minArrival · retrieve

Arrival · retrieve

Three lines are on the board. Read each one. Write true, false, or not sure next to it. 'I am not sure' is a real answer and it counts.

  • A routine means the same steps in the same order every time.
  • Active listening means you can say the words back to the speaker.
  • 'That was good' tells your partner what they did.

You have one minute. Then we check all three together. Keep your sheet for the rest of the lesson.

Do not take hands up. Every pupil writes first, then read each line out and give the answer. Line 1 true. Line 2 true. Line 3 false: 'that was good' does not name what the person did. Praise anyone who wrote 'not sure'.

3 minStarter · predict

Starter · create the need

Look at photo card 3. Two adults were given the same wash card and the same load.

Adult A read the card and did every step in order. Adult B worked from memory. Basket A came out white. Basket B came out pink. One red sock was in the load.

  • Notice: find the step that was missed.
  • Choose: point at that step on the wash card.
  • Explain: say what happened because that step was missed.

One skipped step changed the whole load. That is why the order matters.

The answer is step 1, sort the load by colour. Do not say it. Wait for a pupil to point at it.

7 minI Do · model

I Do · model

Three words for today

  • Routine: the same steps, in the same order, every time.
  • Active listening: listening so you can say it back.
  • Feedback: one clear comment about what the person did.

Watch me at the station. Our wash card has five steps. I say every step out loud.

Step 1 says sort the load by colour. I hold up the white towel. I put it in the white tray. I tick step 1. I do not read step 2 until step 1 is ticked.

Step 2 says check the care label. I hold the label up. It shows a tub with 40 inside. I write 40 on my sheet. I tick step 2.

The next label is faded. I cannot read the number. I do not guess. I write 'not sure, faded label' on my sheet. That item goes in the ask tray. Writing 'not sure' is the correct move, not a mistake.

Step 4 says wipe the trays and put the kit back on the shelf. I wipe each tray with the cloth. I put the cloth and the trays back where I found them. The station is ready for the next person.

3 minWe Do · choose

We Do · everyone commits

The wash card on the board has five steps. Step 4 is covered with a card. Which step is hidden?

A: wipe the trays and put the kit back. B: sort the load by colour. C: check the care label.

Write your letter on your card now. Do not say it out loud.

Everyone shows together on three. Three. Two. One. Show.

Now the reasons. You can still see B and C on the card. B is step 1. C is step 2. So which one is hidden? If you want to change your answer now, say so. That is good work.

The answer is A. B and C are still on the card as steps 1 and 2, so the hidden step must be A. That is elimination, not memory: accept a pupil pointing at steps 1 and 2. A finger on the card counts as an answer.

7 minI Do 2 · test

I Do 2 · connect

There are two jobs. First you do the routine. Then you tell the next person what you did. Somebody always takes over from you.

Watch me listen. My partner reports: 'I did steps 1 to 4. I stopped at step 5.' I do not talk over my partner.

Then I say it back, exactly: 'You did steps 1 to 4. You stopped at step 5.' My partner says yes, that is right. Saying it back proves I was listening.

Now one piece of feedback. I point at the sheet. I say: 'Steps 1 to 4 are ticked in order. Step 5 has no tick.' That is feedback with evidence. 'That was good' does not tell your partner what they did.

Saying it back is a work skill

Saying someone's steps back to them has a name in a workplace. It is called checking understanding.

A supervisor does it so a job is not done twice or done wrong.

Watch me. My partner says: sort darks, check the label, load the drum.

I say it back in the same order, using their words, not mine.

Then I ask: did I get that right? They confirm it or they correct me.

If I miss a step, that is useful. It shows the step needs saying more clearly.

5 minWe Do 2 · rehearse

We Do 2 · lab

Now you try it in pairs. An adult tells you who your partner is. Pick a row. You can change row at any time. The row is a choice, not a label.

  • ◆ Say back one step your partner did. Point at it on the card.
  • ▲ Say back two steps, then give one piece of feedback that names what you saw.
  • ★ Say back all five steps, give feedback with evidence, then name one step to do better next time.

Then swap over. Both of you speak. Both of you listen.

You assign the pairs; pupils do not choose. With an odd number make one three: two report, one listens and says both back. A pupil who will not pair says their steps back to an adult. If a partner has done less, they report what they have finished. Nobody waits.

9 minIndependent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Now it is your turn to do it. You are at the laundry station with the school kit. There is no worksheet to fill in. You read one step of the card at a time and then do it.

Tick each step as you finish it. Sort the supplied load into the trays. Read one care label and write the wash number on your sheet. Wipe the trays and put the kit back on the shelf.

Then do the handover. Your partner reports what they did. You say their steps back. They confirm it or correct you. Write one feedback line that names something you saw on their sheet.

If a step will not work, write 'not sure' next to it and three words to say why. That is worth more than a tick you did not earn.

If you have time left, fold two towels and stack them. Work at your own pace. Three steps done carefully is better than six done fast. If you want the card read out loud, ask an adult.

Assessed core: the load sorted, one care label line, one handover with say-back. Folding is the extension: if time runs short, drop the folding, never the handover. If a pupil stalls, read the next step aloud and step back. As pupils finish, scribe the exit sentence for anyone who needs it; the rest write their own line.

Write the three steps your partner gave you, in their order.

Write one word beside any step you had to ask about. That word is evidence too.

3 minExit · hand forward

Exit · Audience and Influence

Say one sentence: what you will do differently next time you follow a card. Pointing at the line on your sheet counts as speaking.

If an adult wrote your words down, the adult reads them back exactly. If you wrote your own line, read it out. You say yes, that is right, or you correct it. Your correction goes in.

Then we choose one real next action together. It could be the job you take at the laundry station next week.

Your sheet goes in the LAUNCH cover folder. The class TA takes the folder to the adult who runs the laundry station today.
