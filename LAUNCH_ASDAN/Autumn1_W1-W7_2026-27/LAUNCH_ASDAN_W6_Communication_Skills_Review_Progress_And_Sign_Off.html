Signing Off the Unit: Evidence That Matches the Criterion

LAUNCH · ASDAN · Week 6. Personal and Social Development, Communication. Signing Off the Unit.

Objective: match each piece of evidence in your folder to the criterion it proves, write a progress review in the approved three-part frame, and name the one gap you will close before sign-off.

Success evidence: a tracking sheet with three matched rows, a three-part review statement, and one named gap carrying a date.

Everything you write about today comes from your own folder and the approved criterion sheet. You are not asked about your home, your family or your circumstances. The meal plan evidence is judged on the shopping list, the choices and the budget, never on bodies or on how anyone looks.

40 minutes. Offline.

Arrival · retrieve

A criterion is a promise you have to prove

A criterion is one line on the assessment sheet. It states what you have to show.

Evidence is the thing in your folder that shows it. A plan, a list, a photo, a witness record.

Sign-off happens when an assessor can see the criterion and the evidence in the same place.

That is the whole job today. You are not making new work. You are proving the work you already did.

Your folder from this unit holds three kinds of evidence.

There is the healthy meal plan, with its shopping list and its total cost.

There is the promotion work for the anti-bullying and kindness project.

There is the witness record an adult signed after you spoke to the group.

Name the three evidence types aloud and point at each one in a real folder. Pupils who cannot picture their own evidence will spend the independent stage hunting instead of matching.

You need three things on the desk. Your folder, the criterion sheet and the tracking sheet.

3 minStarter · predict

Starter · create the need

Two folders on the desk

Folder A is full. It holds eleven pieces of work and not one of them is labelled.

Folder B holds four pieces. Each one has a criterion number written in the top corner.

The assessor signs off Folder B in six minutes. Folder A goes back for another week.

Folder A did more work. It proved less.

An assessor cannot sign what they cannot find. Labelling is the work that earns the sign-off.

  • 1 · read the criterion.
  • 2 · find the one piece that proves it.
  • 3 · write the number on that piece.
  • 4 · say in one line what it shows.

That takes about a minute for each criterion. Doing it now saves you a whole re-submission.

7 minI Do · model

I Do · model

Matching one piece to one criterion

I read criterion 2.1 out loud: take part in a discussion and respond to what others say.

The key word is respond. So I look for the piece that proves responding, not the piece that proves attending.

My photo of the group shows I was in the room. It does not show I responded. It is the wrong evidence.

The witness record says I answered two questions about the poster budget. That proves responding.

So I write 2.1 in the top corner of the witness record.

Then I write one line underneath. This shows I answered a question about cost during the planning meeting.

Notice what I did not do. I did not add a second piece to the same criterion.

Two pieces for one criterion is not twice as strong. It hands the assessor two things to read for one decision.

One piece, labelled, with one line under it. That is finished evidence.

Say the four step names as you do them: read, find, label, line. The sequence is what is being taught, not the particular criterion.

3 minWe Do · choose

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the evidence cards

Six evidence cards are on your table. Each one describes a real piece of work from this unit.

Sort the six cards into two groups: ready to sign off, and not ready.

Then match each not-ready card to the reason it fails. No criterion number, no line saying what it shows, or the wrong evidence for that criterion.

Sort every card, even the ones you are unsure about. A pile you can see beats a card left out.

Two cards look ready and are not. They carry a number that does not match the line written under it.

When we compare, say which pile you chose before you say why.

7 minI Do 2 · test

I Do 2 · connect

From labelled evidence to a review statement

Labelling proves the pieces. Sign-off also needs a progress review in your own words.

The approved frame has three parts. What I did. What it shows. What is left.

Watch me use it on the meal plan evidence.

What I did: I planned a two-day menu, wrote the shopping list and totalled it at nine pounds forty.

What it shows: I can plan against a budget and explain my choices to another person.

What is left: I have no witness record for explaining it, so I will present the list on Thursday.

A retelling says we went to the shop and then we came back.

A review says the shopping list proves I compared two prices before I chose.

The test is one question. Can the assessor point at a skill inside your sentence?

If the sentence holds only events, add the words this shows and finish it.

  • 1 · write what you did, in one sentence.
  • 2 · write what it shows, starting with this shows.
  • 3 · write what is left, with a date on it.
  • 4 · check a skill is named, not only an event.

5 minWe Do 2 · rehearse

We Do 2 · lab

Find the missing part

A review card sits face down on your table. Turn it over now.

Two review statements are printed on it. Both are about the anti-bullying and kindness promotion.

Work in twos. One of you reads statement one aloud. The other reads statement two.

Statement one says we made the posters, we printed them, and we put them on the wall.

Statement two says the tally sheet shows I chose the wording that most pupils could read.

Decide together which of the three parts each statement is missing. What I did, what it shows, or what is left.

Statement one is all events. It never names what the promotion work shows about you.

Most statements are thin in the same place. The middle part is the one being assessed.

Now take one piece from your own folder. Say its middle part aloud to your partner, starting with the words this shows.

Your partner says one thing back. A skill, or only an event.

If they heard only an event, say it again with the skill named.

Keep those words. You write that sentence down in the next task.

9 minIndependent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Three rows, one statement, one gap

Work on your own now. Your folder and your criterion sheet stay open on the desk.

Fill three rows of the tracking sheet. Every row takes one criterion number, one piece of evidence and one line saying what it shows.

Use a different piece in every row. Repeat a piece and you have proved one thing, not three.

Then write your review statement underneath, in the three-part frame.

Then name the one gap that stops your unit signing off today, and write the date you will close it.

  • Supported · fill one row with a number, a piece and a line, then write what is left.
  • Standard · fill three rows, write the three-part review, and name one gap with a date.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name the criterion your evidence is weakest for, and why.

If you finish early, read your three lines against the criterion wording once more.

Underline any line that repeats the criterion back at itself instead of naming your own work.

3 minExit · hand forward

Exit · Audience and Influence

Read the review to the assessor

The adult at your table is your audience today. They are reading it as an assessor would.

Read your review statement to them. All three parts, in order.

They say one thing back. Sign, or not yet and which part is missing.

Not yet is information, not a judgement. It names the one thing to fix next.

Then choose what happens next. Close the gap this week, ask for a witness record, or bring the folder to Thursday.

Write that choice and today's date at the bottom of the tracking sheet before you leave.
