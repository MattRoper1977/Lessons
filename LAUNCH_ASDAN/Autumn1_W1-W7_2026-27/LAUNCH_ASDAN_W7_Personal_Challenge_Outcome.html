Closing the Personal Challenge: The Outcome and Its Proof

LAUNCH · ASDAN · Week 7. Personal and Social Development. Closing the Personal Challenge.

Objective: compare your end measure with the start measure in your challenge log, write the outcome statement in the approved frame, and name the evidence that proves it.

Success evidence: one outcome statement with a start measure, an end measure, the change and a named piece of evidence.

Every challenge comes from the approved challenge list, and every number comes from your own log. The measure is the activity: a count, a time, a distance, or the days you completed. Nothing here is about body size, weight or how anybody looks. You are not asked about your home or your family.

40 minutes. Offline.

3 minArrival · retrieve

Arrival · retrieve

An outcome is a comparison

An outcome is not a list of what you did.

It is the difference between your start measure and your end measure.

That is why week one asked you for a measure you could repeat.

You repeated it this week, and the log holds both numbers. That pair is your outcome.

A missing start measure does not stop you. You give the end measure and write that the start was not taken.

An honest gap costs you nothing. A number you cannot show costs you the whole record.

Say the honest-gap line once and leave it on the board. Pupils who think a missing number fails them will invent one.

You need three things on the desk. Your challenge log, the outcome frame, and the verification list.

Three words matter today. Measure. Change. Evidence.

3 minStarter · predict

Starter · create the need

Two ways of closing the same challenge

Statement A says: I got much better at my challenge and I worked hard every week.

Statement B says: in week one I walked the marked route in fourteen minutes, and in week seven the same route in eleven.

Both pupils did the same work. Only one of them can prove it.

A moderator was not in the room. They cannot see effort, and they are not allowed to guess.

They read what is written and check it against the log.

Statement A gives them nothing to check, so it earns nothing.

Statement B is shorter and does more. Numbers are what travel out of the room.

  • 1 · name the challenge from the approved list.
  • 2 · give the start measure.
  • 3 · give the end measure from the same test.
  • 4 · name the evidence that backs both.

7 minI Do · model

I Do · model

Building one outcome statement

My challenge is on the approved list. Walk the marked route three times a week.

First I read the log, not my memory. Week one says fourteen minutes, marked route, same shoes.

That is the start measure. I copy it exactly, with its conditions.

The conditions travel with the number. A time with no route attached cannot be repeated by anyone.

Then the end measure. I walked the same route this morning and the log says eleven minutes.

Same route, same shoes, same way of timing it. That is what makes the two numbers a pair.

Then the change. Three minutes faster over seven weeks.

I write the change in the same unit as the measures. Minutes against minutes, not a percentage.

Last comes the evidence. My log page, initialled by the adult who timed me on both days.

Name each part aloud as you write it. The frame is what is being taught here, not the walking.

Watch what I leave out. I do not write that I found it hard, or that I enjoyed it.

That belongs in the reflection on the next page. The outcome statement carries numbers and evidence only.

3 minWe Do · choose

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the statements, match the gap

Six closing statements are cut into cards on your table.

Sort the six cards into two groups. Provable, and not provable.

Then match each not-provable card to what it is missing. The start measure, the end measure, or the evidence.

Take two cards each. Say your group out loud before you put a card down. The table hears every decision.

When your table has finished, put a hand on the card you argued about most.

Sort first, talk second. A card you cannot place is a finding, not a failure.

7 minI Do 2 · test

I Do 2 · connect

Same test, or the comparison breaks

Here is the trap. In week one I timed the marked route. In week seven I timed a shorter one.

The two numbers still look like a pair. They are not. They measure different things.

A moderator who checks the route on your log will find that, and the outcome falls.

So the rule is short. Repeat the same test, in the same conditions, and record both.

Sometimes the test has to change. An injury, a closed gym, a room you could not use.

That is allowed, and it does not spoil your record.

You write what changed and why, on the same line as the end measure.

A recorded change can be verified. A hidden change reads like a false number.

Now connect the statement to the verification list. Every outcome needs one named adult who saw the end measure taken.

Their initials go on the log page, next to the number, not on the frame.

  • 1 · same activity, same conditions.
  • 2 · same unit for both measures.
  • 3 · any change written down on the day.
  • 4 · one named adult who saw the end measure.

5 minWe Do 2 · rehearse

We Do 2 · lab

Could a stranger check yours?

Work in twos. Say your statement out loud from your log, and nothing else.

They act as the moderator. They did not see you do it, and they may not ask what you meant.

They answer with one of three lines. I could check that. I could not check that. The two numbers do not match.

If they could not check it, they name the missing part.

You add that part and say it again.

Then swap over and do the same for theirs.

Do not repair your partner's sentence for them. Naming the gap is the job.

9 minIndependent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Write the outcome and name its proof

Work on your own now. Write the statement you said aloud into the frame, for your own challenge.

Start measure, end measure, the change, then the evidence. Copy every number from the log, not from memory.

Write the conditions beside each measure. Route, count, time of day, whatever your log records.

If the test changed at any point, write what changed on the same line as the end measure.

Then underline both measures and ring the named evidence.

Then read your statement against the verification list and tick each line it passes.

Where a line does not pass, write what would close it and who could sign it.

An unticked line with a named fix is worth more than a tick you cannot back.

  • Supported · the end measure with its conditions, and the evidence named.
  • Standard · start measure, end measure, the change and the evidence, checked against the verification list.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name the verification line your file still fails and who can close it.

If you finish early, write the next challenge you would set, with its start measure already chosen.

3 minExit · hand forward

Exit · Audience and Influence

Read it to the adult who signs it

Read your outcome statement to the adult at your table.

They read it back word for word and say whether they could verify it.

If they could not, change the part they name. Leave the rest alone.

Then choose what happens next. File it as it stands, collect the missing evidence, or set the next challenge.

Write that choice on the frame with today's date before you leave.

The frame goes into your ASDAN folder in front of the log, not into your bag.

A moderator reads the folder in that order.
