LAUNCH · ASDAN · Week 1. ASDAN cross-strand. Choose Our Community Need, and Launch My Challenge.

Objective: Choose one community need for our project from the approved photographs and survey cards. Choose one enrichment challenge to launch from the approved challenge cards. For each one, say what you can point at and who will check it.

Success evidence: one class need agreed and put on the board, and one challenge on your sheet with what you will do, how often, until when, who checks it, and the date it starts, signed today.

Every photograph, need card, survey card and challenge card is supplied by school. You are never asked about home, family or health.

40 minutes. Offline.

Read the four short lines once and leave them on the board.

Two choices, one rule

Today we start two things. Our class picks one community need. You pick one challenge.

A need is something people here do not have and would use.

Evidence is something you can point at. A photograph, an approved survey card, or something that happened in school.

A challenge says what you will do, how often, and by when.

An enrichment challenge is one you choose and do in school time. We say challenge for short.

The maybe pile is the space in the middle of the table. A card goes there when we cannot check it yet.

If you are not sure, you write not sure. That is a real answer and it counts.

Set up before pupils arrive: cards and photographs face up on every table. Each pupil needs three need cards, one record sheet and a pen. Each table needs the photograph set, the survey cards, the challenge card box, and a clear space in the middle for the maybe pile.
If a pupil offers something personal, point back to a card and do not comment on what they said. Every example today comes from the approved cards, never from a pupil's own life.

Everything you use today comes from school.

  • 1 · notice which card names a place and a thing
  • 2 · choose the card you could point at in a photograph

You cannot make better. Better is not a thing. Nobody can point at it.

Card B can be worked on. Someone could stand there and see what is missing.

Turning a photograph into a need we can work on

NEED means something people here do not have and would use. EVIDENCE means what you can point at. CHALLENGE means what you will do, how often, and by when.

Look at the picture. The four boxes are my four steps, left to right. Photograph, name it, who uses it, our check.

I take photograph three. I do not decide yet.

First I say only what I can see. A path, people walking on it, and nowhere to sit.

Then I name the need in one short sentence. There is nowhere to sit on the path by the gate.

Then I check who would use it. Survey card two says twelve people rest on that path.

Then I check we can act on it. We have seven weeks in school time, so the need must be small.

I am unsure about one thing. I do not know who looks after that path. I do not guess. I write not sure, and the card goes on the maybe pile.

Everyone writes an order before anyone speaks.

Put three needs in order, then pick ours

You have three need cards. Each need card has a photograph number on it.

Look at each card and ask one question. How much of this can I point at?

Put them in order. Number one is the need with the most you can point at.

Write your order down first. Nobody speaks yet.

Then two people say why their number one is first. We do not all have to agree.

Now we pick one need for our project. Hold up your number one card.

We count the cards people held up. Then we say what we can point at on the top cards.

The card with the most we can point at goes on the board. That card is our class need.

The other need cards go on the maybe pile.

Take the class decision even if it is rough. Count the number one cards, ask two pupils what they can point at on the leading cards, then put one card on the board and say out loud that this is our project need. If it is close, take the card with the clearest photograph evidence and move on.

From our class need to my challenge

Our class need is one choice. Your challenge is the second. Both are chosen the same way.

Look at the picture. The left box is our need, shown in a photograph. The right box is my challenge, signed by an adult.

I take a challenge card from the box. This one says photography, thirty minutes a week, for six weeks.

I read what the card asks for. What I will do. How often. Until when. Who checks it.

The card says a tutor signs the sheet each week.

So I write today's date as the day I start. I ask the tutor to sign my sheet now. My challenge has started.

If a card does not say who checks it, I put it back and ask.

Some challenges point at our project. The photography card can take pictures of the place on our need card.

Only cards from the box are used today. An adult reads the card with you first.

We never say a challenge is healthy or good for you. We say what you will do and who checks it.

Open the challenge card box only once a need card is on the board. Approve cards from the box only. If a pupil wants a challenge that is not in the box, say it has to be checked first, and offer a card that is in the box.

This is doing time. Do not add reading to it.

Your record

You can write, draw, or place the cards straight onto the sheet.

If it is easier, point at the card and an adult writes it for you.

Record one community need. Write the photograph number and the one thing you can point at in it.

Standard and Stretch: write the need as one short sentence, like the one I wrote.

Stretch: record who would use it. Use a survey card for this, not a guess.

Record one challenge card. Write what you will do, how often, until when, and who checks it.

Write the date you start. Ask the adult to sign your sheet today, so the challenge has started.

Write not sure next to anything you could not check. Do not fill a gap with a guess.

If you finish early, put a second need card on the maybe pile.

Work at your own pace. One need with real evidence beats three with none.

Audience receives; Influence changes one next action

You do not have to speak. You can point at your sheet instead. Pointing counts the same as saying it.

Nobody has to explain a choice in front of the class.

Say two things. The need you chose, and the challenge you chose.

The adult names both back to you, using your exact words.

You then confirm or correct. If the adult got it wrong, say so, and we change it now.

Then choose one next action. Keep your need, swap it for another card, or move it to the maybe pile.

Our class need is the card on the board. Your sheet can name a different need, and that is allowed.

Your sheet is kept and comes back next week.

Name the pupil's words back exactly before you answer. This is the step that makes the choice binding, so do not drop it. If time is short, take the need only and leave the challenge, and have the TA take half the room so every pupil is heard.
