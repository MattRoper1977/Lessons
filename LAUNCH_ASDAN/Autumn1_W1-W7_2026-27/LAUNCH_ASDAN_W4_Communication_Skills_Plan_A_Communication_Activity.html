LAUNCH · ASDAN · Week 4. ASDAN cross-strand. Plan the Talk, Name the Partner, Pick the Skill.

Objective: plan one communication activity on the approved form, name the local group you will work with and what you will ask them, and choose one personal skill to develop with the evidence it will leave.

Success evidence: a completed activity plan, a named partner with one written question, and one skill chosen with the evidence it will produce.

Partners come from the school's approved contacts list. No personal contact details are written on any sheet.

40 minutes. Offline.

Arrival · retrieve

INFLUENCE

Three things that only work together

A communication activity with nobody to communicate with is a rehearsal.

A partner with nothing planned to say to them is a phone number.

A skill with no evidence attached is a claim about yourself.

Today you do all three, because in this unit each one is what makes the next one assessable.

The order matters: plan the activity, name who it is for, then say what you get better at doing it.

The approved contacts list is the only source for a partner. Do not let a pupil write a personal contact.

You need the activity plan form, the approved contacts list, and the skills framework.

Everything here is planned this week and carried out next week. Nothing is contacted today.

That gap is deliberate: a plan you have slept on is the one you can actually run.

Read the whole form before you write anything in it. The last box changes what belongs in the first.

Starter · create the need

SPACE

Two activity plans

Plan A: talk to a local charity about what they do.

Plan B: ring the food bank coordinator on the contacts list, ask what three items they run short of in December, and write the answers on the shared sheet.

Plan A could be done by anybody, on any day, and nobody would know if it had happened.

Plan B names the person, the question and where the answer goes.

That is the difference between an intention and an activity.

  • 1 · name the activity and the day.
  • 2 · name the partner from the list.
  • 3 · write the question you will ask.
  • 4 · say where the answer is recorded.

Read plan A once more and try to say which day it happens on. You cannot.

That is what makes it an intention rather than an activity.

A plan somebody else could run without asking you a question is a finished plan.

Filling the form in the order that works

I start with the activity, not the partner. What am I actually going to do?

Mine is a five-minute phone call, on Thursday, from the office with an adult present.

Now the partner. I take the food bank coordinator from the approved list.

Then the question, written out in full before I pick up the phone.

An unwritten question becomes three vague questions once you are nervous.

Last, the skill. Mine is speaking on the phone to someone I do not know.

The evidence will be the written answers, plus the adult's note that I made the call myself.

Write the question in front of them, slowly. Pupils under-plan this stage more than any other.

The adult present is part of the plan, not an afterthought. Write their name in.

I write the day and the room, not just the day. A plan with no room does not happen.

Sort the questions

Six question cards are on your table. Sort them into answerable and not answerable.

What do you need in December? Are you a good charity? How many people came last week?

Do you like your job? What time do you open on Saturdays? Is what you do important?

Then match each answerable one to where its answer would be written down.

When your table is done, hold up the one you found hardest to place.

Two of the six can be answered but tell you nothing useful. Say which, and why.

Choosing a skill that leaves something behind

A skill you can evidence is one that produces a thing when you use it.

Speaking on the phone leaves written answers and an adult's note.

Being more confident leaves nothing, however true it is.

Pick the one that leaves something, and let the confidence follow it.

Write the evidence next to the skill, before you start.

If you cannot name the evidence now, choose a different skill.

The evidence column is filled in before the activity, not after. That is what makes it a plan.

If two skills both leave evidence, take the one you are worse at. This unit rewards the distance travelled.

5 minWe Do 2 · rehearse

We Do 2 · lab

SPACE
VOICE
AUDIENCE
INFLUENCE

Ask the question cold

Work in twos. Read your written question to your partner as if they were the partner organisation.

They answer as best they can and then say whether the question was clear.

If they had to guess what you meant, add one word and ask again.

Do not lengthen it. A longer question is usually a less clear one.

Then swap over and do the same for theirs.

Answer in role rather than as yourself. A vague question is easiest to spot when you try to answer it.

9 minIndependent · evidence

Independent · evidence

SPACE
VOICE
AUDIENCE
INFLUENCE

Form, partner, question, skill

Work on your own. Fill in the activity plan: what, when, and with which adult.

Name your partner from the approved contacts list.

Write your question out in full, in one sentence.

Then name the skill you are developing and the evidence it will leave.

If you finish early, write the second question you would ask if there is time.

  • Supported · name the activity, the partner and the question, with the skill said aloud.
  • Standard · complete the plan, the partner, the written question and the skill with its evidence.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and write the second question you would ask if there is time.

Write the question as one sentence. Two sentences is two questions and you will only get one answered.

Check the contacts list again before you write the name; the list is the only source.

Read your question aloud once before you write it down. Anything you stumble over needs shortening.

Leave the answer space blank. Filling it in now is guessing, and a guess in an evidence box is a problem.

3 minExit · hand forward

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE
VOICE
AUDIENCE
INFLUENCE

Read the question to an adult

Read your written question to the adult at your table.

They read it back and answer as if they were the partner.

If their answer is not the kind you wanted, change one word in the question.

Then choose what happens next: make the contact this week, next week, or change the partner.

Write that choice on the plan with today's date before you leave.

The plan goes into the unit folder and the contacts list stays on the desk.

Nothing is contacted today. The call happens next week with the adult named on your form.
