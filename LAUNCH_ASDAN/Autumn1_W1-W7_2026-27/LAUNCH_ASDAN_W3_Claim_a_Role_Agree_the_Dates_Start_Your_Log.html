LAUNCH ASDAN. Week 3. Slot 3.

I agree my role, our team aim and our timeline for the community project, and I begin my volunteering section by writing log row one.
A plan sheet with the agreed aim, a named role card and three dates. A volunteering log with row one written and the signature box left empty for an adult.

No pupil is asked about home, family or anything private. Every card, sheet and photograph comes from the approved project pack, not from your own life.

40 minutes. Offline. A screen or a visualiser is needed for the Starter and the I Do. If neither works, print sheet A and sheet B and model on a paper sheet held up.

Set up before pupils arrive. ON THE BOARD: the board card naming the job chosen in week 2, with the two candidate aim sentences on it. ON THE WALL: the date strip, showing start the 6th, an empty middle box, and finish the 20th. ON SCREEN OR VISUALISER: sheet A and sheet B from the approved pack. ON EACH TABLE: six numbered role cards, one commit slip per pupil, a blank plan sheet, a blank volunteering log sheet, a pen. ONE PROJECT FOLDER for finished sheets. If the wall strip is not up, write these on the board before pupils arrive: start ____, check ____ (leave this one blank until We Do), finish ____. Do not sign the logs yourself. Put the project folder on the class teacher's desk at the end. They sign the rows at the next session.

Cards are face up on your table. Do not write yet.

We check four things. Pointing at a card or the wall counts as speaking.

  • A project is a job with a start date and an end date. Say true or false.
  • Point to the board card. Read out the job we chose.
  • Point to the wall strip. Read out the start date.
  • A role is the part of the job that one person does. Say true or false.

If you are not sure, say I am not sure. That is a real answer.

If you were not here that week, say I was not here. That is a real answer too.

Read all four aloud once before anyone answers. Take pointing answers first, then voices. Never correct a wrong answer in front of the group. Let the pupil read the answer off the board card.

Three words

  • Aim: the one thing the team will finish.
  • Role: the part of the job you do.
  • Timeline: the dates the work happens on.

Watch me fill the example plan sheet. I say every step out loud.

Step one. I write the aim: refresh the reading corner by the 20th. This aim comes from the pack.

Step two. I take one role card and read it aloud: book sorter. I write my name on it.

Step three. I copy the dates from the wall strip. Start is the 6th. Finish is the 20th.

Step four. The middle box on the strip is empty. That is the check date. The group agrees it next. Then it goes on the strip and I copy it too. Three dates in all.

Step five. I do not know when the paint arrives. I do not guess. I write a question mark and the words ask the teacher.

Six role cards are on the table. Each card has a number.

Write one number on your slip. Keep it face down. Nobody speaks until every slip is down.

You may write a question mark if you are not sure.

On three, turn your slips over together.

Now say one reason. Say why you can do that role. Use a card, or something you did in school.

If two people pick the same number, both names go on the card. The job is shared.

Question marks are read out too. An adult offers you two cards to choose between.

Now the group agrees two things

An adult reads the two aim sentences on the board card. Point to the one you want.

The sentence with the most points is our aim. It goes on the board. Everyone copies that same sentence.

An adult then offers two check dates. Point to one. The agreed date goes in the empty middle box on the wall strip.

Protect the spoken reasons. If time is short, take three reasons now and the rest at the start of We Do 2. Before Independent, write the agreed aim on the board and the agreed check date on the wall strip.

Read all three rows before you choose.

Choose a row. You may change rows at any time. A row is a choice, not a label for you.

  • ◆ Copy the agreed aim from the board. Copy the three dates from the wall strip. Write today's date in log row one.
  • ▲ Write your role, the agreed aim sentence and the three dates. Then write log row one in your own words.

9 minIndependent · evidence

Independent · evidence

SPACE
VOICE
AUDIENCE
INFLUENCE
SPACE protects a genuine choice · VOICE records learner meaning · AUDIENCE names the receiving role · INFLUENCE is a bounded response and report-back.

This is your writing time. You are writing, not reading.

Record these four things.

  • The agreed aim sentence, copied from the board.
  • Your role card, with your name written on it.
  • Three dates from the wall strip: start, check and finish.
  • Log row one: date, task, minutes, and an empty signature box.

Work at your own speed.

Do one box at a time. A slow, correct box is better than a fast, wrong one.

If you are stuck on a box, write a question mark and move to the next box.

Put your hand flat on the table if you want an adult to come over.

Everything you write comes from the cards, the board and the wall strip.

You do not have to finish every box today. The plan can be added to next week.

When your sheet is done, put it in the project folder. That folder is your evidence.

Carry a copy of the three dates and circulate, so pupils copy them without leaving their seat. Do not read a pupil's sheet aloud.

SPACE protects a genuine choice · VOICE records learner meaning · AUDIENCE names the receiving role · INFLUENCE is a bounded response and report-back.

Say one sentence. Fill the two gaps with your own words.

My role is ____. My next date is ____.

For example: My role is book sorter. My next date is the 6th.

Pointing at your sheet counts as speaking.

An adult will say your sentence back to you, word for word.

You then say yes, or you correct it. Correcting the adult is expected here.

Then we choose one real next action together. It goes on the board and it happens.

Next actions look like this: bring your log on Tuesday, or an adult books the cupboard.

Every pupil gets a turn. A turn can be three words long.

Name it back exactly, including the words you would have tidied. The check is whether the pupil hears their own words, not a neater version.
