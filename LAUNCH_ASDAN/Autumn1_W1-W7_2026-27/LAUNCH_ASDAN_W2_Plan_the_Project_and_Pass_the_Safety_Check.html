LAUNCH ASDAN · Week 2 · Community Project, Enterprise and Vocational · Enrichment Award: Young Duke and PfA Transition.

Objective: I plan a school social-action or enterprise project on paper, and I complete the safety and first-aid challenge that makes the plan safe to run.

Success evidence: a plan sheet with five rows filled in, and an adult tick against the Stop, Tell, Stay challenge.

You will need: the approved project cards including card 3, the courtyard photograph, hazard photograph 2, the Stop, Tell, Stay sequencing cards, one plan sheet each, mini-whiteboards and pens, the adult tick sheet, and the project folder.

Nobody is asked about home, family, money or health. Every project card, photograph and scenario in this lesson is school-approved and supplied on the table.

40 minutes. Offline.

Before the lesson, get two facts and write them on the board: the named adult for this project, and where the first aid kit is. Ask the class TA, or read the room's first aid notice. If you cannot confirm one, pupils write not sure and the class teacher's name in that row, which this lesson's own rule allows, and no plan is run from a not-sure safety row. Miss Ford and Mr Blake are example names in the worked example only; say so if a pupil asks. The pack lives in the project folder box in the class cupboard, marked LAUNCH Week 2. If hazard photograph 2 is missing, use this classroom instead and name one hazard in the room.

What we agreed last week

Last week you chose a community need in school. You chose it from the approved cards.

  • A social-action project is work a group does to help other people.
  • An enterprise project is work a group does that also raises money.
  • A plan names the job, the people, the kit and the time.
  • A plan is not finished until it says how we keep people safe.

Point at any one you are not sure about. I am not sure is a real answer today.

Arrival decision. You can write on the plan sheet. You can point at the cards and an adult writes. Both are full ways to do this lesson.
Read the four statements aloud and take pointing as an answer. Do not open a discussion here. Three minutes, then move on.

Two plans for the same job

Two plans for approved project card 3. The card says: tidy the courtyard planters.

Plan A says: we will tidy the planters. It will be great.

Plan B says: four people, Tuesday, twenty minutes. Gloves, two bags, litter pickers. Miss Ford is the named adult. If someone is hurt, we stop and tell her.

Both plans have the same job. Only one of them can be run on Tuesday.

Plan A cannot be run. Nobody knows who is coming, what to bring, or who to call. That is what today fixes.

  • Notice: find one thing Plan B has that Plan A does not.
  • Choose: point at the plan your table could run on Tuesday.
  • Explain: say one thing Plan A is missing.

Three words, then five rows

A plan says what will happen, who does it, and when.

A hazard is something that could hurt someone.

A control is what we do so the hazard does not hurt anyone.

Watch me fill in card 3. The sheet has five rows. I say every row out loud.

Row 1 is the job. I copy the card. Tidy the courtyard planters.

Row 2 is the people. Four names. Tuesday, twenty minutes.

Row 3 is the kit. Thick gloves, two bin bags, two litter pickers. Row 3 is only a list.

Row 4 is one hazard and its control, on the same row. I look at the courtyard photograph. I can see broken glass. I write broken glass. Then the control, on the same row: nobody picks up glass with their hands. We lift it with the litter picker from Row 3.

Row 5 is safety. The named adult, and where the first aid kit is. Both are on the board today, or we write not sure and who to ask.

Now the part I do not know. I am not sure the gate is open on Tuesday. I do not guess. I write: not sure, ask Mr Blake.

A row marked not sure is a finished row. A guessed row is not.

The safety row, and the three steps

Now we put the plan and the safety steps together. A plan is not approved until Row 5 is done.

If someone is hurt, we use the same three steps every time.

Stop. Put the tools down and stop the job.

Tell. Tell the named adult. Say who is hurt and where they are.

Stay. Stay with the person until the adult comes. The only reason to leave is if an adult tells you to go and fetch someone.

If the named adult is not there, you tell any adult. You never wait and you never carry on with the job.

You are not the first aider. The first aider is a trained adult. Your job is the three steps.

You may not know how bad it is. You do not decide that. Not sure is still Stop, Tell, Stay.

That is the safety challenge. You pass it by saying the three steps in order and pointing at the named adult on the board.

Fill the five rows. Pass the challenge.

Now you do it. There is nothing to read.

Fill five rows on your plan sheet. Use your table's approved card.

  • Row 1: the job, copied from the card.
  • Row 2: the people. Who does each part, and when.
  • Row 3: the kit. What you need to bring.
  • Row 4: one hazard, and its control, on the same row.
  • Row 5: safety. The named adult, and where the first aid kit is.

Then do the challenge with an adult. Say Stop, Tell, Stay in order. Point at the named adult on your plan.

Work at your own pace. A row you understand is worth more than a row you guessed.

If a row is unknown, write not sure and who you would ask. That row still counts.

You can point at the cards and have an adult write your words. That is still your record.

Everything on this sheet comes from the approved cards and from school. Nothing on it is about home.

Circulate with the tick sheet. Tick the challenge when you have seen the learner do it, not when they say they can. Many learners need Row 4 read back to them once before they can write the control.

Say it. Hear it back. Change one thing.

Two tables speak today. Every other table hands the plan sheet in.

One sentence from each of the two tables. Say the first job you will do, and the one hazard you will control.

An adult writes your sentence down. The adult reads it back to you word for word.

You say yes, that is right. Or you correct it. That is your job in this bit. We want you to correct it.

Pointing at your sheet counts as speaking. The adult reads the sheet back the same way.

Next action. One real next action is chosen before you leave. It goes on the plan with a name and a day.
Two tables only, or this stage runs long. You choose the next action after the two sentences: who checks the kit, and on which day. Write it on that table's plan. All plan sheets go in the project folder.
