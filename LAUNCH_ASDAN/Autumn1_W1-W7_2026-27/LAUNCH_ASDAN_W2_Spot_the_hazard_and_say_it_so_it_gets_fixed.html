LAUNCH ASDAN. Week 2. Launch slot.

Objective: I can spot a hazard on a home safety card and a germ problem on a workplace hygiene card, and give one clear report that names the place, the problem and the action.

Success evidence: a record sheet with a card number, a hazard and a report sentence for at least two cards. At least one card must be a home card. At least one card must be a workplace hygiene card.

Every photograph, card and scenario comes from the approved school pack. You are never asked about your own home, your family or your health.

40 minutes. Offline.

Materials. From the approved pack, have Card 3, Card 7 and Card 12 at the front. Card 7 is used in the starter, Card 12 in the We Do, and you hold Card 7 and Card 12 up together in I Do 2, then put both back on a table. Each table needs six cards: three home safety cards and three workplace cards. Every workplace card must show something that lets germs spread, such as raw food on a salad board. Print one record sheet per pupil and one print pack per table. No A or B cards are needed. Pupils vote with fingers.

Read each statement. Say true, false, or I am not sure.

  • A hazard is a thing that could hurt a person.
  • Communication works when the other person understands you and can act.
  • Hygiene means keeping things clean so germs do not spread.
  • I am not sure is a real answer. It is not a wrong answer.

You may point at your answer on the sheet instead of speaking. Pointing counts.

Take the four in any order. Do not correct a wrong answer yet. Tick each statement on the board as the lesson proves it. If a pupil starts to talk about their own home or family, thank them, go back to the card, and tell the DSL after the lesson.

Card 7 is on your table. It is a school photograph of a kitchen from the approved pack.

Two people looked at Card 7. Both reported it.

Report A says: it is messy in there.

Report B says: there is water on the floor by the sink.

The site team can act on Report B. The site team cannot act on Report A. Report A does not say where. It does not say what.

A report that gets a hazard fixed has three parts.

  • Notice. Point at the one thing that could hurt someone.
  • Choose. Name the place and name the problem.
  • Explain. Say what you want done about it.

Three words

  • Hazard: a thing that could hurt someone.
  • Hygiene: keeping things clean so germs do not spread.
  • Clear report: one sentence with the place, the problem and the action.

Watch me do one. Card 3 is a school photograph of a workshop bench.

First I look. I look at the floor, the bench and the bin.

Now I name it. I can see a cable across the floor where people walk.

Now I say it. There is a cable across the floor by the bench. Please can it be taped down.

Now watch what I do when I am not sure. On Card 3 there is a bottle with no label. I do not guess.

I say: there is a bottle with no label on the bench. I am not sure what is in it. Please can someone check it.

I am not sure is part of the report. It tells the adult to check.

There is (the problem) in (the place). Please can (the action).

Card 12 is a school photograph of a cafe kitchen. Everyone look at it.

Report A says: the kitchen is not very nice. Report B says: there is raw chicken on the same board as the salad.

Choose A or B now. Do not say it yet.

On three, everyone shows at once. One finger means A. Two fingers mean B.

Now we reason. Say one reason for your choice. A good reason names a part the other report is missing.

You may change your mind after you hear a reason. Say: I have changed my mind.

Count to three out loud so the reveal is together. Fingers flat on the desk count as showing. Take a reason from two pupils, not from everyone.

Card 7 was a home kitchen. Card 12 was a work kitchen. The hazards were different. The report was built the same way.

Water on the floor makes a person slip. That is true on the home card and on the work card. The place changes. The risk does not.

Raw chicken touching salad lets germs spread. That is a hygiene hazard. The hygiene rule is the same at home and at work.

One thing is different at work. You must tell someone straight away. Reporting is part of the job.

So one skill covers all three. Spot the hazard. Say it in one clear sentence. Use it on both cards.

Both photographs are school cards from the approved pack. You are never asked about your own home.

Six cards are on the table. Three are home safety cards. Three are workplace cards. All are school photographs.

Every workplace card shows a hygiene hazard. Something in the photograph lets germs spread.

Choose a row. You may change row at any time. A row is a choice, not a label.

  • ◆ Take two cards: one home card and one workplace card. Use the sentence to borrow. Read it out, or point at it.
  • ▲ Take three cards. At least one home card and at least one workplace card. Say one report for each card. Each report names the place, the problem and the action.
  • ★ Take four cards. At least one home card and at least one workplace card. Say one report for each card. On one card, say I am not sure what this is, please check.

On a workplace card, name the thing that lets germs spread.

Say your best report to one other person before you write anything down.

This is the doing part. You are making the record. There is nothing to read.

Your record sheet has three rows for each card.

  • The card number, and whether it is a home card or a work card.
  • The hazard you can point at in the photograph.
  • Your report sentence.

Your record must have at least one home card and at least one workplace card. On the workplace card, write the thing that lets germs spread.

Fill in as many cards as you can. Two finished cards are better than five half done ones.

Work at your own pace. You may stop and come back. You may use the sentence to borrow at any time. You may swap a card.

If you cannot name a thing, write I am not sure, then write what you can see. That row is finished, not failed.

Every hazard you write must be one you can point at in the photograph.

Circulate and check every sheet has a home card row and a workplace card row. Prompt with where and what, never with the answer. Keep whole class talk out.

Three people read out today. Everyone else hands the sheet in and reads out next lesson.

Say the best report you wrote today. Pointing at your sheet counts as saying it.

The adult says your report back to you, word for word.

Then you say yes, that is right, or you correct it. Correcting the adult is allowed and useful.

Then we choose one next action together. The action happens here in school.

  • Show the report to the site team.
  • Read the report out at the start of the next lesson.
  • Put the card and the report on the safety board.

The adult writes the action on your sheet. You keep the sheet.

Say the three pupil cap before the first pupil starts, so nobody is left waiting. Collect the other sheets and open the next lesson with those read backs.
