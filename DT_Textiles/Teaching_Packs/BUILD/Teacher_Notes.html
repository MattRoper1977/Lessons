This guide contains answers. Open the lesson slideshow for the pupil view. Use fictional examples and accept speech, pointing, drawing or adult scribing.

The Lundy Loop appears at arrival and exit. Make the response options real, check the pupil’s meaning, then agree one next step or adjustment to support. No personal response needs to be stored.

Week 5 · 40 minutes

Meet the materials and joins

Open lesson slideshow

Objective

Describe two fabric samples, recognise three joining methods and record a supported comparison.

  • I can show or name two fabric properties.
  • I can identify glue, tape and a prepared stitch join.
  • I can use one result to choose a join or ask for another check.

Preparation

  • Print specimen, join and sample-data cards; provide known clean samples with accessible labels A–E.
  • Prepare 100 × 30 mm fabric strips and 20 × 30 mm overlaps; pre-cut where needed. Use only approved cold adhesives and check material sensitivities privately.
  • Prepare cured glue/tape specimens and a stitched display sample before the lesson, following actual product instructions. The default comparison uses supplied sample data.

Materials

  • Known clean cotton/felt/denim/jersey/net samples or the complete property cards; paper/card alternative.
  • Adult-prepared strips, ruler, pencil/chalk, school-approved cold fabric glue and tape, labelled drying tray, adult-prepared stitched display.
  • If an adult demonstration is approved: sealed 100 g test load, tray and suitable prepared holding fixture; pupils do not handle the load.

40-minute plan

TimeActivity
3 minArrive, choose role and introduce the question.
6 minObserve or read material properties.
6 minModel three join methods and fair comparison.
12 minMake/inspect joins and label a drying sample.
7 minInterpret sample results and match a join to a job.
4 minCount and return scissors, tools and sample pieces; close approved adhesive containers, collect tape backings and scraps, wipe the work surface as directed and wash hands if needed.
2 minIndividual evidence-and-limit exit.

Vocabulary

  • property: A feature of a material that can be observed or tested.
  • join: A connection between two pieces.
  • overlap: The area where one piece lies on another.
  • fair test: A comparison that keeps relevant conditions consistent.

Model the task

Bend a specimen, then give an observation rather than a universal claim. Trace the prepared stitched join. Model the distinction between a fresh sample and one ready for testing, then count successes across all three sample repeats.

Support

BUILD core uses two samples, one property contrast at a time and one test row. Offer pre-cut strips, adult-positioned tape and a paper alternative. The pupil can read, point, direct, make or record. Record the actual support and keep inspection distinct from making.

Stretch

Explain one property or join choice using a because sentence, then name one check still needed. Extend only after the core practical or paper-model task is secure.

Fallback

Use the complete specimen descriptions and invented test record. Learners sequence preparation, curing, testing and recording, and inspect a drawing or written stitch description without tools.

Safety and sensitive discussion

Follow the school practical-work risk assessment and individual access plans. The default lessons use no needles, pins, irons, hot glue, cord loops or loose buttons/beads. Adults control, count and return scissors and any small test equipment; pre-cut pieces are available. Use only school-approved cold adhesives according to the actual product instructions, check skin/material sensitivities privately, and keep glue away from eyes and mouths. Touching fabric, wearing an item or accepting physical guidance is never required. Agree support; use demonstration, pointing, an observer role or a paper/card model. Keep personal identifiers and pupil photos off public website files.

What to check

Record a property with evidence, an accurate made/inspected distinction, a sample-result comparison and a reasoned join choice. Practical competence is recorded only for actions directly observed; no formal award is promised.

Common misconceptions

  • Bending is not the same as stretching.
  • Fabric name alone does not establish all properties.
  • A fresh adhesive sample cannot stand in for a correctly cured join.
  • The supplied data do not show that stitch always wins or that a real bag handle is safe.

B: soft, bends. E: bends or see-through. B is a sensible first choice; join still needs testing.

2. Name the joins

Glue; tape; stitch. Record the route actually used; prepared stitch inspection is not making stitches.

3. Read a result

Exit check

Resource answers

Fabric property cards
  • Pocket candidate: B, because this felt sample is soft, flexible and its edge stayed together. Its join still needs a suitable test.
  • See-through overlay: E. Its holes would be unsuitable for containing loose tiny items.
  • Stretch candidate: D. The amount of stretch and recovery must suit the real job.
  • A fabric name alone does not prove a property, suitability or join strength. Check the actual sample.
Three join cards | What changes?
  • A glue specimen must cure as directed before its performance can be judged.
  • Cold tape and iron-on tape are different products; the pack uses only the cold type.
  • Inspecting a stitched sample is valid learning, but must not be recorded as making a stitch.
  • Accept choices supported by the job and evidence; do not award a universal “thread wins” answer.
Join test | Sample data and fair comparison
  • Glue held in 3 of 3 sample tests; tape in 1 of 3; stitch in 2 of 3.
  • In this supplied test, glue gave the most consistent holding result. This does not prove glue is always strongest or suitable for a heavy bag.
  • Repeat 1 alone gives glue and stitch a tie. The other repeats add evidence.
  • Do not compare fresh glue with fully prepared tape/stitch and call that a fair test of the methods.

Week 6 · 40 minutes

Design my card pocket

Open lesson slideshow

Objective

Choose a user, material and decoration, then communicate a simple labelled plan using the supplied pattern.

  • I can choose a purpose and a material.
  • I can show the joins, folds and decoration on my plan.
  • I can put three making stages in order.

Preparation

  • Print the exact-size pattern, 50 mm scale check, brief and two original inspiration cards. Check print scale before class.
  • Provide 100 × 170 mm paper blanks and 60 × 50 mm test cards for dry-fold modelling; no adhesive is required today.
  • Trial the intended cold fabric tape with the actual fabric before Week 7. Choose paper/card model if tape cannot hold or be handled in the session under its product instructions.

Materials

  • Pattern, ruler, pencils/chalk, large paper, brief/idea/checklist cards, optional approved marker.
  • Known fabric samples or property descriptions; pre-cut paper blanks and test cards.
  • For flat-decoration choice: 100 × 70 mm backing and 60 × 50 mm front-panel paper models.

40-minute plan

TimeActivity
3 minRecall one useful property and introduce user choice.
6 minRead brief and compare original ideas.
7 minModel dimensions, joins and a labelled drawing.
13 minCreate a labelled plan and dry-fold paper model.
6 minPeer check, improve and sequence making steps.
3 minStore plans, collect pattern pieces and reset.
2 minIndividual first-check exit.

Vocabulary

  • brief: The job and checks that a design must satisfy.
  • prototype: A first version made to explore and test a design.
  • allowance: Extra space reserved for a join or construction step.
  • dimension: A measured length, width or height.

Model the task

Show the blank with the short edge at the top. Measure 30 mm then 100 mm from that same origin, not from the first fold. Trace the two side strips; demonstrate why tape across the opening would defeat the pocket’s job.

Support

BUILD: offer two user/symbol choices, a pre-marked blank and ready-made labels. Show one fold at a time. Pupils can trace, point, dry-fold, direct a partner or dictate a plan. Maintain the same respectful purpose; do not judge artistic neatness.

Stretch

Explain one property or join choice using a because sentence, then name one check still needed. Extend only after the core practical or paper-model task is secure.

Fallback

All inspiration, dimensions and checks are included in print. Use the paper model only; if ruler access is difficult, offer exact pre-cut shapes and measure together.

Safety and sensitive discussion

Follow the school practical-work risk assessment and individual access plans. The default lessons use no needles, pins, irons, hot glue, cord loops or loose buttons/beads. Adults control, count and return scissors and any small test equipment; pre-cut pieces are available. Use only school-approved cold adhesives according to the actual product instructions, check skin/material sensitivities privately, and keep glue away from eyes and mouths. Touching fabric, wearing an item or accepting physical guidance is never required. Agree support; use demonstration, pointing, an observer role or a paper/card model. Keep personal identifiers and pupil photos off public website files.

What to check

Look for user/purpose, testable checks, dimensioned material/join labels, a feasible sequence and a revision based on feedback. Assess design communication rather than drawing neatness; distinguish paper model from fabric construction.

Common misconceptions

  • A picture without dimensions and join labels may be hard for another maker to follow.
  • Both folds are measured from the original top, not consecutively.
  • Decoration must not close the opening or hide a faulty join.
  • A prototype plan is not evidence that the finished join has passed a test.

Booklet answers

1. Choose the job

Accept one coherent selection. Pocket must contain the card; flat decoration must keep its panels joined and show a symbol.

2. Show the design

Pocket: bottom panel up, flap down, two side joins and clear opening. Flat decoration: centred 60 × 50 mm front on 100 × 70 mm backing, with two side joins and no folds. Accept labelled drawing, tracing, pointing or dictated evidence.

3. Put the stages in order

Check; construct the chosen pocket or flat panel; test when ready. Credit a real added label, dimension, symbol position or join mark.

Exit check

So the maker leaves space for the card instead of joining every edge. The flat decoration does not need an opening.

Resource answers

Design brief | A small card pocket
  • The design is judged against purpose, card fit, side-join function and completeness of the plan. Colour preference alone cannot establish those checks.
  • A 60 mm-wide card fits the planned 80 mm internal width with 20 mm overall clearance, before material thickness and construction variation; test the real model.
  • No load-bearing or waterproof claim is made.
Pattern and fold guide | Exact dimensions
  • Top-origin fold coordinates: y = 30 mm and y = 100 mm; bottom panel height = 70 mm.
  • Internal width estimate: 100 − 10 − 10 = 80 mm.
  • Front panel folds upwards; the top flap folds down. Closing all four edges would trap the card.
Two original design ideas | Compare and change
  • For Alex, a large symbol gives a specific purpose-based reason. For Rowan, a broad stripe can satisfy the stated preference.
  • Many colour and symbol choices are valid; assess labels, purpose and feasibility, not which design the adult prefers.
  • A flat decoration has a different function: check both panel joins stay attached after three gentle lifts from the desk. It is a legitimate simpler textile-item alternative, not a failed pocket.
Plan-review checklist
  • Missing details should lead to one clear revision, such as adding 30/100 mm fold marks or specifying the two side strips.
  • A valid plan for the flat decoration must use its different two-panel dimensions and attachment test rather than the pocket-card test.

Week 7 · 40 minutes

Make my item and check it

Open lesson slideshow

Objective

Follow a supported making plan, check the item against its purpose and name a next step.

  • I can follow or direct the agreed making steps.
  • I can show what happened in a check.
  • I can name one thing that worked or needs changing.

Preparation

  • Retrieve Week 6 plans. Prepare/pre-cut 100 × 170 mm blanks, two 10 × 70 mm tape strips and 60 × 50 mm paper test cards; premark folds where useful.
  • Trial the actual cold tape/material combination and follow its pressing/handling/cure instructions. If readiness extends beyond class, plan the evaluation using a cured model or fictional records.
  • Prepare a paper model for every pupil who prefers it. Ensure the flat-decoration alternative has 100 × 70 mm backing, 60 × 50 mm front and two 10 × 50 mm joining strips.

Materials

  • Prepared fabric/felt or paper blanks, appropriate school-approved cold tape, ruler, pencil/chalk, approved marker and paper test card.
  • Week 6 plan, exact pattern, construction card, brief and fictional prototype records.
  • Adult-controlled counted scissors if needed, table protection, waste tray and flat storage space.

40-minute plan

TimeActivity
3 minRetrieve plan and choose role/material route.
5 minAdult approval and model folds/joins.
17 minConstruct planned prototype or paper model.
7 minRecord function evidence and targeted improvement/retest or sample verdict.
6 minCount and return scissors, tools and sample pieces; close approved adhesive containers, collect tape backings and scraps, wipe the work surface as directed and wash hands if needed.
2 minIndividual design-cycle exit.

Vocabulary

  • construct: Put the planned parts together.
  • criterion: One check in the design brief.
  • evaluate: Judge how well the item meets its stated checks.
  • retest: Repeat a check after making a change.

Model the task

Dry-fold a paper blank; show the open top and two side-strip positions. Model one gentle test cycle and a truthful “not yet” record if a join opens. Explain that a repair requires the product’s proper preparation before retesting.

Support

BUILD: pre-cut and premark all parts; offer one demonstrated action, then pause for a check. Accept a maker-director role, a paper model, pointing to results or adult scribing. Adult support is agreed and recorded without claiming independence.

Stretch

Explain one property or join choice using a because sentence, then name one check still needed. Extend only after the core practical or paper-model task is secure.

Fallback

Build the exact paper model or evaluate all three supplied prototype records. A learner can meet the evaluation objective without handling fabric or adhesive; label the route used.

Safety and sensitive discussion

Follow the school practical-work risk assessment and individual access plans. The default lessons use no needles, pins, irons, hot glue, cord loops or loose buttons/beads. Adults control, count and return scissors and any small test equipment; pre-cut pieces are available. Use only school-approved cold adhesives according to the actual product instructions, check skin/material sensitivities privately, and keep glue away from eyes and mouths. Touching fabric, wearing an item or accepting physical guidance is never required. Agree support; use demonstration, pointing, an observer role or a paper/card model. Keep personal identifiers and pupil photos off public website files.

What to check

Observe plan use, an agreed safe action, accurate evidence recording and a targeted improvement. Mark met/not yet/need evidence against the selected pocket or decoration brief; do not infer practical competence from a finished photo alone.

Common misconceptions

  • A tidy appearance does not prove function.
  • Opening on cycle 2 does not meet a 3-cycle holding check.
  • Decorating cannot fix a structural join.
  • A model or sample-data judgement must not be recorded as tested fabric construction.

Booklet answers

1. What I did

Accept accurate route and action. Do not record a model as a tested fabric product.

2. What the check showed

Fictional pocket: worked; not yet; worked. For a real decoration, record panel centring, both joins after three gentle lifts and the symbol. Record actual results; do not use the pocket’s sample results for a decoration.

3. One next step

Repair/replace or redesign the join; repeat the gentle check; leave it to cure and record not checked.

Exit check

A specific change linked to the brief or evidence, followed by the relevant recheck when safe.

Resource answers

Make the pocket | Complete construction card
  • Correct pocket folds: bottom up at 100 mm; flap down at 30 mm from the original top. Side tape belongs only on the bottom panel’s two outer 10 mm strips.
  • Expected pocket checks: test card slides in/out; both joins remain attached for three gentle cycles; planned symbol is visible; opening stays clear. Actual results may differ and must be recorded.
  • A not-yet-tested record is correct when adhesive or marker needs longer to cure; the pupil can evaluate the provided sample record meanwhile.
  • Flat decoration: finished backing 100 mm × 70 mm; centred front 60 mm × 50 mm; no card-containment claim.
Prototype records | Fictional results

  • C: dimensions and approval are recorded, but card fit and join function need evidence; the symbol is not yet added. Do not test before the join is ready.
