Watch the model

A sandwich is a product. Taking an order is a service. Both need safe, clean work. If water spills by the serving table, I stop, keep clear and tell the adult. I do not step through it to serve food.

Step 0 of 4

1. Stop and look for hazards. Keep the exit clear. Walk in the work area.

2. Tell the adult about a spill or a damaged tool. Keep clear. Do not try to fix it yourself.

3. Before food work, wash and dry hands. Use the gear the adult has checked. Gloves do not replace hand washing.

4. Keep food and garden kit apart. Check the work area with the adult before a task starts.

Words to use

hazard: Something that could cause harm.

hygiene: Habits that help keep us clean and well.

report: Tell the adult what you have seen.

product: An item or offer sold to a customer.

service: An action done for a customer.

range: The variety of things on offer.

Purpose and preparation

  • Health, Safety and Hygiene at Work
  • I can name key workplace safety rules.
  • I can describe good hygiene at work.
  • I can spot an unsafe practice.
  • Prepare: book, pencil, printed cards, word page and one task guide per pupil. Have an accessible seated option. No login or connection is needed.
  • This is one lesson. Suggested time: 40 minutes. Adjust for access; do not rush the practical checks.
  • Safety: The screen describes steps only. Any practical task is off screen, authorised and supervised. The adult checks the space, kit, water and task rules first. Use no hot, sharp, powered or chemical kit without the specific controls and training. Stop for spills or damaged kit. Dry card rehearsal remains available and is recorded as rehearsal.

Arrival and starter

  • Arrival - 4 minutes: Read the goal. Offer a quiet start and the usual aid. Show the picture without reading its labels. Invite the three recall answers in any mode. Keep the first-try count separate from later learning. A pass is welcome.
  • Starter - 4 minutes: Read the four case steps. Use the silent lights one at a time. Ask what comes next and why. Take the class vote; receive the reason before confirming the order.

Modelling and hinge

  • I do - 7 minutes: Reveal one task step at a time. Think aloud: A sandwich is a product. Taking an order is a service. Both need safe, clean work. If water spills by the serving table, I stop, keep clear and tell the adult. I do not step through it to serve food. Ask the hinge question before revealing the answer.
  • Hinge: Water is on the floor. What should you do first?
  • Expected: Stop, keep clear and tell the adult. The adult decides how it is made safe.
  • If a response is wrong or absent, check access first. Model one example, offer two choices and retest with a changed case. Move on when each pupil can show the key check.

Team rehearsal and independent work

  • We do - 7 minutes: Pass the pointer: each team gets one case card. One pupil points to a hazard; a partner gives a safe rule. The teacher moves between teams and checks each reason. Swap roles or pass. Scoring: 1 point for a hazard linked to its case; 1 point for a safe rule and who to tell. Each team can earn both points in each round. Ask one pupil per team to explain a check. If pupils need more help, return to one modelled example and retest privately.
  • Independent - 14 minutes: Offer the three routes as choices of support and depth. Read the book line. Keep the optional making choice optional. Observe each pupil, fade one prompt when secure and record actual help.
  • In your book, record a hazard, a safe rule and who you would tell. Add one product and one service from the food case.

Review exit and evidence

  • Exit - 4 minutes: Use the three exit questions. Ask for a success and a next step. Read back any scribed words for the pupil to confirm. Use the response to choose the next support or task.
  • Next we use safety rules while following instructions in order.
  • Evidence: record the individual action, response mode, task standard and exact support. A quiz score, group product or paper simulation does not prove practical competence. Use the unit award only where authorised and supported by actual evidence.
  • Feedback: receive the pupil response, give one next step and invite an edit in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode. Keep the original response visible.
  • Prompt ladder: wait; point to the guide; ask a short question; rephrase; give two choices; model the first step; return the task to the pupil.

Health, Safety and Hygiene at Work

Misconceptions

Gloves make hand washing unnecessary. Correction: Hands must still be washed and dried. Gear follows the task rules.

Only food work needs hygiene. Correction: Clean hands and tools matter in garden work too. Keep the two sets of kit apart.

I should fix every hazard myself. Correction: Stop, keep clear and report. The adult decides the next safe action.
