Pathways at a glance

Every lesson on this site is written for one of three pathways. The pathways are called BASE, TRACK and SUMMIT. Older pages still show the earlier names, BUILD, GROW and LAUNCH, until they are updated, so you will see both for a while. They mean the same thing.

BASE
shown as BUILD on some pages. The foundation pathway.
TRACK
shown as GROW on some pages. The middle pathway.
SUMMIT
shown as LAUNCH on some pages. The most demanding pathway.

The same topic is taught on all three. What changes is how much text a pupil reads, how much support sits on the page, how much of the work they do before an adult looks, and what the finished piece has to show. The table lower down puts the same lesson side by side at all three levels, because the difference between TRACK and SUMMIT is the one people most often ask about.

How every lesson is tiered

Inside every lesson, on every pathway, the main task comes in three tiers. A pupil picks the tier they start on, with the teacher's steer, and can move up during the lesson.

  • Supported. The task with scaffolding on the page: sentence starters, a word bank, a worked example or a part-filled frame.
  • Independent. The same task without the scaffolding. The pupil decides how to start and how to set it out.
  • Aspire. The same task with a harder demand: a second source, a counter-argument, a tighter word limit or a judgement to defend.
  • Choice. An extension, not a fourth tier. A pupil who has finished Aspire picks a direction of their own and takes the idea further.

The tiers do the same job on every pathway. What moves between pathways is the floor and the ceiling: the Supported tier on SUMMIT is harder than the Aspire tier on BASE.

How progress is recorded

Progress is recorded in books, not on a screen, as a short feedback loop that closes every time:

  1. Pupil response. The pupil writes a line about what they did and what they found hard. On BASE this can be one sentence or a tick against a statement.
  2. Adult acknowledgement. An adult reads it and writes back, briefly, so the pupil knows it was seen.
  3. Next step. One next step, in words the pupil can act on in the next lesson. Not a grade, and never more than one.

The loop is the same on every pathway. The next step gets more demanding as you go up, and on SUMMIT the pupil is expected to write part of it themselves.

BASE shown as BUILD on some pages

Who it is for

Pupils who need the ground made solid first. That might mean reading is still hard work, confidence is low, a long gap in schooling, or a day that needs to be calm before anything else can happen.

What a lesson looks like

Short, plain text, one idea at a time. A lesson opens with something to look at or do, not something to read. The key words are on the page and used again and again. Tasks are small and finishable, and every one ends with something the pupil can point to.

How tasks are tiered

Most pupils start on Supported. Independent is the goal inside the lesson, and reaching it is the win. Aspire is there for the pupil who wants it, and Choice is usually a drawing, a model or a spoken answer.

How progress is recorded

The feedback loop is kept very short. The pupil's response can be a sentence, a circled face or a tick. The adult writes back the same lesson, and the next step is one small, concrete thing: use the word bank for your first sentence.

TRACK shown as GROW on some pages

Who it is for

Pupils who can read a page of text with a little help, and who are ready to explain, not just describe. This is the pathway most pupils are on.

What a lesson looks like

A page or so of text with one source or one set of data. The lesson asks for reasons and evidence. There is a structure on the page, a plan or a frame, and the aim is to need it less by the end of the lesson than at the start. Check-in points are set: the adult looks at a fixed point, not whenever the pupil stalls.

How tasks are tiered

Pupils start on Independent unless they choose Supported. Aspire adds a second source or a counter-argument. Choice is a short piece in a form the pupil picks.

How progress is recorded

The pupil's response names what was hard in a sentence. The adult acknowledges it and gives a next step about the quality of the explanation: give the evidence before the reason, not after.

SUMMIT shown as LAUNCH on some pages

Who it is for

Pupils who can read at length, hold two ideas at once and are ready to argue a case. SUMMIT is not TRACK with more to do. It asks for a different kind of thinking.

What a lesson looks like

Two or more sources that do not agree, or a data set with a problem in it. The lesson asks for a judgement, and for the pupil to show why the other view is weaker. There is no frame on the page by default; a pupil can ask for one. The pupil works through the whole task before the adult looks, and is expected to check their own work against the success criteria first.

How tasks are tiered

Pupils start on Independent. Supported is there, by request, for anyone who needs a way in. Aspire adds a word limit, a wider context or a demand to weigh a third source. Choice is an open question of the pupil's own.

How progress is recorded

The pupil's response is a short evaluation of their own work against the criteria. The adult acknowledges it and the next step is about argument: your judgement is right but you have not said why the second source is weaker. On SUMMIT the pupil drafts the next step and the adult agrees or sharpens it.

The same lesson on all three pathways

This is one topic, why a river floods, as it is taught on each pathway. It is the clearest way to see that TRACK and SUMMIT are different pathways and not two names for the same one.

One lesson, "Why does a river flood?", side by side
Part of the lessonBASE (BUILD)TRACK (GROW)SUMMIT (LAUNCH)
Text length About one hundred words, in short sentences, with the four key words in bold and a picture for each one. About three hundred words, one page, with one diagram of a river and its valley. About six hundred words across two sources that disagree about the main cause, plus a rainfall table.
The question What makes a river flood? Name two causes. Explain why this river flooded. Give reasons and use evidence from the text. Which was the main cause of this flood? Weigh both sources, reach a judgement and say why the other view is weaker.
Support on the page Word bank, sentence starters and a part-filled diagram to label. A paragraph plan with three headings. Sentence starters are available but the lesson aims to drop them by the final paragraph. None by default. Success criteria and a planning grid are available if the pupil asks for them.
Independence The adult works alongside and checks after each sentence or label. The pupil works alone between two set check-in points: after the plan, and after the first paragraph. The pupil plans, writes and self-checks against the criteria before the adult sees it.
Outcome A labelled diagram and two complete sentences that each name a cause. A structured explanation of three paragraphs, each with a reason and a piece of evidence. A short argument that reaches a judgement, uses both sources and explains why one is more reliable than the other.
Next step in the book Use a key word in your next sentence. Put the evidence before the reason in your second paragraph. Say what the second source leaves out, and why that matters. Pupil drafts it, adult agrees or sharpens it.
What makes it different from the pathway below This is the foundation. Explaining rather than naming. The pupil must give reasons and evidence, and works alone between check-ins. Judging rather than explaining. Two sources instead of one, no frame by default, a decision to defend, and the pupil checks their own work before the adult does.

Choosing a pathway

The teacher chooses the pathway, and a pupil can move. Moving up is the normal direction of travel, and the tiers inside a lesson are the ladder: a pupil who is routinely finishing Aspire on TRACK is ready to try Independent on SUMMIT. A pupil can also drop down for a topic, or for a week, without it being a judgement on them.

If you are looking at a lesson page and it says BUILD, GROW or LAUNCH, read it as BASE, TRACK or SUMMIT. The content is the same; only the label has moved.
