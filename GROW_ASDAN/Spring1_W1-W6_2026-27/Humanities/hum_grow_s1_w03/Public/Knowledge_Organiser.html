Knowledge organiser for causes and effects

Learning outcome: Explain causes and consequences of a protest movement.

In Montgomery, Alabama, USA, racist bus rules kept Black and White riders apart. Black residents and local groups had challenged unfair treatment for years. Rosa Parks was arrested on 1 December 1955. The boycott began on 5 December. Protest, shared work and a legal case all mattered.

PartWhat to know
Long-term causesUnfair bus rules; earlier requests brought little change.
TriggerParks’s arrest on 1 December 1955 helped prompt the 5 December boycott.
During the boycottTravel changed and fare income fell. Shared help sustained protest.
Legal processBrowder v. Gayle: district ruling 5 June 1956; upheld 13 November.
By the campaign’s endOrder and boycott end on 20 December; return to buses 21 December 1956.
Later yearsLinks supported wider work; the campaign provided an example for later protest.

A change with limits

The boycott and a legal case worked alongside each other. Bus seating rights changed. Racism and unequal treatment did not end. One famous person did not act alone.

MIA means Montgomery Improvement Association. It helped lead the boycott.

Where this leads: historians and museum staff explain causes with evidence, not just a list of dates.
