Opening · reference

How much did Rosa Parks matter

Evaluate the significance of a person who created change.

Enquiry: How significant was Rosa Parks, and why?

  • I can use four tests to judge why Rosa Parks mattered.
  • I can score each test and give a fact, a reason and a limit.
  • I can write my own view and explain it with evidence.

Rosa Parks lived from 1913 to 2005. She worked for civil rights before and after the Montgomery bus boycott. Her arrest on 1 December 1955 helped prompt the protest. Many people kept it going. A separate court case helped change bus law. Her part must be judged within that shared work.

You may point, write, type, speak privately or direct a scribe. Reading aloud and public answers are optional. You may pass.

I do · 6 min

A score needs a reason

For C3, remembered by many, I might give 3 out of 3. Card F lists national honours and a statue in the US Capitol. These show wide public recognition. But they do not tell us what every person knows. This is one part of her significance. It does not prove she caused all change alone.

Cover the last reason. Do public honours prove that one person won every change? Give a reason privately or point to a card.

A–F are new factual teaching summaries, written in 2026. No old text, speech, testimony or picture is copied. They describe the past; our scores are reasoned views about it.

A Work before the bus protest

1943–1955

In 1943, Rosa Parks became secretary of a local civil rights group. She helped record unfair treatment and worked for equal rights. Her work began years before her bus arrest.

Research basis: H1, H3.

B A refusal and arrest

1 December 1955

In Montgomery, racist bus rules kept Black and White people apart. On 1 December 1955, Parks refused to give up her seat. Police arrested her. Local groups then called for a bus boycott on 5 December.

Research basis: H1, H2.

C A protest kept going by many

5 December 1955–20 December 1956

Many Black residents stayed off Montgomery buses. People walked, shared rides and raised funds. Local organisers helped the protest last. Parks’s action mattered within this joint work. One person could not keep it going alone.

Research basis: H2, H5.

D A separate case changed bus law

1956

The court case Browder v. Gayle challenged bus segregation. Parks was not one of its plaintiffs. Judges ruled against the bus rules. After the court order arrived, the boycott ended. People returned to integrated buses on 21 December 1956.

Research basis: H2, H5, H6.

E Work continued in Detroit

1965–1988

Parks later lived in Detroit. From 1965 to 1988 she worked in a member of Congress’s local office. She helped people with their needs and kept working for civil rights. Her public work reached beyond one bus journey.

Research basis: H1, H3.

F Public honours and remembrance

1999 and 2013

Parks received a gold medal from Congress in 1999. A statue of her was placed in the US Capitol in 2013, after her death in 2005. These are signs of wide public remembrance.

Research basis: H1, H3.

Hinge · 2 min

Fame evidence and limits

Which is the best judgement? A: Parks was famous, so every score must be 3. B: Card B supports her role at the time, while C and D show shared work and legal action. C: Other people helped, so her own action did not matter.

Choose privately. Point, write or explain your reason to the adult.

Check after you try

B. Her action helped prompt a boycott. The shared work and legal case also mattered. Fame alone cannot prove every test, and other people’s work does not cancel her part.

Independent

Aspire

Word help · reference

Words for judging significance

Open word help
significance
How and why a person or event matters in history.
criterion
A test used to make a judgement. Criteria means more than one test.
evidence
A fact or source used to support a claim.
evaluate
Weigh evidence and limits to reach a view.
score
A number that sums up your judgement using a scale.
limit
What a fact or claim cannot show.
boycott
Not using or buying something as a protest.
segregation
Rules that keep groups apart unfairly.
plaintiff
A person who brings a legal case to court.
remembrance
Keeping a person or event in public memory.
revealed
Showed us something we can explain.
civil rights
Rights to fair treatment and equal protection under law.
