Opening · reference

Protest and rights in time order

Place key protest/rights events on a timeline (chronology).

  • I can match each event to its date.
  • I can put all five events from earliest to latest.
  • I can use a date to explain one place on my timeline.

Five selected rights and protest events in the UK and the United States. This is a short selection, not the whole story of rights.

You may point, write, type, speak privately or direct a scribe. Reading aloud and public answers are optional. You may pass.

Arrival · 4 min

A calm start

Sit where you can see and hear. Have your book and five event cards ready.

Read the goal, or ask the adult to read it quietly. Choose how you will show your ideas.

A private pause

Use the agreed quiet signal to ask the adult for a pause. Use the usual supervised place. No public reason is needed. Rejoin with one date check or a private recap. A neutral task with number cards is available.

Starter · 3 min

Earlier and later

Which year comes first: 1965 or 1918? Point to the earlier year. Explain what you noticed.

Check after you try

1918 comes first. It is the smaller year. Earlier events go first on this timeline.

I do · 6 min

How to check a date

Check the year first. If two dates have the same year, check the month. If the month is also the same, check the day.

Illustrative teacher-written teaching model. It is attributed to no real person.

I look at the year first. 1918 comes before 1955, so E1 goes before E2. I then check the other years. 1963 comes before 1964 and 1965. I keep each date with its event. The five places on this strip show order. They do not show equal gaps in time.

We do · 7 min

Our class card sort

Work together to sort these five complete date-and-event cards. They are mixed up below. Start with the earliest year.

Teacher-written factual summaries for learning. These are not historical quotations, testimony or archive extracts.

  • E4 · United States

    Civil Rights Act becomes law

    2 July 1964

    A US law banned unfair race rules in many public places and at work. People still had to act to make the law work.

    Research basis H4

  • E1 · UK

    Some women gain the vote

    6 February 1918

    A UK law let some women aged 30 or over vote. Many women still could not vote.

    Research basis H1

  • E5 · United States

    Voting Rights Act becomes law

    6 August 1965

    A US law helped protect the right to vote. It aimed to stop unfair rules that kept Black voters out.

    Research basis H5

  • E2 · United States

    Montgomery bus boycott begins

    5 December 1955

    Black people and their supporters stopped using buses in Montgomery. They aimed to end rules that kept Black and White riders apart.

    Research basis H2

  • E3 · United States

    March on Washington

    28 August 1963

    People marched in Washington, DC, to call for jobs and equal rights. It was a peaceful mass protest.

    Research basis H3

Hinge · 2 min

A date check

Which comes first: 5 December 1955 or 28 August 1963? Explain what you checked first.

Think first. Point, say your reason privately, or show the adult your choice.

Check after you try

5 December 1955 comes first because 1955 is the earlier year. The year comes before the month when comparing different years.

Independent · 12 min

Choose one route. All routes use all five events.

Supported

  1. Use all five event cards and the year mat. An adult may read the cards. Find the smallest year first.
  2. Build your own strip of five in your book. Use one date-and-event label at a time. Check it before adding the next.
  3. Point, say or write one reason with this frame: ____ comes before ____ because ____ is the earlier year.

Independent

  1. Read all five cards. Sort the complete date-and-event pairs from earliest to latest. Check all years.
  2. Make your own strip of five in your book. Keep the date and title together. Check each one against the source card.
  3. Explain one place using a date. Say what the equal-sized boxes do and do not show about time gaps.

Aspire

  1. Build and check your own five-event strip. Keep every date and event together.
  2. Work out the gap in calendar years from 1918 to 1955. Does equal spacing on the strip show that real gap? Explain.
  3. Test a new date pair from the same year: 2 July 1964 and 28 August 1964. Explain how months settle the order. These extra dates are date practice, not extra event cards.

You may plan in any language. An adult may read a card or write your exact words.

Review · 3 min

Check every date and event

  1. Check that all five events are there.
  2. Keep each event with its full date.
  3. Read the years from earliest to latest.
  4. Use one date to check a place on the strip.

Swap checker roles, then make your own final check. If you change an answer, keep your own change in your book.

Exit · 3 min

One place and a reason

Show one place on your own timeline. Complete this sentence: ____ comes before ____ because ____.

Show, point, speak privately, or direct a scribe. Give the adult your own reason. You may pass.

The adult will give one next step. Choose how to respond to it.

Word help · reference

Words for time and rights

Open word help
chronology
The order in which events happen.
timeline
A way to show events in time order.
earliest
The one that happened first.
latest
The one that happened last.
protest
An action that shows people want change.
rights
Freedoms and fair treatment people should have.
boycott
Choosing not to use or buy something as a protest.
segregation
Rules that keep groups of people apart unfairly.
vote
A choice made in an election or a decision.
