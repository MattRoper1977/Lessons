Our learning goal

Place key protest/rights events on a timeline (chronology).

Five selected rights and protest events in the UK and the United States. This is a short selection, not the whole story of rights.

  • I can match each event to its date.
  • I can put all five events from earliest to latest.
  • I can use a date to explain one place on my timeline.

This table is in order. The spaces between rows do not show real time gaps. From 1918 to 1955 is a gap of 37 calendar years.

How to check dates

  1. Compare the years. Put the smaller year first.
  2. If the years match, compare the months.
  3. If the months also match, compare the days.
  4. Keep each event with its full date.

Example: 2 July 1964 comes before 28 August 1964. July comes before August.

Words to help

chronology
The order in which events happen.
timeline
A way to show events in time order.
earliest
The one that happened first.
latest
The one that happened last.
protest
An action that shows people want change.
rights
Freedoms and fair treatment people should have.
boycott
Choosing not to use or buy something as a protest.
segregation
Rules that keep groups of people apart unfairly.
vote
A choice made in an election or a decision.

Rights and change

A protest shows that people want change. A law can protect a right. People may still need to work to make a law help in daily life. These five events do not tell the whole story.
