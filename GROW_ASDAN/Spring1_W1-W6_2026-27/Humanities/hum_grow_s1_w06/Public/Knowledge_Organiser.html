What to know for account and source review

Learning outcome: Structured account + source evaluation; bank the unit award.

We have studied the Montgomery bus boycott in Alabama, USA, in 1955–1956. Today you finish and improve your own Week 5 account. Then you judge two earlier sources using new summaries. Keep what a source can show separate from what it leaves open.

Account checks

Background; causes; key events in order; consequences; significance. Keep at least three dated events, reasons, the separate court case, shared work and a limit. Keep an earlier draft and a reasoned edit.

Source checkWhat it means
What it showsState one claim this source can support.
Who made itName the teaching-card maker and the maker of the record or history behind it. Use the date and purpose.
How far we trust itSay what claim it helps with, why, and what it cannot show. A maker or purpose alone is not proof.
One question it leavesAsk a real question the source does not settle. Say what other evidence could help.

Keep history clear

Parks’s arrest was on 1 December 1955; the boycott began on 5 December. Parks was not a plaintiff in Browder v. Gayle. People returned to integrated buses on 21 December 1956. Racism did not end.

S1 is a summary of a campaign call; S4 describes rulings through a later history. The lesson writer made the teaching cards in 2026. The records have different makers and aims. Neither original item is supplied.
