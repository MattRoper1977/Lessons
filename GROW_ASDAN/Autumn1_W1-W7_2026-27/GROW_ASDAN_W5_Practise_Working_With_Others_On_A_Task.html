Naming the jobs is the working-together part

Working with someone is not standing next to them doing the same thing.

It is agreeing who does what before either of you starts.

Two people both washing while nobody chops is not teamwork, it is a queue.

So the jobs are named and written down first, and swapped halfway if the card says so.

The card tells you how many jobs there are. You decide who takes which.

Do not assign the jobs yourself. Deciding and recording it is the assessed part of this task.

You need the recipe card, the job strip, the safety card and the partner message sheet.

Two people and three jobs is normal. Most real kitchens run that way.

The swap is what makes it fair, and the written time is what makes it evidence.

Two pairs, one recipe

The first pair started as soon as the card arrived and both reached for the knife.

The second pair spent thirty seconds writing two names against two jobs.

Both finished. Only the second pair can say who did what.

At the end of term, that record is the difference between evidence and a memory.

Thirty seconds now, or nothing to show in December.

  • 1 · read the card and count the jobs.
  • 2 · write a name against each job.
  • 3 · agree when you swap.
  • 4 · start.

Thirty seconds is not an exaggeration. Time yourself writing two names.

It is the cheapest thing in this lesson and the only part that cannot be recovered later.

Naming the jobs, then cooking

I read the card. It has three jobs: wash and chop, mix, and clear as you go.

Three jobs and two people, so one of us takes two and we swap at the halfway point.

I write the names on the strip before I open anything.

Then the safety card: hands, board, knife, and the clear space around it.

Only now do I start, and I do my named job and not the other one.

Halfway through, we swap, and I write the time we swapped on the strip.

That time is the evidence that the swap actually happened.

Refuse to start until both names are on the strip, every time. It is the habit being taught.

I read the whole card before I count the jobs. Counting from half a card gives the wrong number.

Sort the jobs by when they happen

Six job cards are on your table. Sort them into before, during and after.

Washing hands. Chopping. Wiping the board. Reading the card. Mixing. Putting the kit away.

Then match each during card to the person who would do it in a pair of two.

Two of the cards happen more than once. Say which, and when.

When your table is done, hold up the card you were least sure about.

Two of the six happen in more than one place. Put them where they matter most, and say why.

Clearing as you go is a job, not a tidy-up

Most pairs treat clearing as something that happens at the end.

On this card it is a named job that runs the whole time.

That is the difference between a kitchen you can work in and one you cannot.

It also means one person is never standing waiting, which is what makes it teamwork.

So the third job is real work and gets a name like the others.

Whoever has it, has it, until the swap.

If your pair finishes early, the clearing job is not finished; it runs until the bell.

Say your job, then check theirs

Work in your pair. Each say your job out loud, then say your partner's.

If you cannot say theirs, look at the strip and read it.

Then run the safety card between you, one saying and one checking.

Say each step before you do it.

When both of you can say both jobs, you may start.

Say your partner's job as well as your own. That is the check that the naming actually happened.

Snack, strip, and a first message

Work in your pair, on your own job.

Make the snack to the card and swap at the halfway point.

Write the swap time on the strip when it happens.

Then, on your own, write the first message to our partner in three lines.

Who we are, what we are doing, and what we would like to ask. No addresses, no numbers.

  • Supported · name your job, make the snack with support, and say the message aloud.
  • Standard · name both jobs, make the snack, record the swap, and draft the message.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say which job you would take next time and why.

Write the swap time as a real time, not as halfway. Halfway is not a time anyone can check.

Keep the recipe card face up until the last step. Working from memory is where a step gets skipped.

Read the message to an adult

Read your three lines to the adult at your table.

They read them back exactly as you wrote them.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they write the new one.

Then choose what happens next: send this one, change a line, or wait a week.

Your choice goes on the sheet before you leave.

The strip goes in the folder with the recipe card clipped behind it.
