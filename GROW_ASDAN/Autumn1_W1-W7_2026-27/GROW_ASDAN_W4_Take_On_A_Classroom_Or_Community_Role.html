Take the Role, Learn the Drill, Write the Plan

GROW · ASDAN · Week 4. ASDAN cross-strand. Take the Role, Learn the Drill, Write the Plan.

Objective: claim one classroom or community role for this half term, complete the recovery-position drill from the approved card, and write three project steps with the kit each one needs.

Success evidence: one role claimed with a reason, a signed drill card, and three project steps each naming the kit it needs.

The drill is practised on the training mannequin only, never on another pupil. You describe the card, not anything from home.

Arrival · retrieve

A role is a job with your name on it

A role is not a favour you do when you remember. It is a job that is yours every week.

It has a name, it has a person, and it has a time it happens.

Take one of those three away and it stops being a role.

Roles get recorded because the evidence is what the award needs, not because we do not trust you.

Today you claim one and write down when it happens.

Hold pupils to one role each. Two roles means neither gets evidenced and both get dropped by half term.

You need the role board, the first-aid drill card, the mannequin, and the project sheet.

Roles are claimed once. Swapping later is allowed, but it is a decision you write down, not a drift.

The board is the record. If it is not on the board, nobody can sign it off in December.

Starter · create the need

Two people say they help out

The first says: I help out with the recycling sometimes.

The second says: I am the recycling monitor. I do it every Friday after lunch.

Both are telling the truth about the same job.

Only the second one can be signed off at the end of term.

A role has a name, a person and a time. Say all three or it will not count.

  • 1 · name the role.
  • 2 · say who it is.
  • 3 · say when it happens.
  • 4 · write it on the board.

Say your role out loud before you write it. If it takes more than one sentence, it is two roles.

Claiming a role and learning one drill

I claim the equipment monitor role. My reason is that I already check the tray each week.

I write my name and the time: every Tuesday, first five minutes.

Now the drill. The card has four steps and I do them in order.

Check for danger. Check for a response. Open the airway. Roll to the recovery position.

I say each step out loud before I do it, on the mannequin.

Then I sign the card, and so does the adult who watched me.

Two signatures, because a drill nobody watched is a claim, not evidence.

Sign only what you actually watched. A signature on an unwatched drill is worthless to the pupil later.

Notice I say the step before I do it, every time. The saying is what an adult can sign.

A drill done in silence is a movement. A drill said out loud is a procedure.

Sort the roles

Six role cards are on your table. Sort them into weekly and one-off.

Recycling monitor. Tidying up after the trip. Setting out the room. Making the posters.

Checking the first-aid kit. Carrying the boxes on Thursday.

Then match each weekly card to the day it would happen on.

When your table is done, hold up the card you argued about most.

Two of the six could be either, depending on how often they happen. Decide, and say how often is enough.

I Do 2 · connect

The plan is what stops the role fading

Most roles are dropped by half term, and almost never because the person stopped caring.

They are dropped because a step needed something nobody had ordered.

So every step in your project plan gets the kit written next to it.

If a step needs something we do not have, that is not a problem; it is the next thing to sort out.

Write three steps, in order, with the kit for each.

Then look at the list and say which step needs something we have not got.

Order the three steps so the kit for the first one is the kit we already have.

We Do 2 · lab

We Do 2 · lab

Run the drill for a partner

Work in twos, one mannequin between you.

One runs the four steps out loud while the other holds the card.

The person with the card ticks only what they heard said.

Doing a step without saying it does not get the tick.

Then swap and run it the other way.

If your partner misses a step, say which one rather than starting the drill again.

Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Role, card, and three steps

Work on your own. Write your role, your reason and the time it happens.

Get your drill card signed by you and by an adult who watched it.

Then write three project steps in order, each with the kit it needs.

Mark any step where the kit is something we do not have yet.

If you finish early, say who you would ask for that missing kit.

  • Supported · claim a role with a time, and complete the drill with support.
  • Standard · claim a role with a reason and time, get the drill signed, and write three steps with kit.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name the missing kit and who you would ask for it.

Write the day and the time, not just the day. After lunch is a time; Tuesday is not.

If your role clashes with someone else's, write both names and settle it at the board.

Exit · Audience and Influence

Say the role to an adult

Read your role, your reason and your time to the adult at your table.

They read it back exactly and write it on the role board.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they change the board.

Then choose what happens next: start this week, start next week, or swap the role.

Your choice goes on the sheet before you leave.

The drill card goes in the first-aid folder and the project sheet goes in yours.
