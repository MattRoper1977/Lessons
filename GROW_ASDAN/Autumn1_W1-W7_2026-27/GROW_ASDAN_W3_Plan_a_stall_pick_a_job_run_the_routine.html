GROW · ASDAN · Week 3 · Enterprise and Independence.

Objective: carry out one approved daily routine, plan one community or social-enterprise idea for our school, and decide who does each job in the team.

Success evidence: a ticked routine card, a team plan sheet with the idea and who it helps, and one job with your name next to it.

No disclosure. Every routine, idea, photograph and job comes from the approved school cards. You are never asked about home, family, health or money at home.

40 minutes. Offline.

Resources, all prepared, in the GROW ASDAN box on the shelf by the door. Two photographs of last term's cake stall, labelled A and B. Numbered Routine Cards 1 to 8, one per pupil, plus Card 3 for your model. Three Job Cards: SET UP, SERVE, COUNT AND TIDY. Three Idea Cards. One team plan sheet per team. One small 1-2-3 choice card per pupil for the We Do. If the photographs are missing, read the description of A and B in the Starter aloud instead; the whole contrast is written out there. Pacing: the first twenty minutes are input. Plan a stand-and-stretch break after I Do, and split the I Do 2 model into two halves if the group is restless.

Read the four statements. Say true, false, or I am not sure. I am not sure is a real answer.

  • A routine is the same small steps, in the same order, every time.
  • A social enterprise sells things and uses the money to help people.
  • A job on a plan gets done by whoever is free that day.
  • A responsibility is the job that one named person owns.

One statement is false. Point at it. Pointing counts as answering.

Take answers by pointing, by card or by speech. Ask which word made them choose.

Look at the two photographs of last term's cake stall.

Photograph A shows the table set up, the price cards out and the cups stacked.

The stall opened at twelve o'clock. Photograph B was taken at ten past twelve. The boxes are still on the floor.

Both teams had the same idea and the same jobs. Team B wrote no names on their plan.

  • 1 · Notice: find one thing in photograph B that did not get done.
  • 2 · Choose: point at the Job Card that matches it.
  • 3 · Explain: say why a plan with no names can stop.

That is the problem this lesson fixes.

Three words for today.

  • Routine: small steps, in the same order, every time.
  • Social enterprise: a small business that sells things and helps people.
  • Responsibility: one job that one named person owns.

Watch me carry out Routine Card 3, the drinks trolley. I say each step aloud.

Step one. The card says wipe the trolley. I wipe it. I tick the box.

Step two. Put out eight cups. I count them. I tick the box.

Step three. The card says check the float tin. The float tin holds the coins we start with, so we can give change.

I am not sure how many coins go in. I do not guess. I write not sure and ask the adult.

Writing not sure is correct work. Guessing is not.

Everyone commits first. Nobody speaks yet.

Here are the three Job Cards. Job 1 is SET UP. Job 2 is SERVE. Job 3 is COUNT AND TIDY.

Decide which job must be finished before the stall can open.

You each have a card with 1, 2 and 3 on it. Put your finger on your number.

Hold your card against your chest. Nobody can see it. Nobody can copy it.

Now we show together. Three, two, one, turn your card round.

Now say why. Point at photograph A or photograph B as your reason. Changing your mind with a reason is good work.

SPACE VOICE AUDIENCE INFLUENCE

Now connect the routine to the plan.

Our team picks one idea from the Idea Cards. Card 1 is a fruit stall. Card 2 is a plant sale. Card 3 is a card-making stall.

A plan needs four lines: the idea, who it helps, what we need, and who does each job.

Watch me plan Idea Card 2. The idea is a plant sale. It helps the garden club. We need pots, soil and price cards.

The last line is the hard part. Every job needs one name and one routine card. Watering is a job. Watering at the same time each day is a routine. Without it there is nothing to sell.

So the routine, the plan and the jobs are one thing, not three.

Offer the row, never assign it. Tie rule, say it before they start: if two pupils want the same job, both names go on the sheet and they split the steps. The adult decides only if asked.

SPACE VOICE AUDIENCE INFLUENCE

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

This is doing time. Work at your own pace.

You each get a numbered Routine Card now. Do number one first.

  • One: take your Routine Card. Read step one. Do it. Tick it. Then read step two and do that. Work down to the last step.
  • Two: write your team's enterprise idea, and one sentence saying who it helps.
  • Three: write the job you own, with your name next to it.

If a step is unclear, write not sure and go on. We check those together at the end.

Every answer must point at something real: a card number, a photograph, or something that happened today.

If you need to stop, mark where you stopped.

Hand every pupil a numbered Routine Card at the start of this stage. They carry it out here, in this room, while you supervise. Take your Card 3 away first. Nine minutes of doing: add no reading. Photograph the finished cards for the ASDAN folder.

SPACE VOICE AUDIENCE INFLUENCE

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Say one thing. Pointing at your sheet counts as saying it.

Say the job you own for the stall.

The adult names it back in your exact words. For example: you own counting and tidying.

You confirm it or correct it. Yes, that is right. Or: no, I said serving.

Then we choose one next action together. It goes on the board with your name. For example: bring your routine card on the next GROW day.

We check that action on the next GROW day. That is why we write it on the board.

Leave the named actions on the board, with pupil names, for the class teacher on the next GROW day. Do not name a weekday you cannot check.

TA Brief

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
