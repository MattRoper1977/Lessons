Evidence That Proves It Happened

GROW · ASDAN · Week 7. Personal Challenge. Evidence That Proves It Happened.

Objective: record two steps from your approved challenge card as evidence, giving each one a captured proof, a caption that starts with a verb, a date and a signature.

Success evidence: two logged entries, each with proof, a captioned verb, a date and the name of the adult who saw it.

Every step you log comes from the approved challenge card, and you record only what you did. You are never asked about your home, your family or what you believe, and nothing today is about how anybody looks.

Arrival · retrieve

Evidence is proof, not memory

You have already completed part of your personal challenge.

Now you have to prove that it actually happened.

Evidence is anything that shows an assessor what you did.

A photograph is evidence, and so is a short written note.

A date and a name turn either one into a record.

Nobody who marks your folder was there watching you do it.

So your log page has to say it for you instead.

Read the two-word list aloud once and leave it on the board. Step and caption are the words the whole lesson turns on.

You need three things today: your challenge card, your log page and a pen.

Two words matter in this lesson: step and caption.

A step is one part of the challenge you did.

A caption is the words you write under the proof.

Today you record two steps that you have already finished.

Two log pages, one came back

Here are two log pages from a folder last term.

Page A holds four photos and no words at all.

Page B holds one photo, one caption and one date.

Page A came back for more detail, but page B was signed off.

Page A has more material on it, and it still proves less.

An assessor cannot tick a thing they cannot name.

  • 1 · look at what the photograph or the note shows.
  • 2 · look for words naming the action.
  • 3 · look for a date and a name.
  • 4 · decide whether the entry proves anything at all.

More proof is not better proof, because only clear proof counts.

That is why one strong entry beats four weak ones.

I Do · model

I Do · model

Four steps, from doing it to logging it

My challenge card says I will learn three guitar chords.

On Tuesday I practised one chord for twenty minutes.

First I choose one step, and my step is Tuesday's practice.

Next I capture it by photographing my chord sheet on the table.

Then I caption it, writing down what I did with a verb.

I practised the G chord for twenty minutes on Tuesday.

Last I check it for the date, my name and the adult who saw me.

Watch the caption rather than the photograph, because the caption does the work.

My photograph only shows a chord sheet lying on a table.

By itself it cannot say who practised, or when they practised.

Say each of the four step names out loud as you do it. The names are what is being taught, not the guitar example.

If you have no photograph, write three lines of description instead.

Written notes are accepted evidence, and they are not a weaker option.

I Do 2 · connect

I Do 2 · connect

A caption an assessor can tick

Every caption needs a verb, which is a word for doing something.

Useful verbs include practised, carried, asked, paid and finished.

Weak captions use naming words only, like my guitar or the shop.

A naming word tells the assessor what was there.

A verb tells the assessor what you actually did with it.

Put the date in the caption, not only on the page.

Pages get moved between folders, but a caption keeps its own date.

One more rule: your caption names you, not the whole group.

We tidied the hall does not prove your own part.

I stacked twelve chairs in the hall does prove it.

  • 1 · name the step from your challenge card.
  • 2 · start the caption with I and a verb.
  • 3 · add the day or the date.
  • 4 · add the name of the adult who saw it.

We Do 2 · lab

We Do 2 · lab

Test a caption on a partner

Write one caption now for a step from your challenge card that you have finished.

Start it with I and a verb, and put the day in it.

Then work in twos, swapping log pages and reading them in silence.

Find the verb in your partner's caption and underline it.

If there is no verb, say which line is missing one.

Then ask your partner one question: what did you actually do?

Their spoken answer is usually the caption they needed.

Write that spoken answer down for them, using their exact words.

Do not tidy it up, because a caption in your own words proves nothing.

Then swap back and fix your own caption the same way.

Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Two entries, both proved

Work on your own now and log two steps from your challenge card.

The caption you fixed with your partner becomes your first entry.

Each entry needs four things: proof, a caption, a date and a name.

Start each caption with I and a verb.

Keep each caption to one sentence, because long captions hide the verb.

Then read both entries back and mark them yourself.

Give a tick if an assessor could name what you did.

Give a cross if they could not, because a cross is honest.

Then fix one cross before the end of the lesson.

  • Supported · log one step with proof and a captioned verb.
  • Standard · log two steps, each captioned, dated and signed.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say which entry proves the most.

Only log steps you have really done, because an unproved entry is removed later.

Exit · Audience and Influence

Exit · Audience and Influence

Read one caption to an adult

Read one caption to the adult at your table.

They read it back to you word for word.

Then ask them one question: do you know what I did?

If they do not, change the verb and read it again.

That adult signs and dates the entry in front of you.

Then choose what happens next: keep it, add proof, or log a third step.

Write that choice on the page with today's date.

Your page goes in the challenge folder, not in your bag.
