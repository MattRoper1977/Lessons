1. Audit logic

Why does an honest audit beat a flattering one — in cash terms, eventually?

Flattering audits buy nothing; honest ones point effort where it pays. Employers spot the difference in minutes.

2. Evidence test

'I'm good with people.' Design the evidence that would convince a stranger.

Dated, specific, checkable — 'I de-escalated X on Tuesday, staff witnessed it' beats any adjective.

What's harder to audit — strengths or weaknesses? Argue it.

Own argument — most people inflate strengths AND hide the real weakness behind a safe fake one.

The Model — Knowing Myself

Key Idea: You can't grow what you haven't measured.
Step 0 of 3

What personal effectiveness actually means — skills that make everything else work.

Personal effectiveness is the engine behind every other subject — the skills of learning, deciding, communicating and teaming that make maths, work and life actually run.

Auditing strengths and interests honestly — evidence, not guesses.

An audit is claims plus evidence: every strength carries a dated example a stranger could check. No evidence, no entry — that's the L1 standard, and it's the L2 one too.

Turning the audit into a starting point you can build from.

The starting point isn't a judgement, it's a coordinate — you can only plot progress from a fixed point, and today's audit is it.
