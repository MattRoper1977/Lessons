A strength is a thing you can show

A strength is something you can do that someone else could watch you do.

Saying you are good at something is a claim. Saying when you did it is evidence.

Every strength on these cards can be evidenced from something that happens in school, so nobody has to talk about home.

Read the three lines once. The last one is the guard for the whole lesson; do not skip it to save time.

You will need the strength cards, the school-evidence cards and the photograph set. Nothing from outside school is used in this lesson.

Which one could someone check?

Statement A: I am good at helping people.

Statement B: I showed a new pupil where the dining hall was on Tuesday.

Both might be true. Only one of them tells you what happened, when, and where someone could have seen it.

  • 1 · notice which one names a time and place
  • 2 · choose the one you could check
  • 3 · say what makes it checkable

Statement B could be checked by asking someone who was there. That is the whole difference, and it is the difference this lesson is built on.

Turning a card into a claim with evidence

STRENGTH means something you can do and show. EVIDENCE means what happened that proves it. NEED means something an area is missing that people would use.

I take the card that says organised. I do not decide yet whether it is mine.

First I look for something that actually happened in school. I brought the right equipment to every practical last half term.

Then I check it against the card. Does bringing the right equipment show being organised? Yes, so the card is mine and the evidence is attached to it.

If I cannot find anything that happened, I put the card back. A strength with no evidence is a wish, and wishes do not go on a profile.

Last I say the claim as one sentence: I am organised, and the evidence is that I brought the right equipment to every practical last half term.

Say the put-the-card-back step aloud. Pupils will otherwise take every card, and the profile stops meaning anything.

Match one card to one piece of evidence

You have four strength cards and four things that happened in school.

Match one pair. Hold it up. Nobody explains yet.

Then two people give a reason, and we agree the pair before moving on.

If your pair does not match, say which half you are less sure about. Naming the weaker half is what makes the pair better.

From my strengths to what our area needs

A goal is a strength pointed at something. Once you know what you can do, you can ask where it would be useful.

Our community project starts by looking. From the approved photographs I name one thing this area is missing that people would use.

A need is a claim like any other, so it needs evidence. I point to what is in the photograph: no seating along a path people clearly walk.

I keep the two apart. What I can see is one thing. What I think should happen about it is a different thing, and I say which is which.

If I cannot point to it in a picture, I write it down and mark it as a guess. A marked guess is honest and still useful.

Do not let need slide into solution. Naming the need is this week; deciding what to do about it is later in the project.

Decision lab

  • ◆ Supported — match two strength cards to two things that happened in school.
  • ▲ Standard — record three strengths with one piece of school evidence each, and name one thing the area needs.
  • ★ Stretch — as Standard, and say what in the photograph supports the need, and mark anything you cannot see as a guess.

Whichever row you take, the rule is the same: every claim gets something that happened or something you can point to.

If a row feels too easy, take the next one up. Nobody is fixed to a row and you can change part-way through.

Independent record

Record three strengths from the cards. Beside each, write one thing that happened in school that shows it.

If you cannot find evidence for a card, put it back and say why. That is a real answer and it counts.

From the approved photographs, write one thing our area needs and what you can see that supports it.

Finish by writing one goal: a strength you have, pointed at something you noticed in the pictures.

Work at your own pace. Three strengths with real evidence beats five with none, and an honest blank counts as work.

Audience receives; Influence changes one next action

Space stays open. You can point at your sheet instead of speaking, and it counts the same.

Say one strength with its evidence and one thing our area needs. The adult names both back to you exactly as you said them.

Confirm or correct what was named back, then choose the next action: keep the strength, swap the evidence for a better one, or take the card back until you find some.

Your record is kept and comes back to you next week, so nothing you write today has to be finished today.

TA Brief
