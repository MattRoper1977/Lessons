Jobs and Routes: What Fits Me? · ready-to-print cards

Starter: Sort supplied statements into job task, route information or assumption. Explain which assumption is most risky.

Staff pre-stage before the 16-minute transfer

  • Select two current, centre-approved job and route cards for each learner; do not ask pupils to research from scratch.
  • Print or open each learner's existing about-me profile and highlight two relevant evidence points.
  • Prepare the comparison grid and movable markers, and identify the authorised adult or source for unanswered route questions.

Cut, point to, rank or sort

Card 1

A support worker listens to a person, records what matters and agrees the next practical step.

Card 2

The current route card lists a college course, an apprenticeship and supported work experience as possibilities to investigate.

Card 3

Sam says construction jobs are only suitable for boys.

Card 4

A gardener checks the soil, chooses the right tool and records how the plants respond.

Card 5

The route information says entry requirements must be checked with the current provider.

Card 6

The job with the highest pay will suit everybody best.

Decision-lab lenses

ROLE

What tasks does this person really complete?

ROUTE

What could the next realistic step be?

CHECK

Which fact, requirement or support must be verified?

Use: preserve the learner's first prediction and explanation. These cards support rehearsal; completed screen/card activity is not qualification evidence.

PEQ_A2_W1 · authentic-task record

Jobs and Routes: What Fits Me?

Task: Compare two supplied jobs and their possible routes. Record role tasks, personal-fit evidence, one possible barrier or support, a current preference and one question to verify.

◆ Supported

Use two preselected visual role cards and a reduced comparison grid. An adult may read or scribe exact words; the learner owns the comparison.

▲ Standard

Compare two roles and routes, justify a current preference with at least two evidence points and identify one verification question.

★ Optional reach

Test the preferred route against a changed condition or a credible alternative route, then state whether the decision changes.

Staff: select one route before giving this page to the learner. Change access, not authorship.

What people actually do
My evidence of fit
Possible route or next step
Possible barrier or useful support

A fact or question to verify
Exit sentence frame

A route that interests me is ___ because ___; I still need to check ___.

Evidence boundary: keep learner-owned action/decision, authentic source/task/product and learner meaning together. Record adult preparation, approval, prompts and support separately. Leave missing evidence missing; the authorised assessor/coordinator decides mapping and status.

Project Reset: Solve the Snag · ready-to-print cards

Starter: A team task has stalled. Before changing the plan, what must we check so the problem is about the work rather than a person?

Staff pre-stage before the 16-minute transfer

  • Update the live project board and select one genuine, manageable snag that can be discussed without identifying a pupil as the problem.
  • Pre-check whether one safe first action of five minutes or less could be authorised; prepare an approval-pending alternative.
  • Print the problem-solving record and have current resource, risk and partner information ready.

Cut, point to, rank or sort

Card 1

Check the live project board to find the last step that was genuinely completed.

Card 2

Say that one pupil always causes delays and replace them immediately.

Card 3

Check whether the required resource, permission or adult decision is actually available.

Card 4

Change the whole project goal before comparing any smaller response.

Card 5

Ask what changed between the agreed plan and the current position.

Card 6

Choose the smallest safe next test and set a time to review it.

Decision-lab lenses

STATUS

What has genuinely happened, and what remains unknown?

OPTIONS

Which two safe responses could move the work forward?

CHECKPOINT

Who owns the next action, what needs approval and when will we review it?

Use: preserve the learner's first prediction and explanation. These cards support rehearsal; completed screen/card activity is not qualification evidence.

Skill Before Service · ready-to-print cards

Starter: Two people finish the same task: one is fast but skips the check; one prepares, performs and checks. Which result can the team trust, and why?

Staff pre-stage before the 16-minute transfer

  • Choose one approved micro-skill whose setup, two short attempts and reset can fit within ten minutes.
  • Complete the local risk decision, lay out equipment and PPE in sequence, and prepare an honest desk-based simulation if live work cannot proceed.
  • Display two or three observable quality criteria and assign the adult who will give one precise feedback point.

Cut, point to, rank or sort

Card 1

Rowan starts quickly but has not checked the instructions, equipment or required PPE.

Card 2

Ellis prepares the approved equipment, follows the visible sequence and checks the result against two quality points.

Card 3

Tia copies another learner even though their materials and task are different.

Card 4

Mo completes a safe first attempt, uses one specific feedback point and improves the second attempt.

Card 5

Kai notices an unclear step, stops and asks the responsible adult before continuing.

Decision-lab lenses

PREPARE

What must be ready, approved and understood before the skill starts?

PERFORM

Which sequence and technique produce a safe, useful result?

CHECK

What evidence shows the result meets the agreed quality criteria?

Use: preserve the learner's first prediction and explanation. These cards support rehearsal; completed screen/card activity is not qualification evidence.

Starter: Which claim is safest to share: what we made, what somebody said changed, or a wider impact we have not measured? What evidence changes your answer?

Staff pre-stage before the 16-minute transfer

  • Decide before the lesson whether this is an approved live share or a clearly labelled rehearsal; confirm audience, safeguarding and consent.
  • Preselect the authorised evidence and prepare the storyboard or display base so pupils compose rather than search and decorate.
  • Schedule a maximum two-minute slot per learner or team, prepare accessible participation routes and arrange one bounded feedback response.

Cut, point to, rank or sort

Card 1

We produced twelve approved information packs and recorded when each one was completed.

Card 2

One authorised partner said the new layout was easier to follow; the exact comment and context are recorded.

Card 3

Our project transformed the whole community, although we did not measure any wider change.

Card 4

Everybody loved the project because nobody made a complaint.

Card 5

We learned that our first instruction was unclear, changed it and observed fewer requests for help in the second test.

Card 6

We completed the activity, but we do not yet know its longer-term effect.

Decision-lab lenses

BEFORE

What was the verified starting point or need?

CONTRIBUTION

What did the team and learner genuinely decide, do or produce?

DIFFERENCE

What changed or was learned, what evidence supports it and what remains unknown?

Use: preserve the learner's first prediction and explanation. These cards support rehearsal; completed screen/card activity is not qualification evidence.

Starter: Compare three scenarios and identify which is real, simulated or unauthorised. Explain why correct labelling protects the quality of the learning.

Staff pre-stage before the 16-minute transfer

  • Select and label one approved real activity or honest simulation that can run in eight minutes or less; retain a NOT READY preparation route.
  • Lay out the action map, materials and prediction-observation-next record before pupils arrive.
  • Agree the stop signal, one simple observation measure and the adult responsible for all money, food, data, contact, recording or safety decisions.

Cut, point to, rank or sort

Card 1

APPROVED REAL ACTIVITY: The responsible adult has confirmed the task, resources, users and controls for today's small internal trial.

Card 2

HONEST SIMULATION: The team uses fictional customers, play money and sample materials to test the process without claiming a real sale.

Card 3

UNAUTHORISED ACTIVITY: A pupil messages members of the public, collects names and takes payments without centre approval.

Card 4

NOT READY: The idea is clear, but the required permission or safe resource is still missing, so only preparation may happen today.

Decision-lab lenses

OBSERVE

What did you actually see, hear, count or receive?

NEXT

Does the result support continuing, changing, repeating or stopping?

Eco or Sport Purpose Test · ready-to-print cards

Starter: Identify which challenge is a purposeful test and which is merely a stunt. Explain the missing link in the weaker option.

Staff pre-stage before the 16-minute transfer

  • Choose one route—eco or sport—for each group rather than preparing both, and ensure the baseline and changed version each take no more than five minutes.
  • Complete the safety and access plan, prepare a seated or non-physical route, and lay out simple measuring tools.
  • Print the baseline-test record with the project question and permitted single change already entered.

Cut, point to, rank or sort

Card 1

ECO TEST: Compare the same sample in the current packaging and a lower-waste version, then measure material used.

Card 2

SPORT AND INCLUSION TEST: Keep the activity goal the same, change one instruction or layout feature and count successful turns.

Card 3

STUNT: Build the tallest tower in thirty seconds even though height does not answer the project's question.

Card 4

STUNT: Race to collect the most items, with no baseline, agreed measure or link to the intended user benefit.

Card 5

WEAK TEST: Change the equipment, instructions and team size together, then claim one change caused the result.

Card 6

PURPOSEFUL FOLLOW-UP: Repeat one controlled change for a different user and compare the same measure.

Decision-lab lenses

PURPOSE

Which user, project or teamwork decision will this challenge inform?

MEASURE

What observable result will be compared fairly?

LIMIT

What condition, sample or support means the conclusion must remain cautious?

Use: preserve the learner's first prediction and explanation. These cards support rehearsal; completed screen/card activity is not qualification evidence.
