Choose an idea from evidence

Autumn 2 · Week 1 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can improve a community idea using evidence and a small test.

Success criteria

  • Compare two offers against the same need.
  • Choose an offer that fits the resources.
  • Separate an expected benefit from a result.

GROW · Choose an idea from evidence

Autumn 2 · Week 1

SoW

GROW Weekly - Autumn!B151, C151

  • Develop the social-enterprise idea (e.g. cafe/garden).

Aut2 W1; current calendar absolute W9. This is a classroom teaching and planning lesson. It makes no claim of community impact or qualification completion.

Prior: Use the earlier community idea work if available. Here, the supplied kit-station brief gives a complete practice starting point.

Next: Autumn 2 Week 2 plans roles and resources. Week 3 in the earlier exemplar collection runs the small kit-station test.

Preparation

  • Print the idea/evidence/test cards and new book-return brief. No real users or equipment are essential for the reasoning task.

Care and safety

  • Core activities stay on the classroom table using the supplied paper resources. No public contact, sales, spending, photographs or personal disclosures are required.
  • For a real follow-on project, staff confirm the purpose, materials and audience within the school’s existing arrangements.

Teacher response guidance

  • Guided: A; the two users needing a hint; find a ruler and record help.
  • Independent: D directly addresses returns. E provides opening times, which do not address the recorded tray error.
  • Test: ask someone to return a book using the model trays and record chosen tray and hints. D remains a proposal until checked.

Evidence of learning

Check comparison against a common need, justified evidence use and an answerable next test. These tasks are teaching/rehearsal. Use only the learner’s actual contribution; no automatic award sign-off is implied.

Access and independence

Permit paired discussion, diagram arrows and exact-word scribing. The learner must still choose and justify the offer.

Lundy Loop

Receive the idea and its uncertainty; choose one focused next question that helps the learner make the plan more testable.

Arrival · From idea to useful action

Do not assume enterprise means sales or profit. This is a classroom community-service context.

Starter · What do we know?

One of three is a small constructed sample, not a survey of the school. Separate the observed difficulty from an inferred cause.

I do · Compare the same three things

Model A against the criteria, while admitting labels have not yet been tested. B asks for a different behaviour; C exceeds scope.

We do · Make a linked choice

Ask pupils to explain the evidence link before feedback; not all attractive ideas fit this brief.

I do · Expected is not observed

Distinguish a design rationale from causal proof. A later small test gives limited evidence and should not be inflated.

We do · A test needs a clear task

If pupils conduct a real check, their genuine observations replace blanks; no fixed success is required.

Independent · A new service problem

Fresh record has a different user action and error. Treat these as practice records, not real library usage.

Exit · Pitch one reason and one check

Receive the proposal and ask one question about evidence or feasibility. Pupil owns the revision.

Misconceptions

A good idea is already proven to work. State the expected benefit and what remains to test.

The most popular colour is evidence for every decision. Match evidence to the task the service should help with.
