I DO · teacher explains

An experience, planned, pays double

🌍 What counts

Galleries, gigs, films, murals, dance, theatre — live, recorded or virtual. The Arts Award counts the art form, not the postcode.

🗓️ The plan

WHAT (the event) + WHEN (pinned, or it never happens) + WHAT I’LL LOOK FOR (the detective’s shopping list). Three boxes, one page.

🎯 The hope

“What I hope to get” — inspiration for your portrait? A practitioner to study? Proof that art can be about people like you? Name it now; check it after.

🧭 Our anchor

The class experience: a virtual gallery tour, next lesson, together — pausable, replayable, and yours to respond to. Your own plan can add a second experience beyond it.

Misconception: “A proper arts experience needs a coach trip and a ticket.” A virtual tour you can PAUSE beats a real gallery you rush through. The experience lives in the looking, not the travelling — Part C accepts live, recorded and virtual equally.
WE DO · together on the board

Sort the plan lines — WHAT, WHEN, or WHAT I’LL LOOK FOR?

The event itself = what. The pinned time = when. The hunting list — themes, techniques, one work to describe in depth = what I’ll look for.

HANDS-ON · the main event

Plan it 🗓️

Ground rule (60 seconds): every hope named today gets checked after the experience — we plan like it matters, because it does.

Station 1 · SORT THE MENU

The experience menu: virtual tours, a street-mural trail video, recorded dance and performance. Which pulls you? Which would you add?

Station 2 · MY PLAN

The Part C plan page: what / when / what I’ll look for. Verbal drafting first is a route; the plan is yours in your own hand.

Station 3 · THE HOPE

Complete and say it: “I chose this because… I hope to get…” — to a partner or the TA. Hopes said aloud get checked honestly later.

GCSE Stretch: the focus question: one genuine question your experience might answer — written at the top of the plan like a research title.
