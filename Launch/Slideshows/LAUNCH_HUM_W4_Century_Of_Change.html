I Do

I Do: Change Has Dimensions

Key Idea: A century of change is judged on four dials: direction, pace, extent and domain — and the dials rarely agree.

📖 The Four Dials

Direction — Growth, decline, transformation — which way?

Pace — Rapid rupture or slow drift?

Extent & domain — How much, and in WHICH area — economy, work, identity?

🧠 Watch me think

Set the dials for 1915–2015: economy — direction down, pace gradual-then-sudden, extent severe. Daily work — transformed (shift whistle to shift patterns of a different kind). Identity — direction steady, extent shallow: the Boro is still the Boro. One century, three domains, three different readings. 'Everything changed' just died of measurement.

I Do

I Do: Testing a Turning Point

Key Idea: 'Turning point' is a claim, not a feeling: it must show direction DIFFERENT after than before, with the hinge doing the turning.

👀 Look: “turning point” is a claim, not a feeling. Show the bend, or it is only a famous date.

📖 The Hinge Test

Before-line — Establish the trajectory up to the candidate year.

After-line — Establish the trajectory following it.

Attribution — Show the hinge caused the bend — not just sat at it.

🧠 Watch me think

Test 2015 as the hinge: before-line — steel already shrinking for forty years. After-line — continued decline, plus renewal projects. The direction didn't BEND in 2015; it concluded. So 2015 is an ENDPOINT, not a turning point — the bend came earlier, arguably the 1970s oil-and-steel shocks. The test just demoted the famous year. That's LAUNCH: the obvious candidate interrogated, not crowned.
