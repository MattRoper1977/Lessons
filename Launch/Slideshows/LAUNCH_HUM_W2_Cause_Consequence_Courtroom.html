I DO · teacher explains

Chains of consequence — and whether the evidence holds up

➡️ The chain

Causes chain into consequences: war → labour shortage → invitation → arrival → new communities. History is dominoes, not single events.

🁢 Push and pull

PUSH factors drive people out (danger, hardship); PULL factors draw them in (jobs, family, safety). Most migrations have both — GCSE answers name both.

⚖️ Reliability

Before evidence enters your account, test it: who made it, when, and why? An eyewitness at the scene beats a rumour written fifty years later.

✏️ The paragraph

Point → Evidence → Explain. One claim, one source to back it, one sentence on why it matters. That structure carries you to grade 3 and beyond.

Misconception: “One cause, one effect — history is simple.” Real history is CHAINS: several causes feeding one event, one event driving many consequences. The Windrush had push factors, pull factors and an invitation — a strong answer names more than one.
WE DO · together on the board

The courtroom warm-up — does it make the source MORE or LESS reliable?

MORE: close to the event, made at the time, official record, matches other sources. LESS: far from the event, long after, made to sell or persuade, contradicted elsewhere.

HANDS-ON · the main event

Dominoes, then the courtroom ⚖️

Ground rule (60 seconds): in the courtroom, we attack the SOURCE, never the speaker — verdicts need reasons, and verdicts can be given out loud.

Station 1 · HUMAN DOMINOES

Each of you holds a card — causes and consequences of migration. Build the physical chain across the room: each domino must justify its link to the next before it stands.

Station 2 · THE COURTROOM

A source takes the stand. Prosecution argues it is unreliable; defence argues for it; the jury tests both against the checklist. Verdicts can be delivered verbally — then one line goes on paper.

Station 3 · THE PARAGRAPH

Write ONE structured paragraph with the frame: Point → Evidence (name your source) → Explain. Oral rehearsal first, then write. This paragraph seeds the Week 6 account.

GCSE Stretch: the full verdict: “this source is useful because [content]… and because [provenance]… however…”
