I Do

I Do: The Support Spectrum

Key Idea: 'How far' questions demand a position on a spectrum: fully supports — partly — silent — undermines. Every position must cite its details.

📖 The Spectrum

Fully supports — Multiple details align with the claim's full scope.

Partly supports — Details align with some of the claim — name which part.

Silent / undermines — Says nothing on it — or actively cuts against it.

🧠 Watch me think

Watch me place Exhibit A: the claim says 'thriving in the 1900s' — plural years. My photo gives one morning: ships (detail), full quay (detail), gangs working (detail). Strong FOR that morning; silent on the other 3,652 days. Position: partly supports — strongly for its moment, unable to speak for the decade. Cited both ways.

I Do

I Do: Weighing Support Against Provenance

Key Idea: Support is content × credibility: a detail counts for less when purpose, moment or distance weakens the witness.

📖 Weighing Moves

Purpose discount — The postcard's 'bustling' is advertising — support shrinks.

Moment discount — One morning cannot carry 'through the 1900s'.

Distance discount — A 1950 memoir remembers 1907 through 43 years of fog.

🧠 Watch me think

Same spectrum, now weighted: the ledger outranks the photo — years of standardised entries versus one morning's frame. The postcard's caption is enthusiastic and PAID to be. My verdict sentence carries the weights: 'The ledgers and receipts support the claim strongly; the photo adds texture for a single day; the caption adds little beyond intent.'

Key Facts

  • Support is content × credibility — both get weighed.
  • One vivid moment never outweighs years of standardised records.
  • Undermining sources are gifts: they make verdicts honest.
  • 'How far' is the GCSE's favourite question — LAUNCH answers it in degrees.
