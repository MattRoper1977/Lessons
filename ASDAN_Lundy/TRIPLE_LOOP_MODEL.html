One lesson, three processes

The triple loop

Learning, participation and portfolio are three loops that run through the same ASDAN lesson. Each has its own question, its own close and its own owner — and collapsing them into one is how honest evidence goes wrong.

Toolkit homeEvidence states
LEARNING LOOP

Clarify → elicit → interpret → respond → revisit.

Question: what did this response mean, and which next step follows?
Owner: the teacher, with the TA.
Close: the return visit — the adult goes back to the step already given and asks what happened to it.

LUNDY LOOP

Space → Voice → Audience → Influence, around a genuinely open matter.

Question: did the pupil get a genuine say, and did it change anything?
Owner: the pupil, with the adult as audience.
Close: pathway-relative — BUILD when an adult genuinely receives it; GROW and LAUNCH when the pupil writes the line. No initial is expected.

PORTFOLIO LOOP

draft → curriculum-aligned → candidate evidence → centre-verified → submitted → certified.

Question: how far has this item honestly travelled toward an award?
Owner: teacher, then assessor and IQA — states 4–6 are centre and awarding-body actions.
Close: the awarding organisation's confirmation, and nothing earlier.

The one-sentence rule: a photograph, a pupil response and an assessor judgement can all be useful, but they are not interchangeable. Ask which loop an artefact belongs to before asking whether it is good.

Where the loops touch — and the boundary at each seam

Learning ↔ Lundy

The pupil's contribution is both a response to read and a voice to receive. The boundary: the adult's read (which branch, which next step) is never written on the pupil's surface, and the pupil's close stays on their pen on GROW and LAUNCH. One next step per pupil per lesson; the day-close, where used, returns to it rather than minting another.

Learning ↔ Portfolio

Good teaching produces evidence as a by-product. The boundary: the next step is chosen for learning, never to fill an evidence gap. Where a criterion genuinely lacks evidence, the honest move is a real later opportunity — create the occasion, never manufacture the artefact.

Lundy ↔ Portfolio

Participation can produce portfolio-worthy products, and a pupil decides what enters their evidence with the adult, not for them. The boundary: a vote is not evidence, a closure line is never copied into a portfolio note, and "private" and "pass" stay real options with no evidence cost.

All three at tutor time

The proposed Portfolio Studio touches all three loops in fifteen minutes, which is exactly why its two lanes stay separate: the optional day-close read records nothing; the curation lane moves one existing item no further than candidate evidence queued for review. Worked example — one lesson, three verdicts

A BUILD FoodWise lesson ends with a safely prepared snack, a ring on the printed strip, and a photo. Learning: the response showed secure knife technique — next lesson starts one step harder. Lundy: the pupil chose the recipe from three safe options and heard the class plan change because of it — received, closed. Portfolio: the photo plus the pupil's exact words are offered as candidate evidence against the named challenge; the assessor, not the lesson, decides sufficiency. Three verdicts, three owners — and none of them borrows another's authority.

What this model does not do: it adds no mark, no record, no percentage and no second copy of anything. It is a way of talking about work that already exists, so that closure stays honest, participation stays real and evidence claims stay accurate.
