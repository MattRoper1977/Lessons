BUILD learning design

SEE

Notice one concrete example, rule, need or risk.

CHOOSE

Make a genuine bounded choice from safe options.

SAY

Give a reason through the learner’s available communication route.

DO

Complete an observable action or product, then review it.

Prompt-fade ladder: wait → self-prompt → point/visual → general verbal → specific verbal → model → direct support → fade.

Evidence test: pupil decision + observable action/product + pupil meaning/review + honest adult-support record.

Source and scope assumptions

  • The authoritative Autumn 2 SOW contains four weekly lanes, so this draft contains 24 lessons rather than inventing a fifth Careers lane.
  • Estate Week 8 is the existing cross-slot Portfolio Consolidation Studio; Autumn 2 W1-W6 therefore display as global Weeks 9-14.
  • The SOW wording is preserved exactly in each core_outcome. Titles, examples and access routes elaborate the outcome but do not replace it.
  • Careers is cross-linked only where the SOW places it: Living Independently W4 and Community/Vocational W3.
  • All practical work, ingredients, tools, projects, audiences and evidence systems are selected or approved locally. No partner, contact, reply, visit or impact is assumed.
  • These are curriculum-aligned ASDAN/UAS taster contexts. Current module or challenge codes, registration, assessment criteria, centre approvals and certification status must be verified locally.
  • Supported, Standard and Stretch are movable access routes, not fixed pupil labels. Pointing, signing, demonstrating, exact-word scribing and a later re-attempt remain valid response routes.
  • A pupil never self-certifies completion. An authorised adult records observation, support and any assessor judgement separately from pupil authorship.

Time and credit boundary

At one 40-minute lesson per week, a six-week sequence provides four hours and cannot by itself evidence any criterion requiring at least 10 hours of plan use.

Create the learner's plan before plan-use activity begins. Log genuine learner activity with dates, durations, evidence IDs, and assessor verification. Only actual plan use counts; do not pad a log or count unrelated teaching time.

Entry 3 units named by the current compliance source as having a 10-hour plan-use gate: DecMkSkE3, LSkE3, TmWkSkE3, ThSkE3, WellbLeE3. The six lessons in one lane total four scheduled hours; scheduled pack duration is not individual learner evidence.

Authentic, valid, current, sufficient and traceable evidence

authentic

Is this demonstrably the learner's own work, words, decisions, or actions?

Pass: Provenance is clear; staff and technology record or enable the learner's response without supplying it; prompts and support are documented.

valid

Does the evidence directly demonstrate the named assessment criterion at its command verb, level, numerical minimum, and activity condition?

Pass: The evidence is criterion-specific and the activity allows a reliable assessment judgement.

current

Does the evidence represent the learner's current competence?

Pass: Evidence comes from the current assessment window or is admitted through the centre's formal RPL process.

sufficient

Is there enough explicit evidence to cover every element and minimum of each claimed criterion?

Pass: Evidence breadth, quantity, and detail allow a defensible assessor, IQA, and EQA decision.

reliable and traceable

Can assessor, IQA, and EQA locate the evidence and reach a defensible decision?

Pass: Each item has a criterion reference, evidence ID/location, date, learner attribution, and relevant support/observation record.

Record what the learner actually did or said. Do not infer competence or write a model response on the learner's behalf. The observation record may contribute evidence but does not confer criterion, unit, or qualification achievement by itself.

Assessment-plan field checklist

  • Centre name
  • ASDAN Centre number
  • Cohort name — Use the name recorded on the ASDAN account.
  • Additional cohort information — Include learner count and reasonable adjustments to consider; avoid personally identifying details.
  • Course start date
  • Planned course finish date
  • Unit title(s) — Include every actual PEQ unit assessed through activities on the record.
  • Unit code(s) — Include every actual PEQ unit code assessed through activities on the record.
  • Challenge title(s) — Challenges provide context; they are not PEQ units.
  • Challenge code(s)
  • Date / term and week
  • Assessment activity — State what learners will do and record relevant differentiation.
  • Method of assessment
  • Assessment evidence to be generated by learners
  • Unit and assessment-criteria mapping — For mixed-level cohorts, include all mapped units and levels on the same assessment-planning record.
  • Additional mapping — Record curriculum, Gatsby or other organisational links separately from PEQ criteria.
  • Assessor/Educator name
  • Assessor/Educator signature
  • Date submitted for pre-delivery IQA
  • IQA name
  • Pre-delivery IQA feedback/actions record — Store feedback and related actions separately on ASDAN's IQA pre-delivery template or the centre's approved equivalent.

Gate: Centre-devised assessment materials must complete pre-delivery IQA before assessed use.

Observation-record field checklist

  • learner
  • date
  • time
  • qualification aim
  • unit
  • assessor or observer
  • location or context
  • activity observed
  • criterion references potentially evidenced
  • specific learner actions or words
  • neutral prompts or questions used
  • support or reasonable adjustment used
  • evidence ids or linked files
  • assessor judgement contributes
  • assessor judgement more evidence required
  • assessor judgement reassessment or top up required
  • assessor signature
  • assessor signature date

Use neutral prompts only and record them. Completed assessment records must be signed and dated by the learner and assessor and, where sampled, the IQA.

Access without lowering demand

The access method may change; the assessment standard must not. Record all support and reasonable adjustments.

  • visual task sequencing
  • symbol support
  • enlarged or low-clutter layout
  • supported reading or read-aloud instructions
  • additional processing time
  • speech-to-text that records the learner's own response
  • a scribe who records rather than generates the learner's response
  • visual cue cards that prompt sequence without supplying assessed content
  • assessor observation capturing authentic spoken evidence

Models, worked examples, sentence construction, and answer-generating scaffolds belong in teaching or rehearsal. Remove answer-generating support for the assessed attempt; retain only documented access support that preserves the standard.

Separate Short Course, Duke, UAS and PEQ mapping

One authentic project may generate evidence for PEQ, an ASDAN Short Course, Junior/Young Duke, or AQA UAS only where it separately meets each framework's current requirements. No framework's completion, hours, certificate, unit code, or credit transfers automatically to another.

Do not infer AQA UAS codes or achievement from the SOW label. Use the exact current unit selected by the centre and its separate assessment process.

Evidence, privacy and claims guardrails

The HTML stores no pupil response. A photo alone is not evidence of understanding. Adult support is recorded separately from pupil authorship. Evidence status is not a grade. Supported / Standard / Stretch are movable routes, not identities. There is no upload quota or evidence debt.

  • Clicks and displayed answers are rehearsal only.
  • Speech-to-text or exact-word scribing may record the pupil’s response but must not supply it.
  • Model answers and answer-building support belong to teaching/rehearsal, not an unsupported assessed claim.
  • Food, tools, chemicals, audiences, partner contact, off-site work and images follow current local approval and safeguarding controls.
  • Never invent or backfill a pupil quote, date, action, audience response, partner, visit or impact.

Preparation checklist

  • Choose the approved practical, simulated or fictional route.
  • Check current risk assessment, allergy, consent, privacy and safeguarding information.
  • Prepare visual structure and communication routes before demand.
  • Name the adult who will genuinely receive the pupil response.
  • Decide whether any evidence capture has genuine value; no capture is a valid outcome.
  • Record adult preparation, prompts, adjustments and scribing separately from pupil authorship.
  • Tell the pupil what their response changed or what an adult will do next.
