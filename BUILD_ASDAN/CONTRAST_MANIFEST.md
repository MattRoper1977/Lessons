# Contrast manifest — BUILD_ASDAN

Generated by `_framework/contrast_check.js --manifest`. Re-run it after any
change to the visual layer and compare: **the layer must not add a row.**

## How this was measured

Two conditions, both load-bearing:

1. **Walked to the slide first.** Computed styles on a `display:none` subtree
   do not reflect what paints, and Chromium never starts an animation there, so
   a label held by an animation reads as invisible. Answers are revealed and
   every I Do step is out — the worst case for a reader.
2. **Background actually painted, not the immediate parent.** Every element gets
   its translucent backgrounds composited down the ancestor chain, and the
   nearest ancestor that really paints one is named. Then one instance of each
   pattern has its ink hidden and its box screenshotted, and the modal pixel is
   taken — the real painted background including gradients and overlays. Where
   the two disagree the painted pixel wins and the chain value is shown beside
   it. That difference is not cosmetic: it moves rows across the threshold in
   both directions.

Large text is the stricter reading: 3:1 only at ≥24px, or ≥19px bold.

## The rulings applied

- **FIX** — pupil-facing text that carries meaning, below target, and fixable by
  moving the ink without touching a pathway, tier or suite identity hue.
- **left — identity hue** — the failing colour *is* the estate's colour language
  (a pathway, tier or subject hue). A contrast fix that recolours the language is
  a bigger regression than the thing it fixes. Not a contrast lever.
- **left — UI chrome** — a control, not something a pupil reads to learn.
- **left — decorative** — carries nothing the surrounding text does not.
