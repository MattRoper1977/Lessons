3. Success check

By the end you should be able to say: 'I can plan an eco action with a visible result.' What will that take today?

Name one thing you’ll need to do or try.

1. Visible result

Why does an eco action need a VISIBLE result to work as a challenge?

You can't reflect on what you can't see — the before/after photo is both motivation and evidence.

2. Chain it

Link one small eco habit to its big-picture effect in three steps.

E.g. bottle reused → one less made → oil and energy unspent. Small hands, long levers.

3. Keeps working

What makes an eco action keep working AFTER you stop? Name the design trick.

Build a system, not an effort: a labelled bin recycles daily; a one-off tidy lasts a week.

Step 0 of 3

Simple eco challenges (recycle, reduce, tidy).

Recycle, reduce, reuse, tidy — simple moves with visible results. The eco challenge is choosing one and making its result photographable.

Linking to our Community Upcycling project.

Our upcycling project IS eco in action — pallets that were waste becoming a planter that gives. Your challenge can plug straight into it.

Recording the difference made.

Recording the difference means before-and-after: the corner cleared, the bags filled, the kit reused. Measured change is the evidence ASDAN wants.
