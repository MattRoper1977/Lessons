SPACE VOICE AUDIENCE INFLUENCE

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

BUILD · ASDAN · Week 3. ASDAN cross-strand. The card, the snack, the team.

Objective: follow a visual sequence card for a household task, use that same card to complete a simple cookery challenge, and decide who does which job in the team.

Success evidence: a snack card with all five steps ticked in order, one name beside every step including the clear-up step, one word for how your team decided, and one sentence about the finished plate.

No personal or family disclosure. Every card, job card and photograph today is school-approved. You describe the card, never your home. Nobody is asked out loud about allergies, diets or food at home.

40 minutes. Offline.

Before any food is out of the box, check the class allergen and dietary list held by the class team. A pupil with a listed allergy or dietary need takes a non-food job (card holder, ticker, plate-up checker) or the approved alternative. Do not ask pupils out loud what they are allergic to.
Resources, all in the ASDAN box in the food room cupboard, prepped by the class team the day before: the big five-step snack card for the board; one snack card per team; one set of the five step pictures per pupil; job cards with team names already written on; tick sheets; the two school photographs for the Starter; pre-sliced cucumber, cheese slices, crackers and plates; paper towels, wipes and a bin bag. There is no knife in this lesson — the class team slices the cucumber before it comes out of the box, so step two is putting cucumber on the plate. Run this in the food room and wash hands at the sink. If the food room is not free, run it in the classroom and wash hands at the corridor sink on the way in. Teams of three, pre-named on the job cards. If the job cards are not there, work in pairs at usual seats.

Running one card, one step at a time

SEQUENCE means steps done in a fixed order. ROLE means the job one person agrees to do. RESPONSIBILITY means the step that is yours to finish and tick.

Watch me run the approved snack card. It has five pictures: wash hands, cucumber on the plate, cheese on the cracker, plate up, wipe the table and bin the waste.

The cucumber is already sliced. Nobody uses a knife today.

I read all five pictures first. I do not touch anything yet.

Step one is wash hands. I say step one out loud. Then I do it. Then I tick it.

Now I look at picture three. I am not sure if the cheese goes on before the cucumber.

I do not guess. I look at the card again. The card shows cucumber first, so I follow the card.

If the card does not show it, I say I am not sure and I ask an adult.

Every pupil shows a picture before anyone speaks.

Put the snack steps in order

You each have the same five pictures from the snack card. They are mixed up.

Put them in order, first to last, left to right on your desk.

Start with the picture that needs nothing before it. Then ask what that one makes possible.

When your five are in order, sit back. Nobody explains yet.

Then two people say which two pictures were hardest to order, and why.

Keep your pictures in order. You will use them in a minute.

Ordering is the task. Do not accept a reason until every desk has five pictures in a line.

Do the job list before any food is out.

From the card to the team

The card tells you what order the steps go in. It does not tell you who does them.

A snack is finished when every step on the card has one name beside it.

Watch me put names on the card. Step one, wash hands: everyone. Step two, cucumber: one person. Step three, cheese: one person. Step four, plate up: one person. Step five, wipe and bin: two people.

Step five is a household task of its own. Wiping the table and binning the waste is part of the job.

I read the job list back to the team before we start. Anyone can say swap me at that point.

A job you agreed to is a responsibility. It is finished when the step is ticked.

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Rows are choices. Any pupil may change row.

Choose your row. Then choose your step.

  • ◆ Supported — take one step. Say it out loud. Tick it when it is done.
  • ▲ Standard — run all five steps with your team. Write one name beside every step.
  • ★ Stretch — as Standard, and write what your team would change if one person were away.

You work in a team of three. Your team name is on your job card.

Here is how your team decides. Go round the team. Each person says which step they want. If two people want the same step, the person with no step yet takes it. You can pass and take the step that is left.

If you do not want to choose, say pass. You will still have a step.

Write one word on the tick sheet for how you decided: asked, swapped or passed.

These rows are choices, not labels. You can start on one row and move to another.

Read the go-round out loud, then let the team run it. Take swaps now, not during the cooking.

Whichever row you take, point at the card or the tick sheet that backs what you say.

Doing time. Least prompt needed.

Make the snack. Then clear up.

Work the card with your team. Say each step before you do it. Tick it when it is done.

Everyone washes hands first. That is step one for all of you.

Stop making at six minutes. The last three minutes are step five: wipe the table and bin the waste.

On your sheet, write one name beside every step, including step five.

Circle one word for how your team decided: asked, swapped or passed.

Write one sentence about the finished plate. Say what you can see on it.

If you are not sure about something, write it down and mark it not sure. That is a real answer and it counts.

Work at your own pace. One step done properly and ticked is worth more than a step rushed.

Doing time, not reading time. Call six minutes out loud so step five is not lost. Prompt on a missing name.

Name back the pupil's exact words.

You say it. The adult says it back. You choose one thing for next week.

You can stay quiet. Nothing happens if you do not speak. Pointing at your sheet counts as speaking.

Say one job you did today. Say one step your team had to fix.

The adult names both back to you, in your exact words.

You confirm it, or you correct it. Correcting the adult is expected here.

Then choose one real next action: keep your job, swap to another step, or take step five.

Your choice is written down and given back to you next week.

TA Brief

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.

Doing the task

  1. ◆ and up — the step you took is ticked on the card and your name is written beside it.
  2. ◆ and up — the tick sheet says how your team decided. One word is enough: asked, swapped or passed.
  3. ◆ and up — anything you were not sure about is marked not sure rather than guessed.
