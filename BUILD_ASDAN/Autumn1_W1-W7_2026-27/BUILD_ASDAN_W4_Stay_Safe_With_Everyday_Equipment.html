Arrival · retrieve

A check is a list you do in order

A safety check is not a quick look. A quick look is not a check.

It is a list of things to look for, done in the same order every time.

The order matters, because the order is what stops you skipping one.

Today's check has four things on it. Plug. Cable. Body. Space.

You do all four, even when the first one is obviously fine.

Do not let a pupil shorten the list. The whole point of a check is that it does not depend on judgement.

You need one piece of equipment from the tray, one check card, and the project planning sheet.

Nothing gets switched on today. Everything is unplugged before it reaches your table.

Checking is a job you do before the power, not after it.

Starter · create the need

Starter · create the need

Two people check the same kettle

The first person looks at it and says it is fine.

The second person checks the plug, the cable, the body and the space around it.

They both said it was fine. Only one of them can say what they checked.

If something goes wrong later, only one of them has an answer.

A check you can name is a check somebody else can trust.

  • 1 · plug: pins straight, no cracks.
  • 2 · cable: no cuts or joins.
  • 3 · body: no damage, no leaks.
  • 4 · space: dry, clear, near a socket.

Ask yourself which of the two people you would want checking the kettle you use.

Most people pick the second one, and the reason is always that they can say what they did.

I Do · model

I Do · model

Four steps, out loud

I pick up the check card first, before I touch the equipment.

Plug. I look at the pins. They are straight and there are no cracks. I tick plug.

Cable. I run my hand along it. No cuts, no tape, no joins. I tick cable.

Body. I turn it round and look at the whole outside. No dents, nothing loose. I tick body.

Space. The bench is dry, the socket is close, and nothing is on top of the cable. I tick space.

Four ticks and I sign the card. If any one of them fails, I do not tick it and I tell an adult.

A card with three ticks and one blank is a good card. It is not a failed one.

Model one genuine fail if you can plant it. A check that always passes teaches pupils to tick without looking.

Watch me put the card down first. The card leads and the hands follow it.

Picking the equipment up first is how a step gets skipped.

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the faults

Six fault cards are on your table. Sort them into stop and tell, or carry on.

A cut in the cable. A dusty body. A cracked plug. A bench with water on it.

A missing instruction sheet. A socket on the other side of the room.

Then match each stop-and-tell card to the step of the check that would find it.

When your table is done, hold up the card you argued about most.

Two of the six could go either way depending on how bad they are. Decide, and say how bad is too bad.

I Do 2 · connect

From a check to a challenge to a plan

A personal-responsibility challenge is one job. You take it on for the half term.

Checking the equipment before every session is a challenge. So is setting the room out.

It has to happen every week. If it happens once, it is a one-off.

It needs steps. It needs kit. Without both, it never starts.

So write three steps for your challenge, and what each step needs.

A step with no resource next to it is the step that stops the project.

Someone else could do the job too. It is still yours if your name is on it.

What makes it yours is that it is written down with your name against it.

We Do 2 · lab

We Do 2 · lab

Check it for real

Work in twos. One of you runs the check out loud while the other holds the card.

Say each step before you do it, and say what you found.

The person with the card ticks only what they heard checked.

If you did not say it, it does not get a tick, even if you did it.

Then swap over and do the same with a different item.

Say it before you do it, every step. Saying it afterwards is a report, not a check.

Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Card, challenge, and three steps

Work on your own. Finish your check card and sign it.

Then pick your job for the half term.

Write one reason you chose that one and not another.

Then write three steps your project needs, and what each step needs to happen.

If you finish early, say which of the three steps is most likely to go wrong.

  • Supported · complete the check card and choose a challenge.
  • Standard · complete the card, choose a challenge with a reason, and write three steps with resources.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name the step most likely to go wrong, with the reason.

Sign the card with your name, not your initials. A signature is what makes it a record.

If you found a fault, write what you found as well as leaving the box unticked.

Say the challenge to an adult

Read your challenge and your reason to the adult at your table.

They read it back exactly as you said it.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they write the new one.

Then choose what happens next: start this week, start next week, or change the challenge.

Write that choice on the sheet before you go.

The check card goes on the tray with the equipment, not in your folder.
