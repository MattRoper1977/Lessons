Start with what is on the card

A routine is a set of steps done in the same order each time. The order is what makes it a routine.

A step you can see and check is better than a step you have to remember.

In this lesson every routine comes from a school-approved card. You describe the card, not yourself.

Read the three lines aloud once. Do not invite examples from home; this is the guard the whole lesson rests on.

You will need three things this lesson: the routine card, the challenge board and the photograph set. Nothing else, and nothing from outside school.

Why does order matter?

Here are two cards for the same routine. One is in order. One is not.

Look at the first step on each card. Ask yourself: could you do step two if you had not done step one?

A routine out of order still has all its steps. It just stops working.

  • 1 · notice the first step
  • 2 · check what it needs
  • 3 · say what happens if it is skipped

Ordering a routine card

ROUTINE means steps done in the same order every time. CHALLENGE means something you choose to work at over weeks. NEED means something an area is missing that people would use.

Watch me order the handwashing card. I read every step first, before I move anything.

I look for the step that needs nothing before it. Turning on the tap needs nothing before it, so that is step one.

Then I ask what step one makes possible. Wet hands make soap useful, so soap is step two.

I keep asking that same question until the card is in order. If two steps could go either way, I say so out loud rather than guessing quietly.

Last, I check the card backwards. If the last step still makes sense with the one before it, the order holds.

Say the backwards check aloud. It is the part pupils skip and the part that catches the error.

If a step could sit in two places, put it where the arrow still makes sense, and say out loud that you were unsure.

From a routine to a year of challenges

A Duke challenge works like a routine card, but the steps are spread across weeks instead of minutes.

A good challenge for you has three things: you can start it this term, you can show it to someone, and you can keep going at it when it gets dull.

Here is the approved challenge board. Each card says what you would do and how you would show it.

I choose two. I say why each one fits the three things above, and I say what would make me stop, so I can plan around it now.

And from challenges to our area

Our community project starts with looking, not fixing. From the approved photographs, I name one thing this area does well and one thing it is missing.

I say what I can see in the photograph that supports each one. A need I cannot point to in a picture is a guess, and I mark it as a guess.

Keep the photo set closed until the challenge choices are recorded, or the two tasks blur together.

A strength and a need are the same kind of claim. Both need something in the picture you can point at, and both are worth less if you cannot.

Independent record

Order your routine card and run the backwards check. Write the two steps you were least sure about and say why.

Record your two Duke challenge choices. For each, write one reason it fits you and one thing that might make you stop.

From the approved photographs, write one thing our area does well and one thing it needs. Beside each, write what you can see that supports it.

If you are not sure about something, write it down and mark it as not sure. That is a real answer and it counts.

Work at your own pace. Finishing two parts properly is better than rushing three, and an honest not sure is worth more than a guess that looks tidy.

Audience receives; Influence changes one next action

Space stays open for everyone in this room. You do not have to speak to take part, and pointing at your sheet counts.

Say one challenge you chose and one thing our area needs. The adult names both back to you exactly as you said them.

Confirm or correct what was named back. Then choose the next honest action: keep the challenge, swap it, or ask for one more week to decide.

Whatever you choose is recorded and returned to next week. Deciding later is a decision, not a gap.
