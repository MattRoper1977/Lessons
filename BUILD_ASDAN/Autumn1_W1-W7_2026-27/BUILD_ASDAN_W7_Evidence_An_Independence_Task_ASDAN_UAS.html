Do the Task, Show the Proof

BUILD · ASDAN · Week 7. ASDAN cross-strand. Do the Task, Show the Proof.

Objective: work through one independence task card step by step, record proof with a photo and a note, write one line about the step that was hard and what you did next, and write your job on the class contribution page.

Success evidence: one task card worked through, two photos of the work, two note strips carrying a name and a date, one line about a hard step, and one job line on the class contribution page.

Every task comes from the approved task card box. You do not record your home, your family or your beliefs. You do not record how anybody looks. You record the steps printed on the card.

40 minutes. Offline.

Arrival · retrieve

Arrival · retrieve

Proof is not the same as memory

You did a lot of good work last week.

Some of it was written down, but most of it was not.

Work that nobody recorded is gone by Monday.

An assessor cannot mark a thing they cannot see.

Proof has three parts: a photo, a note, and your name with the date.

Miss one part and the evidence stops counting.

Read the three parts once and leave them on the board. Pupils lose evidence to a missing date far more often than to a missing photo.

You need three things today: a task card, a pad of note strips, and the class camera.

Four words matter today: task, step, proof and contribution.

The task is the whole job, and a step is one small piece of it.

Your contribution is the part of the group's job that you did.

You can do a perfect job and still score nothing, because nobody recorded it.

Starter · create the need

Starter · create the need

Two pupils, one job

Two pupils made a hot drink last week, and both did the job safely.

One took a photo and wrote a single line, with a name and the date.

The other did not, and told an adult about it at the end.

Only one of them can be marked.

This is not about who tried harder, it is about what got recorded.

  • 1 · do the step.
  • 2 · take the photo.
  • 3 · write the note.
  • 4 · add your name and the date.

That order matters, because tidying up first puts the proof in the bin with the mess.

I Do · model

I Do · model

One card, one step at a time

My card says: make a hot drink safely.

First I read every step on the card, and I do not start yet.

There are five steps, so I take them one at a time.

Now I do step one, and fill the kettle to the line.

Then I stop and photograph the kettle at the line.

That photo shows one step, but it does not show the whole job.

So I take a second photo at step four.

Now the note, and six words is enough.

My note says: filled kettle to line, poured slowly.

Then my name, and then today's date.

Say each part out loud as you write it. Photo. Note. Name. Date. The words are the thing being taught, not the drink.

Watch where I point the camera: at the work, never at my own face.

A photo of a person is not proof of a step.

One more rule: if a step goes wrong, photograph it anyway.

A step that went wrong is still real evidence, because it records what happened.

We Do · everyone commits

Sort the proof cards

Six cards are on your table, and each one shows a photo and a note.

Sort the six cards into two groups: proof, and not proof.

Then match every not proof card to the part it is missing.

The missing part is the photo, the note, or the date.

Hold up your card even when you are not certain, because a guess we can see beats a blank.

Two tables may sort a card differently, and then both tables give a reason.

I Do 2 · connect

I Do 2 · connect

The step that was hard

Now the second part of the job, which is resilience.

Resilience means you got stuck and carried on anyway.

It is not a feeling, it is something you did.

My hard step was step four, because the jar lid was stiff.

I did not stop, I ran the lid under warm water, and then it turned.

It was hard is not enough on its own.

The lid was stiff so I warmed it is enough.

You are allowed to say it was hard, as long as you also say what you did.

Use this frame every time.

  • 1 · name the step that stuck.
  • 2 · say what you tried.
  • 3 · say whether it worked.
  • 4 · say what you would try next time.

The same frame works on your half term challenge, not only on today's card.

If nothing was hard today, record that as well.

We Do 2 · lab

We Do 2 · lab

Rehearse the line before you need it

Work in twos, and put your own task card face up between you.

Point at the step you think will be the hardest one.

Say the line out loud: name that step, then name what you will try if it sticks.

Your partner holds up one finger for the step and one for what you will try.

If a finger is missing, they name the part you left out.

Say the line again with that part in it.

Then swap over, and you count the parts for them.

You may not say the line is fine, because fine gives them nothing to change. Name the missing part instead.

9 min
Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Your card, your proof

Work on your own now, and take your own task card.

Your card is one job this room needs doing, so your proof is part of the class contribution.

Do the steps in the order they are printed on the card.

Take two photos: one near the start, and one near the end.

Write one note strip for each photo.

Put your name and today's date on both strips.

Then write your hard step line: stuck, and then what you did next.

Your real line may name a different step than you predicted, and that is fine.

Then put your strips into your ASDAN folder.

  • Supported · do three steps, take one photo, and an adult writes your words on the strip and your hard step line.
  • Standard · do all the steps, take two photos, write two strips, and write one hard step line.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name what you would change on the card for next time.

Do not copy my kettle note, because your card is your card.

If a step is not safe on your own, put your hand up and wait.

Waiting for help is a step, so record that as well.

If you finish early, check that both strips carry a date.

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE: the learner may pause or choose a private response.

VOICE: the evidence statement remains in the learner’s chosen communication mode.

AUDIENCE: a familiar adult receives and names back exactly what was heard or seen.

INFLUENCE: one real next action is recorded: retain, add method detail, or leave open.

Read one strip to an adult

Read one note strip to the adult at your table.

They read it back to you word for word.

If that is not what you meant, say it again and they write the new one.

Then they open your ASDAN folder with you and check the date.

Then write the job you did on the class contribution page by the door.

That page records what this group did this half term.

The community project jobs go on the same page.

One thing happens next: keep this card, take the next job on it, or start a new card.

Write that choice on the folder sheet before you leave.

Your photos go onto the class tablet, and never onto a phone.
