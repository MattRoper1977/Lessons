Set Up the Space, Run the Safety Check, Agree the Goal

BUILD · ASDAN · Week 2. ASDAN cross-strand. Set Up the Space, Run the Safety Check, Agree the Goal.

Objective: set up an approved workspace so every item has one place, complete the approved first-aid and safety challenge card in the right order, and agree our project goal by a class vote with two success criteria anyone can check.

Success evidence: a space set up with every item in a named place, the approved safety card completed in order, and the agreed goal written down with two success criteria.

No personal or family disclosure. Every tray, card and goal in this lesson is school-approved and supplied. You describe the approved materials, never your home.

40 minutes. Offline.

Arrival · retrieve

Arrival · retrieve

Start with what can be checked

Last week you put a routine card in order. If you were not here, that is fine. An order means doing the same steps in the same sequence every time.

A check is something another person can do and get the same answer.

Organised means every item has one place and goes back to it. It does not mean it looks nice.

The tray, the cards and the goal all come from approved school materials. You never talk about home.

Print before the lesson: a six-step safety card per pupil with one step scrambled, a three-step card for each ◆ pupil, a six-step card for each ▲ and ★, and a correct six-step card each for the independent stage. If only one version is in the cupboard, print that for everyone and read the missing steps aloud. Set out tray, cards and goal sheet first. Trays are school property; never use pupils' own bags or lockers.

Starter · create the need

One tray, set up twice

Watch this tray twice. The same items go in it both times.

First the safety kit sits in its marked place. One person finds it. We count the seconds aloud.

Then I bury the kit under a pile. We count again. The items did not change. The place changed.

  • 1 · notice the item that is out of place
  • 2 · choose the one item that matters most
  • 3 · explain what goes wrong if it is not found fast
Say each line, take pointed answers, no writing. About one minute per line. Do the timing for real and count the seconds aloud, so the class hears the gap.

I Do · model

I Do · model

PLACE means one spot where an item lives and goes back to. ORDER means doing steps in the same sequence every time. SUCCESS CRITERION means a check anyone can do that gives a yes or a no.

Watch me set up this tray. I take everything off first.

Then I sort. Items I use every session stay near the front.

Each item gets one place and a label. The safety kit goes nearest the front.

Last I run the check. An adult finds the scissors in ten seconds.

One item fitted nowhere. I did not guess. I said out loud that I was not sure.

Now the safety card, the same way

Watch me do the second one. This is the approved safety challenge card.

I read every step before I touch anything. I do not start at the middle.

Step one is check the area is clear. Nothing else can happen until that is true.

I do step one, then I tick it. I tick it after I do it, never before.

If a step does not make sense, I stop and ask. Stopping is the safe move, not a mistake.

3 min
We Do · everyone commits

We Do · everyone commits

Spot the mistake on the card

You each have the same approved safety card. One step is in the wrong place. It is a mistake we put there on purpose.

Find the mistake. Put your finger on the step that is wrong.

Now find where that step should go instead. Put your other finger there.

Nobody explains yet. When everyone has found it, two people say what was wrong and what it should be.

Then I read the correct order aloud and we check the card together.

Wait until every pupil has a finger on the wrong step before taking a reason. Spotting the mistake is the work; the correction comes second.

6 min
I Do 2 · connect

I Do 2 · connect

The safety card works like the tray. The order is the safe part.

The card is the authority. We follow the steps in order. We do not add our own. Step one is always the same. Make the area safe, then get an adult. We never give advice about injuries or health.

Our project needs the same thing. A goal with no check cannot be marked. Nobody can say if it is done.

Here is one goal in the right shape. Set up the approved store cupboard in three weeks, on the Friday.

Two success criteria for it. Every shelf has a label. An adult finds the kit in ten seconds.

A criterion like "make it nicer" does not work, because two people would score it differently.

You have not agreed a goal yet. Two goals are on the board. You vote next.

Two candidate goals go on the board before the lesson. Do not present either as decided. The class votes at the start of We Do 2. Circle the winner and leave it up for the independent stage.

5 min
We Do 2 · lab

We Do 2 · lab

Vote first, then choose your row

First we vote. Two goals are on the board. Raise a hand or point to the one you want.

The goal with more hands is circled. That wording is our goal. It stays on the board.

  • ◆ Supported — set up a four-item tray so every item has one place, put the three-step card in order, copy the circled goal, and tick which of the two printed criteria can be checked.
  • ▲ Standard — set up the full tray, run the ten-second find, order the six-step card, copy the circled goal, and write two success criteria.
  • ★ Stretch — as Standard, then write beside each criterion who would check it and how, and name one thing that would make the goal fail.

Rows are choices, not labels. You can change row at any time.

Point to the tray, the card or the board behind what you say.

7 min
Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Independent record

Do the space first. Then the card. Then the goal.

Set up your space. Record what belongs in each place. Name the one item you were least sure about.

Complete the approved safety card in order. Write the step that matters most and one reason.

Copy the circled goal from the board. Write or tick your two success criteria. Beside each, write who checks it and how.

If you are not sure, write it down and mark it not sure. That is a real answer. It counts.

Work at your own pace. This is doing time, not reading time.

Use the least prompt that works. If a pupil asks you to scribe, write their exact words and keep them separate from their own writing. Protect the order: space first, then card, then goal.

Audience receives; Influence changes one next action

There is time for everyone to speak in this room. You do not have to speak. Pointing at your sheet counts as speaking.

Say one success criterion and one thing you changed in your space. The adult names both back exactly as you said them.

Confirm or correct what was named back. Then choose one real next action: keep the criterion, change its wording, or have it checked next week.

You can choose to decide next week. Write that down. That still counts.

You can point at your goal on the sheet instead of saying it. Pointing counts.

Whatever you agree today is written down and comes back next week, so nothing has to be finished now.
