My route map — Plan A and Plan B

What this lesson teaches
Two different routes can lead towards the same job. Plan A is a first choice; Plan B is a useful backup if plans change. Neither is a promise of entry or work. One next action makes the map usable. Check real requirements and available support with a trusted adult before a real decision.

Success checks
Show one job goal, two distinct routes towards it and one concrete next action. Include help and a time or review point. A sensible information check is a valid action. Preference and private circumstances are not graded.

Prepare
Gather each learner’s career story, three-options grid, apprenticeship fact card, requirement-match card and encounter record. Use anonymous work references. Note absent work; never fill gaps from a model. Cards provide invented recap examples for access and new responses only. Books, normal aids, Vocabulary Mat and Evidence Sheet ready. Confirm private help and pause/return.

Arrival and I Do
0–5: find one clue from prior work, such as training, support or a requirement to check. Staff pre-check the portfolio before the lesson to allow a brief review at the end. 5–9: teach route, backup and action. 9–13: model A, two routes towards food service work and an entry-check action. Point to the job at both ends. Check that the routes differ. The example is invented, not a learner answer.

We Do
13–19: match course route and paid apprenticeship route to the same goal. Use recap Card 4 to link earlier work: careers can change; routes differ; apprenticeships mean paid work with training; strengths and next steps; questions and honest encounter records. Improve “find out” to a specific question with help and a check time. 19–23: private hinge and parallel check; re-model if needed.

You Do
23–35: choose B stockroom or C garden, or an own generic goal. In a book, make Plan A and a different Plan B towards the same job. Add one concrete action, help and when to check it. The card order is mixed; learner selects/labels the first and backup routes. Keep actual route choices and response; a copied adult model is supported evidence, not independent thinking. Check privately for accurate earn-and-learn meaning and unknown entry facts.

Review and exit
35–38: each learner shows the map privately; ask which step they will take next. Give one NS and keep the reply. 38–40: add the map and update the pre-checked six-outcome grid from actual evidence. Record missing work or a pass as not yet seen. If more checking is needed, record follow-up; do not rush six new performances into the exit.

Supported
Offer two route cards and one instruction at a time. Keep model A, pictures, word bank and frames. Learner chooses or orders the routes and an action using voice, pointing, aid, typing or exact scribe. Log any supplied answer.

Independent
Use B or C, or a self-chosen generic job. Keep normal aids and the model. Create two distinct routes to the same goal and one clear next action. Check unknown facts with an adult.

Aspire
Explain when the backup could help or compare one feature of the routes. Keep a concrete action and usual access. No extra public talk or personal disclosure required.

Model check and fade
Model A has course then work, or paid apprenticeship then work. Both aim at food service assistant. Check: A two different routes or B the same route twice? Key A. Show the different starting steps if unclear. Keep words and model; fade only answer cues.

Hinge and parallel check
Main: a map has Plan A only. A it is complete; B it needs a different backup and a clear next action. Key B. Parallel: “I will find out” is vague. A ask an adult about entry by Friday and keep the reply; B wait with no check. Key A. Re-model a missing element, then recheck privately. Pass is unobserved.

Private help route
Use a trusted adult or the school’s help route. Point to HELP, write or ask for a private talk. If one adult is busy, seek another through the route. Pass, pause and supported return are available. No names, private grades, address or family finances requested.

Typed and scribed routes
Evidence Sheet page 2 has blank Type instead fields for either task or an own generic goal. Save through usual routine with lesson code/work reference and show privately. Book, voice, aid or exact scribe also valid. Scribe reads back and permits correction. Log normal access separately from supplied answers.

Evidence and judgement
Exact source evidence: Personal route map with Plan A/B and next action. Shown for Week 6: same goal, distinct viable-in-the-scenario routes and a concrete action. Partly: one element missing or unclear. Not yet seen: insufficient evidence, missing work or pass; comment which applies. Record own generic goal or invented practice mode. Assess the construction, not a preference, real eligibility or a prediction of future success.

Six outcome portfolio check
Evidence Sheet pages 3–4 carry all six exact G16–G21 outcomes as a tick-and-comment grid. Tick one state per outcome: Shown, Partly or Not yet seen. Add work reference/date, what it shows and access or content prompts. Review actual earlier work; source N20 F + S stays mixed and N21 S assessing. An absent encounter record stays missing/pending; staff role-play is labelled practice. New recap responses get a new date and do not prove a real visit occurred.

Misconceptions and feedback
Plan B is not failure or a lesser person’s route. It is a useful alternative. Two job names alone are not two routes to one goal. A training card is not a real offer. Unknown entry needs remain unknown. One NS targets a missing route or vague action; retain first response and reply. VF, WS, I, NS, //, ?, E and R apply. R follows Voice, Audience, NS and reply in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode.
