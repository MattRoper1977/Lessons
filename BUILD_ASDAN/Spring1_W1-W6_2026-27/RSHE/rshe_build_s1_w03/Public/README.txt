BUILD RSHE Spring 1 Week 3
Choose and try a calm step
Year A. Spring 1 Week 3, 18–22 January 2027. Wednesday 20 January 2027, P1. One 40-minute formative lesson for three to four learners. Spring 1 runs 4 January–12 February for six weeks; Week 6 is assessing. No clock time supplied.

Exact outcome: Use a calming strategy independently.
Theme: Resilience & Change.

What this lesson teaches
Teach three short, school-agreed routines: soft breath, look and count, small hand move. Learner chooses one, tries the steps with usual access after a general cue, and gives their own Did it help? feedback. Independence means no adult next-step content prompts, not removal of a card or aids. A pass/stop/switch is respected and recorded honestly.

Prepare
Books, three routine cards, the single-page keep card in Support Cards page 4, vocabulary and staff evidence record. Adult-operated familiar quiet visual timer with no alarm, set only after choice/setup. Confirm safe usual positions, existing sensory/physical adaptations and private help. No distress induction or personal trigger questions. No new breath-rate/count protocol.

Source and calendar
Wednesday 20 January 2027 P1, Year A. Week range 18–22 January; six-week Spring 1, Week 6 assessing. Owner supplies timetable. C66 exact; identifying source resource labels generic. This is low-pressure practice, not treatment or a health assessment.

Where this leads
This helps me choose a safe routine, use agreed aids and say whether a step helped during learning or future work.

Chunk 1
0–3 calm starter: story A and choice of a card; no current-feeling question. 3–7 pre-teach card/help/stop, three choices, pass/stop/switch and own feedback. 7–11 teach soft breath one short step at a time with the IN/OUT image, exact written steps and visible model. Soft breath at comfortable personal pace; no hold or phase counts. Privately check IN/OUT; supported rehearsal is teaching, not independent evidence. 11–12 reset.

Chunk 2
12–18 teach look-and-count 3 see/2 safe contact points/1 sound, eyes open, and the small seated hand movement. Use normal environment and usual access; no touching another person, forced sensation or movement demand. 18–21 main hinge B: card/usual aids can stay without adult next-step cues; false A removes all aids. Parallel A: No/Not sure is valid own feedback, not failure. 21–24 choose/plan one routine with Level 2 bank/starter/model. 24–25 reset.

Chunk 3
25–34 private choice/setup, one quiet practice of up to 30 seconds and brief own check. General cue: choose a card and try its steps if you wish. Adult runs timer without giving the next content step. Observe actual chosen practice, access and prompts; no copied model or scribe can replace it. Stop earlier/pass/switch respected; missing practice stays unobserved. 34–37 one next step and response. 37–40 private exit and own Did it help? choice; keep the card in book/usual accessible place.

Level 2 on every task
All seven tasks keep a short bank, starter and visible worked model: Starter, Model check, Guided count and move, Private hinge, Choose and plan, Timed practice and check, Feedback and exit. They appear on Support Cards pages 5–6. Independent work retains aids; the learner supplies the routine actions rather than adult next-step instructions.

Supported
Teach one short step at a time first. Keep word bank, sentence starter, visible model and routine card. Offer two routine choices if needed. Usual aid or exact scribe records choice/check; actual practice remains the learner’s. Record all adult step prompts faithfully.

Independent
Keep the chosen routine card, bank, starter and visible model. After the general task cue, learner chooses, starts and uses the steps without adult step instructions. Usual sensory, physical, reading, timer or communication access remains. Record actual practice and their own help tick.

Aspire
Keep all Level 2 aids. Use one chosen routine, then explain whether it helped or what to try next. More writing or trying all three is not required. Independence means no adult next-step cues, not removal of normal aids.

Worked example and fade
Use invented A: chosen soft breath, IN then OUT at own pace, no hold. Keep step card and model visible. Teach/rehearse with adult prompts first if needed, recording that as teaching/support. For the later observed try give a general task cue and fade content prompts only; do not remove routine card, reading/sensory/physical aids or quiet timer access.

Scribed route
Exact scribe can record a selected card, own helpfulness feedback or next step, read back and permit correction. It cannot perform the routine or turn a spoken/drawn explanation into evidence of actual use. Record observed action and any gap honestly; no health or breath-depth judgement.

Prompt ladder and TA focus
1 Point to place/instruction. 2 Ask a short question privately. 3 Rephrase. 4 Offer scaffold/choice. 5 Model first step only, then return task. Allow processing. These content prompts support learning, but actual next-step prompts during practice must be logged and prevent an unsupported independent-use claim. Normal access remains separate.

Book first
Routine cards are reference material. The dedicated page-4 keep card has a blank choice and four unticked Did it help? choices for the learner’s own mark/selection; keep in book/usual accessible place. This tick card is intentional. Other responses use book/usual aid. Plan in any language.

Check after the model
IN means a soft breath in; OUT a soft breath out. Own slow comfortable pace, no hold or timed breath phases. No forced deep breath. Learner can stop/change/seek help. Use bank/start/model and private pointing or aid.

Main and parallel hinge
Main B: the chosen card and usual aids may stay; adult does not give each next content step. False A says all aids must be removed. Parallel A: No or Not sure to Did it help? is valid feedback; false B treats it as failure. Re-model/recheck privately; a pass remains unobserved. A useful try need not produce Yes.

Planned adaptations
Cognition and Learning: four short routine steps, visible cue, one chosen option and reduced repetition. Communication and Interaction: wait time, familiar aid, pointing/selected feedback, exact scribe/readback. SEMH: calm stories, private choice, pass/stop/switch and supported return. Sensory and Physical: usual safe position and existing aids/adaptations; avoid uncomfortable sensation/movement, choose another card if needed. EAL: plan in any language. Colour is not the sole cue.

Private help route
Point to HELP, write or ask to talk in private with a trusted adult through the school’s help route. If one adult is busy, use the route to another. Pass, pause and supported return available.

Staff response
If a pupil discloses, listen calmly, reassure and pass the concern to the safeguarding lead through the school’s procedure. Do not probe or promise secrecy. Keep details out of this pack and shared evidence.

Misconceptions
Independent means no card: correction keep normal aids, record next-step prompts. The timer sets breath rate: correction timer bounds an optional try only. Calming must work or get Yes: correction all four feedback options valid. An explanation proves actual use: correction observe chosen practice or record unobserved. Do not induce a big feeling or grade calm appearance.

What to keep
Actual chosen routine, general cue, start/steps seen, duration/stop, usual access, adult content prompts and one of the three observation states. Keep learner’s own Did it help? response on the dedicated card, plus first try, NS/reply and follow-up. A scribe can preserve feedback but cannot substitute for action.

Judgement
After teaching and a general task cue, observe the learner choose/start/use their selected routine with their card, visible model and usual aids, without adult step-by-step content prompts. Record what was actually seen and any prompts. A scribe may record a choice or reflection but cannot substitute for the learner using a routine. Choosing to stop or seek help is respected; do not invent completed practice or claim independence when adult step cues were needed.

Feedback and marking
One next step might use the chosen card’s first step or ask for an agreed access change. Keep first try and reply separate. VF spoken feedback; WS with support; I own use without adult next-step cues; NS next step; // self-edit; ? read back; E actual saved evidence. R only after Voice, Audience, NS and response in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode.

Evidence record
Evidence Sheet page 1 is pupil guidance; page 2 private staff record with fourteen blank entry rows and exact disclosure note. Dedicated keep card is Support page 4, with choice/feedback initially blank. Adult may faithfully mark a choice or feedback given by learner, never fill it from an assumption. Offer to Communication portfolio; staff confirm mapping.

Continuity
Week 3 formative; Week 6 assessing. Builds on naming feelings/help by teaching and observing a chosen routine, without requiring distress. Existing school-agreed routines and access only; no treatment or guaranteed calming claim.

Three chunks of 12, 13 and 15 minutes. Resets 11–12 and 24–25 are already inside the 40 minutes. All seven task stages retain a word bank, sentence starter and visible model. Supported / Independent / Aspire all retain Level 2 aids. Book first; a dedicated choice card is kept in the book or usual accessible place. Plan in any language. No public questions or personal triggers.

Three authored school-agreed routine choices: soft breath with IN/OUT own-pace cue and no holds; look and count, noticing three seen things, two safe points of touch and one sound with usual access or alternative; small hand move, softly opening/closing then resting with no force or performance target. Teach and check access before practice. Calm invented scenes are context only; never induce distress or require a big feeling.

One quiet practice of up to 30 seconds, after the learner has chosen and set up. Stop earlier if needed; no race or requirement to fill the time. Adult operates a familiar quiet visual timer with no sudden alarm. Timer operation is normal access, not a prompt telling the next strategy step. Do not count breath phases with the timer.
Low-pressure practice only, with calm invented third-person scenes. Do not induce a big feeling, ask for a personal trigger, require distress to start, or make calming down a condition of success. Learner may pass, stop, switch or seek private help. A safe try can be recorded even if it did not feel helpful. Pupil feedback remains their own, not filled by an adult.

Pupil feedback
Did it help? Yes; A bit; No; Not sure. All four reports are valid and belong to the learner. A No or Not sure report does not automatically mean unsuccessful independent use. No guarantee of calming is claimed. Keep own feedback separate from the adult observation.
