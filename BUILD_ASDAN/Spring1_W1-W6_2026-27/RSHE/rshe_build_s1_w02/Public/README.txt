Big feelings and helpful steps
Year A. Spring 1 Week 2, 11–15 January 2027. Wednesday 13 January 2027, P1. One 40-minute formative lesson for three to four learners. Spring 1 runs 4 January–12 February for six weeks; Week 6 is assessing. No clock time supplied.

Exact outcome: Name a big feeling and a way to manage it.
Theme: Resilience & Change.

C65 outcome retained verbatim. Inherited named source programmes, resources and accredited units in A64/D64/E64/F64 replaced with generic feelings scenarios, familiar school-agreed help and evidence-record wording. Four original unnamed fictional cases and original four-picture bank. Word banks, starters and visible models retained on all seven tasks. No medical, diagnostic, therapeutic or accredited-outcome claim. No personal account required; first-person sentence frame is practice using fictional or picked words.

What this lesson teaches
Name cross, sad, worried or excited in calm invented scenes. Use a stated feeling rather than diagnosing a face/body clue. Choose a safe, already familiar school help step. Match by possibility, not one fixed pair. Practise “When I feel… I can…” with a fictional scene or picked words, not a personal disclosure.

Prepare
Books, pencils, Support Cards and Vocabulary Mat; usual aids and staff evidence record. Face cards are reference pictures. Body clues, help steps and scenes are editable text. Confirm the school’s familiar breath, movement and supported quiet-space options, available trusted adult and help route. No new technique, compulsory practice or public response.

Where this leads
This helps me name a feeling in a story and choose safe help when a task or plan feels hard in learning or future work.

Chunk 1
0–3 calm arrival and starter: use story A words, choosing cross or sad privately. 3–7 pre-teach the four feeling words and four familiar school help steps. A picture/body clue may fit many feelings; story words state the feeling. 7–11 model A: cross, tight hands, tell a trusted adult; show the written line. Privately check feeling and help. 11–12 reset with familiar neutral option.

Chunk 2
12–18 guide B/C/D one short line at a time. Match a stated feeling and possible safe help; accept more than one safe match. 18–21 main private hinge B: a face/body clue may fit but does not prove a feeling; use the story words. Parallel A: choose a safe help step; do not claim the feeling must go away now. If unclear, point to the quote, re-model and check privately. 21–24 choose a familiar help step without requiring practice. 24–25 reset.

Chunk 3
25–34 each chooses one invented scene and names the feeling plus a safe help step. Use “When I feel… I can…” for the story/picked words, not personal history. Drawing a story face or help step with labels, pointing or exact scribe is valid. 34–37 one next step and response. 37–40 private exit names a feeling/help pair. A pass is unobserved. All seven task scaffolds remain visible on Support Cards.

Level 2 on every task
Each of seven tasks has a short bank, starter and visible model in Support Cards. These stay on all routes: Starter, Model check, Guided match, Private hinge, Help choice, Individual line or drawing, Feedback and exit. Repeat exact model wording as a reference, never as prefilled learner evidence.

Supported
One short story line at a time. Keep word bank, sentence starter and visible model. Offer two feeling/help choices, pointing, aid, drawing or faithful scribe. Same name-a-feeling-and-help goal. Record any answer cue.

Independent
Use a chosen story, word bank, starter and visible model. Name the stated feeling and choose a safe familiar strategy. Normal reading, communication or scribing access remains. No personal story required.

Aspire
Keep all Level 2 aids. Explain a safe choice or show that more than one strategy might fit. Use the same short practice line; more writing or personal disclosure is not required.

Worked example and fade
Read A’s quote “I feel cross.” Point to cross. Tight hands are a stated possible clue, not proof or a diagnosis. Model “When I feel cross I can tell someone.” For B/C/D offer a bank, short frame and A model; reduce answer cues only. Normal reading, drawing, communication and scribing aids stay available.

Scribed route
Let the learner point to a story feeling/help word, use an aid, draw or speak. Record exact selection, read back quietly and permit correction. Label a drawing through the learner’s choice; do not grade art or infer feeling from a face. Record access separately from supplied thinking.

Prompt ladder and TA focus
1 Point to place/instruction. 2 Ask a short question privately. 3 Rephrase. 4 Offer scaffold/choice. 5 Model first step only, then return task. Allow processing. Input: point to the stated word; guided match: accept safe options; individual work: keep all Level 2 aids and log actual help.

Book first
Pupil pages are reference cards, not write-on sheets. Use a book, usual aid, drawing or faithful scribe. Do not copy all source text. Plan in any language. No task asks for personal/current feelings or a public account.

Check after the model
Offer A feeling choices cross/sad, and safe help choices tell someone/breathe. Key feeling cross from the quote. Either familiar safe help can fit; model tell someone. Ask privately or use pointing. Re-model the quote and line if needed.

Main and parallel hinge
Main key B: a face/body clue may fit a feeling; it does not prove it. Story words state cross. A false option says the face proves the feeling. Parallel key A: choose a safe help step. The false option says the feeling must go away now. Leave bank/starter/model visible. Pass/silence unobserved; re-model and recheck privately.

Planned adaptations
Cognition and Learning: one line, labelled images, four simple words and short choices. Communication and Interaction: literal text, wait time, familiar aid, pointing/drawing or exact scribe. SEMH: calm third-person scenes, private turns, predictable resets/pass/return. Sensory and Physical: uncluttered print, comfortable seat, normal aid and agreed supported space/movement. EAL: plan in any language. Numbers and words carry meaning; colour optional.

Private help route
Point to HELP, write or ask to talk in private with a trusted adult through the school’s help route. If one adult is busy, use the route to another. Pass, pause and supported return available.

Staff response
If a pupil discloses, listen calmly, reassure and pass the concern to the safeguarding lead through the school’s procedure. Do not probe or promise secrecy. Keep details out of this pack and shared evidence.

Misconceptions
A face proves the feeling: correct with the story quote and body-clue rule. One feeling has one right help step: offer more than one safe possibility. A step must make the feeling go away: it may help, with support/time. A big feeling is wrong: acknowledge it; judge naming and safe help, not the feeling itself.

What to keep
One dated individual story response or drawing/selection with the chosen feeling and safe familiar help step. Keep story reference, first response, usual access, supplied thinking, next step and reply. A faithful point/aid/scribe record is valid. Do not infer a real-life coping performance from a story line.

Judgement
Secure: names a stated big feeling and a safe familiar way to manage it. Partial: names feeling or help but not both. Not yet: needs further modelling. Unobserved: pass or no collected response. Alternative safe matches accepted with a short explanation/selection. Drawing skill, disclosure and practising a technique are not conditions.

Feedback and marking
One next step: name the story feeling or add a safe help step. Keep first try and reply separate. VF verbal feedback; WS with support; I own thinking; NS next step; // self-edit; ? read back; E actual saved evidence. R only after Voice, Audience, NS and response in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode.

Evidence record
Evidence Sheet page 1 is a book-task reference; page 2 private staff record with blank entries. No prefilled answers or personal concern details. Read exact scribing back. Offer to Communication portfolio; staff confirm any accredited mapping.

Continuity
Spring 1 Week 2 is formative; Week 6 assessing. Builds on recognising that change can feel hard by naming a big feeling and safe familiar help. No clinical advice, treatment claim, promised emotional outcome or requirement to demonstrate coping.
