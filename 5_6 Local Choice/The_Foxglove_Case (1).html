Internal Memo — DCI M. Blackburn

"Detective. You're up."

Lady Henrietta Foxglove was found dead in her library yesterday morning. Poisoned. Her own garden grew the stuff that killed her. There are four suspects in the house, and every one of them had a reason.

I've given you a team. Use them. You've got until sundown to file a theory — who did it, how, and why — and a rival team is on the same case. Only one of you gets the win.

The Challenge & Your Bureau Roles

The Case — Round 1 Rules

  • You'll see 4 suspects and 6 pieces of evidence. Flip each card to read.
  • 20 minutes to study the file, rule suspects in or out, and file your theory.
  • Submit your accusation on the sheet: who did it, why, and 3 pieces of evidence that prove it.
  • Closest to the truth with the strongest reasoning wins the round.
  • You stay at your table. This is thinking work, not running work.

Session 2 · 40 minutes

Back at the Bureau

Debrief — Walk Me Through Your Thinking

3 minutes in your teams. Then 2 minutes whole-class share. You're not defending — you're learning. Where did your reasoning hold up? Where did it wobble?

Question 1

What was your strongest piece of evidence?

Name the specific card. Why did it point so clearly for you?

Question 2

Who did you almost accuse — and why didn't you?

The suspect you ruled out at the last minute. What changed your mind?

Question 3

What's your weakest claim?

Be honest. Which bit of your theory would the rival team attack first?

Push it further: A real detective never "knows" — they just have the best theory the evidence supports. Is that different from being right? What's the risk of being too sure?

New Rule

Silent Deliberation

From now on — no talking.

Write on paper. Pass notes. Tap the evidence card you think matters. Point. Nod. Write. But don't speak. This is how real case rooms work.

5 minutes of silent deliberation

  • Reconsider your Round 1 accusation. Does the new evidence still support it?
  • If you need to change your mind — say so on the sheet. Changing your mind is a strength, not a weakness.
  • Agree your final accusation in writing only.

The Reveal

"Detective. You want to know what really happened? Press the button."

Case Closed

It was Mrs. Holt.

Motive. 25 years of service. Given notice with no pension. Losing everything — home, wages, the only family she had.

Means. She knew every inch of the garden. She knew foxglove mimics heart failure in an older woman.

Opportunity. She carried the tea alone at 10:40pm. The kitchen boy saw her compose herself minutes before.

James had motive but no opportunity. Dr. Vance had means but a confirmed alibi. Clara stole rings — not lives. Mrs. Holt had all three.
