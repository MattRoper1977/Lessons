I do · 📍 5 min

Three icons of the Tees.

You probably know at least one of these. Tap each card for a did-you-know.

Upper
💧

High Force

A 21-metre waterfall in Upper Teesdale. One of the biggest single drops in England. You can hear it before you see it.

👆 Tap for did-you-know
Did you know? High Force sits on the Whin Sill — the same rock Hadrian's Wall is built on. Same geology, opposite ends of the country.
Middle
🚣

Tees Barrage

Stockton, 1995. A barrier that controls the tides. Kayaking, paddleboarding, white-water rafting — built where there used to be mud and smell.

👆 Tap for did-you-know
Did you know? Before the Barrage, the Tees through Stockton was tidal mud twice a day. Now there's a white-water course where Olympic kayakers train.
Mouth
🌉

Transporter Bridge

Middlesbrough's icon since 1911. Carries cars across the Tees in a hanging gondola. Visible from miles.

👆 Tap for did-you-know
Did you know? It's appeared in Auf Wiedersehen, Pet, Billy Elliot, and dozens of films. You can climb to the top — 49 metres up.

Key facts

Source: Cross Fell, in the Pennines (893m above sea level).
Length: About 85 miles (137 km).
Mouth: The North Sea, near Redcar.
Major towns: Barnard Castle · Darlington · Stockton · Middlesbrough · Redcar.
Famous landmarks: High Force (waterfall) · Tees Barrage (Stockton) · Transporter Bridge (Middlesbrough).
Trip (10 July): A stretch of the River Tees — exact location confirmed in lesson 1b.

Key terms

SourceWhere a river starts — usually high up.
TributaryA smaller stream feeding into a bigger river.
MeanderA bendy, curvy part of a river.
MouthWhere a river ends — usually into the sea.
EstuaryThe wide, tidal section near the mouth.
BarrageA barrier that controls the river / tides.

Memory hooks

Source: "Cross Fell — Cross your fingers it doesn't rain." 🏔️

Length: "85 miles — about an hour and a half by car." 🚗

Mouth: "Redcar — Red sky, sea ahead." 🌊

Order downstream: "Cross High Bridges, Darlings, Stop, Make Room for the sea." (Cross Fell · High Force · Barnard · Darlington · Stockton · Middlesbrough · Redcar)

Common mark-losers

  • Saying the Tees starts at Stockton (no — that's a town it passes).
  • Confusing source (start) with mouth (end).
  • Confusing tributary (joins in) with meander (bends).
  • Saying the Tees is in the south of England — it's North-East.
