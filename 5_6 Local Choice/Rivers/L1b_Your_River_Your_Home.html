The Tees through time — tap each era.

200 years of change. Eras opened: 0 / 3

1825 – 1960s
⚙️ 🚂 🚢
The working river

Iron, coal, steel, ships, smoke. The Tees built the world.

👆 Tap to read

1825: Stockton-Darlington Railway — world's first public passenger railway — built to carry coal to the Tees.

1850s: Ironstone found at Eston Hills (yes — where you walked last term). Middlesbrough exploded from a village to a city in 30 years. Nickname: Ironopolis.

1900s: Ships built at Smith's Dock in South Bank. Steel made at Redcar. Chemicals at Billingham (ICI).

The Tees was the engine of the British Empire. Also: filthy.

1960s – 2000s
📉 🏭 💨
Decline & "the Tees stink"

Factories closing. Polluted water. A river you couldn't fish in.

👆 Tap to read

1980s: Shipyards shut. Smith's Dock closed 1986. Thousands lost their jobs.

1990s: The Tees through Stockton was tidal mud, twice a day. It smelled. Locals called it "the Tees stink."

What you lost: ironworks, shipyards, dignity, a generation of jobs.

What you kept: the river. And the people. And the stories.

2000s – Today
🚣 🌳 ⚡
Cleaner river, new jobs

Tees Barrage, white-water, festivals, Freeport, energy hub.

👆 Tap to read

1995: Tees Barrage opens at Stockton. No more tidal mud. Salmon come back. White-water course opens — Olympic kayakers train here.

2010s: Big regeneration plans — Tees Valley Combined Authority, Tees Freeport, offshore wind hub at Teesworks (the old steelworks site).

The Tees today: not the iron giant it was, but cleaner, busier with leisure, and at the centre of the country's energy plans.

The Tees through time

1825 – 1960s · The working riverStockton-Darlington Railway (1825, world's first passenger railway). Ironstone from Eston Hills (1850s) fed Middlesbrough's furnaces — earning the nickname Ironopolis. Ships built at Smith's Dock. Chemicals at Billingham. The Tees built modern Britain.
1960s – 2000s · DeclineYards closed (Smith's Dock 1986). Steel and chemical jobs lost. Tidal mud + pollution earned the Tees its grim "Tees stink" nickname. Identity blow.
2000s – today · RegenerationTees Barrage opens 1995 — stops the tidal mud, brings salmon back, opens a white-water course. Tees Valley Combined Authority, Tees Freeport, offshore wind at Teesworks.

Place → Industry (Tees pairs)

Eston HillsIronstone — mined from the 1850s.
MiddlesbroughIron + steel — "Ironopolis".
Smith's Dock (South Bank)Shipbuilding — closed 1986.
Wilton / BillinghamChemicals (ICI) — fertilisers, plastics.
Tees Barrage (Stockton)White-water leisure — opened 1995.
Teesworks (Redcar)Offshore wind hub — replacing closed steel.

The Walk 1 → Tees connection

Last term you walked past Eston Mine on the way to Eston Hills. That mine produced ironstone. That ironstone was carried down to Middlesbrough on the Tees, smelted into iron in furnaces along the river, and shipped to the world. Your walk last term and your trip this term are 2 miles apart and 175 years apart, but they are the same story.

Memory hooks

1825: "Stockton-Darlington — first train, first time, eighteen-twenty-five." 🚂

Eston Hills → Middlesbrough: "Hill → ironstone → train → furnace → city." 🏔️➡️🏭

Tees stink → Tees Barrage: "1995 — the year the river got its dignity back." 🌊

Common mark-losers

  • Calling Middlesbrough "Steel City" — that is Sheffield. Middlesbrough is "Ironopolis".
  • Saying Eston had coal — it had ironstone. Coal was further west.
  • Saying the Tees Barrage closed the river — it actually made it cleaner.
  • Mixing up the eras — 1825 is railways, 1850s is iron, 1995 is the Barrage.
