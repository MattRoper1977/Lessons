Our stretch of the Tees — three things to know.

Portrack Marsh is where we're going. Tap each card for the story behind it.

History
✂️

The Big Cut

In 1830 people straightened the Tees here to speed up ships. Over 90% of the marshland was drained. Portrack is the last wetland on the lower Tees.

👆 Tap for did-you-know
Did you know? "Port Rack" comes from "racking" — horses dragging ships up the old river loop. The place is named after human muscle.
Today
🏭

The Works Next Door

A large sewage treatment works sits right beside the reserve. Cleaned water flows back to the river — a real source of the nitrate & phosphate we'll test for.

👆 Tap for did-you-know
Did you know? After heavy rain, more pollution gets washed in. That's often when a river's problems actually show up in the readings.
The tide
🌊

Why High Tide?

The Tees Barrage is 300m upstream and holds the river fresh above it. So Portrack is the last tidal stretch — at high tide the sea pushes brackish (salty) water up to the barrage.

👆 Tap for did-you-know
Did you know? Seals swim up on the tide — you might spot one. That's why our river session is timed for high water: more to see, and the tidal river at its fullest.

The water-testing toolkit

Temperature (physical)Warm water holds less oxygen — bad for fish.
Turbidity (physical)How cloudy the water is. Cloudy blocks light & clogs gills.
pH (chemical)Acidic or alkaline. Most river life needs 6.5–8.0.
Dissolved oxygen (chemical)The gas fish & bugs breathe. Low = river in trouble.
Nitrate / phosphate (chemical)Nutrients from sewage & fertiliser. Too much feeds algae.
Invertebrates (biological)Indicator species. Mayfly = clean; worms only = polluted.

Key facts — our stretch

Where: Portrack Marsh, Stockton — the last wetland on the lower Tees, and an SSSI.
Tidal limit: the Tees Barrage (300m upstream) holds the river fresh above it; Portrack is the last tidal, brackish stretch.
Human impact: straightened in 1830 (>90% of marsh lost); a sewage works sits right next door.
Why high tide: the sea pushes brackish water up to the barrage — the tidal river at its fullest (and seals may come up).
Trip: Thu 9 July, 10:00 — Canal & River Trust car park (NOT the Talpore). Finish 2:00–2:30.

Key terms

Indicator speciesA creature whose presence reveals the water's health.
TurbidityHow cloudy or clear the water is.
BrackishA mix of fresh and salt water.
Tidal limitThe furthest point the tide reaches upstream.
Nutrient pollutionToo much nitrate/phosphate feeding algae.
SSSISite of Special Scientific Interest — legally protected.

Memory hooks

pH range: "Six-and-a-half to eight — life feels great." 🧪

Oxygen & heat: "Warm water, less air — fish gasp everywhere." 🫧

Bugs: "Mayfly means clean; worms alone mean mean." 🦐

High tide: "Barrage above, brackish below — that's why at high tide we go." 🌊

Common mark-losers

  • Saying clear water is definitely clean (pollution can be invisible).
  • Mixing up high nutrients (bad) with high oxygen (good).
  • Thinking one test is enough — you need all three types.
  • Forgetting the tide: Portrack is the last tidal stretch, below the barrage.
  • Meeting at the Talpore car park — it's the Canal & River Trust one.
