Scheme of work — at a glance

This is the first block of the Year 4 autumn term. Five lessons take pupils from grouping animals by their features, through vertebrates and invertebrates, to using and making their own classification keys for both animals and plants — building a clear answer to the enquiry “how can living things be grouped and classified?” Every lesson runs the same structure — Arrival → Starter → I do → We do → Independent → Exit — in about 44 minutes, differentiated across three primary tiers.

National Curriculum coverage

  • Recognise that living things can be grouped in a variety of ways.
  • Explore and use classification keys to help group, identify and name a variety of living things in their local and wider environment.

Working scientifically

  • Identifying, classifying and grouping living things by their features (throughout).
  • Using and constructing simple classification keys of closed yes/no questions (L3, L5).
  • Gathering, recording and presenting findings, and answering the enquiry (L2–L5).

Assessment

  • A tiered exit ticket every lesson (Seedling / Sapling / Oak).
  • Interactive independent sorter self-checks understanding in the moment.
  • The block builds to pupils’ own classification keys answering the enquiry.

Prior learning & next steps

Builds on (Y2): animals — mammals, birds, fish, amphibians and reptiles, and their needs. Working scientifically: identifying, grouping and classifying, and using and making keys.

Leads to (Y6): classifying living things into broad groups, micro-organisms, and Carl Linnaeus.

How each lesson is built

Every lesson is a single interactive web page: six-phase structure with on-screen timings; three tiers on the four pupil-facing tasks; a short teaching video; a self-contained animation targeting the lesson’s key misconception; TA Focus and Cold Call prompts; and a one-click print pack.

Differentiation tiers map to attainment bands: 🌱 Seedling = working towards · 🌿 Sapling = expected · 🌳 Oak = greater depth.
