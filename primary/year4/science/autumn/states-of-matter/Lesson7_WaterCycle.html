Starter 5 min

Water's journey

The Sun heats water, which evaporates into vapour. High up it cools and condenses into clouds. It falls as precipitation (rain, snow, sleet, hail) and collects again. The same water is recycled — no new water is made.

Play the animation. Bust: the Sun soaks up water / clouds are a gas.

Round and round

The Sun evaporates water, it condenses into a cloud, then falls as rain — the same water, recycled.

Model the ideaThe Sun doesn't soak up water — its heat makes water evaporate into vapour. Clouds aren't a gas: they are billions of tiny liquid droplets. No new water is ever made; it just moves around.

Watch for: "the Sun absorbs the water" and "clouds are a gas".

TA Focus · The water cycle
Stages: evaporation (liquid→gas), condensation (gas→cloud), precipitation (rain/snow/sleet/hail), collection. For the sorter, ask 'is the water rising, forming a cloud, or falling?' Bust two myths: the Sun 'absorbs' water (it evaporates it) and clouds are a gas (they're tiny liquid droplets).
