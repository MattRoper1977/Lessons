I do · 2 4 min

Play the gravity-only model: the balls start together with no air resistance. It does not show every real fall through air.

Free fall: no air resistance

A gravity-only model: released together from rest at the same height, the balls land together because air resistance is absent.

Model the ruleWith no air resistance, objects have the same acceleration regardless of mass. Released together from rest at the same height, they land together. In air, drag changes their motion: equal size alone does not guarantee an equal fall.

Watch for: "heavier things always fall faster" and "there is no gravity on the Moon" (there is — it's weaker).

TA Focus · Gravity
Two ideas to keep separate: mass (matter, grams) and weight (gravity's pull, Newtons). The big myth is 'heavier always falls faster' — point to the gravity-only model. The balls are released together from rest at the same height with no air resistance. Equal size alone does not guarantee an equal fall through air. Also gently correct 'no gravity on the Moon' — it's weaker, not absent.
