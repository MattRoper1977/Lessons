Scheme of work — at a glance

This White Rose sustainability block is spread across the whole of Autumn 1.2. Seven lessons take pupils from “what is renewable energy?” through solar and wind power to a culminating plan-and-present proposal for their own school. Each lesson runs the same structure — Arrival → Starter → I do → We do → Independent → Exit — in about 44 minutes, differentiated across three primary tiers.

Curriculum coverage

  • A White Rose sustainability block: it develops working scientifically and links to the Year 6 electricity unit and Year 5 work on Earth, space and global warming.
  • Children distinguish renewable and non-renewable sources, and explain how burning fossil fuels drives global warming and climate change.

Working scientifically

  • Identifying scientific evidence used to support or refute ideas or arguments (especially L5, L7).
  • Reporting and presenting findings from research, including causal relationships (L6, L7).
  • Using evidence to build and present a reasoned argument — the school proposal (L7).

Vocabulary across the block

Assessment

  • A tiered exit ticket every lesson (Seedling / Sapling / Oak).
  • Interactive independent sorter self-checks understanding in the moment.
  • The block ends with a designed, evidence-based proposal for the school.

Prior learning & next steps

Builds on (Y5): Earth and space, and global warming / the greenhouse effect. Cross-curricular: the Year 6 electricity unit — circuits and generating electricity.

Leads to (KS3): energy resources, generating electricity, and climate science.

How each lesson is built

Every lesson is a single interactive web page: six-phase structure with on-screen timings; three tiers on the four pupil-facing tasks; a short teaching video; a self-contained animation targeting the lesson’s key misconception; TA Focus and Cold Call prompts; and a one-click print pack.

Differentiation tiers map to attainment bands: 🌱 Seedling = working towards · 🌿 Sapling = expected · 🌳 Oak = greater depth.

1

What is renewable energy?

LO: Understand the difference between renewable and non-renewable energy sources, and know that fossil fuels (coal, oil and gas) are non-renewable because they run out.

🌱 SeedlingSorts a renewable source from a non-renewable one with support.
🌿 SaplingNames renewable and non-renewable sources and the three fossil fuels.
🌳 OakExplains why fossil fuels are non-renewable and can’t be replaced.

Lesson structure (≈44 min)

PhaseMinWhat happens
Arrival4New topic — recap Y5 global warming and Y6 electricity.
Starter5“Will it run out?” — sort sources into runs-out / keeps-going.
I do 16Renewable vs non-renewable; fossil fuels run out (video).
We do 16Sort sources renewable / non-renewable together.
I do 24One keeps going, one runs out (animation: sun vs shrinking coal).
We do 25True/false on the two myths.
Independent10Energy source sorter (renewable / non-renewable / myth) + tiered write-up.
Exit4Three tiered questions.
Misconceptions to address“Fossil fuels will last forever” and “renewables have no downsides at all” — fossil fuels run out; renewables can be weather-dependent.
Resources: video Renewable energy for kids; sun-vs-coal animation; Energy Source Sorter; TA Focus; Cold Call; print pack.
