Scheme of work — at a glance

Seven lessons take pupils from "what counts as alive?" to Linnaeus's classification system, threading the block's enquiry question throughout. Each lesson runs the same structure — Arrival → Starter → I do → We do → Independent → Exit — in about 44 minutes, differentiated across three primary tiers.

National Curriculum coverage

  • Describe how living things are classified into broad groups according to common observable characteristics and based on similarities and differences, including microorganisms, plants and animals.
  • Give reasons for classifying plants and animals based on specific characteristics.

Working scientifically

  • Identifying scientific evidence used to support or refute ideas (L1).
  • Using and developing keys and other records to identify, classify and describe living things (L2–L6).
  • Reporting and presenting findings, including conclusions and explanations (L6–L7).

Assessment

  • A tiered exit ticket every lesson (Seedling / Sapling / Oak).
  • Interactive independent task self-checks understanding in the moment.
  • End-of-block assessment covers living/non-living, grouping, keys and microorganisms.

Prior learning & next steps

Builds on (Y5): life cycles and reproduction of plants and animals. Earlier (Y4): grouping and simple classification keys, habitats.

Leads to (KS3): cells, the five kingdoms, variation and adaptation.

How each lesson is built

Every lesson is a single interactive web page: six-phase structure with on-screen timings; three tiers on the four pupil-facing tasks; a short teaching video; a self-contained animation targeting the lesson's key misconception; TA Focus and Cold Call prompts; and a one-click print pack.

Differentiation tiers map to attainment bands: 🌱 Seedling = working towards · 🌿 Sapling = expected · 🌳 Oak = greater depth.

1

Conditions for life

LO: Understand what makes something living or non-living, and what living things need to survive.

🌱 SeedlingSorts living / non-living with picture and word-bank support.
🌿 SaplingGives evidence for "alive" using a life process (MRS GREN).
🌳 OakJustifies the tricky cases (car, flame, virus) with reasoning.

Lesson structure (≈44 min)

PhaseMinWhat happens
Arrival4Year 5 recap — life cycles & reproduction; bridge to "reproduction is a sign of life".
Starter5"Is it alive?" thumbs task; surface the car / flame / leaf misconceptions.
I do 16Define organism; introduce MRS GREN (video).
We do 16Prove a dog and an oak are alive; "…because it can…" stems.
I do 24Tricky cases + what animals vs plants need (animation: germinating plant bends to light).
We do 25Sort living / non-living / tricky with evidence.
Independent10Alive Sorter + write three pieces of evidence for one organism.
Exit4Three tiered questions; preview the block enquiry question.
Misconceptions to address"Moves = alive" (car, flame); "plants don't move"; a fallen leaf is no longer living.
Resources: video Life Processes — MRS GREN; germinating-plant animation; Alive Sorter; TA Focus; Cold Call; print pack.

Group organisms

LO: Group animals and plants by observable features — vertebrates and invertebrates, the five vertebrate groups, flowering and non-flowering plants.

🌱 SeedlingSorts animals into vertebrate / invertebrate.
🌿 SaplingNames a group and gives a feature as evidence.
🌳 OakSeparates vertebrate groups by feature; explains why a whale isn't a fish.

Lesson structure (≈44 min)

PhaseMinWhat happens
Arrival4Recap L1 (is it alive? / MRS GREN); bridge to grouping.
Starter5"How would you group these animals?" — introduces the enquiry question.
I do 16Vertebrate vs invertebrate + the five vertebrate groups (video).
We do 16Sort animals: backbone or not, then which group and clue.
I do 24Flowering vs non-flowering plants (animation: whale surfaces to breathe).
We do 25Sort plants flowering / non-flowering; quick invertebrate check.
Independent10Grouping Sorter (Vertebrate / Invertebrate / Plant) + tiered write-up.
Exit4Tiered questions; preview classification keys.
Misconceptions to address"All sea animals are fish" (whale = mammal); "all plants have flowers"; the backbone decides — not size or shell.
Resources: video Vertebrates & Invertebrates; whale animation; Grouping Sorter; TA Focus; Cold Call; print pack.

Classify animals

LO: Use and create classification keys with closed yes/no questions to identify animals.

🌿 SaplingUses a key correctly; writes a two-question key.

Lesson structure (≈44 min)

PhaseMinWhat happens
Arrival4Recap L2 (vertebrate/invertebrate, five groups).
Starter5"Twenty questions" — guess the animal using only yes/no questions.
I do 16What a classification key is; closed & observable questions (video).
We do 16Follow a key together to identify animals.
I do 24Good vs poor questions (animation: a branching key builds).

Independent10"Good question" sorter + build your own key.
Exit4Tiered questions; preview classifying plants.
Misconceptions to addressClassifying by habitat ("lives in water") or movement; opinion questions ("Is it cute?") in a key.

Classify plants

PhaseMinWhat happens

We do 16Classify plants with a key together.
I do 24Deciduous / evergreen / coniferous (animation: deciduous drops leaves).

7

Carl Linnaeus

LO: Know Linnaeus's classification system, understand its limitations, and how science has improved it since.

🌱 SeedlingSorts "then / now" statements about his system.
🌿 SaplingStates one strength and one gap.
🌳 OakExplains why the model improved as tools improved.

Lesson structure (≈44 min)

PhaseMinWhat happens
Arrival4Recap the whole block.
Starter5"Sort the world" — organise all life with no modern tools.
I do 16Who Linnaeus was; his hierarchy of groups (video).
We do 16Place organisms into a simple hierarchy.
I do 24Limits of his model (animation: tree of life branches).
We do 25Then vs now — what science added and why.
Independent10"Then & now" sorter + final answer to the enquiry question.
Exit4Tiered questions; block review.
Misconceptions to addressThat scientific models are fixed/perfect — Linnaeus missed microorganisms and didn't separate reptiles; science updates as evidence grows.
Resources: video Carl Linnaeus' Classification System (KS2); tree-of-life animation; then/now sorter; TA Focus; Cold Call; print pack.
