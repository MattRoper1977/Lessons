Our four-beat part

Autumn 2 · Week 2 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can keep four even beats and join a group part.

Success criteria

  • Keep each beat the same space apart.
  • Start on the cue.
  • Keep counting through a rest.

Use Next to begin. Ask for word help or your usual support when needed.

Prior: Retrieve loud/soft and fast/slow from earlier sound exploration. Do not assume a pupil has previously played an instrument.

Next: Use the same four-beat cue in a short class piece. Save an honest note of the part played, with the help used.

Preparation

  • Use desks, knee taps, quiet percussion or silent pointing. Print the four-beat scores. No recording or instrument is required.
  • Agree a start cue and a comfortable pace. Keep the beat and part roles separate.

Care and safety

  • Keep volume comfortable; avoid sudden loud sounds. Let pupils choose silent participation and seated movement. No forced eye closing.

Teacher response guidance

  • Starter A: equal gaps.
  • Practice A: TAP, REST, TAP, REST.
  • New B: REST, TAP, REST, TAP.
  • A beat continues during a rest. Stop at the end of the agreed second row.

Evidence of learning

Observe four evenly spaced beats, entry on a cue and rests held for a full beat. The digital check verifies the written pattern only; it does not measure live timing or award completion.

Access and independence

Use four big boxes, pointing and a single visible cue. An adult can keep the pulse while the pupil joins. Fade spoken counting as appropriate.

Lundy Loop

Offer a private or spoken response. Receive the pupil’s exact evidence and name it back. Let that evidence guide one real next teaching step. No forced personal disclosure.

Arrival · Find our starting point

Accept pointing. Check prior knowledge without marking quiet participation as absence.

Starter · Even spaces

Tap A evenly, then B with uneven gaps. The diagram models time spacing, not sound volume.

I do · Count, then tap

Model at a comfortable steady pace around one beat per second. Count in four before starting. Point or make a comfortable small motion; no instrument needed.

We do · Build our part

The on-screen step control is a score walkthrough, not a timed pulse. Adult gives a steady count; observe timing in the actual performance.

I do · A rest is still a beat

Model a common error: leaving out number 2 and rushing to 3. Replay with a silent but equally long second beat.

We do · Start and stop as a team

Two rows = eight beats after the count-in. The adult can be beat team if only one learner is present. Silent pointing counts as a chosen participation mode; assess timing rather than sound volume.

Independent · Try a new part

Fresh independent score B. Give count-in only, then allow the pupil to maintain the chosen part. If help is needed, record it honestly.

Exit · Show your beat

Expected: same pace, count through rests, shared starting cue. Adult names the observed action without claiming award attainment.

Misconceptions

A rest removes the beat. Keep counting through the empty sound space.

Louder means faster. Keep the pace while changing to a quiet tap.
