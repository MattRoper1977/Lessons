ARRIVAL TASK · do now, no help needed

Match the strength to the kind of work it suits

Tap a card, then tap where it belongs. Have a go — wrong answers are welcome here.

While you wait: tell your partner one thing you are good at outside school.
LEARNING OBJECTIVE

LO: To name my strengths and match them to kinds of work.

✓ I can name three of my strengths.
✓ I can match strengths to kinds of work.
✓ I can start my strengths profile for my careers portfolio.
Success criteria: By the end you will have interviewed a partner, sorted strength cards, and made the first page of your strengths profile — evidence for your careers portfolio (Version A: ASDAN Living Independently Module 8).
Aspire: use the sentence “One of my strengths is ___, which would help me in ___.”
STARTER · talk task

Who do you know who is brilliant at their job?

With your partner: think of someone you know (family, neighbour, someone at school) who is good at what they do. What makes them good at it?

Sentence stems:
“___ is really good at ___ because…”
“You can tell they are good because…”
“A strength they have is…”

Stuck? Think about who made your last meal out, cut your hair, fixed something, or helped you when you were younger.

I DO · teacher explains

Everyone has strengths — including you

🤝 People strengths

Listening, helping, cheering others up, working in a team.

🛠️ Hands & tools strengths

Making, fixing, building, cooking, growing.

🧩 Ideas strengths

Sorting, spotting patterns, remembering, checking carefully.

⭐ Character strengths

Turning up, trying again, being honest, being kind.

Misconception: “I have no strengths — I am rubbish at school stuff.” School subjects are a tiny slice of strengths. Reliability, kindness, and being good with your hands are strengths employers actually ask for.
WE DO · together on the board

Sort the strengths — tap a card, then its work family

Ask: is this strength mostly about people, about making/fixing, or about thinking and sorting?

HANDS-ON · the main event

Strengths stations 💪

Ground rule (60 seconds): no put-downs of yourself or anyone else. In this room we talk about strengths like scientists — with evidence.

Station 1 · SPEED INTERVIEW

Pair up. Ask your partner: “What are you good at? How do you know?” Swap after 2 minutes. Write ONE strength you spotted in them on a sticky note and give it to them.

Station 2 · CARD SORT

Real strength cards on the table. Pick the 5 that sound most like you. Get a partner or TA to challenge one: “prove it — when did you show that?”

Station 3 · PROFILE BUILD

Start your strengths profile poster: name, photo space, top 3 strengths, one sentence of proof for each. This is page 1 of your careers portfolio.

Aspire: link one strength to a real job: “A ___ needs my strength of ___.”
