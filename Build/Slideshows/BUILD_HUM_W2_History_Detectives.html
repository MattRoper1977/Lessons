'The photo shows smoke' vs 'the town was polluted' — which is inference?

3. Sources (Recall)

Why might a diary and a newspaper describe the same day differently?

4. Question (Think)

What question can a photo of a dock NOT answer?

E.g. what people thought or said, what happened before/after the shot.

Define anachronism.

A detail that can't belong to its claimed time.

Each step adds assumption; jobs→migration needs more evidence.

Proves — and historians use it rarely.

I Do

I Do: Show vs Infer

Key Idea: A source SHOWS what is literally there. We INFER meaning — and every inference needs a detail we can point to.

📖 Detective Language

Shows — Literally visible/readable in the source.

Suggests / infers — A reasonable conclusion drawn FROM a detail.

Cannot tell us — Questions this source can't answer alone.

🧠 Watch me think

This 1907 dock photo shows men in flat caps by a crane. I want to say 'they're poor' — but caps don't prove poverty. I'll say it SUGGESTS working men, because caps and work clothes were standard for labourers. See how I anchored the inference to a detail?

I Do

I Do: Asking the Source Questions

Key Idea: Good detectives interrogate a source: who made it, when, why — and what would we need NEXT?

📖 The Detective's Questions

Who? — Author or photographer — insider or outsider?

Why? — Record, sell, persuade, remember?

What next? — Which second source would test our inference?

🧠 Watch me think

If this photo was a POSTCARD, the photographer chose the busiest, most impressive view to sell — so 'the port was always this busy' gets weaker. Purpose changes what an image can prove.
