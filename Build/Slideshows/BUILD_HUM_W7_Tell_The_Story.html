I Do

I Do: Telling with a Spine

Key Idea: An account told aloud keeps the plan's spine but adds signposts, pace and spoken evidence.

📖 Speaker's Tools

Signpost — 'First… next… most importantly…' — the listener's map.

Spoken evidence — Name the source out loud: 'the 1901 census shows…'

Landing — A close that answers, slower and clearer than the rest.

🧠 Watch me think — a worked telling

My 30 seconds, labelled as I go:

🛠️ Hook — “This town exists because of a rock.”

🛠️ Signpost — “First, a railway…”

🛠️ Spoken evidence — “the 1901 census shows ninety thousand names…”

🛠️ Landing — “So — a hamlet became a town because iron needed hands.”

✅ Answer: four moves, thirty seconds, spine intact. Short and clear beats long and lost.

🛑 LOST YOUR PLACE? THAT IS NORMAL. RECOVER LIKE THIS.

1. PAUSE. Silence is allowed. It reads as thinking, not as failing.

2. ANCHOR. One glance at your plan strip resets the spine.

3. RE-SIGNPOST. “So — after the railway…” and you are back in.

The wobble is normal. The recovery is the skill.

❓ TOO SHORT? YOU PROBABLY SKIPPED THE EXPLAINING.

Naming a fact is not telling a story. After your evidence, say out loud:

➔ “and that mattered because…”

That one phrase turns a list into an account.

🤝 YOU CHOOSE HOW YOU TELL IT.

To the room, to one person, to a partner, or into a recorder. All four are real tellings and all four bank the same evidence.
