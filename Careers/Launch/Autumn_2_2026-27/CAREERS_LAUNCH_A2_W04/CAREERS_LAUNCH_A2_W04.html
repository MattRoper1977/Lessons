Practise an interview answer

Worked model

  1. Listen to the question. Ask if it is unclear.
  2. Use a true task and your own action.
  3. Hear one feedback prompt. Try a clearer answer.

Adult example: I checked kit against a list. Feedback: What happened next? Retry: I moved the wrong item, then checked the list again.

  • Class experience can count as a true example.
  • Do not copy the adult’s story as your own.
  • An aid or extra thinking time does not make the answer less valid.

We do · 5 min

Choose and explain a step

Team game

Build it from a clue

Use paper cards for: Hear the question / Give a true action / Hear feedback and retry.

Join one of four bands. Work in pairs in your band. One partner looks away from the model, if agreed. The other gives one clue at a time. Order or draw the cards. Then compare with the model and swap roles.

If agreed, hold the step cards and stand in order. A seated row works too. The teacher moves from team to team to check. You may point, sign or direct an adult. A quiet turn counts.

Earn 1 point for each step in the right place. Earn 1 more for a clear clue or a checked fix. Every team can score. Help each other. No points are taken for a retry.

Teaching script 1

Arrival · 4 min

Invite a private regulation check. Green · Ready, Amber · Help, Red · Pause. Offer a quiet start, usual aid or pass. Use the cue and accept the pupil’s usual communication mode.

Starter · 2 min

Starter question 1 revisits last week. Take one minute for recall and reveal. What did we check before sharing a work draft in the last lesson? Cue: Private details · audience · copies. Expected: We checked private details, permission, the audience and the risk of copies. Keeping it offline was a valid choice. Then ask: An interview question is unclear. What could you ask? Expected: Please repeat or explain the question. Asking for help supports a clear answer. Start in your book. Use the reference cards.

Made by Matt

Teaching script 2

I do · 5 min

Practise an interview answer. Listen to the question. Ask if it is unclear. Use a true task and your own action. Hear one feedback prompt. Try a clearer answer. Adult example: I checked kit against a list. Feedback: What happened next? Retry: I moved the wrong item, then checked the list again. Class experience can count as a true example. Do not copy the adult’s story as your own. An aid or extra thinking time does not make the answer less valid.

We do · 5 min

The question is unclear. What helps? Ask for a choice and a reason before revealing. B. Ask for a repeat or a plain explanation. Give a true example rather than inventing experience. Ask: Tell me about a time you checked work. Invite a true action. Then ask: What did you do next?

Made by Matt

Teaching script 3

You do · 17 min

Allow about twelve minutes for exchanges and five for response capture within the task stage. Supported learners may use one question; leave others unattempted. Record the question, actual first answer, one specific prompt and the learner’s retry or not yet. The adult responds to the retry and agrees a useful next step. In your book, plan a true example for the role. Use the question cards in a quiet exchange. Keep your first answer and the feedback prompt. Try again. Record your response and actual support. Supported route. Choose one question with adult reading help. Use your usual aid and try a response. Independent route. Use the three question cards. Link true examples to the role and retry after feedback. Aspire route. Answer a follow-up with a reason. Explain how your retry made the action clearer. Choice route. Choose a tier and how to respond. Ask for a private turn, an aid or a pause.

Made by Matt

Teaching script 4

Review · 4 min

Check one part. Use feedback to make one change. Optional. Respond to feedback with an adult. You may pass. Mark R only after the pupil has responded to feedback and the adult has engaged with the response. Keep the first response and any change separate. Edit in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode.

Exit · 3 min

Ask the two route checks. What helped you most today, or what would you change next time? Point, speak, sign, draw, write or choose. You may pass. Accept the pupil’s own example or a pass. Record their own words and actual support. At check-out, record E for … against the pupil's target.
