Teacher notes and answers

This guide contains answers. Open the lesson slideshow for the pupil view. Use fictional examples and accept speech, pointing, drawing or adult scribing.

The Lundy Loop appears at arrival and exit. Make the response options real, check the pupil’s meaning, then agree one next step or adjustment to support. No personal response needs to be stored.

Week 1 · 40 minutes

What is a career? My journey so far

Open lesson slideshow

Objective

I can explain what a job and a career are and say one thing I might like to do in the future.

  • I can sort jobs and other experiences.
  • I can explain that a career develops over time.
  • I can name a possibility or something I want to find out.

Preparation

  • Print the worksheet and complete resource cards; prepare cut cards only where used.
  • Have the previous portfolio sheet available, plus the supplied fictional case for absent pupils or those choosing privacy.
  • Prepare the learner’s established reading, communication and regulation supports.

Materials

  • Pupil worksheet and resource cards
  • Pencil or chosen recording method
  • Folder for dated Careers evidence

40-minute plan

TimeActivity
5 minArrival and retrieval; low-stakes response
8 minI Do: explain and model using the supplied case
10 minWe Do: guided task and reasoning check
12 minYou Do: three worksheet tasks, feedback and improvement
5 minReflection, file evidence, exit check and calm reset

Vocabulary

  • job: A particular work role a person does.
  • career: A journey through learning, work and life experiences.
  • experience: Something a person does, learns or takes part in.
  • aspiration: Something a person might want to achieve or explore.

Model the task

Drawing maps is an experience. Asking about sign design is a possible next step; it is not a guaranteed job. It could be an unpaid activity or a life role; if paid, it could be a job. The card does not say, so explain the uncertainty.

Support

Use three cards first, read labels aloud and point to past/now/possible future. Scribe the learner’s words. Supply Casey so no personal history is needed.

Stretch

Explain why the club-organiser card has more than one possible heading and what information would settle it.

Fallback

No internet, photographs or personal history are needed. Read the supplied cards aloud and use the named fictional case. If a pupil needs a reset, resume with one card and record their explanation orally; complete remaining evidence later.

Safety and sensitive discussion

No required disclosure of family work, money, identity, disability, private experiences or future plans. Offer the same learning through fictional case analysis and label it accordingly. Do not invent personal evidence or promise access, a job or an adjustment. Follow school procedures if a concern is shared.

What to check

Secure when the pupil distinguishes job from career and records one possibility or enquiry. The aspiration itself is not marked.

Common misconceptions

  • A career means one job forever → show different steps and the possibility of change.
  • Only paid work counts → learning and activities can also shape a career.
  • Everyone must choose now → uncertainty can lead to a useful question.

Vocabulary

  • strength: Something a person does well or is developing confidence in.
  • skill: An ability learned and practised.
  • evidence: A specific example that supports a claim.
  • reliable: Follows through on an agreed task or communicates when plans change.

Model the task

Reliability: Alex completed the agreed action and communicated. This one event does not prove Alex is always reliable. Sam: problem-solving/carefulness, shown by checking the recipe. Taylor: persistence, shown by using support and continuing.

Support

Reduce the bank to organised/careful/persistent. Highlight one action at a time. Accept pointing plus a scribed explanation.

Stretch

Fallback

No internet, photographs or personal history are needed. Read the supplied cards aloud and use the named fictional case. If a pupil needs a reset, resume with one card and record their explanation orally; complete remaining evidence later.

Safety and sensitive discussion

No required disclosure of family work, money, identity, disability, private experiences or future plans. Offer the same learning through fictional case analysis and label it accordingly. Do not invent personal evidence or promise access, a job or an adjustment. Follow school procedures if a concern is shared.

What to check

Secure when three strengths are identified and at least one is supported by a specific action. Case analysis is labelled separately from personal portfolio evidence.

Common misconceptions

  • Strengths are fixed → practice and support can develop them.
  • Being quiet means poor communication → communication has many forms.
  • Evidence must be impressive → a small accurate example is useful.

Vocabulary

  • value: Something a person considers important.
  • belonging: Feeling accepted and able to take part.
  • respect: Treating people with care and listening seriously.
  • support: Help or a change that enables someone to participate.

Model the task

Support

Stretch

Fallback

No internet, photographs or personal history are needed. Read the supplied cards aloud and use the named fictional case. If a pupil needs a reset, resume with one card and record their explanation orally; complete remaining evidence later.

Safety and sensitive discussion

No required disclosure of family work, money, identity, disability, private experiences or future plans. Offer the same learning through fictional case analysis and label it accordingly. Do not invent personal evidence or promise access, a job or an adjustment. Follow school procedures if a concern is shared.

What to check

Secure when two helpful conditions are named and linked to participation. Check that disrespect is identified as a problem, not ranked as a tolerable preference.

Common misconceptions

  • Belonging means everyone is the same → different people can participate.

Vocabulary

  • vocational profile: A short record of strengths, interests, preferences and possible next steps.
  • provisional: Open to change after new experience or information.
  • target: A specific next step to work towards.
  • review: Look again and update using evidence.

Model the task

Catering or practical design/maintenance could be explored because of planning and making interests. The profile does not establish entry requirements or guarantee fit. It says what to do, who can help and when. It gathers information rather than requiring a final choice.

Support

Use Sam’s completed evidence bank. Build one section at a time with drawings, pointing or scribing. Support naming two areas but accept one explained area as a next teaching step.

Stretch

Fallback

No internet, photographs or personal history are needed. Read the supplied cards aloud and use the named fictional case. If a pupil needs a reset, resume with one card and record their explanation orally; complete remaining evidence later.
