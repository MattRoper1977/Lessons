Team game

Build it from a clue

Use paper cards for: Read the task / Find the role / Show the clue.

Join one of four bands. Work in pairs in your band. One partner looks away from the model, if agreed. The other gives one clue at a time. Order or draw the cards. Then compare with the model and swap roles.

If agreed, hold the step cards and stand in order. A seated row works too. The teacher moves from team to team to check. You may point, sign or direct an adult. A quiet turn counts.

Earn 1 point for each step in the right place. Earn 1 more for a clear clue or a checked fix. Every team can score. Help each other. No points are taken for a retry.

This is a paper team game. Keep the task below and all adult checks.

Teaching script 1

Arrival · 4 min

Invite a private regulation check. Green · Ready, Amber · Help, Red · Pause. Offer a quiet start, usual aid or pass. Use the cue and accept the pupil’s usual communication mode.

Starter · 2 min

Starter question 1 revisits last week. Take one minute for recall and reveal. How could a strength help with a task? Cue: Strength · task. Expected: A strength can help a person do part of a task. Then ask: A task says water the pots. Which role brief fits? Expected: The garden brief includes caring for plants. Use the made-up role cards. This is a paper task.

Teaching script 2

I do · 5 min

Read the task and check the role brief. Read the task card. Find it in a role brief. Explain the match. The card says water the pots. The garden brief includes plant care. That clue supports the match. Use the supplied role briefs. Real jobs may share tasks. Choose two different roles.

We do · 7 min

Which brief fits watering pots? Ask for a choice and a reason before revealing. A. The garden brief includes watering plants. Use that stated task to explain the match. Checking a list could help more than one role. Find an example in the briefs.

Teaching script 3

You do · 15 min

Record two different roles, each linked to a task. Two tasks for one role do not show two different roles. Accept another defensible match when the learner uses the briefs to explain. In your book, match a task to each of two roles. Use a clue to explain one match. Check your choices against the briefs. Supported route. Choose between two role cards. Make two different role matches. Independent route. Sort all six task cards. Explain two different role matches. Aspire challenge: Match two different roles. Explain a skill they share. Choice route. Choose a route and mode. Keep two role matches.

Teaching script 4

Review · 4 min

Check a match against its brief. Improve one reason. Optional. Respond to feedback with an adult. You may pass. Mark R only after the pupil has responded to feedback with an adult. Keep the first response and any change separate. Edit in the same colour, on the same page or usual mode.

Exit · 3 min

Ask the two route checks. What helped you most today, or what would you change next time? Point, speak, sign, draw, write or choose. You may pass. Accept the pupil’s own example or a pass. Record their own words and actual support. At check-out, record E for … against the pupil's target.

Teaching script 1

Teaching script 2

I do · 5 min

Read the task and check the role brief. Read the task card. Find it in a role brief. Explain the match. The card says water the pots. The garden brief includes plant care. That clue supports the match. Use the supplied role briefs. Real jobs may share tasks. Choose two different roles.

We do · 7 min

Which brief fits watering pots? Ask for a choice and a reason before revealing. A. The garden brief includes watering plants. Use that stated task to explain the match. Checking a list could help more than one role. Find an example in the briefs.

Record two different roles, each linked to a task. Two tasks for one role do not show two different roles. Accept another defensible match when the learner uses the briefs to explain.
