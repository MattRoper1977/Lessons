Before We Start: Respiration & ATP in 60 Seconds

New to this? Here is everything you need for today — fast. (It is all on Side A of your bridge sheet if you want it on paper.)

What happens inside a cell

Glucose + oxygen go IN → the mitochondria break glucose down using oxygen → carbon dioxide, water and ATP come OUT.

1. Respiration releases energy from glucose to make ATP — inside your cells (it is not breathing).
2. It happens in the mitochondria. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen → lots of ATP.
3. Energy is released, not made — always say ATP.
Whiteboards — 20 seconds: what two things go IN? What comes OUT? Then we go straight into today’s equations.

I Do

I Do: The Aerobic Respiration Equations (Spec 2.37)

Key Idea: Aerobic respiration has two equations that mean the same thing — a word equation and a symbol equation.

1 · The Word Equation

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon Dioxide + Water

Reactants (LEFT): glucose and oxygen go in.

Products (RIGHT): carbon dioxide and water come out (plus ATP, the energy).

2 · The Symbol Equation

C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O

The same equation in chemical "code". The big 6 means six molecules. C₆H₁₂O₆ is the formula for glucose.

Copy it exactly: keep the small subscript numbers and the big 6s in front of O₂, CO₂ and H₂O.

🧠 Memory Trick: "Giant Octopus Caught Water" → Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water. Reactants first, products after.

Watch it flow: reactants IN → products OUT

Reactants — go IN (left)
🍬 Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)
🫁 Oxygen (6O₂)
→
Mitochondria

Products — come OUT (right)
💨 Carbon dioxide (6CO₂)
💧 Water (6H₂O)
🔋 ATP (energy!)
Reactants go IN → broken down in the mitochondria → products come OUT.

Why so much energy (ATP)?

Because there is oxygen, glucose is broken down completely into CO₂ and water. Almost all the energy stored in glucose is released — so aerobic respiration gives a large amount of ATP.

Aspire: Why does aerobic respiration release so much ATP? Because oxygen lets glucose break down completely, releasing almost all of its stored energy as ATP.

Don't write: C6H12O6 + O2 → CO2 + H2O (the 6s are missing)

Write: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (with the 6s!)

The big 6s in front of O₂, CO₂ and H₂O are part of the correct equation — copy them every time.

Talk to your partner: What are the two reactants and the two products of aerobic respiration? Be ready to build the equation next.
