⭐ The Fab 4 — Quick-Fire Recall

Four to warm up the brain — recall from last lesson (Respiration & ATP). Try all 4 before the reveal!

1. What does ATP stand for?

Adenosine triphosphate

2. Which organelle makes most ATP?

Mitochondria

3. Which gas does aerobic respiration need?

Oxygen

4. Where does the glucose come from?

Digested & absorbed food

1. ATP (Recall)

What does ATP stand for, and what is its job?

Adenosine triphosphate. It provides energy for cells.

2. Reactants (Recall)

Aerobic respiration uses glucose. Which gas does it also need?

Oxygen.

3. Products (Recall)

Name the two waste products made when glucose reacts with oxygen.

Carbon dioxide and water.

4. Lead-in: Equations

Last lesson you met the word equation. Today we write it as a proper equation. What goes on the left of the arrow — reactants or products?

Reactants (the things that go IN).

1. Word Equation (Recall)

Write the word equation for aerobic respiration from memory.

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon Dioxide + Water (+ ATP)

2. Location (Recall)

In which organelle does aerobic respiration mostly happen, and why do muscle cells have lots of them?

Mitochondria. Muscles need lots of ATP for contraction.

3. Respiration ≠ Breathing

Explain the difference between respiration and breathing.

Respiration = chemical reaction in cells making ATP. Breathing = moving air in/out of the lungs.

4. Lead-in: Equations

The formula for glucose is C₆H₁₂O₆. Suggest what the symbol equation for aerobic respiration might look like.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O

1. Energy (Recall)

Explain why we say respiration releases energy rather than makes energy.

Energy cannot be created (conservation of energy). It is already stored in glucose bonds and is released/transferred to ATP.

2. Mitochondria (Recall)

Explain why an actively respiring cell would contain many mitochondria.

Each mitochondrion carries out aerobic respiration. More mitochondria = more respiration = more ATP for high energy demand.

3. The Journey (Recall)

Trace glucose from a slice of bread to a respiring muscle cell.

Starch digested → glucose → absorbed in ileum → blood → muscle cell → mitochondria → respiration → ATP.

4. Lead-in: Balancing

Why must a symbol equation have the same number of each atom on both sides?

Atoms can't be created or destroyed in a reaction (conservation of mass) — they are only rearranged, so each side must balance.

I Do: The Aerobic Respiration Equations (Spec 2.37)

Key Idea: For aerobic respiration you must know both the word equation and the balanced symbol equation.
0/2

The Word Equation

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon Dioxide + Water

Reactants (LEFT): glucose and oxygen go in.

Products (RIGHT): carbon dioxide and water come out (plus ATP, the energy).

The Symbol Equation

C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O

The 6 in front means six molecules of that substance. C₆H₁₂O₆ is the formula for glucose.

Reactants → Products

⚠️ Exam Tip: The spec says you must know the balanced symbol equation. Don't drop the 6 in front of O₂, CO₂ and H₂O!

Where does it happen?

🧠 Memory Trick: "Giant Octopus Caught Water" — Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water. Reactants first, products after.
Aspire: Why does aerobic respiration release a lot of ATP? Oxygen lets glucose be broken down completely, so almost all the stored energy is released as ATP.

Don't write: C6H12O6 + O2 → CO2 + H2O (no numbers)

Write: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (balanced!)

An unbalanced equation loses the mark. The number 6 belongs in front of O₂, CO₂ and H₂O.

Talk to your partner: What are the two reactants and the two products of aerobic respiration? Be ready to build the equation next.

Balance Check

🧠 Memory Trick: "Charlie Has 12 Oranges, 6 On 2 sides" — C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O. The number 6 appears everywhere!

Why does aerobic release so much ATP?

Oxygen lets glucose be broken down completely into CO₂ and water. Almost all the energy stored in glucose is released — so aerobic respiration gives a large amount of ATP.

Easy marks: the spec wants the balanced symbol equation. Prove it by counting: 6 C, 12 H, 18 O on each side.

Watch the oxygen: 6 O in glucose + 12 O in 6O₂ = 18 on the left; 12 O in 6CO₂ + 6 O in 6H₂O = 18 on the right.

Talk to your partner: How would you prove to an examiner that the symbol equation is balanced? Next we'll spot which equations are balanced and which aren't.

We Do: Balanced or Not Balanced?

Instructions: Drag each equation into the right bin. Count the atoms on each side — are they the same? (Works by touch or mouse.)
Sorted: 0/6
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + H₂O
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

✅ Balanced

Same atoms each side

❌ Not Balanced

Atoms don't match

Pick one atom at a time. Count it on the LEFT, then the RIGHT. If every atom matches, it's balanced. Missing numbers (like a lone O₂ → CO₂) usually means it isn't.
