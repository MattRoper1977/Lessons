Starter · Hook

Your breath is full of clues

The air you breathe IN is about 0.04% carbon dioxide.

The air you breathe OUT is about 4% carbon dioxide.

That's roughly 100× more CO₂ coming out than going in. Your body is making it.

Predict: After 90 seconds of exercise, will your breath have more or less CO₂ than at rest? Turn and tell your partner why.
Exercise makes your muscles work harder, so they respire faster to release energy. Respiration makes CO₂ — so after exercise there is more CO₂ in every breath out. Today we prove it.

I Do · Quick lead-in

You know this test — here's the safe way to do it

Recap: you did the limewater test in gas-tests week, and you made this limewater on Friday. So the test itself is old news. Only two new things today: (1) do it safely with a straw, and (2) turn "cloudy" into a 1–5 score.
The danger: sucking the limewater up the straw. Limewater is an irritant. We only ever blow OUT.

🥽 Rule 1 — Goggles ON

Splash protection. On before you touch a beaker.

💨 Rule 2 — BLOW, never suck

Gentle out-breaths, like blowing out candles. If you feel like breathing in — take the straw out of your mouth first.

🧍 Rule 3 — Your straw, your beaker

No sharing. Wash hands after. Tell me instantly if any gets in your mouth or eyes.

Quick reminder — cloudy is the endpoint

CO₂ + limewater → the liquid goes cloudy / milky. The more CO₂, the cloudier it gets.

How we measure — the fair test: everyone blows the same number of gentle breaths (I'll count us in). Then rate your beaker on the cloudiness scale, 1–5, using the reference strip. Cloudier = higher score = more CO₂.
Same beaker, same amount of limewater each time (about 3–4 cm deep, never more than half full — gentle breaths so it doesn't froth over). View it the same way every time so the comparison is fair.

We Do · Together

Reading 1 — at REST (we do this as one team)

  1. Goggles on. Same amount of limewater in your REST beaker (about a third full). Straw in that beaker only.
  2. On my count, everyone blows the same number of gentle breaths together — then straws out, hands still.
  3. Look through your beaker (same amount, same way each time) and rate it 1–5 against the strip. That's your rest score.
