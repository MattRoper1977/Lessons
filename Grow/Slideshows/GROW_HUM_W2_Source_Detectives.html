STARTER · talk task

What can a suitcase prove?

With your partner: a packed suitcase from 1948 sits on the evidence table. What could it PROVE about its owner — and what would still be a guess? Where is the line between evidence and guessing?

Sentence stems:
“The ___ proves…”
“We can work out ___ because…”
“But it can’t tell us…”

Stuck? Best clothes packed = a big occasion. Tools packed = coming to work. But feelings, hopes, fears? No suitcase can prove those — that’s where detectives stay careful.

I DO · teacher explains

Sources: what they tell us, and what they only suggest

📷 Kinds of source

Photos, objects, letters, extracts — things made AT THE TIME. Each kind answers different questions.

🔍 The detective method

LOOK closely → DESCRIBE what is there → WORK OUT what it tells us. Describing before deciding is what separates detectives from guessers.

🎭 Ask the witness

Today a migrant character takes the hot-seat. Good questions get good evidence: where, when, why, what did you bring?

⚖️ Stay honest

A source TELLS us some things and only SUGGESTS others. Saying “this suggests…” instead of “this proves…” is proper historian talk.

Misconception: “If it’s old, it must be telling the whole truth.” Every source shows a PIECE of the truth, chosen by whoever made it. A photo shows one moment; a letter shows one voice. Detectives combine sources — and always name what’s missing.
WE DO · together on the board

One Windrush photo — what can it TELL us, SUGGEST, or not prove at all?

TELLS = it is actually in the photo. SUGGESTS = a fair working-out from a clue. CAN’T PROVE = you would need a different source to know it.

HANDS-ON · the main event

Open the case files 🔍

Ground rule (60 seconds): two hands on every object, ask before you lift, and describe before you decide — evidence is protected in this room.

Station 1 · CASE FILES

Rotate the stations — photos, handling objects, short extracts, the suitcase. Every station has a photo/object/audio route as well as the text route: choose your way in. One finding per station.

Station 2 · HOT-SEAT

Question the migrant character. Use the question cards or your own: where from, why leave, what did you pack, what surprised you? Listen for evidence in the answers.

Station 3 · FOUND-OUT SHEET

Complete your “what I found out” detective sheet: voice-note or have your findings scribed FIRST, then add the key words in your own hand.

GCSE Stretch: pick your strongest source and judge it: “this source is useful because… but it can’t tell us…”
