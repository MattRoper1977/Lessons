I Do: Tells, Suggests, Withholds

Key Idea: Every source tells some things, suggests more, and withholds the rest — and its provenance predicts which is which.

👀 Look: the silence is evidence too — ask why THIS source would leave that out.

📖 The Three Layers

Tells — Explicit content — what it states outright.

Suggests — Inferences anchored to details.

Withholds — What we'd expect but don't find — absence as evidence.

🧠 Watch me think — three layers, worked

The source: a proud 1890s Middlesbrough postcard.

🛠️ TELLS — the street’s layout, the gas lamps, the tram.

🛠️ SUGGESTS — civic pride. Someone paid to print this, so someone expected buyers.

🛠️ WITHHOLDS — the damp and the fever the inspector found two streets away.

✅ Answer: the absence is not a flaw in the source — it IS the finding. This is what 1890s Middlesbrough wanted seen, and that is a fact about the town, not a fault in the postcard.

I Do: Provenance Changes the Reading

Key Idea: Who made it, when, and why — answer these and the same content reads differently.

📖 Provenance in Action

Who — Insider/outsider, paid/unpaid, powerful/powerless.

When — Before, during or after the events described?

Where — Local voice or distant observer? Proximity changes what could be known.

Why — Sell, reform, reassure, record — purpose predicts selection.

🛑 HOW DO I SPOT A WITHHOLD? I CANNOT SEE WHAT IS NOT THERE.

Correct — so do not look. Predict first, then check.

1. Ask: given the date and place, what would I EXPECT to find?

2. Now look for it. Missing? That is your withhold.

1890s Middlesbrough: expect overcrowding, fever, hard work. A postcard shows none of it. That gap is the finding.

❓ IT IS BIASED. DOES THAT MEAN IT IS A LIE?

No. Bias tells you what the maker WANTED, and that is evidence in itself.

A boastful advert may be weak proof of working conditions and strong proof that employers were competing hard for workers.

So never write “this source is biased so it is useless”. Write “weak for ___, strong for ___”.
