STARTER · talk task

One decision, many ripples

With your partner: a family takes a job in a new town. List everything that changes — for them, AND for the town they join. Who else is affected by one family’s move?

Sentence stems:
“They moved because…”
“So for the family, ___ changed…”
“And for the town, ___ changed…”

Stuck? Think both directions: the family gets new schools, new neighbours — the town gets new workers, new customers, maybe new food and music. Consequences ripple BOTH ways.

I DO · teacher explains

Every migration has causes — and consequences that ripple

💼 Cause: work

Teesside’s steel, docks and railways pulled in thousands of workers. The Windrush generation answered Britain’s own call for workers in 1948.

🛟 Cause: safety

The Huguenots in the 1680s and refugees today: when home turns dangerous, people move to survive. Safety is one of history’s oldest causes.

👨‍👩‍👧 Cause: family

One person settles, others follow — chain migration. Family is a cause that keeps working for generations.

➡️ Consequence

Came for work → new communities grew → new shops, foods, music, friendships. Consequences become the Britain we live in.

Misconception: “Migration only changes the people who move.” Consequences ripple both ways: arrivals change AND are changed by the place they join. Teesside’s towns, foods and accents were built by exactly this exchange.
WE DO · together on the board

Sort the cards — CAUSE or CONSEQUENCE?

Causes happen BEFORE the move and push or pull it. Consequences happen AFTER the arrival. Ask: which side of the journey does this card sit on?

HANDS-ON · the main event

Build arrows from evidence ➡️

Ground rule (60 seconds): every family’s story is different and nobody has to share their own — our arrows come from the SOURCES, not from each other’s private lives.

Station 1 · SORT THE REASONS

Picture reason cards: jobs, safety, family. Sort them fast as a pair — then find ONE source on the evidence table that matches each reason.

Station 2 · ARROW FROM A SOURCE

Choose a source. Build its arrow: what CAUSE does it show → what CONSEQUENCE followed? Stick the cards, then check the arrow reads aloud correctly.

Station 3 · THE STRIP

Complete your written “People came to Britain because ___, so ___” strip. Picture-supported cards first if you want them; the sentence goes down in your own hand.

GCSE Stretch: chain it: “because… so… and this meant…” — three links, one story.
