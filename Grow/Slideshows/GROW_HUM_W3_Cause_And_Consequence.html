I Do

I Do: Motive, Condition, Trigger

Key Idea: Causes come in kinds: motives move individuals, conditions set the stage, triggers fire the starting gun.

👀 Look: a trigger with no conditions is just a bad day. Name all three kinds.

📖 The Three Kinds

Motive — Personal reason — wages, safety, ambition, family.

Condition — Background state — boom here, hardship there, routes open.

Trigger — The event that converts intention into a journey.

🧠 Watch me think — one journey, dissected

The case: a Welsh ironworker leaving Merthyr for the Tees.

🛠️ ❤️ MOTIVE — better pay. His own reason, on the wage slip.

🛠️ 🌍 CONDITION — Merthyr slowing, the Tees booming. Neither is his doing.

🛠️ ⚡ TRIGGER — the letter: lodgings sorted, start Monday.

✅ Answer: all three, and you can test it. Remove the trigger and he waits. Remove the condition and there is nothing to wait for.

I Do

I Do: Chains and Weighting

Key Idea: Causes chain — condition enables motive, trigger releases it — and historians argue about which link bore the weight.

📖 Weighting Moves

Counterfactual — 'Without X, would it still have happened?'

Comparison — Same conditions, different outcomes — what differed?

Timing — The cause nearest the decision is often the trigger, not the heaviest.

Then the other half: every cause has CONSEQUENCES — and they arrive at different speeds. ⏱️ Short-term (weeks), 📅 Medium (years), 🏛️ Long-term (generations).

🧠 Watch me think

Test the letter with a counterfactual: no letter — does he still go? Probably, later, somewhere on the Tees — the CONDITIONS were pulling hard. So the letter chose the WHEN and WHERE; the boom chose the WHETHER. That's weighting: decisive vs contributory.

KindTest questionLocal example

Weighting — which link bore the weight?

Counterfactual: without X, would it still have happened?   Comparison: same conditions, different outcome — what differed?   Timing: nearest is not heaviest.

Worked: no letter — does he still go? Probably, later, somewhere on the Tees. So the letter chose the WHEN and WHERE; the boom chose the WHETHER. Decisive vs contributory.

Part two — consequences arrive at different speeds

SpeedRoughlyIronstone, 1850
⏱️ SHORT-TERMWeeks to monthsRecruiters arrive; the first crews are hired
📅 MEDIUM-TERMYearsTerraces thrown up; whole families follow; chapels built
🏛️ LONG-TERMDecades to generationsA town of 90,000; a steel identity that outlives the works

⚡ The GROW move: a consequence can outlive its cause. The ore ran out; the town did not.

🛑 CONDITION OR TRIGGER? THEY BOTH FEEL LIKE “WHY”.

Ask: could you put a DATE on it?

A single dateable event → TRIGGER ⚡ (the letter arrived that Tuesday)

A state of affairs lasting years → CONDITION 🌍 (the boom ran for decades)

And if it is one person’s own reason, it is a MOTIVE ❤️ whatever its date.

❓ IS IT A CAUSE OR A CONSEQUENCE? IT LOOKS LIKE BOTH.

Often it is both — and saying so is the stronger answer.

Migrants arriving is a consequence of the iron boom AND a cause of the housing rush. Write it as a chain: “___ caused ___, which in turn caused ___.”
