I Do

I Do: Scale as an Analytical Choice

Key Idea: Choosing a map scale is choosing what can be seen — the historian picks the scale that serves the enquiry, and says so.

📖 Scale Toolkit

Large scale — Town plans — streets, works, docks visible.

Small scale — Nations and seas — routes and distances visible.

Declared choice — 'At the scale of the town… / at the scale of the sea…'

🧠 Watch me think

Watch scale change the story: at world scale, Teesside is a dot receiving arrows — migration looks abstract. At town scale, the arrows become STREETS — Cannon Street's Irish rows, the Welsh chapels two blocks east. Same history, two scales, different insights. I choose per question, and I declare the choice.

I Do

I Do: Spatial Evidence Deepens — Never Replaces

Key Idea: Distances, routes and clustering refine the causal account; they cannot supply motives. The atlas joins the archive; it doesn't dismiss it.

📖 Synthesis Rules

Deepen — Distance data sharpen cost/commitment claims.

Cluster — Street-level patterns evidence networks at work.

Never replace — Motives still live in letters, not latitude.

🧠 Watch me think

The clustering on Cannon Street looks like Week 3's networks made visible — families landing where cousins already lived. But watch the discipline: the map shows the CLUSTER; the letters show the COUSIN. Spatial pattern plus documentary motive — that's the full sentence, and neither half may fire the other.
