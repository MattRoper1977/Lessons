I Do

I Do: Dates, Periods and Intervals

Key Idea: A date locates an event; a period groups events; an interval measures the gap — and intervals are arguments.

👀 Look: the interval is where the thinking is. It tells you whether that was fast or slow.

📖 Time Toolkit

Date — A fixed point — 1850, 1911.

Period — A named span — Victorian, Edwardian, inter-war.

Interval — The measured gap between events — and what it implies.

🧠 Watch me think — an interval as an argument

The claim on trial: “the iron boom followed the railway”.

🛠️ Anchor 1 — railway, 1825.   Anchor 2 — ironstone, 1850.

🛠️ Measure — 1850 − 1825 = 25 years.

🛠️ Read the gap — 25 years is not “followed”. It is a wait.

✅ Answer: the claim survives, but refined. The railway alone did not boom the town — it waited for the ore. The interval did not decorate my causation; it corrected it.

⚡ The GROW move: an interval is an ARGUMENT. Anyone can state a date. Measuring the gap and reading what it means is the historian’s work.

Period names you will need

Victorian 1837–1901  ·  Edwardian 1901–1910  ·  Inter-war 1918–1939  ·  Post-war 1945 onwards

So the Transporter Bridge (1911) is Edwardian by a whisker, not Victorian — a period label is a claim you can be wrong about.

Intervals already measured for you

From → toGapWhat it tests
1825 → 185025 years“Iron followed the rail instantly”
1830 → 184212 years“Docks came at once”
1829 → 190172 years“The town grew overnight”
1850 → 2015165 years“Iron-making lasted forever”

Centuries, if you need them: first two digits + 1. 1825 → 19th, 1911 → 20th, 2015 → 21st. The rule holds for every year on our timeline.

🛑 SUBTRACTING ACROSS A CENTURY?

Do not try to hold it in your head. Break the jump at the round year:

1825 → 1911. 1825 to 1900 is 75. 1900 to 1911 is 11. 75 + 11 = 86 years.

Two small sums beat one big one, and you can show your working.

❓ I HAVE THE NUMBER. WHAT DO I SAY ABOUT IT?

A gap on its own is arithmetic. Turn it into history with one of these:

➔ “___ years is too long for ‘straight away’ — something was waiting.”
➔ “___ years is short enough that one caused the other.”
➔ “___ years is within living memory, so people still remember it.”
