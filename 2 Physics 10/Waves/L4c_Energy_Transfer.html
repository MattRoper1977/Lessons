I Do

I Do: Waves Transfer Energy, Not Matter (3.4)

Key Idea: Waves transfer energy and information from one place to another without transferring matter.

🌊 The Duck Test

Drop a stone in a pond. Waves ripple outward. But a floating duck just bobs up and down — it doesn't travel with the wave.

This proves the water (matter) stays put. Only the energy moves outward.

📡 What DO Waves Carry?

✅ Energy — a tsunami carries massive energy from an earthquake across an entire ocean.

✅ Information — radio waves carry music, Wi-Fi carries data, light lets you see.

❌ Matter — the particles vibrate in place but don't travel with the wave.

Energy Travels, Matter Doesn't

🌟 Think Deeper: A Mexican wave in a stadium is a great example — the pattern moves around the stadium but the people (matter) stay in their seats. They just stand up and sit down.

Don't write: "the wave pushes the water forward to the beach" (matter doesn't travel)

Write: "the wave transfers energy — the water particles vibrate but stay roughly in place"

This is the single most common wave misconception — always check pupils separate "energy travels" from "matter travels".

Talk to your partner: Pick one example — a tsunami, a Mexican wave, or a shout across a field. Explain what travels and what stays put. Be ready to sort more examples yourself next.

Exit Ticket – Check Your Learning

Instructions: Answer independently. No notes.

🖨️ Print written exit ticket:

1) Do waves transfer energy or matter?

Energy (and information). Not matter.

2) In the duck test, does the duck travel with the wave?

No — it bobs up and down, it doesn't travel with the wave.

3) Name one thing waves DO carry.

Energy, or information (either answer is correct).

1) Explain why a duck on a pond doesn't move forward when a wave passes.

The wave transfers energy, not matter. The water particles bob up and down but don't travel forward, so the duck stays in place.

2) Give one everyday example that shows waves transfer energy without transferring matter.

Any valid example: a Mexican wave, shouting across a field, a radio signal, etc. — with a brief explanation of what travels and what stays put.

3) What is the "misconception" that this lesson corrects?

The idea that the wave "pushes the water/matter along" — actually the particles vibrate in place and only the energy moves.

1) Explain how a tsunami can destroy buildings 10,000 km away without the original water travelling that distance.

The wave transfers energy through the water without the water itself travelling. Each water particle passes the energy to the next by vibrating, so the energy can travel 10,000 km while the water particles only move up and down locally. The energy arriving at the coast is enough to cause destruction.

2) A pupil says "sound is just moving air." Explain why this is an oversimplification.

Sound is a wave — the air particles vibrate back and forth in place, passing energy to neighbouring particles. It's the energy (as sound) that travels to your ear, not a "gust" of the original air.

3) Compare a Mexican wave and a water wave in terms of what travels and what stays still.

In both, the pattern (energy) travels while the matter stays roughly in place — fans stand up and sit down without moving seats; water particles bob up and down without travelling with the wave.
