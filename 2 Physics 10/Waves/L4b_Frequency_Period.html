I Do: Frequency & Period (3.3)

Key Idea: Frequency and period both describe how fast a wave repeats — and they're linked by one formula.
0 / 4 revealed

🔄 Frequency, Period & the Link

Frequency (f) — the number of complete waves passing a point per second. Measured in hertz (Hz).

Period (T) — the time taken for one complete wave to pass. Measured in seconds (s).

They're inverses of each other: T = 1 ÷ f    f = 1 ÷ T

Example: if f = 5 Hz → each wave takes T = 1 ÷ 5 = 0.2 s. If T = 0.02 s → f = 1 ÷ 0.02 = 50 Hz.

Frequency vs Period

FrequencyfHz
PeriodTs
If they ask "what happens to the period when frequency increases?" — the answer is always: period decreases. They are inversely proportional.
Talk to your partner: If f = 10 Hz, what is T? Work it out together, then check with T = 1/f. Be ready to match some pairs yourself next.

🖨️ Print written exit ticket:

1) What is frequency the measure of?

The number of complete waves passing a point per second.

2) What unit is frequency measured in?

Hertz (Hz).

3) What unit is period measured in?

Seconds (s).

1) Define period.

The time taken for one complete wave to pass.

2) A wave has a frequency of 4 Hz. What is its period?

T = 1/f = 1/4 = 0.25 seconds.

3) A wave has a period of 0.1 s. What is its frequency?

f = 1/T = 1/0.1 = 10 Hz.

1) Explain why frequency and period are described as "inverses" of each other.

As one increases, the other decreases proportionally — multiplying them together always gives 1. A high frequency means each individual wave takes very little time (a short period), and vice versa.

2) A wave has a period of 0.005 s. Calculate its frequency.

f = 1/T = 1/0.005 = 200 Hz.

3) Two waves have frequencies of 100 Hz and 1000 Hz. Which has the shorter period, and why?

The 1000 Hz wave has the shorter period (0.001 s vs 0.01 s) — a higher frequency always means a shorter period, since T = 1/f.

ConceptDetails
Frequency (f)Number of complete waves per second. Hertz (Hz).
Period (T)Time for one complete wave. Seconds (s).
T = 1/fPeriod equals 1 divided by frequency.
f = 1/TFrequency equals 1 divided by period.
Inverse relationshipHigh frequency = short period. Low frequency = long period.
Worked examplef = 5 Hz → T = 1 ÷ 5 = 0.2 s.

🧠 Memory Tricks

Frequency = how Fast they come (per second).

"Period" like a class period = a length of time.

Flip it: T and f are always on opposite sides of "1 ÷".
