4. Lead-in 🌊

If a wave has a "trough", what do you think this means?

A) The highest pointB) The lowest pointC) The middleD) The edge

✅ B) The lowest point of a wave.

1. 🔵

What is the crest of a wave?

A) The lowest pointB) The highest pointC) The middleD) The distance between waves

✅ B) The highest point. (The trough is the lowest point.)

2. 🔵

What do you think a "wavefront" might be?

A) A single point on a waveB) A line joining points at the same stage of a waveC) The front of a boatD) The end of a wave

✅ B) A line joining all the points on a wave at the same stage of vibration (e.g. all the crests).

3. 🔵

The "rest position" of a wave is…

A) Where the wave never movesB) The middle level the wave oscillates aroundC) The highest pointD) The lowest point

📐 The 6 Key Definitions

Crest — the highest point of a wave.

Trough — the lowest point of a wave.

Rest position — the middle level a wave oscillates around when there's no disturbance.

Amplitude — the maximum displacement from the rest position. How "tall" the wave is. Measured in metres (m).

Wavelength (λ) — the distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave (e.g. crest to crest). Measured in metres (m).

Wavefront — an imaginary line joining all the points on a wave that are at the same stage of vibration (e.g. all the crests).

🎛️ Live Wave Machine — come up and drive it!

🌍 Why This Matters

Surfers read wave height (amplitude) and spacing (wavelength) to catch the perfect wave. Engineers measure amplitude to predict flood risk from waves. Every wave — water, sound, or light — has the same basic anatomy.

💡 Memory tricks:
Amplitude = how Amazing (tall) the wave is
λ (lambda) = Length of one wave
Crest is at the top, like the crest of a hill

AmplitudeHow TALL the wave is — maximum displacement from rest. Metres (m).
Wavelength (λ)Distance from one crest to the next. Metres (m).
