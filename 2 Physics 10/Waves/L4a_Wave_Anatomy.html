A) The lowest pointB) The highest pointC) The middleD) The distance between waves

📐 The 6 Key Definitions

Crest — the highest point of a wave.

Trough — the lowest point of a wave.

Rest position — the middle level a wave oscillates around when there's no disturbance.

Amplitude — the maximum displacement from the rest position. How "tall" the wave is. Measured in metres (m).

Wavelength (λ) — the distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave (e.g. crest to crest). Measured in metres (m).

Wavefront — an imaginary line joining all the points on a wave that are at the same stage of vibration (e.g. all the crests).

Anatomy of a Wave

🌍 Why This Matters

Surfers read wave height (amplitude) and spacing (wavelength) to catch the perfect wave. Engineers measure amplitude to predict flood risk from waves. Every wave — water, sound, or light — has the same basic anatomy.

💡 Memory tricks:
Amplitude = how Amazing (tall) the wave is
λ (lambda) = Length of one wave
Crest is at the top, like the crest of a hill

🔑 Keywords to Know

Crest / TroughHighest / lowest point of a wave.
AmplitudeHow TALL the wave is — maximum displacement from rest. Metres (m).
Wavelength (λ)Distance from one crest to the next. Metres (m).
WavefrontLine joining points at the same stage of vibration.

🖨️ Print written exit ticket:

1) What is the amplitude of a wave?

The maximum displacement from the rest position (height of the wave).

2) What is the highest point of a wave called?

The crest.

3) What is the lowest point of a wave called?

The trough.

1) Define wavelength.

The distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave (e.g. crest to crest). Measured in metres.

2) What unit is amplitude measured in?

Metres (m).

3) Explain what the "rest position" of a wave is.

The middle level the wave oscillates around when there's no disturbance — the flat line the wave moves up and down from.

1) Distinguish between a wavefront and a wavelength.

A wavefront is a line joining points at the same stage of vibration (e.g. all crests). Wavelength is the distance between two adjacent wavefronts (or between any two identical points on consecutive waves).

2) A pupil says amplitude is "the distance from crest to trough". Explain why this is not quite correct.

Amplitude is measured from the rest position to the crest (or trough) — not crest to trough directly. Crest-to-trough distance is actually double the amplitude.

3) Sketch a simple wave in your head. Explain where you would draw the amplitude arrow and the wavelength arrow.

Amplitude arrow: a vertical line from the rest position up to a crest. Wavelength arrow: a horizontal line from one crest to the next crest (or any two matching points one wave apart).
