    2. Recall 🟢

    The ammeter goes in series or parallel?

    3. Recall 🟢

    What unit is current measured in?

    1. Recall 🟠

    I = 2 A, R = 5 Ω. Find V.

    V = I × R = 2 × 5 = 10 V

    2. Recall 🟠

    Where does the voltmeter go, and what does it measure?

    In parallel across the component. It measures voltage (V).

    3. Recall 🟠

    Name two components you drag onto the PhET workspace to start a circuit.

    Battery and bulb (plus wires to connect them).

    1. Recall 🔵

    Why must the ammeter be in series, not parallel?

    Current must flow through the ammeter to be counted — only series puts it inside the loop.

    2. Recall 🔵

    V doubles from 6 V to 12 V (same R). What happens to I?

    I doubles. (I = V ÷ R — V doubles, R unchanged → I doubles.)

    3. Recall 🔵

    V = 8 V, I = 4 A. Find R.

    R = V ÷ I = 8 ÷ 4 = 2 Ω

    🌉 4. Today's Bridge — Lead-in Question

    Have a go: If V = 12 V and I = 4 A, which formula would you use to find R — and what answer do you get?

    R = V ÷ I = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 Ω. That's exactly the skill we're sharpening today.

    ⏱️ 4 min
    I Do

    I Do: Three Ways to Use the Triangle

    Just watching: Pens down. I'll work through all three rearrangements. Notice — they all follow the same 3 steps.

    ①  Find R (today's main skill)

    Given: V = 10 V I = 2 A

    Step 1 — Formula: Cover R → R = V ÷ I

    Step 2 — Substitute: R = 10 ÷ 2

    Step 3 — Answer + Unit: R = 5 Ω ✅

    ②  Find I

    Given: V = 12 V R = 4 Ω

    Step 1: Cover I → I = V ÷ R

    Step 2: I = 12 ÷ 4

    Step 3: I = 3 A ✅

    ③  Find V

    Given: I = 0.5 A R = 12 Ω

    Step 1: Cover V → V = I × R

    Step 2: V = 0.5 × 12

    Step 3: V = 6 V ✅

    🧠 The Memory Rule

    Finding V? → MULTIPLY  (V = I × R)

    Finding I? → DIVIDE  (V ÷ R)

    Finding R? → DIVIDE  (V ÷ I)

    V sits on top of the triangle, so whenever you divide, V goes on top of the fraction.

    📋 Always Show:

    Formula → Substitute → Answer + Unit. Even a wrong final calculation earns marks if the formula and substitution are right.

    ⚠️ Three traps to avoid:

    • Multiplying V and I to find R (R = V × I is wrong)

    • Adding or subtracting — Ohm's law only uses × and ÷

    • Forgetting the unit (an answer of "5" without "Ω" loses marks)

    Grade 5

    6. A bulb's voltage doubles from 6 V to 12 V (same bulb). The original current was 2 A. What's the new current? Explain in one sentence.

    R = V ÷ I = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 Ω (R doesn't change). New I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 3 = 4 A. When V doubles and R stays the same, I doubles too.

    Grade 3–4

    5. V = 9 V, I = 0.3 A. Find R.

    R = V ÷ I = 9 ÷ 0.3 = 30 Ω

    Grade 2

    4. V = 12 V, R = 6 Ω. Find I.

    I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 A

    Grade 1

    3. I = 3 A, R = 4 Ω. Find V.

    V = I × R = 3 × 4 = 12 V
