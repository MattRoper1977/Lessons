Arrival Task – How Much Do You Remember?

Instructions: No notes. Answer from memory. One question from each of L1, L2 and L3.

1. 🟢 (L1)

Draw the circuit symbol for a cell and a lamp.

Cell: long line + short line. Lamp: circle with a cross.

2. 🟢 (L2)

Write the formula linking voltage, current and resistance.

V = I × R

3. 🟢 (L3)

What particles carry current in a metal wire?

Electrons (negatively charged).

4. Bridge 🌊

Name two types of wave you know about.

Any from: water waves, sound waves, light waves, radio waves, etc.

1. 🟠 (L1)

State how an ammeter and voltmeter must be connected.

Ammeter: in series. Voltmeter: in parallel.

2. 🟠 (L2)

A 12 V battery is connected to a 6 Ω resistor. Calculate the current.

I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 A

3. 🟠 (L3)

In which direction do electrons flow — + to − or − to +?

− to + (opposite to conventional current).

4. Bridge 🌊

If you drop a stone into a pond, what happens? Does the water travel outward, or just the energy?

Ripples spread outward. The energy travels, but the water itself stays roughly in place (bobs up and down).

1. 🔵 (L1)

Explain why houses are wired in parallel, not series.

Each appliance gets full voltage (230 V). If one breaks, the others keep working.

2. 🔵 (L2)

A hairdryer uses 230 V and draws 10 A. Calculate its resistance.

R = V ÷ I = 230 ÷ 10 = 23 Ω

3. 🔵 (L3)

A 15 V battery connects to 5 Ω and 10 Ω in series. Find the current and V across the 10 Ω.

R = 15 Ω. I = 15 ÷ 15 = 1 A. V = 1 × 10 = 10 V.

4. Bridge 🌊

Both electricity and waves involve energy transfer. In a circuit, energy is transferred by electrons. In a wave, how do you think energy is transferred without anything physically travelling?

Particles vibrate and pass the energy to their neighbours — the energy moves forward, but the particles stay roughly in place.

⚡ L1: Circuits

8 symbols: cell, battery, switch, lamp, resistor, variable resistor, ammeter, voltmeter.

Series: one loop, current same, voltages add up.

Parallel: branches, voltage same, currents add up.

Ammeter = series. Voltmeter = parallel.

🔋 L2: V = I × R

V = I × R  |  I = V ÷ R  |  R = V ÷ I

V = volts (V)   I = amps (A)   R = ohms (Ω)

Current = rate of flow of charge. 1 A = 1 coulomb per second.

🔬 L3: Electron Flow

Current in metals = flow of negatively charged electrons.

Electron flow: − → +  |  Conventional: + → −

Metals conduct because they have free (delocalised) electrons.

🔗 L3: Series Calculations

Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ (add resistances)

Find I = V ÷ Rtotal → then V across each = I × R

Check: V₁ + V₂ must = supply voltage.

I Do: Top 4 Mistakes to Avoid

Before you practise, let's fix the most common errors.

❌ Mistake 1: Multiplying to find I

"I = V × R = 12 × 4 = 48"

✅ Fix: I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A

❌ Mistake 2: Multiplying series R

"R total = 3 × 6 = 18 Ω"

✅ Fix: R total = 3 + 6 = 9 Ω (ADD in series)

❌ Mistake 3: Forgetting the unit

"V = 3 × 4 = 12" (no unit!)

✅ Fix: V = 3 × 4 = 12 V (always write V, A, or Ω)

❌ Mistake 4: Current changes in series

"The current is smaller after the resistor"

✅ Fix: In series, current is the SAME everywhere. It's voltage that is shared.

💡 Rule of thumb: If you're finding I or R, you DIVIDE. If you're finding V, you MULTIPLY. The triangle never lies!

I Do: Full Series Worked Example

Watch the method carefully. You'll do your own in the next We Do.

✏️ Problem

An 18 V battery is connected to a 6 Ω and a 12 Ω resistor in series.

Find: Rtotal, current, voltage across each resistor.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ = 6 + 12 = 18 Ω

Step 2: I = V ÷ R = 18 ÷ 18 = 1 A

Step 3: V₁ = I × R₁ = 1 × 6 = 6 V

Step 4: V₂ = I × R₂ = 1 × 12 = 12 V

Check: 6 + 12 = 18 V = supply ✅

Series Circuit

🌟 Notice: The 12 Ω resistor gets double the voltage of the 6 Ω — because it has double the resistance. Voltage is shared in proportion to resistance.
