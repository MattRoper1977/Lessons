Arrival Task – What Do You Already Know?

Instructions: Answer from memory. This is a new topic — do your best!

1. 🟢

Name two examples of waves you have seen or heard.

Any from: water waves, sound waves, light, radio waves, microwaves, Mexican waves, etc.

2. 🟢

If you drop a stone into a pond, what happens to the water?

Ripples (waves) spread outwards in circles from where the stone entered.

3. 🟢

Does a duck sitting on a pond move across the water when a wave passes? Or does it bob up and down?

It bobs up and down — the duck doesn't travel with the wave. The wave passes under it.

4. Lead-in 🌊

If the duck doesn't move forward, what does the wave carry from one place to another?

Energy (and information). The wave carries energy outwards but the water/matter stays in roughly the same place.

1. 🟠

Name three different types of wave.

Water waves, sound waves, light waves, radio waves, seismic waves, microwaves, etc.

2. 🟠

What do you think the word "frequency" means in everyday English? How might it apply to waves?

Frequency means how often something happens. For waves, it means how many waves pass a point each second.

3. 🟠

When you shout across a field, your friend hears you. Did any air travel from your mouth to their ear, or just the sound?

Just the sound (energy). The air particles vibrate back and forth but don't travel across the field.

4. Lead-in 🌊

If waves don't move matter, what two things do they transfer from one place to another?

Energy and information.

1. 🔵

Explain, using a water wave example, what the terms "crest" and "trough" might mean.

Crest = the highest point of a wave. Trough = the lowest point.

2. 🔵

A wave has a frequency of 5 Hz. What does this tell you?

5 complete waves pass a point every second. Hz means "per second".

3. 🔵

Earthquakes send seismic waves through the Earth. Do the rocks travel from the earthquake to your house, or just the energy?

Just the energy. The rocks vibrate in place — the wave transfers energy through them but the matter doesn't travel.

4. Lead-in 🌊

Radio stations broadcast at different frequencies. Why do you think frequency is measured in hertz (Hz) rather than metres?

Frequency measures how many waves per second (a time-based measurement), not a distance. Metres measure wavelength (distance). Different quantities need different units.

I Do: Wave Definitions (3.3)

Key Idea: You must know 5 definitions: amplitude, wavefront, frequency, wavelength, period.

📐 The 5 Key Definitions

Amplitude — the maximum displacement from the rest position. How "tall" the wave is.

Wavelength (λ) — the distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave (e.g. crest to crest). Measured in metres (m).

Frequency (f) — the number of complete waves passing a point per second. Measured in hertz (Hz).

Period (T) — the time taken for one complete wave to pass. Measured in seconds (s). T = 1/f.

Wavefront — an imaginary line joining all the points on a wave that are at the same stage of vibration (e.g. all the crests).

Anatomy of a Wave

🌍 Why This Matters

Music producers adjust amplitude (volume) and frequency (pitch). Doctors use ultrasound waves to see babies. Surfers read wavelengths to catch the perfect wave. Understanding wave properties explains sound, light, Wi-Fi, earthquakes, and even how your microwave heats food.

💡 Memory tricks:
Amplitude = how Amazing (tall) the wave is
Frequency = how Fast they come (per second)
λ (lambda) = Length of one wave

🔑 Keywords to Know

AmplitudeHow TALL the wave is — maximum displacement from rest.
Wavelength (λ)Distance from one crest to the next. Measured in metres.
Frequency (f)How many waves per second. Measured in hertz (Hz).
Period (T)Time for ONE complete wave. T = 1/f.

I Do: Waves Transfer Energy, Not Matter (3.4)

Key Idea: Waves transfer energy and information from one place to another without transferring matter.

🌊 The Duck Test

Drop a stone in a pond. Waves ripple outward. But a floating duck just bobs up and down — it doesn't travel with the wave.

This proves the water (matter) stays put. Only the energy moves outward.

📡 What DO Waves Carry?

✅ Energy — a tsunami carries massive energy from an earthquake across an entire ocean.

✅ Information — radio waves carry music, Wi-Fi carries data, light lets you see.

❌ Matter — the particles vibrate in place but don't travel with the wave.

Energy Travels, Matter Doesn't

🌟 Think Deeper: A Mexican wave in a stadium is a great example — the pattern moves around the stadium but the people (matter) stay in their seats. They just stand up and sit down.

I Do: Period & Frequency — The Link

Key Idea: Period and frequency are inverses. T = 1/f and f = 1/T.

🔄 The Relationship

T = 1 ÷ f     f = 1 ÷ T

If f = 5 Hz → 5 waves per second → each wave takes T = 1 ÷ 5 = 0.2 s

If T = 0.02 s → each wave takes 0.02 s → f = 1 ÷ 0.02 = 50 Hz

📋 Summary — All 5 Quantities

Frequency vs Period

🌟 Exam Tip: If they ask "what happens to the period when frequency increases?" — the answer is always: period decreases. They are inversely proportional.

1) What is the amplitude of a wave?

The maximum displacement from the rest position (height of the wave).

2) What unit is frequency measured in?

Hertz (Hz).

3) Do waves transfer energy or matter?

Energy (and information). Not matter.

1) Define wavelength.

The distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave (e.g. crest to crest). Measured in metres.

2) A wave has a frequency of 4 Hz. What is its period?

T = 1/f = 1/4 = 0.25 seconds.

3) Explain why a duck on a pond doesn't move forward when a wave passes.

The wave transfers energy, not matter. The water particles bob up and down but don't travel forward, so the duck stays in place.

1) Distinguish between a wavefront and a wavelength.

A wavefront is a line joining points at the same stage of vibration (e.g. all crests). Wavelength is the distance between two adjacent wavefronts (or between any two identical points on consecutive waves).

2) A wave has a period of 0.005 s. Calculate its frequency.

f = 1/T = 1/0.005 = 200 Hz.

3) Explain how a tsunami can destroy buildings 10,000 km away without the original water travelling that distance.

The wave transfers energy through the water without the water itself travelling. Each water particle passes the energy to the next by vibrating, so the energy can travel 10,000 km while the water particles only move up and down locally. The energy arriving at the coast is enough to cause destruction.
