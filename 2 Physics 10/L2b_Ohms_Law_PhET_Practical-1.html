    ⏱️ 3 min
    Arrival Task

    Quick Recall — From Last Week

    Instructions: 3 quick questions. One word or one number is fine. Answer in your books. Go!

    1. Units 🟢

    What unit is voltage measured in?

    Volts (V).

    2. Formula 🟢

    Fill the gap: V = I × ___

    R (resistance).

    3. Equipment 🟢

    What does an ammeter measure?

    Current (in amps).

    1. Units 🟠

    Match: V → ?, I → ?, R → ?

    V → volts, I → amps, R → ohms (Ω).

    2. Quick Calc 🟠

    I = 2 A, R = 3 Ω. What is V?

    V = I × R = 2 × 3 = 6 V

    3. Equipment 🟠

    Where in a circuit do you put a voltmeter?

    In parallel — across the component.

    1. Definition 🔵

    Current is the rate of flow of ___?

    Charge (carried by electrons).

    2. Quick Calc 🔵

    V = 12 V, I = 4 A. Find R.

    R = V ÷ I = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 Ω

    3. Reasoning 🔵

    If R doubles but V stays the same, what happens to I?

    I halves (because I = V ÷ R, larger R = smaller I).

    V = I × R   |   I = V ÷ R   |   R = V ÷ I

    ⏱️ 3 min
    I Do

    I Do: The Quick Recap (3 minutes — eyes up!)

    Just listening: No need to write. We're recapping last week so you can apply it today.

    ⚡ The Three Quantities

    V — Volts the "push" — energy per coulomb of charge.

    I — Amps the rate of flow of charge.

    R — Ohms (Ω) what slows the flow.

    V = I × R

    ✏️ Worked Example (1 min)

    Q: A bulb has I = 0.5 A and R = 12 Ω. Find V.

    1. Formula: V = I × R

    2. Substitute: V = 0.5 × 12

    3. Answer: V = 6 V ✅

    🔑 Three Things You'll Need Today

    ✓ Read voltmeter (V across component)

    ✓ Read ammeter (I in series)

    ✓ Use V = I × R (or rearrange) to find what's missing

    📋 Always Show:

    Formula → Substitute → Answer + Unit. Even if you make a calc mistake, you still get marks for the formula and substitution.

    🌟 Today's twist: You won't be given imaginary numbers — you'll read your own from a circuit you build.

    Exit Ticket

    Exit Ticket — Show What You Know

    Instructions: No notes. No sim. Just you. Answer in your books or on the printed exit slip.

    Tap how confident you feel — this prints onto your evidence report.

    1) What is current the rate of flow of?

    Charge.

    2) Write the formula linking voltage, current and resistance.

    V = I × R

    3) A 4 A current flows through a 3 Ω resistor. Calculate V.

    V = I × R = 4 × 3 = 12 V

    1) A 9 V battery is connected to a 3 Ω resistor. Calculate I.

    I = V ÷ R = 9 ÷ 3 = 3 A

    2) What does "1 amp" mean in terms of charge?

    1 coulomb of charge flowing past a point every second.

    3) A lamp: R = 15 Ω, I = 0.4 A. Find V.

    V = I × R = 0.4 × 15 = 6 V

    1) A hairdryer on UK mains (230 V) draws 10 A. Find R.

    R = V ÷ I = 230 ÷ 10 = 23 Ω

    2) Explain why increasing voltage across a fixed resistor increases the current. Use "charge" or "electrons".

    Higher voltage gives each coulomb of charge more energy ("more push"), so electrons flow faster. Since current = rate of flow of charge, faster flow = bigger current. Resistance hasn't changed, so by I = V ÷ R, larger V gives larger I.

    3) Two resistors of 10 Ω and 20 Ω in series, with a 6 V battery. Find I.

    Total R = 10 + 20 = 30 Ω. I = V ÷ R = 6 ÷ 30 = 0.2 A
