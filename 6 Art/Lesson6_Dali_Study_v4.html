Starter

Starter — Annotation Writing Frame

Instructions: Recall the writing frame. We'll use this every time we annotate. Three stages — you build them in order.

1️⃣ STATE

What is the basic info?

Artist's name · Title of work · Medium (oil, watercolour…) · Date / year painted

2️⃣ DESCRIBE

What can you see?

Foreground / mid-ground / background. Objects. Colours, tone, line, texture. Visual elements. Juxtaposition / scale / unusual choices.

3️⃣ EXPLAIN

Why? Meaning? Your reaction?

What the artist might mean. What it makes YOU feel. Connection to the artist's life or ideas (e.g., subconscious / Freud / dreams).

📋 Quick test yourself

For each phrase, decide: STATE, DESCRIBE or EXPLAIN?

"Painted in 1944 in oil." → STATE

"In the background, an elephant on long stilt-legs." → DESCRIBE

"Reminds me of a chaotic dream — feels uneasy." → EXPLAIN

Aspire: Top-band annotations weave the three stages together — they don't write three separate paragraphs labelled "state", "describe", "explain". Use the frame as a checklist, not a wall.

Click below to reveal the DESCRIBE stage…

"In the background there is an elephant with absurdly long stilt-legs carrying a large rock-like crystal. In the mid-ground we see two tigers leaping from one another, and from a fish which itself leaps from a pomegranate. In front of the leaping tiger a rifle points at an unclothed reclining woman. There are many juxtapositions here — bizarre imagery contrasted with the relaxed pose of the woman, calm against the aggression of the tigers. Dalí emphasises warm colour and tone to give the work a Renaissance feel."

3️⃣ EXPLAIN

Click below to reveal the EXPLAIN stage…

"This piece feels chaotic and dreamlike. I think Dalí is showing the woman's subconscious mind — the bee, the rifle, the tigers may all be dream-images created at the moment of waking. The realistic detail makes the impossible scene feel believable, which makes me feel uneasy. This links to Dalí's interest in Freud's ideas about dreams."

We Do

🔍 We Do: Spot the Mistake

Instructions: A pupil wrote this annotation about Dalí's painting. Find 3 mistakes. Whiteboards out.
"This painting by Dalí is called The Persistence of Memory. It is a watercolour painted in 2004. In the painting I can see lots of weird stuff — an elephant with long legs, some tigers and a pomegranate. Dalí used bright primary colours like Miró did. The work is just weird and I don't really get it. I think it's lazy because anyone could paint random things."

Here are the mistakes:

  • Mistake 1 (STATE): Title is wrong. The painting described is Dream Caused by the Flight of a Bee around a Pomegranate, NOT Persistence of Memory. Medium is oil on panel, not watercolour. Date is 1944, not 2004.
  • Mistake 2 (DESCRIBE): "Bright primary colours like Miró" — wrong. Dalí used warm sunlit naturalistic tones, not bold primaries. He's the opposite of Miró colour-wise.
  • Mistake 3 (EXPLAIN): "It's lazy because anyone could paint random things" — completely wrong. Dalí trained as a realistic painter. His work uses juxtaposition deliberately to access the subconscious mind — there's nothing random about it.

And missing: the EXPLAIN stage doesn't link to Freud, dreams, or the subconscious — that's the meaning the writer skipped.

Quality check before YOU write: Did I get the title, medium and date right? Did I describe what's actually IN the picture? Did I link the EXPLAIN to Dalí's BIG idea (the subconscious / dreams)?

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket

Instructions: Answer independently. No notes.

1) What are the 3 stages of the annotation writing frame?

State, Describe, Explain.

2) Why do we use a grid when copying an image?

To draw accurately / get proportions right by copying box by box.

3) Name one thing that should go in the STATE part of an annotation.

Artist's name OR title of work OR medium OR date.

1) What goes into each stage — STATE, DESCRIBE, EXPLAIN?

STATE: artist, title, medium, date. DESCRIBE: what you can see (objects, colours, layout, juxtaposition). EXPLAIN: meaning, your reaction, link to artist's ideas.

2) Why do Dalí annotations need to mention the SUBCONSCIOUS?

Because Dalí's whole purpose was to paint the subconscious mind (Freud's idea). Skipping this misses the meaning.

3) Give an example of a juxtaposition in the Bee/Pomegranate painting.

e.g., calm reclining woman vs aggressive leaping tigers; tiny bee causing huge dream; elephant on impossibly long stilt-legs.

1) Why must STATE come before DESCRIBE before EXPLAIN — does the order matter?

Yes. STATE establishes context (who/when/medium). DESCRIBE catalogues what's actually there before interpreting. EXPLAIN can only be trusted if the first two are accurate. Without STATE, your reader doesn't know which work you mean; without DESCRIBE, the EXPLAIN floats free of evidence.

2) How does GRIDDING actually help artistic accuracy — and what does it NOT teach you?

Helps: proportion, scale, placement — by isolating each square. Doesn't teach: free-hand drawing, observational sketching, line confidence. It's a scaffold to be removed once you can see proportions independently.

3) Argue: should artists ALWAYS link technique to meaning when annotating, or can technique stand alone?

Top-band annotations link both — technique without meaning is just description; meaning without technique is opinion. The strongest writing weaves them: "Dalí's tight Renaissance brushwork (technique) makes the impossible elephant feel believable, deepening the subconscious unease (meaning)."
