1) Juxtaposition — meaning and master?

Unrelated things fused for a dreamlike effect — Magritte.

2) The five gridding steps, in order?

Tonal scale → grid → box-by-box outline → rub out → add tone.

3) A final idea differs from an initial idea because it is…

It is developed — bigger, changed, influenced and annotated.

4) In a fair test, what changes and what stays?

One variable changes (the media); the drawing stays identical.

1) Trace your idea's journey in one sentence: spark → final idea → tests.

Personal — e.g. "Eye+galaxy spin → looking-up doorway composition → pencil on gradient won."

2) Why does a plan beat launching straight into the final piece?

The plan catches problems small and cheap; the final piece then executes proven choices.

3) Copying vs influence?

Copying repeats an artist's image; influence borrows their thinking for your own.

4) What should an evaluation contain?

What worked (with reasons), what you'd change, and what you learnt.

I Do

I Do: Plan Like a Pro 🗺️

A Mini Masterpiece is built in the right order. Reveal the five moves.
Step 0 of 5background before detail — always

1️⃣ Frame it

Rule a neat border box — postcard to half-page. Title: Final Piece Plan.

2️⃣ Light outline

Your final idea, in its winning composition, sketched faint.

3️⃣ Background FIRST

Lay in your winning background before any detail — detail on top, never under.

4️⃣ Main image

Build the eye and the surreal element in your winning media.

5️⃣ Darks + details last

Deepest tones, crisp details, catchlight rescued — then annotate the choices.

Working detail first, background squeezed around it ruins edges — background first, detail on top keeps everything crisp.

We Do

We Do: You Be the Judge 🧑‍⚖️

Two pupils' plans walk in — only one is stronger. Tap the better plan in each round, then defend your verdict.

Round 1 — the plan

Plan A

"I'll decide my background when I get to it, and I might swap media halfway if it looks boring."

Plan B

"Pencil on a tonal gradient, eye off-centre — because those won my tests. Background first, darks last."

✅ B wins: every choice is tested and ordered. A is guessing — the tests were wasted on A.

Round 2 — the annotation

Annotation A

"I chose oil pastel because my tests showed it gave the boldest sky, like Höch's cut-paper energy."

Annotation B

"This is my final piece. It is an eye with a sky in it. I used pastel and it took a long time."

✅ A wins: it justifies with evidence AND names an influence. B only describes what we can already see.

Round 3 — the evaluation

Evaluation A

"It went fine. I liked it. 10/10 would draw again."

Evaluation B

"The gradient worked because it pushed the eye forward; next time I'd test a colder colour scheme for a stranger mood."

✅ B wins: a strength with a reason plus a specific next step. Honest beats enthusiastic.

The whole unit in five lines

Surrealism = the believable + one impossible thing. Magritte swaps, Dalí melts, Höch cuts-and-fuses. Grid for accuracy, tone for depth. Develop = change, don't copy. Test before you commit — then justify everything.

Self-quiz — cover the answers, write, then check

  1. The five build steps in order? — Frame, light outline, background, main image, darks/details.
  2. Three evaluation ingredients? — Strength + reason, evidence, next step.
  3. Why background before detail? — Detail on top stays crisp.

Common mark-losers

Ignoring your own test results · detail first, background squeezed around it · evaluations that cheer ("it went fine!") instead of judge · no title or signature.

Memory hooks

"Background first, darks last." · "Strength, reason, evidence, next step." · "Small and finished beats big and abandoned."
