I Do

I Do: The Two Collage Moves ✂️

Every surreal collage uses one or both of these. Reveal them one at a time.
Step 0 of 2read each one aloud

1️⃣ JUXTAPOSE

Place two unrelated things next to each other. The viewer's brain links them and a new meaning appears. An eye next to a clock.

2️⃣ TRANSFORM

Let one object become part of the other, so the join disappears. The clock IS the iris. This is the stronger, more surreal move.

two things with a gap between them is just two pictures. overlap and merge them so they read as ONE new object.

I Do: Annotate in 3 Lines ✍️

A great annotation is just THREE short lines. Look at this surreal eye — then read the three lines that describe it.
A clock iris + a butterfly-wing lash
👁️ I SEE: a clock face where the iris should be, and a wing for a lash.
💭 I LIKE: the clash of living and mechanical — it feels dreamlike. (always say WHY)
🎨 MEDIA: soft pencil for the lid, fine-liner for the numbers.

Three short lines. One each. That's it ✅

Independent Work

Independent Work — Make Your Surreal Eye

In your sketchbook (or on A4/A3). Make ONE strong surreal eye — combine, transform, trace — then add a 3-line annotation.
15:00

📋 Make your surreal eye

1. Combine & transform: cut 2+ images from the image bank, merge them into ONE surreal eye — no gaps, one becomes part of the other (a clock that IS the iris).

2. Trace: scribble-back — scribble soft pencil on the BACK of your collage, lay it on your page and draw over it; OR tape it to a window and trace the lines.

3. Annotate (3 short lines): 👁️ SEE · 💭 LIKE (+ why) · 🎨 MEDIA — use the sentence starters in your pack.

Key distinctions

Juxtapose = place two unrelated things together; transform = merge one INTO the other so the join disappears. Collage = any stuck materials; photomontage = specifically cut photographs.

Self-quiz — cover the answers, write, then check

  1. What does juxtaposition mean? — Two unrelated things placed side by side.
  2. Name two ways to trace a collage. — Scribble pencil on the back, or trace at a window.
  3. What three things do you write when you annotate an image? — What I see, what I like (with a reason), a media idea.

Common mark-losers

Leaving a gap between two images (not transformed) · reacting with no reason ("I like it" — say why) · forgetting the media idea.

Memory hooks

"Juxtapose = pose them together." · "Transform = one becomes part of the other." · "Eye + anything = surreal."
