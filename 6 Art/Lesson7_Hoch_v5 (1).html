🔗 The shared word

JUXTAPOSITION drives both. Dalí juxtaposes painted objects; Höch juxtaposes cut-out photos. The clash is the point.

📐 What is PHOTOMONTAGE?

Photomontage = cutting photographs out of printed media (magazines, newspapers, adverts) and gluing them together to make a brand-new image. Höch helped invent it as part of Dada — a wild anti-art movement born from disgust at the First World War.

Three things to mention about a Höch photomontage: What's cut & clashed (juxtaposition) · It's made of mass media · Its social message

Hit all three and you're analysing, not just describing.

Don't write: "It's just a messy collage"  or  "She painted a weird face"

Write: "Höch juxtaposed cut-up photos from mass media to question how society constructs the image of women."

Höch did NOT paint — that's the key difference from Dalí. Her material (real printed photos) is part of the meaning: she turns the media's own images against it.

In one sentence each: how is Höch DIFFERENT from Dalí? Say it to your partner, then swap and improve each other's sentence.

Top tip: the strongest sentences use the words "cut", "photomontage" and "juxtaposition".

Exit Ticket

Instructions: Answer independently. No notes.

1) What nationality was Hannah Höch?

German.

2) What is photomontage?

Cutting up photos and gluing them together to make a new image.

3) What does juxtaposition mean?

Placing two unrelated things side by side for contrast.

1) How is Höch's way of working different from Dalí's?

Höch CUT and glued real photos from mass media (photomontage); Dalí PAINTED dreamscapes by hand from his imagination.

2) What was the "New Woman", and why did Höch explore it?

The 1920s ideal of a modern, independent woman. Höch questioned how media constructed and sold that image.

3) What was Dada?

A radical anti-art movement born from disgust at the First World War.

1) Why is juxtaposition central to BOTH Dalí and Höch, despite their different methods?

Both clash unrelated images to jolt the viewer — Dalí to reveal the subconscious through painted dream-objects; Höch to expose how society/media assembles artificial images. The clash carries the meaning in each case.

2) Why might cut-up REAL photos critique society more directly than a painting?

The material IS the target — Höch turns the media's own photographs against it, so the critique feels factual and immediate rather than imagined. Painting invents; photomontage re-uses real evidence.

3) Dada vs Surrealism — how do they relate?

Dada (Höch) came first — anti-art, chaotic, anti-war, mocking society. Surrealism (Dalí) grew partly out of Dada but focused inward on dreams and the subconscious. Both reject ordinary logic and love juxtaposition.

Höch Fact Sheet — note 5 facts for your research page

Hannah Höch (1889–1978) was a German artist and one of the inventors of photomontage. She was born on 1 November 1889 in Gotha, Germany, and studied applied arts and graphic design in Berlin. From 1916 she worked at the Ullstein publishing house, designing embroidery, knitting and crochet patterns for women's magazines — surrounding her with printed photographs and mass-media imagery.

She became the only woman in the Berlin Dada group, a radical anti-art movement that grew out of disgust at the First World War. Höch is famous for cutting up photographs from newspapers, magazines and adverts and reassembling them into strange new images — a technique called photomontage. Her most famous work, "Cut with the Kitchen Knife…" (1919), is a chaotic collage attacking German politics and society.

Much of her work explored the role of women, beauty standards and the idea of the "New Woman" in 1920s Germany. The Nazis branded her work "degenerate" and she lived quietly through the Second World War, continuing to make art in private. She died in Berlin in 1978, aged 88 — now remembered as a pioneer of collage.
