Collage = any stuck-down materials; photomontage = specifically cut-up photographs.

A fact can be checked/proved; an opinion is what someone thinks.

1) Define photomontage AND name what makes it different from painting.

Cutting and reassembling real photographs; it re-uses real media images rather than inventing by hand.

2) Why is juxtaposition the engine of a photomontage?

The clash between unrelated pieces is what creates the new meaning/message.

🖍️ Highlighted: 0

Hannah Höch was born on 1 November 1889 in Gotha, Germany. She liked drawing as a child. She studied applied arts and graphic design in Berlin.

From 1916 she worked at the Ullstein publishing house, designing patterns for women's magazines — so she was surrounded by printed photos all day. The office was in the centre of Berlin. She became the ONLY woman in the Berlin Dada group — a radical anti-art movement born from disgust at the First World War.

Höch helped invent photomontage: cutting photographs from magazines, newspapers and adverts, then reassembling them into strange new images. She used ordinary scissors and glue. Her most famous work, "Cut with the Kitchen Knife…" (1919), is a chaotic collage attacking German politics and society.

Many people saw her work in galleries. Much of her art questioned beauty standards and the "New Woman" ideal sold by 1920s magazines; later the Nazis branded her work "degenerate", and she kept making art quietly in private until her death in Berlin in 1978. She is still talked about today.

I Do: Building a Fact File 📇

Key Idea: A fact file isn't a list of copied sentences — it's facts SORTED under headings, written in YOUR OWN WORDS.
Step 0 of 4

Höch Bio Sheet — mine it for facts, then write in YOUR OWN WORDS

Hannah Höch was born on 1 November 1889 in Gotha, Germany. She studied applied arts and graphic design in Berlin. From 1916 she worked at the Ullstein publishing house, designing embroidery and sewing patterns for women's magazines — surrounding her with printed photographs and mass-media imagery every day.

She became the only woman in the Berlin Dada group, a radical anti-art movement that grew out of disgust at the First World War. Höch helped invent photomontage: cutting photographs from newspapers, magazines and adverts and reassembling them into strange new images. Her most famous work, "Cut with the Kitchen Knife…" (1919), is a chaotic collage attacking German politics and society.

Much of her work questioned beauty standards and the idea of the "New Woman" sold by 1920s magazines. The Nazis branded her work "degenerate" and she lived quietly through the Second World War, continuing to make art in private. She died in Berlin in 1978, aged 88 — remembered as a pioneer of collage.
