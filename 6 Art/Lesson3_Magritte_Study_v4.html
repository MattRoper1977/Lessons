Opposite on the wheel, e.g. Red↔Green — create strong contrast.

Name two Magritte techniques and explain one.

e.g. Blending — smooth tonal transitions. Scale Change — objects impossibly large.

3. Style (Recall)

Why does Magritte's work look realistic despite impossible scenes?

He used realistic painting techniques (blending, detail) on impossible subjects.

4. Lead-in: Drawing

What's the difference between "accuracy" and "detail"?

Accuracy = correct proportions/placement. Detail = fine features like highlights.

1. Comparison (Recall)

How does Magritte use juxtaposition differently from Dalí?

Dalí creates dreamscapes; Magritte places ordinary objects in extraordinary contexts.

2. Style (Recall)

Describe Magritte's painting style — what makes it distinctive?

Smooth, realistic oil paint with precise tonal blending and meticulous detail.

3. Meaning (Recall)

Why did Magritte call his pipe painting "The Treachery of Images"?

A painting of a pipe is not a pipe — it's a representation. He challenged the relationship between objects and images.

4. Lead-in: Annotation

What should annotations include beyond just describing what you see?

Techniques, visual elements, meaning, personal response, connections to own work.

I Do

I Do: How to Grid Up Your Study

Key Idea: Gridding helps you draw accurately by breaking an image into small, manageable squares.

6 Steps to Grid

1️⃣
Choose your Magritte image from the resource sheet.
↓
2️⃣
Grid the image — 2cm × 2cm squares over the printed image.
↓
3️⃣
Grid your page — same number of squares, bigger to scale up.
↓
4️⃣
Draw box by box — outlines first, one square at a time.
↓
5️⃣
Erase the grid — gently rub out grid lines.
↓
6️⃣
Add tone with paint — light colours first, then darker shadows.

Animated Gridding Demo

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Don't press too hard — light lines are easier to erase. Don't skip squares — work systematically.

We Do: Put the Steps in Order

Instructions: Tap the steps in the correct order (1st → 6th).
Steps placed: 0/6
Think about it like this: You can't paint before you've drawn. You can't draw before you've gridded. You can't grid both until you've picked an image. The grid should come OFF before you add paint (so paint goes over clean lines).
I Do

I Do: How to Annotate Your Study

Key Idea: Annotate your artwork with arrows pointing to specific areas. Cover these 4 areas:

1. Describe

"In this section I can see…" — What is literally there?

2. Techniques

"The artist has used [blending/layering] to create…" — How was it made?

3. Visual Elements

"The visual elements are [line/tone/colour/shape]…" — What principles are used?

4. Opinion & Meaning

"I think this means… It reminds me of… I could use this by…" — What does it mean to you?

Top Tip: Good annotations are specific — say which colours, where, and why they work.

Don't write: "It looks nice"  or  "He liked drawing eyes"

Write: "I think this represents perception because the sky inside the eye suggests…"

An opinion in art annotation is NEVER just "it looks nice" — it must link to meaning with a "because…" reason.

The 4-area framework that makes a good annotation: Describe · Techniques · Visual Elements · Opinion linked to meaning

A pupil who hits all 4 areas with full sentences earns the highest mark band. Skipping "Opinion" or only using one-word answers caps the grade.

"The False Mirror" (1928)

Sentence starters: "I can see…" | "The artist used _____ to create…" | "The visual elements are…" | "I think this means _____ because…"
👥 Check your work: Click below to reveal model answers one at a time — compare to your own annotations and see what you could add.
1. Describe: "I can see a close-up of a single eye. The iris contains a blue sky with fluffy white clouds instead of a normal coloured iris. The pupil is a deep black circle at the centre, with the whites of the eye clearly visible around the edge."
2. Techniques: "Magritte has used smooth oil paint blending to create seamless tonal transitions between the sky and clouds. There are no visible brushstrokes, giving the painting a photographic quality that tricks your eye."
3. Visual Elements: "The main visual elements are colour (blue sky, white clouds, black pupil), tone (light iris against dark pupil), shape (concentric circles) and texture (smooth, glossy, realistic)."
4. Opinion & Meaning: "I think this represents how our eyes don't just SEE reality — they shape it with imagination and memory. The sky in the eye suggests we project our thoughts onto what we see. It makes me feel small, like the world is bigger than what I think I'm looking at."
Spot the Mistake

🔍 Spot the Mistake — Student Annotation

Instructions: A student annotated "The False Mirror" like this. Find the 3 mistakes!
"In this picture I can see a painting of an eye. Magritte has used rough textured brushstrokes so you can see exactly how he made it. The visual elements are pattern and form. I think it means he liked drawing eyes."

Here are the 3 mistakes:

  • Mistake 1: "Rough textured brushstrokes" — Magritte's painting is smooth with no visible brushstrokes. That's his signature style.
  • Mistake 2: "Pattern and form" — these aren't the main elements. The big ones here are colour, tone and shape.
  • Mistake 3: "He liked drawing eyes" — this isn't a meaning, it's a guess. A proper opinion links to what the artwork might represent — e.g. perception, seeing, imagination.
👥 Turn to your partner: Rewrite the "Opinion" sentence properly — what might the eye with a sky inside it actually represent?
