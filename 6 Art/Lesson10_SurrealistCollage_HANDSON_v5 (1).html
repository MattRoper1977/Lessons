Eyes on me — I'll make a surreal eye in under two minutes. Watch the two moves, then watch the ORDER I work in: cut → overlap → trace → three short lines.

Place two unrelated things together. Your brain links them and a new meaning appears. An eye next to a clock.

Let one object become part of the other so the join disappears. The clock IS the iris. Stronger and more surreal.

Make · Phase 1

Make: Quick Eye Experiments 🧪✂️

Warm up your scissors. No neatness, no writing yet — just play. Cut an eye, cut some objects, overlap them. Make as many quick surreal eyes as you can. There are no wrong answers.

🎯 How to play

1. Grab an eye — from the image bank or a magazine face.

2. Cut 1–2 objects — anything that catches your eye.

3. Overlap them onto the eye so one becomes part of it. Don't glue yet — just try it.

4. Like it? Keep it. Don't? Try another. Bin nothing.

💡 Stuck? Fuse the eye with…

⏰ clock🌊 wave🦋 butterfly🔑 key🌸 flower⚙️ gear🐦 bird💡 bulb
Ask yourself: what does this clash make me feel or think?
⭐ Aim for 3 or more quick eyes. Then STAR your favourite — that one becomes the starting point for your best build next.
Speed beats neatness here. The best surreal ideas usually come from a combination that surprised even you.
Make · Phase 2

Make: Your Best Surreal Eye 🏆👁️

Pick your starred experiment (or start fresh). Now make ONE strong eye properly: transform it, glue it, trace it, then add three short lines.
15:00

📋 Build your eye

1. Transform & glue: overlap your images so one becomes part of the eye — no gaps (a clock that IS the iris). Glue it down.

2. Trace: scribble-back (pencil on the back, lay it on your page, draw over it) OR tape it to a window and trace.

3. Three short lines beside it — use the starters →

🎯 Aim for

☐ One transformed eye (no gaps)
☐ Glued + traced neatly
☐ Three lines: see · like · media
☐ A reason ("because…") in your LIKE line
