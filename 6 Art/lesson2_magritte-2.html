Placing unexpected objects together, e.g. a fish with human legs.

4. Lead-in: Magritte

What's the difference between painting something "realistic" and something "surreal"?

Realistic = copies real life. Surreal = changes/distorts reality.

1. Unconscious (Recall)

Explain the link between Surrealism and the unconscious mind.

Artists explored the unconscious to reveal hidden thoughts, dreams and emotions through art.

2. Compare (Recall)

Compare two Surrealist artists — how do their styles differ?

3. Techniques (Recall)

Describe all four Surrealist techniques with examples.

Juxtaposition (unexpected objects), Scale Change (wrong size), Transformation (morphing), Dislocation (strange places).

4. Lead-in: Magritte

Can completely realistic artwork still be Surreal? Explain.

Yes — Magritte's work looks realistic but shows impossible scenes, which makes it even more unsettling.

I Do

I Do: Magritte's Signature Techniques

Key Idea: Magritte's paintings look incredibly realistic but the scenes are impossible. That contrast is what makes his work powerful.

📏 Scale Change

Objects become impossibly large — a giant apple fills an entire room, leaving no space for anything else.

🎭 Hidden Identity

Faces are constantly hidden or obscured — behind apples, cloths, or turned away. The bowler hat man never shows his face.

👁️ Visual Illusion

Paintings within paintings — windows that merge with the view. What's real? What's the painting?

Aspire: Why might Magritte have called his pipe painting "The Treachery of Images"? Because the painting of a pipe is NOT a pipe — it's a representation. He wanted viewers to question the gap between an object and its image.

Spot the Mistake

🔍 Spot the Mistake — Student Analysis

Instructions: A student wrote this about Magritte's "The Human Condition". Find the 3 mistakes!
"In this painting I can see an easel with a painting on it. Magritte has used bright, bold primary colours like red and yellow. The painting on the easel is completely different from the view behind it. Magritte was a French artist who wanted people to feel happy."

Here are the 3 mistakes:

  • Mistake 1: "Bright, bold primary colours" — Magritte used naturalistic, muted colours (blues, greens, browns). Bold primaries are more Miró's style!
  • Mistake 2: "Completely different from the view behind" — actually the painting perfectly merges with the view. That's the whole point! It's a visual illusion.
  • Mistake 3: "French artist" — Magritte was Belgian. And he wanted viewers to question reality, not feel happy.
👥 Turn to your partner: Which was the biggest mistake? Explain to your partner what a correct version should say.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket

Instructions: Answer independently. No notes.

1) What nationality was Magritte?

Belgian.

2) Name one object that appears often in his paintings.

Bowler hat (or green apple).

3) What did Magritte want viewers to do?

Question what they see / question reality.

1) Describe one technique Magritte used and give an example.

e.g. Scale Change — a giant apple fills an entire room, making a normal object feel strange and impossible.

2) Why do Magritte's paintings feel unsettling even though they look realistic?

Because the scenes are impossible — the realistic style makes impossible things look like they could be real, which is disturbing.

3) What was "The Treachery of Images" about?

A painting of a pipe with "This is not a pipe" — challenging the idea that a painting of something IS that thing.

1) Compare Magritte's approach to Surrealism with Dalí's.

Magritte used realistic painting of everyday objects in impossible contexts. Dalí created entire dreamscape worlds with melting/distorted forms. Both explored the unconscious but Magritte is more subtle — the strangeness comes from ordinary things being wrong.

2) Explain how Magritte's use of "hidden identity" connects to Surrealism's goals.

By hiding faces, Magritte removes identity and makes viewers uncomfortable — we expect to see faces but can't. This links to the Surrealist goal of accessing the unconscious and creating feelings of unease and mystery.

3) Why is "The Human Condition" considered a masterpiece of visual illusion?

The painting on the easel perfectly merges with the landscape behind it — you can't tell where "reality" ends and "art" begins. It forces the viewer to question whether any painting (or any perception) is truly "real".
