I Do: Magritte vs Miró — Two Surrealists, Two Styles

Key Idea: Both are Surrealists — but their art looks completely different. There's no single "right way" to do Surrealism!

🎩 René Magritte

Style: Hyper-realistic
Colours: Naturalistic, muted
Shapes: Recognisable objects
Technique: Smooth blending, fine detail
Message: Questions reality through impossible scenes

🎨 Joan Miró

Style: Abstract, symbolic, childlike
Colours: Bold primaries — red, blue, yellow, black
Shapes: Organic blobs, stars, crescents
Technique: Flat colour, bold lines, spontaneous
Message: Pure imagination through symbols

📝 New Keyword — Lithography

A printing process using a flat stone or metal plate. Greasy areas hold ink; wet areas repel it. Miró used it for bold, graphic prints.

Aspire: Why might simple shapes be MORE powerful than realistic paintings in some situations? Simple shapes are universal — everyone can connect with them. They bypass the brain's need to "recognise" and go straight to emotion. A child's drawing can be more emotionally raw than a photorealistic painting.

Don't write: "Miró is just like Magritte"  or  "Miró's art is childish and easy"

Write: "Miró's abstract symbols communicate emotion directly, where Magritte's realism creates unsettling impossible scenes — opposite approaches to the same goal."

Both Magritte and Miró are Surrealists — but they are opposites in style. And Miró's "childlike" look is a deliberate adult choice built on years of realistic training.

The 3 things to mention when comparing artists: Style · Colours · Meaning / Message

A pupil who covers all 3 with full sentences earns the highest mark band. Skipping "Meaning" or only naming colours caps the grade.

Spot the Mistake

🔍 Spot the Mistake — Student Answer

Instructions: A student wrote this about Joan Miró. Find the 3 mistakes!
"Joan Miró was a French artist born in the 1800s. He painted realistic pictures of cities using lots of dark grey and brown colours. His style is very similar to Magritte — they both paint things that look exactly like real life."

Here are the 3 mistakes:

  • Mistake 1: "French artist" — Miró was Spanish, born in Barcelona in 1893 (late 1800s is a stretch — he made his name in the 1900s).
  • Mistake 2: "Realistic pictures of cities" in "dark greys and browns" — completely wrong. Miró painted abstract, symbolic compositions using bold primary colours (red, blue, yellow, black).
  • Mistake 3: "Very similar to Magritte" — they are opposites! Magritte = hyper-realistic, recognisable objects. Miró = abstract, symbolic shapes.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket

Instructions: Answer independently. No notes.

1) What nationality was Miró?

Spanish.

2) Miró's style is best described as…

Abstract and symbolic.

3) What is lithography?

A printing process using stone or metal plates.

1) Name three ways Miró's style differs from Magritte's.

Miró: abstract/symbolic, bold primaries, organic shapes. Magritte: realistic, muted naturalistic colours, recognisable objects.

2) What kinds of symbols did Miró use? Give examples.

Stars = dreams, eyes = seeing, birds = freedom, moons = night/mystery.

3) Explain how lithography works.

An image is drawn on stone/metal with greasy material. The plate is wetted — greasy areas hold ink, wet areas repel it. Paper is pressed onto the plate to create a print.

1) Compare how Magritte and Miró each explored the unconscious mind through their art.

Magritte used realistic painting to show impossible scenes — subtle strangeness. Miró used abstract symbols and bold colours to access pure imagination — direct emotional expression bypassing rational thought.

2) Why might Miró's "childlike" style actually require MORE skill than realistic painting?

Miró trained as a realistic painter first. His "simple" style was a deliberate choice — distilling complex ideas into essential symbols requires deep understanding. As he said, he wanted to paint "as a child would, but with adult intelligence".

3) Argue whether abstract Surrealism or realistic Surrealism is more effective at exploring the unconscious.

Both valid — realistic creates cognitive dissonance (looks real but isn't), abstract bypasses conscious thought entirely. The "best" approach depends on what the artist wants viewers to feel.
