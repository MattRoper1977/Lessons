I Do

I Do: What is Surrealism?

Key Idea: Surrealism was an art movement that wanted to unlock the power of dreams and the unconscious mind.

📖 Origins

Surrealism began in the 1920s in Europe. It was started by a French writer called André Breton, who published the Surrealist Manifesto in 1924.

🧠 The Big Idea

Surrealist artists wanted to unlock the unconscious mind — the part of your brain you're not fully aware of. They explored dreams, deep imagination, and hidden emotions.

Think-Pair-Share: Have you ever had a dream that felt completely real — but was really strange? What made it weird? Surrealists tried to capture that exact feeling.

Spot the Mistake

🔍 Spot the Mistake — Student Answer

Instructions: A Year 10 student wrote this answer. Find the mistakes — there are 3!
"Surrealism was an art movement that started in the 1820s in America. It was started by Salvador Dalí who painted realistic pictures of landscapes. Surrealist artists wanted to paint things exactly as they are in real life."

Here are the 3 mistakes:

  • Mistake 1: "1820s in America" — it started in the 1920s in Europe (Paris, France).
  • Mistake 2: "Salvador Dalí started it" — it was founded by French writer André Breton. Dalí was a famous Surrealist but didn't start the movement.
  • Mistake 3: "Paint things exactly as they are" — this is the OPPOSITE of Surrealism. Surrealists painted dreams, imagination, and impossible scenes.

I Do

I Do: Meet the Surrealists

Key Idea: Four key artists — each with a completely different approach to Surrealism.

🎨 Salvador Dalí (Spanish)

Painted hyper-realistic dreamscapes — melting clocks, strange creatures, impossible desert landscapes. Famous for "The Persistence of Memory".

🎩 René Magritte (Belgian)

Painted everyday objects in impossible situations. Used bowler hats, hidden faces, and visual tricks. His work looks realistic but the scenes can't exist.

🌺 Frida Kahlo (Mexican)

Created deeply personal self-portraits filled with symbolic meaning — exploring identity, pain, and Mexican culture through Surrealist imagery.

✂️ Hannah Höch (German)

Pioneer of photomontage — cutting and combining photographs and magazine images to create strange, provocative new compositions.

Exit Ticket

Instructions: Answer independently. No notes.

1) What does "surréalisme" mean?

Beyond reality.

2) Name two Surrealist artists.

Any two: Dalí, Magritte, Kahlo, Höch.

3) Name one Surrealist technique.

Any one: Juxtaposition, Scale Change, Transformation, Dislocation.

1) Define Surrealism in your own words.

An art movement from the 1920s that explored dreams and the unconscious mind, meaning "beyond reality".

2) Name two Surrealist techniques and explain one.

e.g. Juxtaposition — placing unexpected objects together, like a fish with human legs. Scale Change — making objects the wrong size.

3) Which artist is famous for melting clocks? What nationality were they?

Salvador Dalí — Spanish.

1) Explain the link between Surrealism and the unconscious mind.

Surrealist artists wanted to bypass rational thought and access the unconscious — the part of the mind containing dreams, hidden emotions, and imagination. They used techniques like automatism and dream imagery to create art that felt irrational and surprising.

2) Compare the styles of two Surrealist artists.

e.g. Dalí used hyper-realistic painting to show dreamlike impossible scenes, while Höch used photomontage — cutting and combining photos to create strange collages. Both explored the unconscious but through completely different media and techniques.

3) Why might Surrealism still be relevant to artists today?

Surrealism showed that art doesn't have to copy reality — it can explore emotions, identity, and imagination. Modern artists, designers, filmmakers, and advertisers still use Surrealist techniques to surprise, challenge, and emotionally engage their audiences.
