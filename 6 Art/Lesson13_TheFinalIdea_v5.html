Retrieval Quiz

Arrival Task — Retrieval Quiz (Do Now)

Answer from memory. Surrealists and the language of ideas.

1) What is Surrealism about?

Dreams and the unconscious — strange, dreamlike images.

2) Which is a Surrealist artist we've studied?

René Magritte, Salvador Dalí or Hannah Höch.

3) What is a thumbnail sketch?

A tiny, rough sketch used to test an idea quickly.

4) What is an annotation?

Short notes explaining what you did and why.

1) What does juxtaposition mean?

Placing unrelated things together for a strange, dreamlike effect.

2) Hannah Höch built her images using…

Photomontage — cutting and combining photographs.

3) What is Dalí famous for melting?

Clocks (The Persistence of Memory).

4) What does composition mean?

How the parts of an image are arranged on the page.

1) Copying an artist vs being influenced by one?

Copying repeats their image; influence borrows their technique or idea for YOUR image.

2) To develop an idea means to…

To change and improve it — new composition, scale, setting or media, not a neater copy.

3) Why make several ideas before one final piece?

To test, compare and refine — the best final pieces come from choosing, not guessing.

4) How could scale make an image surreal?

Making something impossibly big or small — a room-sized eye, a person-sized moth.

I Do

I Do: Develop, Don't Copy 🔁

Four moves turn a thumbnail into a final idea. Reveal them in order.
Step 0 of 4every move gets annotated

1️⃣ Start from the spark

Redraw your chosen thumbnail bigger — half a page minimum.

2️⃣ Change ONE big thing

Composition, scale, setting or viewpoint. Development must be visible.

3️⃣ Add artist influence

Borrow a technique or idea from Magritte, Dalí or Höch — make it obvious.

4️⃣ Annotate the WHY

Label 3 choices: "I changed… I borrowed… I placed… because…"

A final idea is my thumbnail drawn neater — it is my thumbnail developed and changed. If nothing changed, nothing developed.

We Do

We Do: Borrow From the Masters 🎨

Influence must be visible. Tap each artist to reveal exactly HOW to borrow from them — then tell your partner which one your final idea will use.
🎩

René Magritte

The quiet trickster

Borrow: one impossible swap in a believable scene. Paint your eye realistically — but replace the iris with sky, sea or fire. Flat, calm, clean edges.
🌀

Salvador Dalí

The dream engineer

Borrow: melting, stretching, distortion. Let your eye droop over a ledge like his clocks, or stretch its reflection into a dream landscape with long shadows.
✂️

Hannah Höch

The photomontage pioneer

Borrow: cut-and-fuse. Combine your drawn eye with cut or collaged fragments — magazine textures, printed skies — so drawing and photo collide.

Tell your partner: which artist is YOUR final idea borrowing from, and what exactly will you take?

Naming the borrow now = an easy annotation later.

Key distinctions

Copying repeats an artist's image; influence borrows their thinking for yours. Initial idea = fast test; final idea = developed, composed, annotated.

Self-quiz — cover the answers, write, then check

  1. The four development moves? — Redraw bigger, change one big thing, add influence, annotate why.
  2. What did Magritte swap in The False Mirror? — The iris for a cloudy sky.
  3. What does composition mean? — How parts of an image are arranged.

Common mark-losers

Final idea identical to the thumbnail (no development) · influence claimed but invisible · annotations that describe ("this is an eye") instead of explain ("I placed it off-centre because…").

Memory hooks

"Change one BIG thing." · "Borrow the trick, not the picture." · "Every arrow needs a because."

Scaffolding – Foundation

Order: redraw big → change ONE thing → add your borrow → 3 labels.

Change ideas: look UP at it · make it giant · put it somewhere strange.

Label starter: "I changed ______ because ______."

Scaffolding – Middle

Your roulette winner IS your composition — use it, don't default to centre-of-page.

Influence check: could a stranger name your artist just by looking? If not, push the borrow harder.

Annotations explain, never describe: every arrow ends in "because".

Scaffolding – Higher

Combine two borrows deliberately (e.g. Magritte's swap + Dalí's shadow) and say why they work together.

Add a small rejected version and annotate why the final idea beats it — visible decision-making.

Think ahead: which media will this idea NEED? Note your prediction for the Test Lab.
