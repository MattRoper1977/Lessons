## A-P149 · RATIFIED — the access route stands

**Ruled: keeping the hands-only video station was the right call. Recorded here as ratified.**

§0.2 of the original ruling said "align pack to decks: carousel/paired", naming two formats
where the pack had three. PROP-1 renamed rather than deleted the third, and flagged it for
an explicit word rather than letting a vocabulary alignment quietly remove provision. That
judgement is now ratified.

What stands, and why it matters:

- The pack's Channel A/B/C vocabulary is the decks' vocabulary — **paired session ·
  carousel station · hands-only video station** (22 strings across the pack and the GROW
  hub; 0 occurrences of "channel").
- **Channel C was renamed, not deleted.** It is the access route for a pupil who cannot
  present live — "you never have to speak to the group", "no face". Deleting it would have
  removed provision the House Standard protects, on a row whose stated job was vocabulary.

This closes the question DECISIONS left open ("If the owner wants only two formats, that is
one further edit and should be ruled explicitly"). The owner wants three. **No further edit
is owed on A-P149.**

## A-P87 · the unabridged clause won, and the separator split closed with it

DECISIONS put both readings on the table: the ranked row's regex, which keeps the card
heading and inserts a separator, and the unabridged clause in `_eca1/PROPOSED_A.md` P87,
which drops the heading. PROP-1 applied the keeping form for consistency and named the cost
— `3) Point to it: Point to safety gear you have seen.` on supported-tier print text.

The owner picked the unabridged clause. The heading is gone:

```
1) 1. Say it Name one thing you are good at.    (fused, 22 decks, 66 lines)
1) Say it: Name one thing you are good at.      (kept, 7 decks, 21 lines)
1) Say it — Name one thing you are good at.     (kept, em-dash, 1 deck, 3 lines)
->  1) Name one thing you are good at.
```

Two things this settles beyond the row:

1. **The separator split is closed rather than propagated.** DECISIONS listed `:` vs `—`
   across one chassis string as an unresolved residual. Both variants collapse into the one
   form, so there is no split left to propagate.
2. **Scope was the row's own** — "Regex over all 30 LAUNCH decks". 30 decks carry the
   pattern, 90 supported print-arrival items, every one inside `#print-arrival`. Fixing
   only the 8 the pass had reached would have left 22 decks garbled and the estate
   inconsistent with itself, which is the defect class this work exists to close.

**Print parity both ways:** the substitution is scoped to `#print-arrival`; all 30 decks
keep their on-screen arrival card headings (`<h3>1. Say it</h3>`) untouched. The heading was
never the defect on screen — only its concatenation into the printed line. The result is the
format standard and stretch print items already used, which the row itself cited as proof
of intent.
