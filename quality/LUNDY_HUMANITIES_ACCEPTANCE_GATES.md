which more of it may be built. It governs the toolkit at

## Gate H1 — One account of closure, and Humanities does not get its own

BUILD keeps the ring; GROW and LAUNCH keep the written line. The humanities toolkit adds
**interpretation** — how an adult reads what a pupil produced — and adds no closure mechanism of
any kind. No adult initial, receipt, sign-off, "seen by", second next step, queue, tally, tracker,
upload register or closure count enters humanities practice through this material.

The toolkit's own prohibitions already say this in nineteen places. They are prohibitions, and
they stay prohibitions; none of them is a design waiting to be built.

## Gate H2 — One account of interpretation, and it is not this folder's

`LundyLoop/5_staff_training/Reading_the_Response_Card.html` is the **general instrument** for
reading a pupil response. The humanities branches are its disciplinary dialect — a source
response may be secure, may confuse observation with inference, may carry the misconception that
bias makes a source useless, may be blocked by source density, or may rest on a source with
missing provenance.

**A humanities page that uses the branches must point at the card.** It must never read as a
free-standing rival account. Two vocabularies for one instrument is the mis-training hazard that
already cost this estate a pass (**R-H08**), and it is cheaper to prevent than to reconcile —
see `HANDOVER.md` queue 18, which is still open on exactly this problem in a neighbouring domain.

## Gate H3 — Sources carry their provenance, or they do not get used

Humanities is the one subject here whose raw material is other people's work. The standing rule:
**an unattributed source is not a source, it is a picture.** Provenance, licence and credit
travel with the item, per `quality/LUNDY_HUMANITIES_SOURCE_GATE.md`, which owns this in detail.

A **constructed** source — one written or adapted for teaching — must say so on its face. A pupil
who is taught to interrogate provenance, and is then handed an unlabelled fabrication, has been
taught the opposite of the lesson. The wording standard is the toolkit's
`CONSTRUCTED_SOURCE_LABEL_STANDARD.md`; the proposed label diffs for the live decks are held for
Matt's read on the SEMH-2 proposal set, and are **not applied by this pass**.

## Gate H4 — No display artefact speaks in a voice that is not its owner's

Derived, not enumerated: test every artefact for **first-person fill-in text that an adult would
display, print or read aloud**. Where it exists, the artefact says whose voice it is.

The one member found in this pack is `INFLUENCE_REPORT_BACK_GUIDE.html`, and its frames are the
**adult's** voice reporting a decision back — *"We could not do __, but your evidence led us
to __."* It landed with that stated on its face, and with the usage rule already recorded for the
influence board at `_close/OPEN_ITEMS.md` #30:

> **A "could not" line never appears without its Because / boundary filled.**

A bare refusal record is the anti-Lundy artefact: it converts "your voice changed something, or
here is exactly why it could not" into a wall of no. The rule is the board's, not this pass's —
this file defers to it rather than minting a second copy.
