## The boundary, in one sentence

Unchanged, and quoted from the owning record rather than reworded: **a disclosure leaves the
ordinary evidence workflow entirely.** It goes to the DSL by the school's safeguarding route, and
the record lives in the safeguarding system — never in a source register, a moderation sample, an
influence board, a report-back log or a pupil's written account.

The wording that sits beside a free-text field is **already ruled and is used verbatim** from
`DATA_GOVERNANCE.md`:

> **Not for disclosures.** If a pupil tells you something that worries you, it goes to the DSL by
> the school's safeguarding route — never into this box.

## Why humanities needs its own page at all

Science invites disclosure through the body — food, health, digestion. **Humanities invites it
through the family.** Chronology, migration, local history, identity, conflict, empire and
"where you are from" are the subject's ordinary content, and for a child in an SEMH setting they
are rarely neutral ground. A looked-after child asked to bring in their family's story is being
asked a question the curriculum has no right to require an answer to.

This is not an argument against the content. It is an argument for the routes below always
existing, and for the alternative never being the visibly lesser option.

## Humanities-specific: where a disclosure actually turns up

Named because these are the moments the general rule is hardest to apply, not because they are
new rules.

| moment | the risk | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| **A family-history or "your own story" route** | care history, estrangement, immigration status, bereavement surface as *task content* | **an equally-weighted non-personal route always exists and is offered first-class, not as a fallback**; a pupil never has to disclose in order to have something to write about |
| **Local and migration history** | a pupil's own street, community or arrival story becomes the worked example | use the approved local case studies; a pupil volunteering their own is welcome, never solicited, and never becomes the class exemplar without consent |
| **Oral history and interviewing a relative** | homework that requires access to a willing adult who may not exist | the task must be completable without a relative; no pupil explains why they cannot do it |
| **The written account (GROW W7) and the source assessment (LAUNCH W7)** | assessed writing pulls personal material into a graded artefact | assessed conditions are the firewall — see `ASSESSMENT_CONDITIONS_FIREWALL.md` in the toolkit; a disclosure inside assessed writing still leaves the workflow entirely |
| **Week 7 sensitive content** | conflict, loss, persecution, empire landing on a specific child | `WEEK7_PROTECTION_AND_AFTERCARE.md` governs the before/after; aftercare is planned before the lesson, not improvised after it |
| **Display and the influence board** | pupil material on a wall outlives the lesson and the consent | `REPRESENTATIVE_WORK_AND_CONSENT.html`; the board records **adult decisions**, not pupil lines (`_close/OPEN_ITEMS.md` #30) |
| **A source register entry** | a pupil-supplied family photograph or document acquires a provenance row | it is someone's private material, not a classroom source; consent and return are the default, retention is not |

## What no humanities evidence artefact may contain

- a disclosure, a safeguarding narrative, or a wellbeing check-in;
- care status, placement history, immigration status, or reason-for-placement;
- a family member's personal data captured through a pupil task;
- a pupil's full name where an approved learner reference serves;
- **initials treated as anonymous** — in a small SEMH setting, initials plus a class plus a date
  can identify a child. **Pseudonymisation is not anonymisation** (`DATA_GOVERNANCE.md`).

## What this file does not decide

- the approved platform, folder structure, learner-reference convention or filename pattern;
- retention and deletion schedules — proposed defaults live in `DATA_GOVERNANCE.md` and are
  **PENDING-LOCAL-APPROVAL (DPO/SLT)**;
- photo, video and display permission rules and the device estate;
- whether any humanities evidence route is approved at all. **No upload workflow is authorised by
  this file**, and the tutor-time routine that would have created one is deferred
  (`_close/OPEN_ITEMS.md` #24).

## Standing rule

**A disclosure copied into an evidence export is a safeguarding failure wearing an evidence
costume** (`DATA_GOVERNANCE.md`). In humanities the costume is a better fit than anywhere else in
the estate, because a family story genuinely *is* historical source material. That is precisely
why the boundary is written as *leaves the workflow entirely* rather than *is handled carefully*.
