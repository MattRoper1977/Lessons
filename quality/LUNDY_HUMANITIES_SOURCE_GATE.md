## Gate S1 — An unattributed source is not a source, it is a picture

Every source used with pupils carries, at minimum: **what it is, who made it, when, where it came
from, and what may be done with it.** A source with a blank provenance row has not been cleared;
it has been forgotten. The register is the instrument, and an empty row is a *stop*, not a
formatting problem.

This is not administrative. Provenance **is** the humanities content — a pupil is being taught to
ask exactly the questions the register asks. A source pack that cannot answer them models the
opposite of the discipline it is teaching.

## Gate S2 — A constructed source says so, on its face

A source written, adapted, simplified, translated or composited for teaching is **constructed**,
and is labelled as constructed where the pupil meets it — not in a teacher note, not in a
footnote on a different page.

A pupil taught to interrogate provenance and then handed an unlabelled fabrication has been
taught that provenance is a classroom ritual rather than a real check. The wording standard is
`CONSTRUCTED_SOURCE_LABEL_STANDARD.md`.

**Status of the live decks:** proposed constructed-source label wording for the humanities decks
is held for Matt's read on the SEMH-2 proposal set (PR #45, `_semh2/proposed_constructed_source_labels.diff`)
and is **applied nowhere**. This pass did not apply it and did not restate it.

## Gate S3 — Rights are per item, never per collection

A collection being "open" or "public domain" is a statement about the collection, not a licence
for the item you took from it. Wikimedia, Wellcome, national and local archives all host material
under mixed terms, and a museum's own photograph of an out-of-copyright object may itself be
restricted.

**Per item: licence named, attribution string recorded, restrictions noted.** Where any of the
three is unknown, the item is a **wishlist entry, not a source** — it may be sought, and it may
not be used. Nothing enters a pupil-facing pack on an assumption inherited from its neighbours.

## Gate S4 — Ordnance Survey and mapping have their own terms

Map material used in the mapping and fieldwork lessons carries its own licence and credit
requirements, which are not satisfied by a general "educational use" belief. The toolkit's
`MAP_AND_FIELDWORK_EVIDENCE_CARD.html` names the evidence moves; **it does not clear the rights**,
and this gate does not clear them either. An unlicensed map is a wishlist entry under S3.

## Gate S5 — Accessible versions are part of the source, not an accommodation bolted on

A source pack ships with its accessible route already built — enlarged, chunked, transcribed,
audio, colour-safe, tactile where the task is spatial — per `ACCESSIBLE_SOURCE_PACK_STANDARD.md`.

The standing reason: **source density is one of the five interpretation branches.** A pupil
blocked by the *format* of a source, whose response is then read as a misconception about its
*content*, has been mis-assessed by the pack rather than by the teacher. The accessible version
exists so that the branch "access barrier" means what it says.

Print surfaces in this toolkit are **PRINT-UNVERIFIED** until a physical check.

## Gate S6 — A pupil's own material is not a classroom source

A family photograph, letter or document a pupil brings is private material on loan. It does not
acquire a provenance row, it is not retained, it is not displayed without consent, and it is
returned. See `quality/LUNDY_HUMANITIES_DATA_FIREWALL.md`, which owns this boundary; this gate
only points at it so that a person assembling a source pack meets it in the right place.

## Gate S7 — The assessed sources are frozen

The source load in `GROW_HUM_W7_Write_The_Account.html` and
`LAUNCH_HUM_W7_Source_Assessment.html` is not adjusted by this gate, by a source review, or on an
arithmetic hunch (`HANDOVER.md`, ruled out list; **R-A01**). The recorded 30-vs-8 reading/judgement
imbalance is an **observation for review after the first sitting**, and remains one.
