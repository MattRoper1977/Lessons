Arrival Task

Arrival Task – What we already know about people

You do not have to talk about your own family. You can do every task about the example person on the card. Nobody has to say who they live with. Nothing you say leaves this room.
You can stop for a bit. You can work on your own. You can point instead of speak. You can use the card only. You can tell an adult what to write. Each one is a full way to do this lesson.

A person can be special for what they do. They do not have to be family.

People live in all sorts of homes. Every one of them is normal.

A reason answers the question why. Naming a person is not yet a reason.

You can talk about the example person on the card. You do not have to talk about your own life.

Special does not mean famous. It does not mean family. It means this person matters to someone. And you can say the reason out loud.

Two people can pick the same person. Their reasons can be different. Both are right.

Arrival decision: Pick the example card, or pick your own idea. Both are full answers. You can change your mind later. Nobody will ask which one you picked.

Before we start, look at the example card on your table. It shows a house, the people who live there, and two neighbours. Everyone in this lesson can work about that card from beginning to end and finish with a complete piece of work.

Offer the example card first, not as a fallback. A learner who takes it should not be prompted toward personal content. Wait; take the least prompt that works.

Starter

Starter: Why is naming someone not enough?

Here are two answers about the same person on the supplied card.

Answer A: “The neighbour on the card.”

Answer B: “The neighbour on the card, because they walk the dog when the family is out.”

Answer B tells you more than Answer A. Answer A just names the person. Answer B names the person and then says why. So someone who has never met them still knows why they matter.

The need for this lesson is that missing part: the reason. By the end you will be able to give one every time, using a chain with three links.

Try it in your head: think of the person who opens the school gate in the morning. Now add the word because and finish the sentence. That second half is the part we are practising today.

Some reasons are big. Some are small. Small ones count too. A person who knows how you take your tea is a real reason. So is a person who drove you to hospital. Both count.

Do not accept a first-name-only answer as complete, and do not treat it as wrong either. Name the gap: we have the who, we still need the why.

I Do

I Do: Three rings of people

The diagram above has three rings. The middle ring is me. The next ring out is people at home. The outside ring is people who help me — a teacher, a neighbour, a bus driver, a friend.

A person in the outside ring is just as special as one in the middle. The rings show how often you see someone, not how much they matter.

Notice what the rings do not mean. They do not rank people. They do not say who cares most. A person in the outside ring can be the one you would tell your news to first.

Watch me do it: I put the neighbour in the outside ring. I do not live with them. Then I say the reason. They walk the dog when the family is out. I say the ring first. Then I say the reason. The ring is the easy part.

Now watch one more: the teaching assistant goes in the outside ring too. But the reason is not the same. They read the hard words with me first. Same ring. Different reason.

So one ring can hold many people, and each one needs its own reason. That is why the next diagram has three boxes instead of one.

Read the diagram left to right and inside out, naming each ring. Some learners will place an adult they see daily in the outer ring; that is correct and should not be corrected.

We Do

We Do: Which ring? Everyone shows at once

Card 1: the person who cooks the tea at the example house.

Card 2: the lollipop person at the crossing.

Card 3: the cousin who visits at the weekend.

Commit first: hold up 1, 2 or 3 fingers for the ring you would choose — 1 for me, 2 for people at home, 3 for people who help me. Everyone shows at the same time. Then we reveal and compare.

Evidence check

Card 3 has more than one right answer. A cousin who visits at the weekend could go in ring 2. Or in ring 3. It depends on the reason you give. When we show, we check the reason. We do not check the number.

After the reveal, one person from each answer says their reason out loud. We are listening for the word because. If two people chose different rings and both gave a reason, both stay on the board.

Changing your mind after you hear someone else is allowed, and it is a good sign. Put your new number up and say what changed it.

Count down and have the whole class show together, so nobody copies. Reveal after every hand is up. A changed answer after the reveal is a success, not a correction.

I Do

I Do: The three-link reason chain

The chain above has three boxes: who, then what they do, then why they are special. The arrows show the order. The last box is the reason, and it is the one most often missed.

Look at the picture. The lollipop person. Then: stops the traffic. Then: so I can cross safely. That is a full answer. You can hear all three links in it.

Here is one that is not complete: the lollipop person, then stands at the crossing. That stops at the middle box. It says what they do, but it never says why it matters to anyone.

Fixing it together: add the last link. Stands at the crossing, so that children can cross without waiting for a gap in the traffic. The words so that often start the third box.

Point to each box as you say it. Learners who give only the first box have not failed; they have given one of three links, and the diagram shows what is still to come.

We build one chain on the board first. Nobody writes yet. Everyone puts the middle box on a whiteboard. Then we show them all at once. Then we agree a reason.

The person for this chain is the one on the example card who cooks the tea. The first box is filled in for you. Your job is the middle box: what do they actually do? Write one action, not a describing word.

When we show, we will have lots of middle boxes. We keep the ones that name an action. We put the others to one side. They are not wrong. But a word that describes cannot lead to a reason.

Then, together, we add the third box and read the whole chain aloud from left to right.

Take the middle box from a learner who has not yet spoken today. Scribe exactly what is said; do not improve the wording.

Independent Work

Independent Work — Your record: people special to me

16:00

Make one record. You may do it about the supplied example person, about someone real, or about both. All three are complete answers.

◆ Supported

One person. Place them in a ring, then choose or sign the reason card that fits. An adult may scribe your exact words, and the words stay yours. Point to the ring first, then to the reason.

▲ Standard

Two people. For each one, write the three links: who they are, what they do, and why that makes them special. Use a separate row for each person so the links stay in order.

★ Stretch

Two people, and for one of them a second reason. Say which reason you would give first and why that one comes first. A reason that only you would know is still a good reason.

Check yourself: does every person you named have a reason next to them?

Check yourself: is the reason about something the person does?

Check yourself: would someone who has never met them get your reason?

Check yourself: did you use the word because, or the words so that, at least once?

Finished early? Take one chain. Add a second reason for that person. Then pick the one you would say first if you only had time for one. Mark it.

If you are stuck on the third box, say the middle box out loud and then add the words so that. Whatever comes next is usually your reason.

You may not want to write about a real person. That is fine. The card is a full answer. Nobody will ask you why. You can use the card for every person on your record.

Your record is done when each person has an action and a reason. Read one of them out loud to yourself. Check it makes sense.

Nobody is asked to explain a choice to work about the example card. Record the route used and any scribing on the evidence line, keeping adult support visible and separate from the learner's own words.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket — Audience receives; influence changes something

Read one chain to a partner or to an adult. The listener says back the reason they heard, in their own words.

Did the listener hear a different reason? Then change one word in your chain. Make it say what you meant. That change is your influence. It belongs to you, not to them.

Did the listener hear just what you meant? Write that down too. A chain that already works is worth noting. It needed no change.

Did the listener say your reason back right? Then your chain worked. That is the check. It does not have to sound clever.

Nothing goes outside this room unless you say so. Your record stays in your folder. You decide if it goes on the wall.

Close by naming one change a learner made after hearing their partner. That is the influence record, and it belongs to the learner.
