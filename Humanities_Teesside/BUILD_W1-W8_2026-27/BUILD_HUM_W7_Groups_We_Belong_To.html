A place in the group

Autumn 1 · Week 7 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can name a group I am part of and show how someone can join in.

Success criteria

  • Name or point to one group I belong to.
  • Show something that group does.
  • Choose one action that helps a member join in.

Use Next to begin. Ask for word help or your usual support when needed.

Arrival · 2 min

Who does this together?

Choose a group card.

Look at what the people are doing.

Point, say or write one word. You can use our class for every task today.

Starter · 3 min

One person, more than one group

Could someone be in both?

A person can be in a class and a games club.

People in one group do not all need to like the same things. They do not all need to do the same job.

Ari — imagined example

Ari learns with a class. After lunch, Ari plays a table game with a club.

Constructed classroom scenario, not a real pupil.

Can Ari belong to both groups?

I do · 5 min

Group → activity → welcome

Watch me make one match.

The gardening group grows plants.

A new member can watch first or try watering. I ask what helps them join in.

  1. Name the group.
  2. Point to its activity.
  3. Offer a useful choice.

Garden group

Members grow plants together. They can plant, water, label or watch a demonstration.

Constructed teaching card.
We do · 6 min

Build a group match

Match each group to an activity.

Choose a group. Choose what it does.

Check the picture and words. If your match does not fit, change it and try again.

Paper or spoken route

Cut out or point to the four printed group cards and four activity labels. Make a pair, check the card text, then explain one match.

I do · 4 min

What helps someone take part?

Look for the need.

Sam wants to try the game. Sam does not know the rules yet.

I show one turn, then offer Sam a turn. I do not make Sam read every rule aloud.

Sam — imagined newcomer

Sam says: “I want to try. Can you show me one turn?”

Dialogue authored for this lesson; not a quotation from a real pupil.

Which action fits Sam's request?

We do · 5 min

Test a welcome choice

Does your idea help this person?

Lee wants to join the garden task. The long list is hard to follow.

Choose: a picture of the next step, or a longer list. Explain which one helps Lee start.

  1. Name the need.
  2. Choose an action.
  3. Check it against Lee's words.

Lee — imagined example

Lee says: “Show me the next step, please.”

Constructed teaching scenario.
Independent · 12 min

My group card

Make a card for one group you belong to.

Our class is a full answer.

Name the group. Show one thing it does. Add one helpful welcome action.

Point, draw, speak or write. An adult can write your exact words.

  2. Use the group and action cards if they help.

Supported

Use our class card. Point to what we do. Choose a helpful welcome action and give a word, sign or short reason.

I am part of ____. We ____. This helps because ____. Read two action cards aloud if needed.

Standard

Name class or another group you belong to. Describe its activity and explain a welcome action for a new member.

Group → activity → need → helpful action.

Stretch

Complete your group card. Then adapt the welcome for a member who prefers to watch first. Explain why one welcome choice might not suit everyone.

I could offer ____ or ____. I would ask because ____.

TA Brief

Current stage

BUILD · A place in the group

Autumn 1 · Week 7

SoW

BUILD Weekly - Autumn!C52

  • Share a group/team/community I belong to.

The operative workbook places belonging in Autumn 1 Week 7. This is the World About Me sequence; the retained migration/history route is a separate alternative. Class membership meets the named outcome without requiring disclosure about home. Fictional cards support rehearsal; record separately if a pupil only describes a fictional group.

Prior: Week 6 created a community map. Retrieve one place where people do something together; no home address is needed.

Next: Autumn 2 begins festivals of light. Carry forward the idea that people can share an activity while joining in differently.

Preparation

  • Display or print the four picture cards and activity labels.
  • Provide pens, blank paper and optional small picture/action cards.
  • Identify one achievable class welcome choice to discuss honestly.

Care and safety

  • Do not require personal or family disclosure. Class membership meets the outcome.
  • Use normal school safeguarding procedures; do not promise absolute confidentiality.

Teacher response guidance

  • Ari can belong to class and games club.
  • Matching: class—learn; football team—practise football; garden group—grow plants; games club—table games.
  • Sam: show one turn then offer a turn; it matches Sam's request.
  • Lee: show the next step, preferably the requested short visual support; a longer list does not address that request.
  • Example independent answer: I am in this class. We learn together. I would show a new pupil where the task cards are so they can start. Other justified responses are valid.
  • Exit: no, being the best is not a condition for belonging.

Evidence of learning

Check whether the pupil identifies an actual group they belong to, links it to an activity and chooses a helpful action with a reason. Record a fictional-only answer as rehearsal, not full evidence for the first criterion.

Access and independence

Offer pointing, speaking, signing, drawing or exact scribing. Read a single card first. Fade the choice prompt before the independent reason. Do not infer a reading age from pathway membership.

Lundy Loop

Pupils choose a response route and a possible welcome action. An adult listens, restates the idea and explains whether and when it can be tried. Record the response; do not promise a change before feasibility is known.

Arrival · Who does this together?

Retrieve a community place from Week 6. Accept school, garden, pitch or club room. Read labels on request. Do not ask pupils to identify relatives, faith, postcode or out-of-school membership.

Starter · One person, more than one group

Avoid implying that someone must enjoy, attend daily or perform a role to deserve belonging. Distinguish membership from a person's feeling of belonging: a member can still feel left out.

I do · Group → activity → welcome

Say: 'This is the garden group. They grow plants. I can offer a choice and wait.' Model asking rather than deciding for the newcomer. The choice must be possible and safe in the current classroom.

We do · Build a group match

Allow several roles within each activity: observing a demonstration, choosing, explaining, keeping score. Ask what evidence on the card supports the match. Check one card first, then fade support.

I do · What helps someone take part?

Model need → action → reason. A pupil may offer another useful action, such as a picture rule card. Accept it if it addresses Sam's request. Avoid making pupils act out rejection.

We do · Test a welcome choice

Expected: picture of the next step, because Lee asked for the next action. This is not a rule that pictures always beat words. A different pupil may prefer written steps; ask the person. Paper route: point to printed choices.

Independent · My group card

This is the direct C52 evidence. If the pupil only describes an imagined club, record successful rehearsal and invite a private class-membership response; do not count a hypothetical preference as actual membership. Record prompts, not a score.

Exit · A welcome we could try

Look for a named group, shared activity and reasoned inclusion action. If a suggestion cannot happen, explain the boundary honestly. Avoid public votes about who belongs.

Misconceptions

People can belong to only one group. Use Ari's two activities to show overlapping membership.

Only people who are best should join in. Different roles and beginners belong; ask what makes participation possible.

The same support helps everyone. Compare Sam's request for a demonstration with a pupil who prefers written steps. Ask, then offer choices.

Sources
