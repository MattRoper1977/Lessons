Arrival Task – What we know about groups

You never have to name anyone you live with. This class is a group, and everybody here is in it. That is a full answer for every task today. Nothing you say leaves this room.
You can stop for a bit. You can point instead of speak. You can use the cards only. You can tell an adult what to write. Each one is a full way to do this lesson.
Belong means you are one of the people in a group.

A group is more than one person doing something together.

You can be in more than one group at once.

This class is a group. Everybody in this room is in it.

Nobody has to say who they live with. That is not what a group means here.

Arrival decision: Pick a group card from the table, or use this class as your group. Both are full answers. You can change your mind later.

On your table there are ten group cards. Each card shows a group doing something. A team on a pitch. A choir. A gardening group. A reading group. A class like this one. Everyone in this room can work with these cards from start to end.

Look at one card now. What are they doing together? Point to it. That is all you need to do to begin.

Offer the cards first, not as a fallback. Name this class as a group early, so nobody feels they must supply one from home.

Starter

Starter: Two kinds of group

Here are two answers about groups.

Answer A: “A choir is a group. They meet on a day and they sing.”

Answer B: “Everyone on our street is a group. They do not meet. They are just all there.”

Both answers are right. They are two different kinds of group. One meets to do a thing. One you are simply part of.

The need for this lesson is that difference. If you know which kind a group is, you know what to expect from it.

Try it in your head: a football team. Do they meet to do a thing? Yes. Now: everybody who catches the same bus. Do they meet to do a thing? No. Two kinds.

Neither kind is better. A street that never meets still helps you. Somebody puts your bin back.

Some groups are both. A class meets to learn, and you are also just part of it all day.

Do not treat a both answer as confused. It is the most accurate one; ask which part of the day they mean.

I Do

I Do: Sorting groups into two kinds

The picture has two boxes. The first is meets to do a thing. The second is you are part of it.

A card goes in the box that fits it best. The choir meets to sing. The street does not meet at all.

Look at the line under the boxes. This class fits both. A card that fits both is not a mistake. It is a card with a good reason on each side.

Watch me do it: I take the gardening card. Do they meet? Yes, on a day. Do they do a thing? Yes, they plant. So it goes in the first box. I say the doing first. Then I pick the box.

Now watch a harder one: people on the bus. Do they meet? They are all there at once, but nobody planned it. They do not do a thing together. Second box. Same steps. The doing first, then the box.

Once a card is sorted, it can be matched. Each group is matched to the thing it does together. That is the next picture.

Sort means putting things into sets that go together.
Name each box before any card is placed. A learner who puts the bus card in the first box has a reason; ask for it rather than moving the card.

We Do

We Do: Sort the cards. Match each group to what it does.

Card 1: the choir. Which box? What do they do together?

Card 2: the people waiting at the stop. Which box? What do they do together?

Card 3: this class. Which box? What do we do together?

Sort first: put your three cards into the two boxes on your table, then match each one to a doing card. Everyone commits at the same time, and then we hold up the matches together.

Evidence check

Card 3 can go in both boxes. This class meets to learn, and you are also just part of it. When we check, we ask for the reason. We do not check the box. A reason makes a sort right.

After the sort, one person from each table says one match out loud. We are listening for a group and a doing. If two tables sorted the same card differently and both gave a reason, both stay up.

Changing a card after you hear someone else is allowed. Move it and say what changed it.

Have every table sort before any answer is taken, so nobody copies. A card moved after the check is a success, not a correction.

I Do

I Do: Group, doing, and what it would lose

The chain has three boxes: group, then doing, then lose. The arrows show the order.

Look at the choir. Then: they sing together. Then: without the person who starts the song, nobody knows when to begin.

Here is one that stops early: the choir, they sing. That says who and what. It never says why the people matter.

Finishing it together: add the last box. Without one singer the sound is thinner. Without the one who starts, nobody comes in on time. Either is a full third box.

A chain with all three boxes shows that a group is people, not just a name.

Point at each box as you say it. A learner who names only the group has given one of three, and the diagram shows what is still to come.

◆ Supported

▲ Standard

Say all three boxes for the reading group.

★ Stretch

Say what this class would lose if one person stopped coming.

We match one chain on the board first. Nobody writes yet. Everyone writes the last box on a whiteboard, then we hold up the boards at the same time.

The group for this chain is this class. The first two boxes are filled in. Your job is the last one. What would we lose?

When we hold up, we keep every answer that names something the group does less well. We move the ones about a single person to one side, because we are talking about the group.

Then we read the whole chain together, from left to right.

Take the last box from a learner who has not spoken yet today. Scribe exactly what is said; do not improve the wording.

Independent Work

Independent Work — Your record: a group I belong to

16:00

Make one record. You may use a group card, or this class, or a group you are in. All three are complete answers.

◆ Supported

One group. Match it to what it does. An adult may scribe your words, and the words stay yours. Point to the box it sorts into.

▲ Standard

Two groups. For each one write the group, what they do, and which box it sorted into.

★ Stretch

Two groups from different boxes. For one of them, write what it would lose without one person.

Check yourself: did you write what the group does together, not just its name?

Check yourself: did you sort each group into one of the two boxes?

Check yourself: is your last box about the group, not about one person being missed?

Check yourself: would someone who has never met this group understand it?

Finished early? Take one group and write a second thing it does together. Then say which one people would miss first.

If you are stuck on the last box, say the doing out loud and add the words without them. What comes next is usually the answer.

You may not want to write about a group outside school. That is fine. This class is a full answer and nobody will ask you why.

Your record is done when each group has a doing next to it. Read one out loud to yourself.

Nobody is asked to explain a choice to use this class. Record the route used and any scribing on the evidence line, keeping adult support visible and separate from the learner own words.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket — Audience receives; influence changes something

Read one group and its doing to a partner or an adult. The listener says back what the group does, in their own words.

Did the listener hear a different doing? Then change one word in yours. That change is your influence. It belongs to you, not to them.

Did the listener hear just what you meant? Write that down too. A chain that already works is worth noting.

Next week we look at a festival that uses light. Keep your record.

Nothing goes outside this room unless you say so. Your record stays in your folder.

Close by naming one change a learner made after hearing their partner. That is the influence record, and it belongs to the learner.
