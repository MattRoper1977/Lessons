Special to me means you chose it

You have handled an object from the approved box before.

Today you pick one of them yourself, and then you say why.

Special to me does not mean the object belongs to you.

It means you chose this one out of the six in the box.

A choice with a reason behind it is worth listening to.

A choice with no reason is only a hand in the air.

So your reason has to come from the object itself.

You point at a part of it, then you say what that part tells you.

That is the job today, and it is the whole job.

Say the point-then-say rule once and leave it on the board. Pupils who skip the pointing give reasons nobody in the room can check.

You need the box, one object, its handling card, and a talk sheet.

The reason is why you picked that one and not the one beside it.

The group is the people you say it to, and they decide whether it landed.

You are not asked about your family, and nobody asks what you believe.

Two people, one bowl

Here are two pupils holding up exactly the same bowl.

The first one says: this is a bowl and I like it.

The group looks at the bowl for a second, and then looks away.

The second one says: this is a bowl, and the rim is worn smooth.

She points at the worn rim while she is saying it.

Now the whole group is looking at the rim with her.

One of them moved twelve pairs of eyes, and the other moved none.

The difference was pointing at something real that everybody could see.

  • 1 · say what the object is.
  • 2 · point at one part of it.
  • 3 · say what that part tells you.
  • 4 · stop there, before you spoil it.

Worn means hands, and it means a lot of hands over a long time.

Nobody had to tell you that, because the bowl told you.

A good reason hands the group the evidence and then gets out of the way.

One object, four steps

I open the box and I pick out the small metal cup.

Step one is pick, and that part is done.

Step two is look, and I name three things that I can see.

The cup is metal, and it is dented near the base.

The inside of it is darker than the outside.

That is three things, and all three of them are on the cup.

Not one of them is about me or about my week.

Step three is reason, and I choose one thing from my three.

Step four is say, and I say three sentences to the group.

This is a metal cup. I chose it because of the dent near the base.

A dent means it was used by somebody, and not kept in a case.

If you only see one thing you get one reason, but three things let you pick the strongest.

Model naming all three things out loud before choosing any of them. A pupil who chooses first stops looking, and the third thing is usually the good one.

Count slowly to five before you name your first thing.

Turn the object over in your hands once while you are counting.

Most of the worn parts are on the side you cannot see yet.

One more thing before you start. I never said the cup was nice.

Nice tells the group nothing at all that they can look at.

Sort the reasons

There are six talk cards on your table, all of them face up.

Sort them into two groups: reasons you can see, and reasons you cannot.

This is worn at the edge. This is old. I like the blue on it.

There is a crack down one side. It is nice. The handle is thinner than the rest.

Then match each cannot-see card to a part of an object it could point at.

So I like the blue on it becomes the blue band round the top.

When your table has finished, hold up the card you argued about most.

Old can be seen, but only if you say how you can tell.

Saying that you need to look at the object again is a right answer here.

The word because does the work

Now I join the two halves of my talk together.

The first half is what I can see on the object.

The second half is the reason it made me choose that one.

The word sitting between them is because.

Because is a hinge, and what comes after it holds up what came before.

Here is my sentence again. I chose it because of the dent near the base.

Then I say what the dent actually tells me about the cup.

It was used by somebody, and it was not kept in a case.

I chose it because it is special. Special is not on the cup.

The group cannot look at special, but they can look at a dent.

So every because has to land on a part of the object.

  • 1 · Say what it is. This is a metal cup.
  • 2 · Say because, then the part. I chose it because of the dent.
  • 3 · Say what that part tells you. It was used, not kept in a case.
  • 4 · Check it. Somebody else can point at the part you named.

A stranger cannot see inside your head, and they will not ask.

They can only see the object you are holding in your hands.

Point at what I said

Work in twos, and put your object on the desk between you.

Say your three sentences to your partner, in order.

They point at the part your reason named, as soon as you finish.

If the finger lands on it, then your reason is on the object.

If they cannot point at anything, your reason is still in your head.

Say the sentence again with the part named out loud this time.

Then swap over, and do exactly the same job for them.

You may not say good or nice about your partner's talk.

Or say that you could not find it, and say where you looked.

Could not find it is useful information. It is not an insult.

Fill the talk sheet

Work on your own now, and keep the object on your desk.

Box one is what it is, so write the name of the object.

Box two is what you can see, so write three things in it.

All three of them have to be on the object itself.

Box three is your reason, so ring one of your three things.

Then write one sentence underneath the box you have ringed.

Start that sentence with the words: I chose this because.

Read it back to yourself, and check the because lands on the object.

If it lands on a feeling instead, change the second half of it.

The handling card sits under your object in the box.

It says who the object matters to.

If you use that line, copy it word for word.

You report what the card says. Nobody asks if you agree.

  • Supported · name the object and write two things you can see on it.
  • Standard · three things you can see, one of them ringed, and a because sentence.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and add one line from the handling card saying who the object matters to.

Do not copy the cup that I did. Write what is in front of you.

If you finish early, write a second because for a different part.

Then ring the one of the two you would rather say out loud.

Say it to the group

Stand up or stay sitting down. Both of those are fine.

Hold the object up where the whole group can see it.

Say your three sentences, and point when you say the part.

The group then does one thing back, and they name the part you pointed at.

If they get it right, your reason landed.

If they get it wrong, say the part again on its own.

The adult writes your sentence down word for word on the sheet.

Then you choose what happens next.

Keep the sheet, change the reason, or take the same object again next week.

Write that choice on the sheet before you leave the room.

Your sheet goes into the humanities folder, and not into your bag.

TA Brief

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
