Staff card: the cards show buildings and books, never people, and no card is about anybody in the room. Every faith is presented as an equal and none is ranked above another. No pupil is asked, on any route, what they believe or where they go; a learner can sort and match every card without disclosing anything about their own home. Keep the talk on the cards.
Staff and source drawer: the card set is eight cards from the school-approved source pack: a church, a mosque, a synagogue and a gurdwara, and a Bible, a Qur'an, a Torah scroll and the Sikh holy book. The word bank lists Christian, Muslim, Jewish and Sikh in a different order on each table, so no order suggests a rank. Book names are read aloud for any learner who wants them, and Hindu and Buddhist cards from the same pack can be added where a table is ready for six.

Arrival Task – What we know about special books and places

Nobody is asked what they believe. Every task is about the cards on the table. Nobody is asked where they go, what they read or what they believe. You can do every part without saying a word about your own home. Nothing you say leaves this room.
You can stop for a bit. You can point instead of speak. You can move a card instead of writing. You can tell an adult what to write. You never have to say what you believe. Each one is a full way to do this lesson.
Faith means a set of beliefs that people share. A faith often has a special book and a special place.

A faith is a set of beliefs that people share.

Arrival decision: Pick one card from the table. Is it a book or a place? Put it on the book side or the place side. You do not have to say why. You can move it later.

On your table there are eight cards. Four show a place. Four show a book. A building with a tall tower. A building with a dome. A scroll. A book with a cross on the cover. Everyone in this room can work with these cards from start to end.

Look at one card now. Is it a book or a place? Point to your answer. That is all you need to do to begin.

Offer the cards first, not as a fallback. If a learner starts to talk about their own home or their own place of worship, thank them and move back to the card. Do not ask any learner which card matches their own home. Wait; take the least prompt that works.

Starter

Starter: One card, two piles?

Here is a card. It shows a scroll. Two people put it in two different piles.

Answer A: “Place. You see it in a building.”

Answer B: “Book. It has words to read.”

Answer B is the match. A scroll is a book that rolls up. But the first answer saw a real clue too. You do see a scroll inside a special place. Nobody was silly.

The need for this lesson is this. A book and a place go together. A faith has both. When we know which is which, we can match them to the faith.

Try it in your head: a building with a dome and a tall thin tower. Book, or place? Hold your answer. Do not say it yet.

Sorting is the first step. Book or place. Matching is the next step. Which faith does it go with?

That is what we do today. Sort. Then match.

Do not supply the faith word at this stage. The starter only separates book from place; the faith match comes in the model. Do not take an early answer aloud.

I Do

I Do: How to sort, then match

The diagram has three steps. First you look at the card. Then you sort it. Book or place. Then you match it to a faith word from the bank.

The sort comes first. A book and a place look very different. That is an easy sort. The match is harder. Use the clues on the card.

Nobody is timed. If you are not sure of a match, put the card on the “not sure” spot. We come back to it together.

Watch me do it: I take a card. It shows a building with a cross on top. Is it a book or a place? It is a building. So it is a place. I put it on the place side. Now I look at the word bank. A cross is a clue for the word Christian. I match the card to that word. This place is a church.

Now watch a second card: a book with a cross on the cover. It has pages. So it is a book. I put it on the book side. The cross is the same clue. I match it to the word Christian too. Now the church and the book sit next to the same word. One faith. One place. One book.

Matching is the whole lesson. When a book and a place sit by the same faith word, you have made a set.

Match means put the card with the faith word it belongs to.
Model the clue aloud each time: the cross, the dome, the scroll, the flag. A learner who matches wrongly has still read a clue; name the clue they used and offer a second one. Never link a card to a learner.

We Do

We Do: Sort the cards. Then match them.

Card 1: a building with a dome and a tall thin tower. Book or place? Which faith word?

Card 2: a scroll on two wooden rollers. Book or place? Which faith word?

Card 3: a building with an orange flag on a tall pole by the door. Book or place? Which faith word?

Sort first, then match: put each card in the book pile or the place pile on your table. Then move each card next to one faith word from the bank. Everyone sorts and matches at the same time. Nobody looks at another table until every card is placed.

Evidence check

Every match must use a clue on the card. The dome. The scroll. The flag. If you cannot see a clue, put the card on the “not sure” spot. A match with a clue is a real match. A guess is just a guess.

After every card is placed, we check one card at a time. One person says the clue they used. Then we look at the card together. If the card moves, we say why.

Moving a card after you hear a clue is allowed. It is a good sign. Say what clue changed your mind.

Wait for every card to be placed before checking any. Ask for the clue the learner used, not the answer. A learner who moves a card after hearing a clue has done the thing this lesson is for. Do not ask any learner whether a card is their own faith.

I Do

I Do: What a book and a place are for

Look at the picture. A faith word sits in the middle. A book sits on one side. A place sits on the other. Both belong to the same faith.

A special book is for reading. People read it to learn how to live. Some people read it every day. Some read it on a special day.

A special place is for meeting. People go there to pray, to sing and to be together. Many faiths have a special place like this.

What we do not say: “that one is best”, or “that one is odd”. Every faith on the table has a book and a place. We match them. We do not rank them.

You will use one book and one place in your record. Pick the set you can match with a clue.

Point to each box as you read it. Keep the tone even across every faith: the same sentence shape and the same time for each. Keep the wall picture up for the rest of the lesson.
