BUILD Humanities. Week 1. Strand: RE and World Views.

Objective

I can pick one card from the approved pack, say what makes it special, say why I picked it, and point at evidence.

Success evidence

A record sheet with a card number, a because sentence, my own reason for picking that card, and evidence. Not sure counts as evidence work.

Nothing private is asked for

Every card and every example comes from the approved Special Things pack. Nobody is asked about home, family, money or belief. Your own reason is a school reason.

40 minutes. Offline.

Resources: the Special Things card pack lives in the Humanities cupboard. If it is missing, ask the Humanities lead, or the school office, before the lesson. One pack per table. Check first that card 2 is a paper lantern, card 6 the trophy with the year under the photograph, card 9 a stone bowl. If the numbering differs, use the cards that match and say the new numbers aloud. No pack: number six objects in the room one to six and use the same sentences. The printed sheet is the record sheet, one per pupil.

The card pack is on your table. Every card has a photograph and one printed line. For each statement say true, false, or not sure.

  • Special means a thing matters more than an ordinary thing.
  • A reason is the part after the word because.
  • Every card is checked by school. You may pick any card, for your own reason.
  • Not sure is a real answer. You may write it down.

You may point instead of speaking. Pointing counts.

Not sure means I do not know this yet. Write it down. It is a real answer.

Read each statement aloud twice. Take answers by pointing or thumbs, not round the room. Some cards show remembrance objects. If a pupil says someone has died, or becomes upset, thank them and ask no questions. Offer another card or a break. Tell the class teacher and the DSL the same day, per school policy. Never promise secrecy.

Two pupils looked at the same card. Card 6 shows the school trophy.

Answer A: card 6 is special. Answer B: card 6 is special because a whole class won it together. The year is printed under the photograph.

Answer A stops too early. Nobody listening can check it.

Answer B names the thing, gives a reason, and points at something you can look at.

One thing is still missing. Neither pupil said why they picked that card.

Here are the three steps.

  • Notice: look at the photograph and read the printed line.
  • Choose: pick one card. The pick is yours.
  • Explain: say why it is special, say why you picked it, then point at your evidence.

Three words we will use

  • Special: a thing that matters more than an ordinary thing.
  • Reason: the part after the word because.
  • World view: what a person or group treats as important.

I will do three cards. Nobody writes yet.

I pick card 2. The photograph shows a paper lantern. The printed line says many people light lanterns at a festival.

Sentence one is about the card. Card 2 is special because many people light lanterns. My evidence is the printed line.

Sentence two is about me. I picked card 2 because I like the way the lantern looks. That reason is about the picture, not my home.

Now card 6, the trophy. Card 6 is special because a whole class won it together. My evidence is the year under the photograph. I picked it because I walk past that cabinet.

The order is: card number, because, my own reason, evidence.

Borrow a reason: I like sport. It is on our display. I see it every week. It looks calm.

Now a harder card. I pick card 9. It shows a stone bowl. The line says it is used at a festival. I do not know which festival.

I do not guess. I say out loud: I am not sure which festival this is. I write not sure. Then I write what would help. I can ask an adult.

A second one, the same way

Watch me do one more. I pick the shell card.

I say what I can see. The shell is small. It has stripes.

I say why I picked it. I picked it because I like the stripes.

Then I point. I point at the stripes on the card.

See means I can point at it. Why means it is my reason.

They are different things. Both are allowed.

So far we said what the card shows. Now we say why it matters. Today we do both.

Here are two pupils from another class.

Alex picked card 2, the lantern. Alex said: card 2 is special because many people light lanterns. And: I picked card 2 because I like the colours.

Jo picked card 6, the trophy. Jo said: card 6 is special because a whole class won it together. And: I picked card 6 because I am in the school team.

Alex pointed at the printed line. Jo pointed at the printed year.

Alex and Jo picked different cards. Neither is wrong.

That is what world views means. Different people treat different things as important.

Your own reason stays in school. Something you like. Something you see here. No home, no family, no belief.

If a pupil gives a reason about home, family or belief, thank them. Then ask for a school reason for that card. Write the school reason. Do not ask a follow-up question.

When two cards feel the same

Sometimes two cards both feel special. That is fine.

Pick one. You are not saying the other one is bad.

You can say: I picked this one today.

Special to me does not mean best. It means it matters to me.

Somebody else can pick a different card. Both people are right.

Now choose a row. A row is a choice, not a label. You may change row. Nobody will ask why.

  • ◆ Supported: pick one card. Say the number. Say both sentences out loud: card __ is special because __, and I picked card __ because I __. Point at your evidence.
  • ▲ Standard: pick one or two cards. For each card write both sentences and name your evidence.
  • ★ Stretch: pick two cards that two different people might choose. Write both sentences for each. Then write one sentence about how both reasons can be true.

An adult will read all three rows aloud.

Every row finishes with four things: a card number, a because sentence, your own reason, and evidence.

Independent · evidence

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

This is doing time. Nine minutes. You are writing or pointing, not reading.

The printed sheet is your record sheet. For each card, put four things on it.

  • The card number.
  • Card __ is special because __.
  • I picked card __ because I __.
  • Your evidence: the printed line, something you can point to in the photograph and name, or something that happens in school.

If you cannot check something, write not sure. Then write one thing that would help. That is a finished answer.

One finished card is a finished piece of work. A second is a bonus. Work at your own pace.

An adult can write your words for you. The words stay yours.

Nothing about home goes on this sheet.

Scribing: reach pupils who do not write independently first, then pupils who have stopped. Circulate with the pack, not with a pen. If a pupil stalls, hand them two cards and ask which one they can say more about.

Write or draw the card you picked.

Write one thing you can see on it. Point at that thing too.

Write your reason. Start with the words: I picked it because.

If you cannot find a reason yet, draw the card and put a circle next to it. An adult will come and help. A circle is a real answer.

SPACE stays available. VOICE is received. AUDIENCE names back exactly. INFLUENCE changes one real next action.

Bring your sheet to an adult. Pointing at your sheet counts as speaking.

  • You say or point: your card number and why you picked it.
  • The adult says it back in your exact words.
  • You say yes that is right, or you correct it. Correcting it is best.
  • Together you pick one next action.

Choose one: add your card to the class display, add one more piece of evidence, or write your not sure question for your class teacher.

What you said changes what happens next.

Write the agreed next action at the top of each sheet. Leave the sheets with a note for the class teacher, listing the not sure questions and any action needing an adult. Promise nothing that needs staff who are not here.

TA Brief

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
