Arrival Task

Arrival Task – What we know about same and different

You never have to say anything about your own home. Every task is about the cards on the table. Nobody is asked what they eat, what they wear or what they believe. Nothing you say leaves this room.
You can stop for a bit. You can point instead of speak. You can hold up a card instead of writing. You can tell an adult what to write. Each one is a full way to do this lesson.
Respect means treating a person well, even when they are not like you.

Two things can be the same kind of thing and still look different.

Two bikes can be the same kind of thing. One is red. One is blue.

Different does not mean wrong. It means not the same.

You can like a thing that your friend does not like. You are both allowed.

A card shows a thing. It does not show a person.

We look at the cards. We do not ask about each other.

Arrival decision: Pick a card pair from the table. Both cards stay face up. You do not have to say why you picked it. You can change your mind later.

On your table there are eight card pairs. Each pair shows two things. Two hats. Two meals. Two ways of saying hello. Two kinds of music. Everyone in this room can work with these cards from start to end.

Look at one pair now. Are the two things the same kind of thing? Point to your answer. That is all you need to do to begin.

Offer the cards first, not as a fallback. If a learner starts to talk about home, thank them and move to the card. Wait; take the least prompt that works.

Starter

Starter: Two people, one card pair, two answers

Here are two answers about the same card pair. The pair shows two hats.

Answer A: “Same. They are both hats.”

Answer B: “Different. One is warm and one is for sun.”

Both answers are right. Answer A looks at the kind of thing. Answer B looks at what it is for. Nobody made a mistake.

The need for this lesson is what we say next. When two people give different answers, we do not say “wrong”. We ask for the reason. That is respect.

Try it in your head: two shoes. One is a boot. One is a trainer. Same kind of thing? Or different? Hold your answer. Do not say it yet.

Holding your answer matters. If we say answers out loud one at a time, the first answer changes everyone else. So today we all show at the same time.

That is called commit and reveal. Commit means choose and hold. Reveal means show together.

Do not take an early answer. Name the reason out loud: a first answer said aloud moves everybody else, and today we want each learner own choice.

I Do

I Do: How to commit, then reveal

The diagram has three steps. First you choose. Then you hold, with your card face down. Then we all show at the same time.

The middle step is the one people skip. Holding is what makes the choice yours. If you show early, the person next to you sees it.

Nobody is timed. If you need longer to choose, we wait. The show only happens when everyone has a card face down.

Watch me do it: I take the hats pair. I look at both. I think: are these the same kind of thing? I choose same. I put my card face down. I do not say a word. Now I wait.

Now watch what I do at the show: cards turn over. Some say same. Some say different. I look for one that is not like mine. I ask that person for their reason. I say “that is a good reason”. I do not change their card for them.

Asking for the reason is the whole lesson. It is what respect looks like when two people do not match.

Commit means choose it and hold it.
Model the hold. A learner who shows early has not failed; turn the card back and wait with them. Do not name who chose what during the reveal.

Commit first: take your same card or your different card. Put it face down. Everyone commits at the same time, and nobody shows until every card is down. Then we all hold up together.

Evidence check

At the show we will not have one answer. That is the point. Some will say same. Some will say different. We ask the reason, not the card. A reason makes an answer right.

After the show, one person says a reason. Then a person who chose the other one says their reason. We hear both before anyone changes anything.

Changing your card after you hear a reason is allowed. It is a good sign. Turn it over and say what changed it.

Wait for every card to be face down, however long that takes. A learner who changes after hearing a reason has done the thing this lesson is for.

I Do

I Do: What respect sounds like

There are three lines in the picture. Any of them is a respectful reply. You can read one off the wall. You do not have to make up your own words.

Look at the middle line. “That is a good reason.” You can say it even when you still disagree. It says: I heard you.

Look at the last line. It has two parts. First you say yours is different. Then you say why. Both parts matter.

What is not on the list: “that is wrong”, and “no it is not”. Those two lines stop the talking. The three lines on the wall keep it going.

You will use one of these lines in your record. Pick the one you would really say.

Point to each line as you read it. Keep the wall card up for the rest of the lesson; a learner reading a line off the wall is using the lesson, not avoiding it.

Finished early? Take one pair. Write a reason for the answer you did not choose. Both reasons can be true at once.

If you are stuck, say the two things out loud. “A boot and a trainer.” The reason is usually in what they are for.

Nobody will ask you why you chose a card. You do not have to explain what you eat or what you wear.

Your record is done when every pair has a reason next to it. Read one out loud to yourself. Check it makes sense.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket — Audience receives; influence changes something

Read one reason to a partner or to an adult. The listener says back the reason they heard, in their own words.

Did the listener hear a different reason from the one you meant? Then change one word in yours. That change is your influence. It belongs to you, not to them.

Did the listener hear just what you meant? Write that down too. A reason that already works is worth noting.
