Lesson overview

Listen to Ruth, Then Point at the Line

BUILD · Humanities · Week 3. RE and World Views. Listen to Ruth, Then Point at the Line.

Objective: Listen to the whole approved story of Ruth read aloud, then respond by naming one sign of belonging in it, pointing at the line that shows it, and marking anything you cannot point at as Not sure - I need to hear it again.

The story is Ruth and Naomi. It comes from the Jewish tradition, from the Book of Ruth, chapters 1 and 2, in the Tanakh. Christians read the same book in the Old Testament.

Source label, printed on the pupil card: CONSTRUCTED TEACHING SOURCE - created for classroom practice. It is not an archival original. Simplified retelling of the Book of Ruth, chapters 1 and 2, written by the school Humanities team, September 2026. The card lists the lines that were added so that every sign card has a line.

Materials, one set per pupil: the story card, eight line strips, five sign cards, one three-row record sheet. The RE lead prepares and cuts them. They are in the Week 3 wallet in the Humanities cupboard.

Every line strip is copied word for word from the story card. The story card is the exact text you read aloud. Nothing is reworded.

The five sign cards say, word for word: Invited in · Given a job · Joined in · Missed when away - Ruth · Not sure - I need to hear it again.

The Missed when away card names Ruth on its face, so the sign stays about Ruth in the story and not about anybody in the room.

Success evidence: rows on your sheet. Each row holds a sign of belonging, the line from the story that shows it, and whether you are sure or not sure yet.

No personal or family disclosure. Belonging today means belonging inside the approved story of Ruth. You are never asked about your home, your family, your attendance or your own beliefs.

40 minutes. Offline.

Four things we already know

Belonging means having a place in a group. The group knows you are there. A story can show belonging happening to somebody.

A story is something you listen to. You are allowed to ask to hear it again.

A response is what you say back after listening. A response is not a guess.

Evidence in a story is a line you can point at. If you cannot point at it, you are not sure yet.

Read the four lines aloud once, in order. Do not invite examples from pupils.

Everything today comes from the story card and the line strips. Nothing comes from anyone's home.

How I listen for a sign

BELONGING means having a place in a group. SIGN means a moment in the story that shows belonging. LINE means the exact words from the story that you point at.

Watch me do this once. I have heard the whole story. I wrote nothing while I listened.

I notice one moment. Boaz tells Ruth she can gather the grain that the workers leave behind.

I ask myself which sign card that is. It is the card that says Given a job.

Now I hunt for the line. I read the strips one at a time until I find the exact words.

I put that strip next to my sheet. Then I copy the words onto my sheet.

Here is what I do when I am not sure. I think somebody said Ruth's name out loud as well. I cannot find a strip that says that.

I do not guess. I write the sign, I leave the line blank, and I mark that row Not sure - I need to hear it again.

That card names what I need. I need a second listen. You get a second listen in this lesson, before you start your own rows.

Not sure - I need to hear it again is a real answer. It counts.

Model the Not sure step slowly and out loud. Pupils copy what you do far more than what you say.

I Do 2 · connect

I Do 2 · connect

Accept no sign without a line in this stage.

From a sign to a response another person can check

A sign on its own is only a start. A response needs the line as well.

Watch me again. This time my sign is Joined in.

First I say the sign out loud. Then I read the line from the strip, word for word.

The strip says that at mealtime Ruth sat down and ate with the workers.

Then I add one sentence that joins them. This line shows Joined in, because Ruth ate with the workers.

Every joining sentence starts with the same three words. This line shows.

The joining sentence is the part people leave out. Say it every single time.

Sometimes the line does not really show the sign. Then I change the sign, not the line. The story stays exactly as it is.

A response like this shows you listened properly. You wrote what the story really says, not what you thought it would say.

Ask one pupil to repeat your joining sentence back before you move on.

We Do 2 · lab

We Do 2 · lab

Offer the Supported row to the table first, so choosing it looks normal.

Choose a row and try it

These are rows, not levels. Choose one now and change it whenever you want.

  • ◆ Supported · Match one sign card to one line strip. Say the sign, or point at it.
  • ▲ Standard · Match two sign cards to two line strips. For each one, say how the line shows the sign.
  • ★ Stretch · Match three signs to three lines. For one of them, say why a different sign would not fit.

Every row uses the same story and the same line strips. The row only changes how many matches you make.

If a match will not work, put the strip back and try another one. It is fine to try a strip and put it back.

Say your sign out loud before you write it down.

You can move up a row or down a row at any point. The row is a choice. It is never a label on you.

8 min
Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Two minutes of reading, then seven minutes of doing. Add no more reading. Scribe on request. Write the pupil's words, not a tidier version.

Build your rows

First comes the second listen. I read the whole story aloud one more time. It takes two minutes. You write nothing while I read.

This is the listen that the Not sure card asks for. If you marked a row Not sure, listen for that line now.

Then you have seven minutes to work. Work at your own pace.

Do as many rows as you can do properly. Three is the target. One row done properly is a good lesson.

In each row write the sign, then the line from the story, then sure or not sure.

Use the line strips. Copy the words exactly. Short is fine.

Pointing at a strip and asking an adult to write it counts as your answer.

If you still cannot find the line, write the sign, leave the line blank, and mark it Not sure - I need to hear it again.

There is no prize for finishing first. There is no penalty for being last.

A joining sentence says how the line shows the sign. Start it with the words this line shows.

Add one joining sentence under one of your rows.

Read the whole story card aloud again first, straight through. That is the second listen. Then circulate silently.

Exit · Audience and Influence

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACE: the learner may pause or choose a private response.

VOICE: the evidence statement remains in the learner’s chosen communication mode.

AUDIENCE: a familiar adult receives and names back exactly what was heard or seen.

INFLUENCE: one real next action is recorded: retain, add method detail, or leave open.

Name it back word for word. Do not tidy the phrasing.

You say it, we name it back

Everyone gets a turn. You do not have to use your voice.

Pointing at your sheet counts as speaking. It is the same answer.

Say one sign and the line that goes with it. You can read it from your sheet.

The adult names it back to you, using your exact words.

Then you confirm it, or you correct it. Correcting the adult is expected here.

Every pupil gets that turn. Each turn is short. Nobody is skipped.

Then we choose one next action for the whole class. The adult writes it down.

You may also choose your own action. Say it, and it is written next to your name.

An action can be: keep this row, change the line in this row, or start next lesson with my Not sure row.

Next lesson we start from the actions in this room, not from a new sheet.
