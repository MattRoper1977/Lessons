Belonging is something people do

The Day of Peace falls on 21 September, and it asks people to stop and think about peace.

Peace starts small. It starts with who gets let in.

Belonging is a feeling, and you cannot see a feeling.

But you can see the signs of belonging, because a sign is a thing that somebody does.

Somebody saves a seat, or says a name right, or hands you half of the job.

Those are signs, because you can point at them.

Keep the lesson on the cards. Do not open the topic out to the room; the pack is the guard this lesson rests on.

You need the Day of Peace card pack, the log sheet, and one pencil.

There are ten cards in the pack, and each shows a small scene.

Each card names a place: a school, a team, a street, or a shop.

Under the scene there is one line of writing, and that line is your source.

Three words matter today: sign, place, and report.

A sign is what was done, and a place is where it was done.

To report is to say what the card shows, and nothing more.

Two cards, one chair

Here is card three, and it shows a new pupil standing by a table.

Six people sit there, and nobody moves a bag.

Here is card four, with the same pupil, and this time somebody lifts a bag off a chair.

The line under card four is short, and it says a bag was moved.

That is the whole difference. One bag.

One card shows a sign of belonging, and the other one does not.

You did not need to know anybody here, because you read what was done.

  • 1 · read the line under the scene.
  • 2 · find the thing that somebody did.
  • 3 · name the place from the card.
  • 4 · write one sentence about it.

Look at card three again, because nothing bad happens on it.

Nobody is unkind, and a sign can still be missing.

So we hunt for the doing, and not for the mood.

Reading one card out loud

I take card seven out of the pack, and I look at the scene first.

It shows a football team at the gate.

One player is new, and two players wait until that player catches up.

Now I read the line under the scene, word for word.

It says the team waits at the gate for everyone.

So I have found the sign, and the sign is waiting.

Waiting is a thing you can watch somebody do.

Next I name the place, and the place is the team.

The card says team, so I write team and not school.

Now I write my sentence.

On card seven the team waits at the gate, so the new player is not left behind.

Say each part of the sentence as you write it, and number the parts one, two, three out loud.

Watch where my sentence starts, because it starts with the card number.

The number is how anybody can check me.

Then it says what was done, and then it says who was let in.

Three parts, one sentence, every time.

If a part is missing, the sentence is not done yet.

Sort the cards, then match them

Six cards are on your table, and you sort them into two groups.

Group one shows a sign you can see, and group two shows no sign at all.

Sort by what was done, and not by the faces.

Then match each card in group one to its place.

The places are on four small cards: school, team, street, and shop.

Match the pair, then hold the two of them up together.

Nobody holds anything up until every table is done.

Two of the six are hard, on purpose.

If your table cannot agree, say out loud what was done on the card.

Then sort it again, and take the answer you can point at.

Report the card, do not guess the feeling

There are two ways to write this sentence, and only one of them works.

The first way uses the card, and the card says a bag was moved.

So that is what I write.

Anybody can pick the card up and check me.

The second way guesses, and I write that the new pupil felt happy.

The card does not say that. I made it up.

Nobody can check it, so it is not evidence.

This is the rule for the whole lesson. Write the doing, not the feeling.

A guessed feeling sounds kind, and it still fails.

It puts words in the mouth of a person on a card.

You may write what the card says a person said, because that is still the card.

  • 1 · start with the card number.
  • 2 · say what somebody did.
  • 3 · name the place the card gives.
  • 4 · check that every word came from the card.

Read your sentence back, and hunt for guessed words.

Happy, sad, lonely and scared are all guesses.

Cross them out, and put the doing back in their place.

Card, sign, check

Work in twos, and keep your own card.

Read the line on your card out loud, word for word.

Then say the sign in three words or fewer.

Your partner says one thing back: seen, or guessed.

If they say guessed, do not argue. Read the line again.

Then give a new sign, straight from that line.

Then swap over and do it the other way, and the adult calls the time.

If you both say seen every time, trade cards with the next pair.

A card you did not choose is the harder test.

One quiet minute, then three cards

First we take one quiet minute, because it is the Day of Peace.

Put your pencil down, and look at the top card.

You do not have to shut your eyes.

You do not have to say anything at all.

When the minute ends, you start to write.

Work on your own, and take three cards from the pack.

Fill one row for each card on your sheet.

Box one is the card number, and box two is the sign.

Box three is your sentence, with the place inside it.

Then ring the sentence you think is clearest.

  • Supported · two cards, each with the sign said out loud.
  • Standard · three cards, each with a named sign and a full sentence.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and say why the ringed one is clearest.

Use three different places on your three cards.

Three school cards is really one card, done three times.

If you get stuck, read the line under the scene again.

The sign is nearly always the doing word in that line.

If you finish early, find the card with the smallest sign.

Read one sentence to an adult

Read your ringed sentence to the adult at your table.

They read it back to you word for word.

If that is not what you meant, say it again.

They write the new one down for you.

Then they ask you one thing: which word came from the card?

Point at that word on the card, and you do not have to explain it.

The clearest sentences go on the Day of Peace board.

Your name goes on the board only if you say yes.

Then choose what happens next: keep the sheet, add a fourth card, or write your question down.

Write that choice on the sheet before you go.
