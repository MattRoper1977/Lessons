Arrival Task

Arrival Task – Retrieving how a sequence carries an argument

Nobody is asked what they would have done, or about their own family. This lesson works from dated records. There is no role play and no imagined first person, and you can step out at any point without saying why.
You can work from the strip alone. You can dictate and the words stay yours. You can leave the room without explaining. Each is a full route.
Measure is a rule made in law, published with a date, that changed what people were allowed to do.

These cards are dated records of laws. They are not stories and nobody in them is imagined.

Sequencing shows that. A list of laws in the wrong order hides it.

Arrival decision: Take the six measure cards. Everyone works from the same six, and nobody is asked to add anything of their own.

Say the boundary aloud at the start: dated records, no role play, and leaving is a route. Learners work better when the rule is stated rather than implied.

Starter

Starter: Two orderings of the same six measures

Two learners order the same six cards.

Ordering A puts the most severe measure first, because it is the one that stands out.

Ordering B follows the dates, and the most severe measure comes last.

Ordering B shows something A cannot: that the severe measure came after five smaller ones, each of which made it less remarkable.

Do not let severity drive the ordering. Correcting that instinct is the historical point of the lesson.

I Do

I Do: What each step made possible

The diagram shows the six measures as steps. The height is not severity; it is how far the law had moved from where it started.

Modelled: the register comes first, dated. The next measure excludes a named group from a profession, and it can be applied because a register already exists. So step one made step two administratively possible. That is the sentence pattern for every pair.

Made possible means the earlier step supplied something the later one needed, such as a list or a precedent.
Keep the analysis on mechanism, not on motive or feeling. Mechanism is what the records support and what the outcome asks for.

We Do

We Do: Put the six in date order, then say what each made possible

Cards 1 and 2: which is earlier, and what does the earlier one supply?

Card 4: what had to exist before this could be enforced?

Card 6: what makes this one possible that was not true at card 1?

Sequence first: put your six cards in date order along the table, then write on the strip what each step made possible. Everyone commits at the same time, and then we hold up the first pair together.

Evidence check

If your order disagrees with a date, the date wins. This is one of the few sequences in this course that is not a matter of judgement, and that is itself the finding.

Take the joining sentence from a learner who has not spoken today, and scribe it exactly.

I Do

I Do: What a society can do afterwards

The second diagram shows three things a society does afterwards, and what each one is actually for.

Forgiveness is not on this diagram as a public policy, and that is deliberate. It belongs to the people who were harmed, and nobody else can offer it on their behalf.

Ranked together: for the purpose of establishing a record that outlasts everybody present, the trial ranks first. For the purpose of preventing repetition, teaching does. The ranking depends on the purpose, and the purpose has to be stated.

Hold the line on forgiveness: it is not an obligation, not a stage, and not a task the class sets anyone. If a learner offers a religious framing, receive it as their view and do not generalise it.
