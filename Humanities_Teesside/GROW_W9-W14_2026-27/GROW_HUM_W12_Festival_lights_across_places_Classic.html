Festival lights across places

Autumn 2 · Week 4 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can use named sources to describe festival examples in different places.

Success criteria

  • Locate three country examples by continent.
  • Separate place, practice and meaning.
  • Use a source limit to avoid an overclaim.

Preparation

  • Open the lesson and check all source cards are legible. Print the supplied resources if pupils need a paper route.
  • Use the independent evidence after the model. Keep answers in this guide separate.

Care and safety

  • Use third-person source cases. Do not require a pupil to share religion, home address, family practice or personal experience.

Teacher response guidance

  • Guided: UK/Europe; China/Asia; one named event in each place.
  • C: United States, North America; Hanukkah lights in unspecified cities; no exact city supplied.
  • D: London, UK, Europe; BAPS food collection/distribution for charities; one institution’s action in 2024.
  • Festival practice, nationality and residence are different categories. No supplied source measures all people.

Evidence of learning

Receive the actual decision and reason. A completed activity does not itself certify an award or unit. Record the support actually given.

Access and independence

Offer exact reading or scribing. Allow pointing to evidence. Reduce the number of cards first, then fade the prompt; the pupil still makes the judgement.

Lundy Loop

Give space and a private response route. Receive the pupil’s own choice, name back the evidence heard and agree one actual next teaching move.

Arrival · Place is not belief

Check one country/continent match and the difference between a source creator and a participant. A single example cannot establish what everyone does.

Starter · A festival far from one place

Receive one supported fact from each card. If a pupil generalises to everyone in a city, point to the words one event and ask them to narrow the claim.

I do · Read place and meaning

Do not teach winter as a worldwide property of these festivals. Do not assign 2026 dates from the old event reports.

We do · Build an evidence trail

Expected: London–UK–Europe; Beijing–China–Asia; a named source reports one event in each place. Ask for the source code as well as the location.

I do · Eight days: one source view

Ask which source answers meaning and which supplies an event location. The Reform guide explains rededication; the WJC report locates a Beijing example.

We do · Can we place an unnamed city?

Expected country-level label: United States, cities not named. Reject an invented New York pin even if the pupil knows events may occur there.

Independent · Make a world evidence board

Fresh evidence C and D shifts from the model cases to a country-level report and a new year/action. Avoid inferring recipients’ religions.

Exit · One place, one careful claim

Listen for source, supported action/place and a limit in one sentence. If the pupil claims all participants felt the same, ask what evidence the source actually gives.

Misconceptions

Sources

BAPS London · Diwali and Hindu New Year Annakut 2023
The institution’s account of its 12–13 November 2023 sequence: Sunday prayers and evening fireworks, followed the next day by food offerings and later distribution. One institution and year; practices vary.

World Jewish Congress · Jews around the world celebrate Hanukkah, 19 December 2006
Historical reported candle lighting in Beijing and menorahs in US cities. This is a selected organisational account, not a current population survey.

Movement for Reform Judaism · Chanukah
An archived named Reform Jewish perspective: eight days, rededication, visible lights and public/community gatherings; not every Jewish practice.

BAPS · Diwali and Annakut Celebrations 2024, London
The 2024 institution account records local food-bank/charity support and public community contributions; one named group, not universal Hindu practice.
