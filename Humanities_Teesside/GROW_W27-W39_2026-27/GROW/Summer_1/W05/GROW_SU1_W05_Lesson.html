Opening

GROW · Humanities · Summer 1 · Week 5

Middlesbrough and Helmsley

Learning goal

Compare my locality with a contrasting place.

How does our locality compare with other places?

I can locate places, use maps and grid references, collect observations, distinguish physical and human features and support a comparison with evidence.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Middlesbrough and Helmsley / GROW_SU1_W05

Outcome: Compare my locality with a contrasting place.

Evidence to retain: F: supported comparison and two exit questions.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Co-construct criteria with pupils: size, land use, jobs, transport and physical setting. Use the supplied cited cards for both places. Mark size and job totals as not evidenced in this pack, unless a teacher supplies comparable dated sources. Do not invent a population comparison or infer a lack of a feature from a short source. Compare places rather than people. The diagrams are conceptual teaching pictures, not maps or surveyed street plans.

Access and regulation

Chunk sources and tasks; pre-teach vocabulary with images. Offer sentence stems, organisers, oral rehearsal and typing. Start with one modelled example; extend through a second source, comparison or supported judgement. Check the learner can begin within two minutes, then fade prompts. Begin with a simplified map or data set, then add a stretch layer. Use clear fieldwork roles and a return-to-recording routine.

Give a calm entry and a private way to respond. Offer an agreed pause and one clear step for returning. Accept pointing, speech, drawing, typing or writing without personal disclosure.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Record the learner’s response and the support provided. Use one specific improvement and a fresh attempt to inform the next lesson.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

A Our locality: Middlesbrough

Middlesbrough is on the River Tees. The Tees Transporter Bridge links the Middlesbrough side with Port Clarence across the river. This is evidence about physical setting and a built feature.

Middlesbrough Council: Tees Transporter Bridge

Limit: This source does not establish the size of either settlement or its job totals. It does not mean the bridge is open.

B Contrasting place: Helmsley

Helmsley is a market town in North Yorkshire by the North York Moors. The River Rye, a market square, shops, cafés and restaurants are described by the National Park Authority.

North York Moors National Park Authority: Helmsley

Limit: A visitor page selects features. It does not describe every land use or every job.

C Travel evidence

Middlesbrough Council identifies a town-centre bus station and rail station. The Helmsley visitor page identifies bus routes and a cycleway through the town. Both sources give evidence of transport.

Middlesbrough Council: Public transport and getting around; National Park Authority: Helmsley

Limit: Do not conclude that an unlisted transport type is absent. No journey time is supplied.

D A fair comparison grid

Agree the criteria before comparing. For each criterion, record evidence for Middlesbrough and Helmsley, then explain a similarity or contrast. Write not evidenced when a source cannot answer. Choose one important contrast and cite both sides.
