Read the whole-school card first. The interpretation branches used on this page are the humanities specialisation of the whole-school Reading the Response card, which remains the general instrument. This page layers subject detail on top of it; it does not replace it and is not a second account of the same theory.

Current strengths

  • Motive, condition and trigger are taught explicitly.
  • Supported, Standard and Stretch tasks change the quality of reasoning.
  • The current Lundy slide offers a real next-case decision.

Before the lesson

  1. Prepare two causal chains with similar conditions and different outcomes.
  2. State that the case selection is evidence-led, not popularity-led.
  3. Plan the next-arrival report-back sentence.

Formative hinge

Prompt: If the trigger disappeared, would the outcome still happen? Which cause changes most, and why?
Response patternOne teaching move
SecureCompare a second family or apply a counterfactual.
MixedLabel and connect motive, condition and trigger.
Shared misconceptionRe-model temporal proximity versus causal importance.
Access barrierUse physical chain cards or exact scribing.
Method/data problemRepair a chain built from unsupported or unclear source details.

Active Audience

A challenger first restates the chain and decisive-cause claim, then asks about one causal link. Confidence and speed are not evidence.

Bounded Influence and report-back

From an approved set, pupils select the Week 4 agency case by causal evidence. Staff report what was selected and what evidence constrained the choice.

Evidence boundary

The causal chain can support candidate evidence only when the exact UAS outcome and evidence form are confirmed.

Failure checks

  • Most popular person wins without evidence.
  • A confident speaker dominates Audience.
  • An adult finishes the because-clause.
  • A receipt mark is added to the pupil-written close.
  • Tutor time issues a second next step.
