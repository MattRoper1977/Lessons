# Humanities assessment and Lundy implementation guide

## Status

PROPOSED staff-facing companion. It does not define closure and it does not modify a lesson.

## One architecture, three distinct questions

### Participation

What can pupils genuinely influence, who will hear them and how will they know what happened?

### Historical learning

What disciplinary move is being elicited, what does the response show and what will teaching do next?

### Evidence status

Does the item map to the exact UAS outcome and who is authorised to judge its status?

## The active-receiver protocol

Before praise, challenge or correction, the receiver restates:

1. the enquiry;
2. the pupil's claim;
3. the evidence;
4. the uncertainty, limitation or counterpoint.

This is Audience without a receipt mark.

## The bounded-influence protocol

Before offering a vote or choice, staff state:

- what is open;
- which options are viable;
- what evidence constrains the choice;
- who can act;
- when report-back will happen.

If none of the options can genuinely change, do not present the activity as Influence.

## Historical validity

Participation does not make every claim historically valid. The pupil's view receives due weight; the teacher still protects evidence, chronology, provenance, concepts and assessment criteria.

## Formative response

Use the existing Reading the Response card:

- Secure → transfer or reduce one prompt.
- Mixed → contrast examples and recheck.
- Shared misconception → re-model or counterexample.
- Access barrier → keep the goal, change route.
- Method/data problem → repair source/map/evidence set and repeat.

One next step only.
