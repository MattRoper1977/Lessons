GROW · Humanities · Week 6. RE and World Views. Two Festivals of Light, Compared Fairly.

Objective: read two approved source cards on festivals of light, record what each source says the light stands for, and write one comparison sentence that names both festivals and ranks neither.

Success evidence: a comparison grid filled from both cards, and one sentence that names both festivals with no ranking word in it.

Everything you write comes from the source cards. You report what a source says. Nobody is asked what they believe, what they celebrate, or what happens at home.

40 minutes. Offline.

Building one comparison from the cards

I take Card A first, the Diwali card.

The card says rows of small clay lamps are lit.

It says those lamps are called diyas, and that the light stands for good over ignorance.

I write those three things in the Card A column.

I add nothing I half remember from somewhere else.

Now the step people skip: I ask both cards the same question.

My question is what the light stands for.

If I ask Card A about meaning and Card B about dates, nothing lines up.

Say the same-question rule aloud twice. Mismatched questions are the usual reason a full grid still produces no comparison.

So each row of the grid holds one question only.

Reporting a difference without ranking it

Card B says one more light is added each night.

Card A says many lamps are lit on the same night.

That is a real difference and you may write it.

The trap is the next word you reach for.

Better, worse, only and proper all rank a festival.

Both, each and while report it instead.

So write that both festivals light lamps, while each gives its own reason.

One more rule: write according to Card A, and never write everyone thinks.

One festival can also hold more than one reason.

Card A says Sikhs mark a different event at the same time.

You report both reasons, and you choose between neither.

Hunt the ranking word

Work in twos. Swap grids and read your partner's sentence.

Underline any word that puts one festival above the other.

Name the word you found. Do not say the sentence is wrong.

Your partner swaps that word for both, each or while.

Then swap back and check your own.

If there is no ranking word, check that both festivals are named.

Fill the grid, then write the sentence

Work on your own now. Both cards stay on your desk.

The grid has three rows: what is lit, when, and what it stands for.

Every box reports one card in your own words.

Leave a box empty if the card does not say.

An empty box is honest, and a guessed box is not.

Then write one comparison sentence under the grid.

Name both festivals and use both, each or while.

Then underline the part of your sentence that came from Card B.

  • Supported · fill one row for both cards, then write one sentence naming both.
  • Standard · fill all three rows and write one comparison sentence with no ranking word.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and add a second sentence on a difference the cards report.

Read your sentence to one adult

Read your comparison sentence to the adult at your table.

They read it back exactly as you wrote it.

Then they ask one question: does this rank either festival?

If it does, change that one word and read it again.

Then choose what happens next: keep the grid, add a third source card, or rewrite the sentence.

Write your choice and today's date on the sheet before you leave.
