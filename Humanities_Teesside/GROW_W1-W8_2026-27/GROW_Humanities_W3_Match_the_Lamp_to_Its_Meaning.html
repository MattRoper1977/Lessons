GROW Humanities · Week 3 · RE and World Views.

Objective: explore the festival of Diwali and say what its lamps, patterns and stories mean to the people who celebrate it, using the cards on your table.

Success evidence: a record sheet with at least three rows. Each row names an object, gives the meaning from a source card with its card number, and says how sure you are.

No disclosure. Every photograph, object and story today comes from the approved card set. You are never asked about your own home, your own family or your own beliefs.

The card set is on your table. Everything you say today comes from those cards. Nobody is asked about their own home, their own family or their own beliefs.

True, false, or not sure

  • Diwali is a festival of light.
  • A festival is a special time a group of people celebrate every year.
  • A photograph on its own tells you what an object means.
  • If no card says it, "I am not sure" is a correct answer.

Thumb up means true. Thumb down means false. A flat hand means not sure. A flat hand is a real answer and it is never wrong to use it.

Read each statement aloud once. Count the flat hands first and never name who was wrong. Answers: statement three is false, the other three are true. Do not ask who celebrates this at home. Do not ask a pupil to speak for their faith. Every answer comes from a card.

Photo card 1 shows a doorway at night. The doorway is dark. Photo card 2 shows the same doorway. Now a row of small lamps is lit along the step.

You can see the lamps. You cannot see why they are there. A photograph shows you an object. It does not tell you what the object means. For the meaning we need a source card.

  • Notice: find one thing that changed between the two photographs.
  • Choose: point to one object you want to ask about.
  • Explain: say why the photograph on its own cannot give you the meaning.

Four words

  • Festival: a special time a group of people celebrate every year.
  • Celebrate: to do the same special things at the same time each year.
  • Diya: a small lamp made of baked clay.
  • Symbol: an object that stands for an idea.

Watch me work photo card 3. First I look. I say what I can see: one small clay lamp, lit, on a step. Then I read source card 3. It says many Hindu families light diyas at Diwali. It says the light stands for good.

Next I read the bottom line of the card. That line says who made the card and when. Some cards say written for school. That means an adult wrote the card for lessons, and I say so when I use it.

Now the last column. Source card 3 does say this meaning. So I write: sure, the card says it. I write the card number next to the meaning.

Then I get stuck. I want to know how many lamps each family lights. No card says that. I do not guess. I write: not sure, the card does not say. That row still counts.

An object on its own is only an object. The meaning comes from what people say about it. A source card holds what people say.

Source card 4 is a Hindu story. It says many Hindu families remember Rama and Sita coming home, with lamps lit along the path. Rama and Sita are two people in that story. The card tells you who they are.

Source card 5 is about Sikh people. It says many Sikh people celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas at the same time of year. They remember Guru Hargobind coming out of prison.

Source card 6 is about patterns. A rangoli is a pattern made on the floor by a door. The card says the pattern welcomes visitors to the house.

Same festival. Different objects. Different stories. Every part of that is printed on a card, so every part can be pointed at.

An honest sentence has two parts. Part one is what I can see. Part two is what the card says it means.

Say the names before the slide goes up. Diya = DEE-ya. Rangoli = run-GO-lee. Bandi Chhor Divas = BUN-dee CHOR dee-VAAS. Guru Hargobind = GOO-roo HUR-goh-bind. Read each source card aloud with its bottom line, so the maker and the date are heard too.

Each table set has six object cards and six meaning cards. The board showed only three of each. Your set has more.

  • ◆ Supported: match three object cards to three meaning cards. Use the word bank on your table.
  • ▲ Standard: match four pairs. Then write one sentence: what the card shows, and what the card says it means.
  • ★ Stretch: match four pairs. Then find two cards where different groups celebrate in different ways, and say what is different.

A row is a choice, not a label. You can change row whenever you want to.

Read all three rows aloud before anyone chooses, then read the choice line. Let a pupil move up or down with no comment. Table key: D goes with 4, E with 5, F with 6.

Independent · evidence

Now it is your turn to work. Fill in your record sheet.

Your sheet has three columns. One: the object I can see. Two: the meaning the card gives, with the card number next to it. Three: how sure I am.

There are two answers for how sure I am. Write: sure, the card says it. Or write: not sure, the card does not say. Both of those are correct answers.

Use the words from the card, not words from your head. If no card gives the meaning, that row is a not sure row. It still counts.

Work at your own pace. Three careful rows are better than six rushed ones. The word bank and the cards stay on your table the whole time.

An adult repeats your words back to you, exactly as you said them. Then you say yes, that is right, or no, change it. You are allowed to change it.
