Arrival Task

Arrival Task – What we already know about cause and consequence

You do not have to talk about your own family or why anyone moved. Every task works with the statement cards on the table. Nobody has to say where their family is from. Nothing you say leaves this room.
You can stop for a bit. You can work on your own. You can point instead of speak. You can use the cards only. You can tell an adult what to write. Each one is a full way to do this lesson.
Cause means a reason something happened.
Consequence means what happened as a result.

Last week we read a source. This week we use sources to explain why people moved and what changed.

Last week's timeline showed order. Order is not cause. Today we add the evidence that turns order into explanation.

Arrival decision: Use the statement cards, or add one cause or consequence you can point to in the source pack. Both are full answers. Nobody will ask which one you picked.

Before we start, look at the ten statement cards on your table. Each card is one sentence from the school-approved source pack, with a locator printed under it. Some are causes. Some are consequences. Two event cards sit in the middle: the 1948 Windrush arrival and the 1972 arrivals from Uganda. Everyone in this room can work with these cards from start to end.

Offer the cards first, not as a fallback. A learner who talks about their own family's reasons for moving is not prompted for more; note the type of cause, not the family. Wait; take the least prompt that works.

Starter

Starter: Why is a list not an explanation?

Here are two answers to the question: why did people come to Britain from the Caribbean in 1948?

Answer A: “There was a war. There were jobs. There was a ship.”

Answer B: “After the war, Britain was short of workers, so it invited people from the Caribbean to come and fill jobs, and one consequence was that new communities grew in British cities.”

Answer B tells you more than Answer A. Answer A is a list of true things. Answer B joins them with so and one consequence was. Those joining words turn a list into an explanation.

The need for this lesson is the join. Anyone can list facts. Today you will sort the facts into causes and consequences, and then join them. By the end you will be able to explain one chain and point to the source for it.

Try it in your head: think of a time a lesson was moved to a different room. What caused it? What happened as a result? Say it with the word so in the middle. That is a cause-and-consequence chain.

Do not accept a list of true facts as complete and do not treat it as wrong. Name the gap: we have the facts, we still need the join.

I Do

I Do: Cause, event, consequence

The diagram above has three boxes joined by arrows: cause, then event, then consequence. The cause sits before the event in time. The consequence sits after it. The arrows carry the joining word so.

Notice what the diagram asks for that a timeline did not. Every box needs a source locator. The cause box is dashed because a cause is a claim until a source backs it. Last week's dashed box is now the one we fill.

Watch me do it: I take a statement card. It says the government advertised jobs in the Caribbean. Is that before or after 1948? Before. Does it help explain why people came? Yes. So it is a cause. I put it in the cause pile and read its locator out loud. I ask before or after first. Then I ask does it explain. Then I sort.

Locator means the label that says where in the source pack a sentence came from.
Read the diagram left to right, naming each box and its locator before any card is sorted. A learner who places a consequence card in the cause pile has usually read "before or after" wrongly; ask for the date on the card.

We Do

We Do: Sort three cards. Match one to its event.

Card 1: in August 1972 the ruler of Uganda ordered people of Asian heritage to leave within ninety days.

Card 2: shops and businesses run by families from Uganda opened in towns such as Leicester during the 1970s.

Card 3: Britain's hospitals and transport services recruited workers from the Caribbean in the 1950s.

Sort first: put the three cards into two piles on your table: cause and consequence. Then match each card to one of the two event cards, 1948 or 1972. Everyone sorts at the same time. Then we hold up one card per table and check together.

Evidence check

Card 3 is the one people disagree on. It is a cause of people coming in the 1950s, and a consequence of the 1948 shortage. Both readings are true. When we check, we ask which event you matched it to and which question you used. We do not check the pile alone. A sort with a reason is an explanation.

Have every table sort before any answer is taken, so nobody copies. A card moved after the check is a success, not a correction.

I Do

I Do: Push and pull

The diagram above sorts causes into two kinds. Push causes are on the left: things that make it hard to stay. Pull causes are on the right: things that make a new place attractive. The arrow in the middle is the move itself.

Here is a sort that goes wrong: putting "jobs advertised in Britain" in the push box. Jobs in Britain do not push anyone out of the Caribbean. They pull. The direction of the arrow is the test.

Fixing it together: ask which place the cause is about. If it is about the place people left, it is a push. If it is about the place they went to, it is a pull. "Jobs advertised in Britain" is about Britain, so it goes in the pull box.

Point to each box as you say it. A learner who puts every cause in the push box has usually not applied the direction test; ask which place the card is about.

We Do

We Do: Match causes to consequences together

◆ Supported

Point to the push card and the pull card for 1972. Then choose the consequence card that fits.

▲ Standard

Say a full chain for 1948: one push, one pull, the event, one consequence, in order.

★ Stretch

Build the 1972 chain, then say which of the two causes mattered more and why the source supports that.

We build one chain on the board first. Nobody writes yet. Everyone puts one cause card on a whiteboard, marked push or pull. Then we show them all at once. Then we agree the chain.

The event for this chain is 1972. The consequence is done: shops and businesses opened in towns like Leicester. Your job is to match a cause. Write the card and its locator, not a describing word.

Take the cause card from a learner who has not yet spoken today. Scribe exactly what is said; do not improve the wording.

Independent Work — Your record: why people moved and what changed

16:00

Make one record. You may use the statement cards, or add one cause or consequence you can point to in the source pack, or both. All three are complete answers.

Six cards sorted into cause and consequence, and one cause matched to its event. An adult may scribe your exact words, and the words stay yours. Point to the pile first, then the event.

▲ Standard

Ten cards sorted and matched. Then one chain written in a sentence: cause, so event, so consequence, with a locator for each part. Use a separate row for each card.

★ Stretch

All cards sorted and matched. One chain with a push and a pull. Then a judgement: which cause mattered more, and what on the cards supports that? A judgement that names a limit is still a judgement.

Check yourself: is every card in a pile, cause or consequence?

Check yourself: does every cause match an event card?

Check yourself: does your chain sentence use the word so at each arrow?

Nobody is asked to explain a choice to work from the cards. Record the route used and any scribing on the evidence line, keeping adult support visible and separate from the learner's own words.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket — Audience receives; influence changes something

Read your chain to a partner or to an adult. The listener says back the cause they heard, in their own words.

Did the listener hear a consequence where you meant a cause? Then change the join. That change is your influence. It belongs to you, not to them.

Next week you will look further back, at people from many backgrounds who have lived in Britain for centuries. Keep your chain. It will help you then.
