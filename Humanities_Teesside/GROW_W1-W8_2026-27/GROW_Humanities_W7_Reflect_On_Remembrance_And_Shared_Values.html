GROW · Humanities · Week 7. Remembrance and Shared Values. Remembrance, Reported From the Source.

Objective: report what two cards from the approved Remembrance pack actually say, name the shared value each one shows, and write one sentence saying what the two values share.

Success evidence: two report sentences, each with a quote and a card number, plus one link sentence naming the shared value.

Every word you use comes from the approved source pack. You report what a card says. You are never asked about your home, your family or what you believe, and nobody is asked who agrees.

Remembrance is a public act

Remembrance happens at the same time every year.

It is marked on Armistice Day and on Remembrance Sunday.

People stand still for two minutes. Many wear a poppy.

Different groups mark the day in different ways.

The cards in your pack say so in their own words.

Your job today is to report those words. It is not to settle them.

Read the three key words once and leave them on the board. The pack is the guard the whole lesson rests on; no examples are invited from the room.

You need three things. The source pack. The value list. Your report sheet.

Three words matter today. Source. Value. Report.

A source is a card holding words somebody really wrote or said.

A value is what a group says matters, such as respect or peace.

A report tells a reader what the card says, with the card named.

Two sentences about one silence

Here are two sentences about the two-minute silence.

Sentence A says everyone in the country feels the same thing.

Sentence B says the Royal British Legion asks for silence at eleven o'clock.

Only one of those can be checked by a reader.

Sentence B names who said it, so a reader can go and look.

Sentence A speaks for a whole country. No source can carry that weight.

Naming who said the words is called attribution.

Attribution is the difference between evidence and a guess.

  • 1 · find the words on the card.
  • 2 · say which card they came from.
  • 3 · name the value those words show.
  • 4 · keep your own view out of the report.

Reporting one card

I take card three from the pack. It is a memorial inscription.

The card says the names were carved so that they would be read.

Step one. I quote it. I copy six words exactly, inside speech marks.

Six words is enough. A long quote buries the point I am making.

Step two. I attribute it. These words come from card three.

Step three. I name the value from the list. This card shows remembrance.

Step four. I join the three parts into one sentence.

Card three shows remembrance, because the names were carved to be read.

Notice what I left out. I did not write how the card makes me feel.

A feeling is real, but a reader cannot check it against the pack.

Say the four step names aloud as you write. Quote, attribute, value, sentence. The steps are the thing being taught, not the finished sentence.

Sort the cards, then match the value

You have six statement cards on your table.

Sort the six cards into two groups: reported and unsourced.

A reported card names who said the words. An unsourced card does not.

Then match each reported card to one value from the value list.

Two cards are hard on purpose. Put those in the middle and say why.

If your table disagrees, both sides give one reason. We do not vote.

Two cards, one shared value

Now I take card seven, a Commonwealth war graves guide.

It says the graves are kept alike, whatever rank the person held.

That card names a different value. The value is equal respect.

So I have two cards and two values. Now I look for what they share.

Both cards keep something in public so that others can see it later.

That is my link sentence, and it still names both card numbers.

The word everybody is your warning sign.

No card in the pack can speak for everybody.

Swap everybody for a card number and the sentence becomes evidence.

Check a partner's report

Work in twos. Swap sheets and read your partner's in silence.

Find the card number in each sentence and underline it.

If a sentence has no card number, tell them which one is missing it.

They add the number. They do not rewrite the whole sentence.

Then hunt for three words: everyone, always and nobody.

Ring any you find. Those words claim more than a card can show.

Swap back and run the same two checks on your own sheet.

Two reports and one link

Work on your own now. Choose two cards from the pack.

Write one report sentence for each card, using the four steps.

Each sentence needs a six-word quote, a card number and a named value.

Then write one link sentence saying what the two values share.

Read all three sentences back and check none of them speaks for everybody.

Cards stay on your desk while you write. Do not work from memory.

  • Supported · one report sentence with a quote, a card number and a value.
  • Standard · two report sentences and one link sentence naming the shared value.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and name one value the two cards do not share.

If you finish early, find a third card showing the same shared value.
