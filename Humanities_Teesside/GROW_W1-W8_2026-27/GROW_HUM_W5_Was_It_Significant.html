3 min
Arrival · retrieve

Retrieving the tests

Two weeks ago you explained why people moved and what changed. Today you judge how much a change mattered.

The three tests are: how many, how long, and how different afterwards.

Arrival decision: Choose an event card from the set on your table. Every card carries the same three tests on its back.

On the table are eight event cards from the local source pack. A dock opening. A works closing. A new railway line. A bridge built. Each card gives the date, roughly how many people it touched, and what the place looked like ten years later.

Offer the frame first. A learner who reaches for fame as a reason has used the everyday meaning of the word; name the shift rather than marking it wrong.

3 min
Starter · create the need

A judgement that does not hold

Two judgements about the same event, the opening of a dock.

Judgement A: “The dock opening was significant because everybody has heard of it.”

Judgement B: “The dock opening was significant because it brought work to about four thousand people, and the town was still built around it fifty years later.”

Ask both of them one question: how do you know? B answers with a number and a span of years. A answers with fame, and fame is not one of the tests.

That is the error you will hunt today. It is common. It sounds confident. It falls over as soon as somebody asks for the evidence.

Try it: take Judgement A and ask how many, how long, how different. Notice that none of the three has an answer in it.

Do not supply the correction. The gap between the claim and the tests is what the learners are meant to find.

4 min
I Do · model

The three tests, applied

Look at the picture. Each box wants an answer, not an adjective. How many is a number. How long is a span of years. How different means asking what would have happened anyway.

Two boxes with answers is enough. Three is stronger. None, and the claim has nothing under it.

Modelled: the works closing. How many: about eleven hundred jobs. How long: the site stood empty for thirty years. How different: the town would have kept growing without it, so this is a change, not a drift. Two numbers, one comparison, judgement holds.

Evaluate means weighing something against a test, not just describing it.

Model a weak judgement as well as a strong one. Learners who only ever see confident examples learn to sound confident rather than to weigh.

3 min
We Do · spot the error

Find what is wrong with this judgement

Sentence 1: “It was significant because it was in the papers for weeks.”

Sentence 2: “It was significant because about four thousand people were affected.”

Sentence 3: “It was significant because everyone still talks about it.”

Spot the error first: decide which sentences fail a test and which one holds. Everyone commits on a whiteboard, then we hold up the boards together and correct the sentences as a class.

Two of these fail for the same reason, and one holds. What is wrong with the failing ones is not that they are untrue. It is that what they offer is not one of the three tests.

Take the correction from a learner who has not spoken today. Scribe the exact words; the wording is the evidence.

3 min
I Do 2 · connect

Rewriting so the evidence carries the claim

The diagram above has three boxes. The claim, the evidence, and the link that joins them.

Evidence next to a claim does not prove it. The link is what turns two facts into an argument.

Rewritten together: the dock opening was significant, because it brought work to about four thousand people, which is why the town was still built around it fifty years later.

Insist on the link. A learner who writes claim and evidence has done two thirds; name the missing third rather than accepting the pair.

4 min
Exit · Audience and Influence

Audience receives; influence changes something

Read your judgement to a partner. The listener says back which test your evidence answered.

Did the listener name a different test, or none? Change one phrase so the evidence points where you meant. That change is your influence.

Next week you plan a structured account, and this judgement is the kind of sentence it will end on.

Close by naming one learner whose phrasing changed after their partner named the wrong test. That is the influence record.
