Eight Nights, One Lamp, and What the Light Is For

GROW · Humanities · Week 4. RE and World Views. Eight Nights, One Lamp, and What the Light Is For.

Objective: read the approved Hanukkah source, record three things the lamp does in the festival, and explain in one sentence what the light is said to stand for.

Success evidence: three recorded details from the source, each with the line it came from, and one sentence explaining what the light stands for.

Everything comes from the approved source cards. You report what the source says, not what anyone in the room believes.

40 minutes. Offline.

Arrival · retrieve

Arrival · retrieve

Reporting is not agreeing

In this subject you write what a group of people say and do.

You are not asked whether you think it is true, and you are not asked what you believe.

That rule protects everybody, including anybody in this room who does keep this festival.

It also makes the work checkable, because a report can be compared with the source.

Today the source is two cards about Hanukkah and one picture of a hanukkiah.

Do not ask who in the room is Jewish, and stop the question if a pupil asks it. The source is the subject.

You need both source cards, the picture card and the three-column record sheet.

Starter · create the need

Starter · create the need

Two records of the same card

Record A says: they light candles for eight days.

Record B says: one candle is lit on the first night, and one more is added each night until eight are burning, card one line four.

Both are honest and both came from the same card.

The second one can be checked, because it says where it came from.

It also carries the detail that makes the festival make sense: the number grows.

So every line you write today ends with the card and the line it came from.

  • 1 · read the line.
  • 2 · write what it says, in your words.
  • 3 · write the card and line number.
  • 4 · check somebody could find it.

I Do · model

I Do · model

Three details, three references

I read card one twice, then look away before I write anything.

Detail one: the lamp has nine branches, not eight. Card one, line two.

The ninth is the shamash, the one used to light the others.

Detail two: a new candle is added each night, so the light grows across the eight nights.

Card one, line four. That is the detail record A missed.

Detail three: the lamp is placed where it can be seen from outside. Card two, line one.

Three details, three references. Anybody at this table could check all three in a minute.

Read the shamash detail slowly and let pupils say the word. Getting a term right is part of the respect.

Sort the statements

Six statement cards are on your table. Sort them into on the source and not on the source.

The lamp has nine branches. Everyone enjoys the festival. A candle is added each night.

It is the most important Jewish festival. The lamp is placed in a window. It is celebrated in winter.

Then match each on-the-source card to the card and line it came from.

When your table is done, hold up the one that surprised you.

7 min
I Do 2 · connect

I Do 2 · connect

From details to what it stands for

Three details on their own are a description. The next step is what they add up to.

The light grows each night, and the lamp is put where people outside can see it.

The source says the light stands for a small amount lasting longer than anyone expected.

Notice how the details and the meaning fit together. The growing light is the meaning, shown.

Write the meaning as the source gives it, and keep it separate from anything you think about it.

If you want to add what you think, put it on the back of the sheet where it belongs.

We Do 2 · commit on the second pair

We Do 2 · lab

Find it on the card

Work in twos. Read your partner one of your three details.

They take the card you named and find the line.

If they cannot find it in fifteen seconds, your reference needs to be more exact.

Fix the reference, not the detail.

Then swap over and do the same for theirs.

Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

Three details and one meaning

Work on your own. Read, look away, then write.

Record three details from the source, each with its card and line.

Take at least one from card two, so you are not working from a single card.

Then write one sentence saying what the source says the light stands for.

If you finish early, write one question the source does not answer.

  • Supported · two details with references, and the meaning sentence said aloud.
  • Standard · three details with references, and a written sentence on what the light stands for.
  • Stretch · as Standard, and write one question the source does not answer.

Exit · Audience and Influence

Read the meaning sentence to an adult

Read your sentence about what the light stands for to the adult at your table.

They read it back exactly and check it against card two.

If it does not match, say it again with the card in front of you.

Then choose what happens next: keep the sheet, add a fourth detail, or write your question down.

Your choice goes on the sheet before you leave.
