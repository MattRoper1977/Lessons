3 min
Starter · create the need

Two ways of marking the same paragraph

Two comments on the same model account.

Comment A: “Good work. Well done.”

Comment B: “Claim in line one. Evidence in line two. No explanation between them, so step two has not started yet.”

Comment B tells the writer what to do next. Comment A tells them nothing they can act on.

The difference is that B names parts and points at a place. That is what annotating is, and it is what you will do today.

Try it: find the line in the model where you would write no explanation yet. Do not write on it yet. Just find it.

Do not name the gap in the model. Finding it is the task.

4 min
I Do · model

Annotating against the ladder

The diagram shows the ladder. Step one asks whether all four parts are there. Step two asks whether the explanation says why. Step three asks whether the source is evaluated.

You annotate by writing the step number in the margin at the point where you can see it, and by marking the line where the next step would begin.

Modelled: I read line one. Claim, so I write step one starts here. Line two gives me numbers, still step one. Line three tells me what the numbers were, not why they matter, so I mark on the margin: step two would begin here.

Evaluate a source means asking who made it, when, and how much weight it can carry.

Model the margin mark, not just the judgement. Learners copy the format they see; a step number and an arrow is faster and clearer than a sentence.

We Do · annotate the model

Label the model against the ladder

Line 1: what part is this, and which step does it belong to?

Line 3: does this say why, or does it say what again?

The quotation: what is missing before it can be evaluated?

Annotate first: label the model on your own copy, in the margin, and mark on the line where step two would begin. Everyone commits at the same time, then we hold up the copies together.

We will not all mark the same line. When we compare, we ask what the line does, not who marked where. A mark with a reason stands.

Take the margin note from a learner who has not spoken today. Scribe the exact words; the note is the assessed evidence.

3 min
I Do 2 · connect

Marking your own account

Look at the picture. Your own account gets exactly two marks. One says what you have reached. One points at where the next step would start.

If you reached step one and marked where step two starts, that is a complete piece of assessment.

Marked together: reached step one, all four parts present. Step two would start at the sentence beginning the numbers show, which currently repeats the numbers instead of explaining them.

Discourage long self-comments. Two marks against a ladder is the skill; paragraphs of self-criticism are not.

Independent · evidence

Your record: the account and its marks

Write the account from your plan, then annotate it. The marks are as much the evidence as the writing is.

◆ Supported

Write two sentences of the account and label the parts on the model. An adult may scribe and the words stay yours.

▲ Standard

Write the account and mark the step reached plus where the next one starts.

★ Stretch

Write the account, mark both, and annotate the source evaluation.

Check yourself: does every part of your plan appear in the account?

Check yourself: does your explanation sentence say why rather than repeating the evidence?

Check yourself: do your two marks point at places, not at feelings?

A finished record: account of four sentences; margin mark step one reached, all parts present; second mark at the sentence beginning the table shows, where step two starts because it repeats the numbers instead of explaining them.

Record route and scribing on the evidence line, keeping adult support visible and separate from the learner own words.

4 min
Exit · Audience and Influence

Audience receives; influence changes something

Read your marked sentence to a partner. They say back what they think the next step needs.

Did they name a different step? Change one mark, or one word in the sentence, so the page says what you meant. That change is your influence.

Next week the map and the atlas, and a different kind of evidence.

Close by naming one learner whose mark moved after a partner read it. That is the influence record.
