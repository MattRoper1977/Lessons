Why move? What changes?

Stage 1 of 8 · 3 minutes

Arrive

Distinguish a reason for moving from a consequence.

Think, point, speak, draw or write. You may pass or ask for a quiet start. An adult may scribe your own words.

Stage 2 of 8 · 5 minutes

Read the source

CONSTRUCTED TEACHING SOURCE: fictional classroom example, not an archival original.

Fictional case: Sam moves to a new town to start an offered job.

After the move, Sam uses a new bus route and meets new colleagues.

Sam still keeps in contact with old friends.

This case illustrates a possible experience, not every migrant's experience.

Stage 3 of 8 · 5 minutes

Watch a model

  1. Cause: the offered job helps explain why Sam moved.
  2. Consequence: the new bus route happens after the move.

Point to the detail that supports the answer.

Stage 4 of 8 · 4 minutes

Choose

Which is a cause in this case?

Choose first. Give a reason before checking.

Stage 5 of 8 · 3 minutes

Check

Reveal the deciding clue

C

The job helps explain the move; the other details follow it.

Keep your first choice. If you change it, say what changed your mind.

Stage 6 of 8 · 12 minutes

Your evidence

Your tasks

  1. Sort job, new bus route, new colleagues and old friends into cause, consequence or continuity.
  2. Write: Sam moved because ___. As a result, ___.
  3. Give one reason this case cannot explain every person's move.

Writing stays in this open page only. It is not saved or sent. Copy what you want to keep before leaving.

Stage 7 of 8 · 5 minutes

Improve

Check your answer against the source. Show the detail you used.

Add evidence, improve a link, or mark a claim you cannot support.

Keep your first attempt visible.

Stage 8 of 8 · 3 minutes

Reflect

A cause explains why. A consequence explains what followed.

Teacher guidance

Preparation, access and misconceptions

Print the matching pupil sheets. Provide pencils and coloured pencils; pointing or adult scribing is available.

Think, point, speak, draw or write. You may pass or ask for a quiet start. An adult may scribe your own words.

Watch for: Order alone does not prove causation. Avoid a single-reason story about all migration.

Use the matching pupil sheets for any map, table or writing frame named in these tasks.

Teacher key · task answers
  1. Cause: offered job. Consequences: new bus route/new colleagues. Continuity: old friends.
  2. Job; new bus route or colleagues, linked accurately.
  3. People move for different or multiple reasons; one fictional case is not evidence of everyone.
