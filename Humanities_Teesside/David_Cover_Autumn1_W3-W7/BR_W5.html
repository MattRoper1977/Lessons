Similar actions, different meanings

Stage 1 of 8 · 3 minutes

Arrive

Identify one similarity and one difference between two source cards.

Offer pointing, circling, drawing or scribed answers. Read cards aloud. No personal, family or faith disclosure is required.

Stage 2 of 8 · 5 minutes

Read the source

CLASSROOM FACT CARDS — short paraphrases of named community sources. These examples do not represent every person in a tradition.

D1. At Diwali, many Hindus place small lamps called diyas around homes or places of worship.

D2. Hindu American Foundation describes the light as connected with good overcoming evil and knowledge overcoming ignorance.

C1. At Christmas, Christians celebrate the birth of Jesus.

C2. Church of England worship may use a candle when describing Jesus as the light of the world.

Stage 3 of 8 · 5 minutes

Watch a model

  1. Both cards describe using light.
  2. The sources connect that light with different religious meanings.

Point to the detail that supports the answer.

Stage 4 of 8 · 4 minutes

Choose

Both groups use light. What can we conclude?

Choose first. Give a reason before checking.

Stage 5 of 8 · 3 minutes

Check

Reveal the deciding clue

B

A shared visible action does not establish an identical belief or a ranking.

Keep your first choice. If you change it, say what changed your mind.

Stage 6 of 8 · 12 minutes

Your evidence

Your tasks

  1. Underline the object or action using light in D1 and C2.
  2. Complete a two-row grid: festival / source meaning.
  3. Finish: Both ___, but Diwali ___ while the Christmas source ___.

Writing stays in this open page only. It is not saved or sent. Copy what you want to keep before leaving.

Stage 7 of 8 · 5 minutes

Improve

Check your answer against the source. Show the detail you used.

Point to one match. Say or show why it fits.

Keep your first attempt visible.

Stage 8 of 8 · 3 minutes

Reflect

Name a similarity without saying the beliefs are the same.

Teacher guidance

Preparation, access and misconceptions

Print the four lines and comparison grid. Pupils may draw a lamp/candle as a learning diagram; no worship action or flame is needed.

Offer pointing, circling, drawing or scribed answers. Read cards aloud. No personal, family or faith disclosure is required.

Watch for: Do not turn a source about many people into a claim about everyone. Do not ask pupils to speak on behalf of a religion.

Use the matching pupil sheets for any map, table or writing frame named in these tasks.

Teacher key · task answers
  1. Diyas in D1; a candle in C2.
  2. Diwali: good/knowledge overcoming evil/ignorance. Christmas: Jesus as light of the world.
  3. Example: Both use light, but Diwali links it with good and knowledge while the Christmas source links it with Jesus.
