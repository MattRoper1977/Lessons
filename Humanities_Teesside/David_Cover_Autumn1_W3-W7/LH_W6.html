Arrive

Write a connected account and explain a viewpoint on peace.

Stage 2 of 8 · 5 minutes

Read the source

SECONDARY TEACHING SUMMARY: The National Archives and UK Parliament.

A. In 1948, Empire Windrush brought passengers from the Caribbean to Britain.

B. In 1963, Bristol campaigners opposed a bus company's racial discrimination.

C. Paul Stephenson and other organisers helped lead that campaign.

D. The 1965 Race Relations Act prohibited racial discrimination in some public places.

Stage 3 of 8 · 5 minutes

Watch a model

  1. Question: How did people challenge unfair treatment in Britain?
  2. Use a claim, selected evidence and explanation. Qualify claims that go beyond the sources.

Point to the detail that supports the answer.

Stage 6 of 8 · 12 minutes

Your evidence

Beliefs and values

Distinguish an observance from the reasons different people may give for valuing peace.

CLASSROOM SOURCE SUMMARIES — paraphrases or explicitly labelled fictional applications; not quotations. Use within the one 40-minute Humanities/RE lesson.

R1. The Royal British Legion links remembrance with hope for peace and says wearing a poppy is a choice.

A1. Matthew 5:9 presents Jesus praising people who make peace.

B1. Humanist ethical thinking can use empathy and experience to consider how actions affect others.

Your tasks

  1. Write two connected paragraphs answering the question.
  2. Add a source judgement: usefulness and limitation.
  3. Using A1 and B1, suggest two different reasons for peaceful action. Use R1 to explain why this does not require identical remembrance practices.

Writing stays in this open page only. It is not saved or sent. Copy what you want to keep before leaving.

Keep your first attempt visible.

Stage 8 of 8 · 3 minutes

Reflect

Which claim is strongest, and what supports it?

Teacher guidance

Preparation, access and misconceptions

Print the matching pupil sheets. Provide pencils and coloured pencils; pointing or adult scribing is available.

Think, point, speak, draw or write. You may pass or ask for a quiet start. An adult may scribe your own words.

Watch for: Remembering is not the same as celebrating conflict. No personal bereavement disclosure is required.

Use the matching pupil sheets for any map, table or writing frame named in these tasks.

Teacher key · task answers
  1. Accept campaign and legal change with dates and explained links.
  2. The summaries identify key events; need more primary voices and outcome evidence.
  3. A1 offers a religious reason through Jesus’ teaching; B1 offers a non-religious reason through people’s experiences and effects on wellbeing. R1 makes poppy wearing a choice. These sources do not establish how every member of a group observes remembrance.
