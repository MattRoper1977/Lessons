LAUNCH · Humanities

A growing town, an urbanising region?

Autumn 2 · Week 3 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can explain settlement growth and judge whether evidence shows urbanisation.

Success criteria

  • Calculate and compare urban population shares.
  • Build an evidence-linked possible cause chain.
  • Weigh a planning trade-off and a limit.

Use Next to begin. Ask for word help or your usual support when needed.

Arrival · 2 min

What changed, and at what scale?

Retrieve the geography tools.

What does a map key explain?

What is a settlement?

What is the difference between a number and a share?

Today we separate town growth from urbanisation.

Settlement

A place where people live.

Urbanisation

A growing share of people living in urban areas.

Definition follows the UN Population Division.

Starter · 3 min

A bigger town proves urbanisation?

One town grows from 18,000 to 31,000 people.

The town has grown.

What else must we know to judge the region’s urban share?

Commit to an answer before the model.

Known

One town’s population rose.

Missing

The whole region’s urban and total population.

Constructed starting example.

I do · 5 min

Use the whole population

A share compares a part with the whole.

Divide urban people by all people.

Multiply by 100 to get the percentage.

Compare the two percentages.

Use the same region and definitions.

Method

Urban share = urban people ÷ all people × 100.

Example

54,000 ÷ 100,000 × 100 = 54%.

Conclusion

67% is greater than 54%. Urbanisation is shown in this model.

Constructed data. This is a change in share, not just a rise in population.

We do · 6 min

Urban share lab

Three regions have different changes.

Choose a region and read both totals.

Predict whether urbanisation is shown.

Check the shares.

Explain why rising urban numbers may not be enough.

I do · 4 min

Explain a possible cause

Build a connected chain.

Connect transport, access, jobs and homes.

Use may or could until the cause is tested.

Identify one possible cost.

A possible explanation to test. It is not proof that a bridge always causes growth.

We do · 5 min

One change, different effects

Compare the two building options.

Who might gain from homes near the station?

What could make the old works site costly?

What might be lost if fields are built on?

Name the evidence still needed.

Standard

Use R to judge urbanisation. Explain T’s possible growth through two quantified changes. Weigh the two housing options and give one limit.

Use because, therefore and however, then remove the frame.

Stretch

Distinguish a five percentage-point rise from relative percentage change. Explain why a reclassified boundary could alter an apparent trend, and what comparison you would need.

Care and safety

Teacher response guidance

  • A:54%→67%; B:60%→60%; C:50%→40%. Urban numbers alone do not establish urbanisation.
  • R:48,000/80,000×100=60%;78,000/120,000×100=65%. Increase is five percentage points; urbanisation is shown under the fixed definitions.
  • T: population+7,000; built land+3 km²; jobs+2,000; bus trip shorter by15 minutes. A plausible chain links access/jobs/housing, with causation kept tentative.
  • Old works: proximity to station versus cleaning need; fields: space versus open-land loss and possible extra car trips. Either argued choice can be valid; there is no cost or traffic measurement to settle it.
  • Stretch relative urban-share increase is (65−60)/60×100 ≈8.3%, different from five percentage points.

Check settlement means a place where people live, a key explains symbols and share compares part with whole. Clarify urbanisation before any calculation if needed.

Starter · A bigger town proves urbanisation?

Expected missing evidence: the region’s urban and total populations at both times. One growing town alone cannot establish a rising regional urban share.

I do · Use the whole population

Separate a 13 percentage-point increase from a 13% relative increase. No global contemporary population statistic is required.

We do · Urban share lab

Expected A54→67%, B60→60%, C50→40%. Require prediction first. If urban numbers drive a wrong answer, compare the whole-population denominators.

I do · Explain a possible cause

The donor’s planning-token gauges are replaced by transparent demographic evidence. The chain is a hypothesis, not a causal result derived from co-occurrence.

We do · One change, different effects

Accept reasoned trade-offs, not a predetermined winning policy. Site condition, cost, transport capacity and residents’ views would need actual enquiry.

Independent · Give a qualified explanation

The independent data are new. R is the region; T is one settlement inside it. Do not substitute T’s population for the region’s whole population.

Exit · State what the numbers prove

Expected independent urban share60→65%, a five percentage-point rise. Receive one observed change and distinguish it from a tentative causal explanation.
