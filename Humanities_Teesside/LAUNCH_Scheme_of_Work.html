LAUNCH Humanities — Teesside Migration & Identity (GCSE Bridge)

Upgraded Scheme of Work · Autumn 1 (8 weeks) · AQA UAS 'History around us' (unit unconfirmed — centre record) + GCSE AO bridge · LAUNCH tier · ★ Week 7 assessed

Unit enquiry

How can pupils reason like GCSE historians — weighing sources, arguing causation in a courtroom, judging utility, change and agency, and sustaining a thesis — all rooted in the migration story of the Tees Valley, and transferring that reasoning to an unfamiliar problem?

Connected studio journey

INVESTIGATE THE SOURCE → TRY THE CAUSES → JUDGE THE ARCHIVE → WEIGH THE CHANGE → DEBATE AGENCY → SUSTAIN A THESIS → ⚖ ASSESS THE SOURCES (assessed) → TRANSFER TO AN UNFAMILIAR MAP

Each week retrieves a judgement from the last and hands one forward; Weeks 1–6 build directly toward the Week 7 assessed source assessment, and Week 8 transfers the reasoning to an unfamiliar OS-map problem and turns a pupil's recommendation into a real change a named adult acts on and reports back.

LAUNCH-tier (GCSE-bridge) design

Progression is GCSE reasoning made accessible: weigh how far a source supports a claim, argue causation as decisive/contributing/background, judge source utility for a specific enquiry, weigh change against continuity, debate agency against structure, and sustain a controlling thesis. "Receipt before response" governs the courtroom gallery — reasoning is validated, not popularity. Personal disclosure is never required; whose evidence and whose voice are explicit questions. Adult support and pupil authorship are recorded separately; the loop closes with a report-back contract a named adult must honour. The AO mapping is developmental, not a GCSE grade.

★ Assessment (Week 7)

Week 7 is the assessed source assessment. Apply the centre-approved assessment conditions and each learner's agreed access arrangements (documented in the Week 7 support record). Weeks 1–6 build the thesis plan and the agreed support; Week 8 uses the assessment evidence to steer transfer and intervention.

Evidence lift (the ladder)

LAUNCH entry = weigh or judge with support; LAUNCH secure = substantiate with evidence and counter-evidence; GCSE reach = sustain a thesis, weigh competing evidence with counter-argument, and transfer the reasoning to an unfamiliar enquiry.

Week 1: Source Investigation

Enquiry: How far does a source support a claim? (content → provenance → context → corroborate → weigh)

Retrieves: unit start. Feeds: the unresolved source issue sets Week 2's causal charge.

Week 2: Cause and Consequence Courtroom

Enquiry: Which causes were decisive, contributing or background?

Retrieves: Week 1's source issue. Feeds: the appeal question selects Week 3's archive source.

Week 3: Archive — Nature, Origin and Purpose

Enquiry: What is the source genuinely useful for?

Retrieves: Week 2's causal verdict. Feeds: utility decisions control the evidence admitted to Week 4.

Week 4: Century of Change

Enquiry: How far was the period a turning point? (change vs continuity)

Retrieves: Week 3's admitted evidence. Feeds: the turning point and continuity open Week 5's agency debate.

Week 5: People and Modern Britain

Enquiry: How far did individuals shape modern Britain? (agency vs structure)

Retrieves: Week 4's turning-point judgement. Feeds: the defended judgement becomes Week 6's thesis choice.

Week 6: Structured Account

Enquiry: How can a thesis control a sustained argument?

Retrieves: Week 5's defended judgement. Feeds: the plan and access contract set Week 7 conditions.

★ Week 7: Source Assessment (assessed)

Enquiry: How useful are the sources for the enquiry?

Retrieves: Week 6's thesis plan and access contract. Feeds: assessment evidence determines Week 8 transfer and intervention.

Week 8: OS Map Skills — Unfamiliar Problem

Enquiry: How can map evidence solve an unfamiliar problem?

Retrieves: Week 7's assessed judgement. Feeds (closes): a named adult acts on one evidence-led recommendation and reports back.
