Independent application

Choose one route and one response mode. Keep the same learning goal.

Supported task

  1. Use the practice numbers or the teacher’s checked record and label which it is.
  2. Choose one role and complete evidence, action and indicator boxes with a frame.
  3. Independently explain your contribution and how another person can check it.

Use highlighted evidence and the frame. An adult may read or scribe your own words.

Explain a second detail if ready.

Standard task

  1. Identify one manageable paper-use action from the supplied evidence.
  2. Agree roles, baseline, counting rule, proposed target and review date.
  3. Independently explain one contribution, its indicator and a limit on the conclusion.

Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Compare your reasoning with one alternative.

Stretch task

  1. Check whether baseline and trial conditions can be compared fairly.
  2. Plan roles and a measurable indicator with an anonymised recording method.
  3. Explain another factor that could change the result and how to check it.
  4. Independently justify your contribution and the next action if the target is not met.

Use the source limits and test a different priority or assumption.

Propose a further evidence check and explain its value.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Prepare the cards, organiser and one task route. Model the four steps before independent work. Use the supplied source record and distinguish real evidence from authored scenarios. The teacher supplies the school’s copy of No One Is Too Small by Greta Thunberg; no extract or quotation is included. Use a dated anonymised school record if the teacher can supply one. Otherwise use the clearly labelled 12-to-8 practice count. Check permission, clean paper, comparable conditions, roles and a review date. Link the small action to the school’s Community Project.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. Use labelled examples and a reduced data set before the full task.

Offer a calm start, an agreed pause and a private response option. Accept speech, pointing, typing or writing. Invite reasoned disagreement without personal or family disclosure.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Record the learner’s own reasoning and support used. Keep the conclusion individual and use the exit to plan one next step.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Arrival answers

1. Four fewer new sheets for the stated task.

2. The checker.

3. New sheets issued per repeated task.

4. Otherwise another difference might explain the changed count.

Shared activity reasoning

1. AUTHORED PRACTICE baseline: 12 new sheets for the task — Cause. This is the labelled starting count, not a real school finding.

2. Trial an adult-approved clean-paper reuse tray — Consequence. This is the planned contribution between the two checks.

3. Count new sheets for the same task: practice trial 8 — Indicator. The repeated count checks the result; it is not a CO2 measurement.

Exit responses

Explain how your contribution supports the action. Link the chosen role to the action and its indicator, for example recording new sheets issued.

How will the indicator be checked fairly? Repeat the same counting rule for a comparable task and group, and state any difference in conditions.

A measurable community contribution

Learning goal: Link learning to local action with Community Project links.

Words to use

WordMeaning
baselineA starting measurement for comparison
indicatorA measure used to check progress
roleA defined contribution to a shared task
contributionWhat a person adds to an agreed action

Remember

Label the baseline and trial as local evidence or authored practice.

Use the same indicator and comparable conditions for both checks.

How to show your learning

Check whether baseline and trial conditions can be compared fairly.

Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Evidence matters

Fictional cases are teaching examples. Historical, geographical and religious claims use the named sources. A model video demonstrates a method.

You can respond by speaking, pointing, drawing, typing or writing. A fictional personal example is welcome.

Sources: Evidence and factual claim record; Exact unit scheme and access text; Authored scenarios and limits; Teacher-supplied book and source checks

Arrival choices

Start here: a baseline is a starting count; an indicator checks progress. Card B gives a complete labelled practice example if local evidence is unavailable.

Start with 1 and 2. Try 3 and 4 when ready. Reading and exact scribing are available.

1 What difference do the practice counts show?

Support: Subtract the trial from the baseline.

2 Which role repeats the count?

Support: Read card C.

3 What is the indicator in card D?

Support: Name exactly what is counted.

4 Why keep conditions comparable?

Support: Think about task and group size.

Stretch: use a precise example or explain which detail supports your answer.

A Use local evidence first

The teacher supplies a dated, anonymised record of new sheets issued for one repeated classroom task. Label the task, period, group size and counting method. If no record is available, use card B as AUTHORED PRACTICE, not a fact about our school.

Teacher-supplied evidence requirement; authored method

Limit: No real school count or Teesside claim is supplied in this pack.

B Practice before and after

AUTHORED PRACTICE: the same task uses 12 new sheets before a reuse tray and 8 during the trial, with the same group size. The difference is 4 fewer new sheets. These numbers are invented to practise comparison.

Authored practice record, 21 September 2026

Limit: One trial does not prove the tray caused the change. Task difficulty, attendance or counting could differ in real use.

C Roles for a small action

AUTHORED PLAN: the adult checks permission and suitable paper. A tray monitor organises clean sheets. A recorder counts new sheets issued; a checker repeats the count. Agree a review date and rotate pupil roles. No names go in the shared record.

Authored Community Project planning example

Limit: Pupils do not handle confidential paper, electrical equipment or household records.

D Check the contribution

Use the same task, group size and counting rule when comparing. An indicator is new sheets issued per repeated task; a proposed target is fewer than the baseline, not a promised result. Share an anonymised conclusion with the class and agree one next check.

Authored indicator and review framework

Limit: A sheet count is not a CO2 measurement. Do not claim an emissions saving from these numbers.

Shared investigation

1. Build the practice chain: 12 new sheets before, a reuse-tray trial, then a count of 8 using the same task. Identify evidence, action and indicator.

2. Agree adult, monitor, recorder and checker roles. Explain one contribution and a review date; replace practice counts with checked local evidence if available.

Work with a partner or adult, or use your own table. One person suggests; the other checks the evidence. Swap after one turn. Passing is allowed.

Supported independent task

Complete this route only. Link learning to local action with Community Project links.

1. Use the practice numbers or the teacher’s checked record and label which it is.

2. Choose one role and complete evidence, action and indicator boxes with a frame.

3. Independently explain your contribution and how another person can check it.

Support: Use highlighted evidence and the frame. An adult may read or scribe your own words.

Explain a second detail if ready.

Standard independent task

Complete this route only. Link learning to local action with Community Project links.

1. Identify one manageable paper-use action from the supplied evidence.

2. Agree roles, baseline, counting rule, proposed target and review date.

3. Independently explain one contribution, its indicator and a limit on the conclusion.

Support: Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Compare your reasoning with one alternative.

Stretch independent task

Complete this route only. Link learning to local action with Community Project links.

1. Check whether baseline and trial conditions can be compared fairly.

2. Plan roles and a measurable indicator with an anonymised recording method.

3. Explain another factor that could change the result and how to check it.

4. Independently justify your contribution and the next action if the target is not met.

Support: Use the source limits and test a different priority or assumption.

Propose a further evidence check and explain its value.
