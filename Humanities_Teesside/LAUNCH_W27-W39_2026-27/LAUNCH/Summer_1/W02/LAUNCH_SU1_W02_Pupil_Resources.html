Evidence cards

Check the source and limit before using a detail

A Cause impact response

NASA links fossil-fuel burning to added CO2. Its response page describes sea-level rise as a climate impact and flood defences as an adaptation example. Keep cause, impact and response in separate parts of the decision grid.

Source: NASA Causes; NASA Mitigation and Adaptation

Limit: A general example does not establish the risk at our school.

B Mitigation option

Mitigation reduces greenhouse-gas emissions or increases their removal. AUTHORED OPTION: an adult reviews avoidable fossil-fuel energy use in a community building. Intended benefit: reduce a source of emissions. Check actual energy use before claiming a saving.

Source: NASA Mitigation and Adaptation; authored application

Limit: Reducing emissions does not instantly remove existing climate impacts. Costs and feasibility need local evidence.

C Adaptation option

Adaptation adjusts to actual or expected climate impacts. AUTHORED OPTION: a community assesses flood protection. Intended benefit: reduce exposure to a stated impact. Use a checked local risk assessment before choosing a design.

Source: NASA Mitigation and Adaptation; authored application

Limit: A flood defence is not, by itself, a reduction in fossil-fuel emissions. Do not promise protection everywhere.

D The decision test

Use a response grid: stated problem, response type, expected benefit, limitation and evidence needed. Different choices can be justified with different priorities. Our options are authored scenarios, not approved works or instructions to pupils.

Source: Authored evaluation framework, 21 September 2026

Limit: The classroom cards cannot establish a real building budget, energy saving or flood risk.
