Judging climate responses

Learning goal

Evaluate responses to climate change.

How can we make a justified decision about sustainability?

I can interpret resource and climate evidence, evaluate responses, compare options against criteria and justify a practical decision.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

Teacher card

Judging climate responses / LAUNCH_SU1_W02

Outcome: Evaluate responses to climate change.

Evidence to retain: F: evaluation, targeted questioning and two exit questions.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Prepare the cards, organiser and one task route. Model the four steps before independent work. Use the supplied source record and distinguish real evidence from authored scenarios. The teacher supplies the school’s copy of No One Is Too Small by Greta Thunberg; no extract or quotation is included. Use the authored options rather than assuming local flood risk or an energy saving. Compare both benefit and limitation for mitigation and adaptation.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. Use labelled examples and a reduced data set before the full task.

Offer a calm start, an agreed pause and a private response option. Accept speech, pointing, typing or writing. Invite reasoned disagreement without personal or family disclosure.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Record the learner’s own reasoning and support used. Keep the conclusion individual and use the exit to plan one next step.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Model and answers

Read these pages before teaching. Hide the model during independent assessment.

Question: Does a flood-defence proposal address a cause or an impact?

Evidence: NASA identifies flood defences as an adaptation example.

Explain: The proposal aims to reduce exposure to an impact; it is adaptation.

Check: It does not itself reduce fossil-fuel emissions. A real design needs local assessment.

Arrival answers

1. A cause contributes to change; an impact is an effect of it.

2. Reducing a source of greenhouse-gas emissions.

3. It does not itself reduce fossil-fuel emissions and needs local assessment.

4. No. Checked local evidence is required.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Reduce a source of greenhouse gases — Mitigation. It targets emissions. Verify local feasibility and any claimed saving.

2. Reduce exposure to a climate impact — Adaptation. It targets effects. It does not itself prove an emissions reduction.

3. Address causes and existing impacts — Both together. The two approaches can complement each other; each still needs evidence and checks.

Exit responses

Justify one response using evidence. Accept a justified mitigation or adaptation choice with a sourced benefit and a limitation.

What local evidence is still needed? Identify a needed local check, such as actual energy use, cost, permission or flood-risk assessment.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Distinguish causes, impacts and responses using maps/data. Compare mitigation and adaptation options in a decision grid; independently justify a response with evidence.

Look for

F: evaluation, targeted questioning and two exit questions.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.

A Cause impact response

NASA links fossil-fuel burning to added CO2. Its response page describes sea-level rise as a climate impact and flood defences as an adaptation example. Keep cause, impact and response in separate parts of the decision grid.

NASA Causes; NASA Mitigation and Adaptation

Limit: A general example does not establish the risk at our school.

B Mitigation option

Mitigation reduces greenhouse-gas emissions or increases their removal. AUTHORED OPTION: an adult reviews avoidable fossil-fuel energy use in a community building. Intended benefit: reduce a source of emissions. Check actual energy use before claiming a saving.

NASA Mitigation and Adaptation; authored application

Limit: Reducing emissions does not instantly remove existing climate impacts. Costs and feasibility need local evidence.

C Adaptation option

Adaptation adjusts to actual or expected climate impacts. AUTHORED OPTION: a community assesses flood protection. Intended benefit: reduce exposure to a stated impact. Use a checked local risk assessment before choosing a design.

NASA Mitigation and Adaptation; authored application

Limit: A flood defence is not, by itself, a reduction in fossil-fuel emissions. Do not promise protection everywhere.

D The decision test

Use a response grid: stated problem, response type, expected benefit, limitation and evidence needed. Different choices can be justified with different priorities. Our options are authored scenarios, not approved works or instructions to pupils.

Authored evaluation framework, 21 September 2026

Limit: The classroom cards cannot establish a real building budget, energy saving or flood risk.

Standard independent task

Complete this route only. Evaluate responses to climate change.

1. Complete the grid for both mitigation and adaptation options.

2. Use a cited detail to identify a benefit and a limitation of each.

3. Independently choose one for a stated problem and explain what needs checking locally.

Support: Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Compare your reasoning with one alternative.

Stretch independent task

Complete this route only. Evaluate responses to climate change.

1. Compare each response against the same problem, timescale and evidence needs.

2. Explain why mitigation and adaptation can complement one another.

3. Challenge one unsupported promise in an option discussion.

4. Independently justify a choice and a condition that could change it.

Support: Use the source limits and test a different priority or assumption.

Propose a further evidence check and explain its value.
