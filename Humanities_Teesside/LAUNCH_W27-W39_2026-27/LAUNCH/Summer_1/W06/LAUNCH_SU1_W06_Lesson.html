Opening

LAUNCH · Humanities · Summer 1 · Week 6

Our sustainability decision report

Learning goal

Complete a sustainability decision report.

How can we make a justified decision about sustainability?

I can interpret resource and climate evidence, evaluate responses, compare options against criteria and justify a practical decision.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Prepare the cards, organiser and one task route. Model the four steps before independent work. Use the supplied source record and distinguish real evidence from authored scenarios. The teacher supplies the school’s copy of No One Is Too Small by Greta Thunberg; no extract or quotation is included. Use the two authored options and the explicit three-criterion scoring grid. Accept the pupil’s supported conclusion or a faithful dated adult note. Record support used; this is adult-life work, not an examination.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. Use labelled examples and a reduced data set before the full task.

Offer a calm start, an agreed pause and a private response option. Accept speech, pointing, typing or writing. Invite reasoned disagreement without personal or family disclosure.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Retain the individual report, criterion grid and support record. Keep access arrangements while removing answer models and recording any content prompts.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Our sustainability decision report

Learning goal: Complete a sustainability decision report.

Words to use

WordMeaning
criterionA stated feature used to compare options
trade-offA choice involving benefits and disadvantages
limitationSomething the evidence cannot establish
decisionA choice justified by evidence and criteria

Remember

Use the stated criteria and attach an evidence note to each score.

Check the report for a preference, trade-off, limitation and next action.

How to show your learning

Audit the criteria and explain any extra priority before scoring both options.

Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Evidence matters

Fictional cases are teaching examples. Historical, geographical and religious claims use the named sources. A model video demonstrates a method.

You can respond by speaking, pointing, drawing, typing or writing. A fictional personal example is welcome.

Sources: Evidence and factual claim record; Exact unit scheme and access text; Authored scenarios and limits; Teacher-supplied book and source checks

Arrival choices

Start here: a decision uses criteria and evidence. Cards A to D restate the options, scoring method and report requirements; earlier work is optional.

Start with 1 and 2. Try 3 and 4 when ready. Reading and exact scribing are available.

1 What five elements should the report explain?

Support: Read card C.

2 Are the scenario scores environmental measurements?

Support: Check card A’s limit.

3 Can NOAA prove a paper-tray saving?

Support: Compare the quantities and coverage.

4 Why record a realistic next action?

Support: Consider an evidence gap.

Stretch: use a precise example or explain which detail supports your answer.

A Explicit decision criteria

AUTHORED options: A uses spare paper before new sheets for suitable drafts. Assume teacher permission and a daily checker; it does not suit every task. B proposes an accessible cool break space for hot weather. It needs adult assessment and permission before use; no result is measured. Compare resource benefit, access and feasibility. Score each criterion 1 for weak support, 2 for some support or 3 for clear support, with an evidence note.

Authored decision-scoring framework, 21 September 2026

Limit: Scores are judgements supported by evidence, not measured environmental savings or an examination grade.

B Evidence available

The NOAA table records CO2 concentration. NASA explains the climate link and response types. The school options and 12-to-8 sheet comparison are AUTHORED PRACTICE. A real school decision needs the teacher’s checked local evidence as well.

NOAA GML; NASA Causes and Mitigation and Adaptation; authored practice record

Limit: A global climate source cannot establish a particular school action’s result.

C Explain the preferred option

State your priority and preferred option. Give criterion scores with evidence, a trade-off, an evidence limit and a realistic next action. A higher total informs a decision but does not remove the need to explain who can use the option.

Authored report framework, 21 September 2026

Limit: Different priorities can lead to different justified decisions. Nobody must disclose a family or personal view.

D Keep the report your own

Check figures, units, sources and whether a claim is observed, inferred or proposed. Record the access support and any content prompts used. An adult may faithfully record your spoken response with a date. Select the report and supporting grid as evidence.

Authored evidence-retention checklist

Limit: Do not copy the shared model as your independent conclusion. No qualification code or award is assigned.

Shared investigation

1. Score the two authored options against resource benefit, access and feasibility. Require an evidence note for each 1-to-3 judgement.

2. Preview a practice report selection in the portfolio. Check preferred option, trade-offs, limits and next action before independent reports.

Work with a partner or adult, or use your own table. One person suggests; the other checks the evidence. Swap after one turn. Passing is allowed.

Use the evidence cards and any visual sheet. Follow the two shared steps above, then record one decision.

Support: Read one evidence card, identify its source and plan a reason before responding.

Further challenge: Propose a further evidence check and explain its value.

Supported independent task

Complete this route only. Complete a sustainability decision report.

1. Use both option cards and score resource benefit, access and feasibility with the frame.

2. Write a report naming your preferred option, one evidence detail and a trade-off.

3. Independently give a limitation and next action; record support or an adult note.

Support: Use highlighted evidence and the frame. An adult may read or scribe your own words.

Explain a second detail if ready.

Standard independent task

Complete this route only. Complete a sustainability decision report.

1. Score both options against the three stated criteria and justify each score.

2. Write the report with preferred option, evidence, trade-offs, limitations and next action.

3. Independently check the report against unit success criteria and retain the support record.

Support: Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Compare your reasoning with one alternative.

Stretch independent task

Complete this route only. Complete a sustainability decision report.

1. Audit the criteria and explain any extra priority before scoring both options.

2. Test how one changed assumption might alter the preference.

3. Write a report weighing the alternative fairly with evidence limits and a realistic next action.

4. Independently justify the final decision and record what stronger evidence could change it.

Support: Use the source limits and test a different priority or assumption.

Propose a further evidence check and explain its value.

Exit ticket

Choose a response mode. You may give your answers privately to an adult.

Explain your decision using criterion scores and evidence.

What trade-off and limitation have you retained?

Support: Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Further challenge: Propose a further evidence check and explain its value.
