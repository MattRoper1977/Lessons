Opening

LAUNCH · Humanities · Summer 1 · Week 3

Choosing a school sustainability option

Learning goal

Make a reasoned geographical decision.

How can we make a justified decision about sustainability?

I can interpret resource and climate evidence, evaluate responses, compare options against criteria and justify a practical decision.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

Teacher card

Choosing a school sustainability option / LAUNCH_SU1_W03

Outcome: Make a reasoned geographical decision.

Evidence to retain: F: reasoned decision and two exit questions.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Prepare the cards, organiser and one task route. Model the four steps before independent work. Use the supplied source record and distinguish real evidence from authored scenarios. The teacher supplies the school’s copy of No One Is Too Small by Greta Thunberg; no extract or quotation is included. Use the paper-reuse and cool-space scenarios. State that all local details are assumptions; accept different justified choices and overlap between criteria.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. Use labelled examples and a reduced data set before the full task.

Offer a calm start, an agreed pause and a private response option. Accept speech, pointing, typing or writing. Invite reasoned disagreement without personal or family disclosure.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Record the learner’s own reasoning and support used. Keep the conclusion individual and use the exit to plan one next step.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Arrival answers

2. Social.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Option A uses spare sheets before new ones — Urbanisation. This concerns resource use; the benefit is an intention, not a measured saving.

2. Option B must be accessible to users — Social. This concerns who can use the proposed space.

3. Option B needs adult assessment and permission — Practical. This concerns whether the proposal can be carried out.

Exit responses

Justify your choice using two criteria. Accept either option when its evidence is compared under the same stated criteria.

Which trade-off or uncertainty matters most? Require a real scenario disadvantage or uncertainty, such as unsuitable tasks for the tray or permission still needed for the break space.

Teaching and evidence record

A Option A reuse paper

AUTHORED OPTION A: put clean spare paper in an accessible classroom tray for suitable drafts. Assume the teacher approves the paper and a pupil checks the tray daily. Intended resource benefit: use spare sheets before taking new ones. Disadvantage: it does not suit every task.

Authored school-option scenario, 21 September 2026

Limit: No measured reduction or emissions saving is claimed. Never reuse paper with personal information.

B Option B plan a cool space

AUTHORED OPTION B: ask an adult to assess an accessible shaded break space. Assume a suitable space can be checked but permission is not yet given. Intended social benefit: a usable option during hot weather. Disadvantage: it needs adult assessment before use.

Authored option; NASA Mitigation and Adaptation gives heat planning as an example

Limit: This is a proposal, not evidence that a particular school is safe, cooler or at risk.

C Sort the evidence

For these authored options, a statement about using spare sheets fits Environmental. A statement about who can reach the break space fits Social. A statement about obtaining permission fits Practical. Some statements fit more than one category; explain your main reason.

Authored sorting criteria, 21 September 2026

Limit: A category organises evidence. It does not decide which option everyone must prefer.

D A reasoned choice

Compare A and B against all three criteria. Name the evidence behind each judgement, one disadvantage of your preferred option, and what must be checked next. The options solve different immediate problems, so explain your priority.

Authored decision framework, 21 September 2026

Limit: Do not present assumed benefits as measured outcomes or judge the people who prefer another option.

Shared investigation

1. Sort the three statements into Environmental, Social or Practical. Read options A and B and discuss statements that could fit more than one criterion.

2. Build a two-option grid across all three criteria. Model a choice with a disadvantage, then compare another justified priority.

Work with a partner or adult, or use your own table. One person suggests; the other checks the evidence. Swap after one turn. Passing is allowed.
