An accurate Earth Day campaign

Learning goal

Create a digital sustainability campaign linked to Earth Day.

How can we make a justified decision about sustainability?

I can interpret resource and climate evidence, evaluate responses, compare options against criteria and justify a practical decision.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

Teacher card

An accurate Earth Day campaign / LAUNCH_SU1_W04

Outcome: Create a digital sustainability campaign linked to Earth Day.

Evidence to retain: F: campaign accuracy, evidence and two exit questions.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Prepare the cards, organiser and one task route. Model the four steps before independent work. Use the supplied source record and distinguish real evidence from authored scenarios. The teacher supplies the school’s copy of No One Is Too Small by Greta Thunberg; no extract or quotation is included. Use only a school-approved classroom device. Create a digital slide or document, not a real social-media post. No accounts, names, faces or home details. Earth Day is 22 April; the message may be prepared for that date or reviewed afterwards.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. Use labelled examples and a reduced data set before the full task.

Offer a calm start, an agreed pause and a private response option. Accept speech, pointing, typing or writing. Invite reasoned disagreement without personal or family disclosure.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Record the learner’s own reasoning and support used. Keep the conclusion individual and use the exit to plan one next step.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Arrival answers

1. A small unmeasured action cannot be claimed to stop climate change.

2. Use approved clean spare paper for suitable drafts.

3. New sheets issued for the same repeated task.

4. A claim describes what is true; a request asks someone to act.

Shared activity reasoning

1. AUTHORED WEAK DRAFT: This will stop climate change — Try approved spare paper for suitable drafts; we will check new-sheet use. The repair proposes a bounded action without promising a global result.

2. AUTHORED WEAK DRAFT: Our tray has already saved a tonne of CO2 — We have no measured carbon saving; count new sheets before and during the same task. The repair removes an invented result and names a checkable indicator.

Exit responses

Explain your message’s intended effect. The proposed paper trial intends to reduce new-sheet use; compare counts for the same task.

How did you repair an unsupported claim? Explain which unsupported promise was removed and how the revised wording matches the supplied evidence.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Analyse how a short campaign uses claims and evidence. Identify an audience and feasible action; independently create a digital message with accurate supporting evidence and explain its intended effect.

Look for

F: campaign accuracy, evidence and two exit questions.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.

Learning goal: Create a digital sustainability campaign linked to Earth Day.

Words to use

WordMeaning
audienceThe people a message is intended for
claimA statement that needs support
evidenceInformation used to support a claim
campaignA planned message asking for a stated action

Remember

Keep the message accurate, sourced and feasible for its audience.

Describe an intended effect separately from a measured result.

How to show your learning

Audit both drafts for evidence gaps and implied promises.

Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Evidence matters

Fictional cases are teaching examples. Historical, geographical and religious claims use the named sources. A model video demonstrates a method.

You can respond by speaking, pointing, drawing, typing or writing. A fictional personal example is welcome.

Sources: Evidence and factual claim record; Exact unit scheme and access text; Authored scenarios and limits; Teacher-supplied book and source checks

Arrival choices

Start here: a claim needs evidence. A request asks someone to act. The cards give the date, classroom brief and action, even if earlier work is missing.

Start with 1 and 2. Try 3 and 4 when ready. Reading and exact scribing are available.

1 What makes the first weak draft an overclaim?

Support: Compare the promise with evidence.

2 What feasible action does card D propose?

Support: Read the invitation.

3 What indicator can check that action?

Support: Use the counting plan.

4 How do a claim and a request differ?

Support: Compare says with asks.

Stretch: use a precise example or explain which detail supports your answer.

A Earth Day and our audience

Earth Day is observed on 22 April. Our product is a classroom digital message for school users. Name one feasible action and its intended effect. Keep it on the school-approved classroom device; do not create accounts or publish it online.

EARTHDAY.ORG History; authored classroom brief

Limit: The date does not prove that a campaign action has worked.

B A supported climate link

NASA explains that burning fossil fuels releases CO2, which contributes to warming. A campaign may explain that link with a citation. It must not turn a small proposed action into a promise to stop climate change.

NASA Causes; authored campaign application

Limit: This source does not calculate a CO2 saving for a school paper tray or a particular campaign.

C Weak drafts to repair

AUTHORED EXAMPLES, not facts: This will stop climate change. Our tray has already saved a tonne of CO2. Both go beyond the supplied evidence. Repair the claim, name the audience and action, and state what you will measure.

Authored weak-draft examples, 21 September 2026

Limit: These are deliberately unsupported claims for editing, not estimates to repeat.

D An honest action message

AUTHORED PLAN: invite school users to choose clean spare paper for suitable drafts, with teacher permission. Count new sheets issued for the same task before and during a short trial. Explain the intended effect as reducing new-sheet use, then check it.

Authored campaign and measurement plan, 21 September 2026

Limit: No guaranteed result or carbon saving is supplied. Include no pupil names, faces or personal details.

Shared investigation

1. Repair both AUTHORED weak drafts in the editor. Replace each overclaim with a feasible action, supporting evidence and a measurable intention.

2. Identify school users as the audience. Prepare a classroom-only Earth Day message with a source, intended effect and a clear limit.

Work with a partner or adult, or use your own table. One person suggests; the other checks the evidence. Swap after one turn. Passing is allowed.

Move or match the cards

Supported independent task

Complete this route only. Create a digital sustainability campaign linked to Earth Day.

1. Choose school users as the audience and highlight one feasible action from card D.

2. Make a digital message on the approved classroom device using the supplied frame.

3. Independently explain its intended effect and check one claim against its source.

Support: Use highlighted evidence and the frame. An adult may read or scribe your own words.

Explain a second detail if ready.

Standard independent task

Complete this route only. Create a digital sustainability campaign linked to Earth Day.

1. Repair the two drafts and plan a message for a stated school audience.

2. Create a classroom digital message with Earth Day’s date, a feasible action and a named source.

3. Independently explain the intended effect, indicator and one limit on your claim.

Support: Use the cards and organiser. Choose your own evidence and explanation.

Compare your reasoning with one alternative.

Stretch independent task

Complete this route only. Create a digital sustainability campaign linked to Earth Day.

1. Audit both drafts for evidence gaps and implied promises.

2. Create a concise classroom message that separates evidence from a request to act.

3. Justify the audience, action, intended effect and indicator using source limits.

4. Independently revise one phrase after a peer accuracy check and explain the revision.

Support: Use the source limits and test a different priority or assumption.

Propose a further evidence check and explain its value.
