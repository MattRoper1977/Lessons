Applied 2026-09-22 by Claude on Matt Roper's STOP-R2 ruling, option (a).

WHY. The estate publishes these pages through an adapter that injects its usage
furniture, and that adapter finds its insertion points by looking for the literal
strings </head> and </body>:

    ValueError: Usage adapter needs an HTML shell: .../START_HERE.html

These pages were never fragments. Each one opens

    <!doctype html><html lang="en-GB"><meta charset><meta viewport><title><style>

and closes </html>. In HTML5 that is a WHOLE document: <head> and <body> are both
implied, and every browser parses it correctly. What the pages lacked was only the
EXPLICIT closing tags the adapter greps for.

WHAT WAS NOT DONE. An earlier corrective wrapped these pages in a second document.
That was withdrawn before it landed: it nested a whole document inside another, two
doctypes and two </html> per page, which the adapter's substring test cannot see.

WHAT WAS DONE. tools/hum/explicit_document_tags.py (sha256 9fa0d799133e5975)
writes down the tags the page already implies, and inserts nothing else:

    <head>          immediately after the page's own <html ...> tag
    </head><body>   immediately before the first element that is not head content
    </body>         immediately before the page's own </html>

Not one byte of content moves. The tool reports the offset of every insertion and
its own checker undoes exactly those, demanding the original back, then asserts that
no document furniture was duplicated or lost. A page that already writes head and
body is returned byte-identical, so running the tool twice is running it once.

THE ORACLE. Chromium - the parser that actually serves these pages - loads the bytes
before and after and compares the doctype, head.innerHTML, body.innerHTML, the title
and the element count. All seventy served pages in the three trees parse to an
IDENTICAL document before and after. lxml cannot be that judge and is red-proved
unusable for it: libxml2 does not implement HTML5's head/body inference, so on a real
Knowledge Organiser it puts the first <button> inside <head> and never opens <body>.

The eighteen signed lesson files are untouched, and so are the twenty pages that
already wrote their tags down.
