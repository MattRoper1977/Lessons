Rails Meet the River

Starter · question before verdict

🔍 Enquiry

Why did the Stockton & Darlington Railway link coal areas to the River Tees when it opened in 1825?

Retrieval question: What was the main early job of the railway?

Choose, then use the frames to check the evidence.
FRAME 1/4
Coal started inland.
Pause for evidence or a question before advancing.
FRAME 2/4
Rails linked it to Stockton and the Tees.
Pause for evidence or a question before advancing.
FRAME 3/4
On 27 September 1825 the opening steam train carried passengers in wagons; coal freight was the railway’s principal purpose.
Pause for evidence or a question before advancing.
FRAME 4/4
The planned freight purpose and the opening-day passengers can both be true.
Pause for evidence or a question before advancing.

ADAPTED PRIMARY-SOURCE CARD

HUM-B-W10-C01 · John Backhouse reports the opening train

Provenance source: HUM-B-W10-S01

Fourteen-year-old John Church Backhouse described the 27 September 1825 opening train to his sister. His sketch labelled Locomotion, wagons for visitors and a coach called Experiment. He reported a very large crowd, but his reported numbers are not treated as an exact count.

Can establish
Establish what one young witness noticed and reported about the opening train.
Cannot establish
Establish exact passenger totals, every wagon’s contents or every observer’s view.
Accessible route
Numbered details: 1 — young witness; 2 — opening day; 3 — locomotive; 4 — visitor wagons; 5 — Experiment coach; 6 — reported estimate, not verified total.

ADAPTED SECONDARY-SOURCE CARD

HUM-B-W10-C02 · Why the route met the Tees

Provenance source: HUM-B-W10-S02

The railway linked County Durham collieries to Stockton’s wharves so coal could continue by ship. Coal was its most important early traffic. Stockton’s wharves later proved too limited for the volume, helping prompt an extension to a new port at Middlesbrough.

Can establish
Supports the coal purpose and the reason for seeking a downstream port.
Cannot establish
Does not make the 1830 extension part of the original 1825 route.
Accessible route
Route strip: collieries → railway → Stockton wharves → ship; later branch → Middlesbrough port.
