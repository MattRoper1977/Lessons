Lesson overview

A Town Grows Fast

BUILD · Humanities · Teesside Humanities Studio · Week 12 of 14

BUILD teaching route · evidence preparation, not qualification self-certification.

🎯 Learning objective: I can use dated evidence to explain town growth without claiming the data tells the whole story.
✅ Success criteria
  • ◆ Order four growth milestones.
  • ▲ Link two drivers to two signs of growth and ask what evidence is missing.
  • ★ Judge the strongest driver and name missing evidence.

ADAPTED SOURCE CARD

HUM-B-W12-C01 · From small farming community to planned town

Provenance source: HUM-B-W12-S01

Middlesbrough Council records 25 people in the farming community in 1801. In 1828 the estate owners bought land and planned a port and grid-pattern town; the railway branch and port followed within the 1828–30 development sequence.

Can establish
Supports selected population, planning and development milestones.
Cannot establish
Show individual motives, feelings, housing quality or one uncontested birthday for the town.
Accessible route
Date strip: 1801 — 25 people; 1828 — estate and plan; 1828–30 — town, port and railway development sequence; 1850 — ironstone accelerated growth.

ADAPTED OFFICIAL-SYNTHESIS CARD

HUM-B-W12-C02 · Rounded population endpoints

Provenance source: HUM-B-W12-S02

Historic England describes growth from around 25 people in 1801 to over 90,000 by the end of the nineteenth century. The words ‘around’ and ‘over’ matter, and changing boundaries mean this is not a perfect like-for-like count.

Can establish
Establish that Middlesbrough became dramatically larger.
Cannot establish
By itself explain the causes of growth or how people lived.
Accessible route
Two large-number tiles: 1801 — around 25; end of nineteenth century — over 90,000.
