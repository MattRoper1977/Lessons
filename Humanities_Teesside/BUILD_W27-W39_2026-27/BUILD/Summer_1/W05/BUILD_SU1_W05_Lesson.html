Continents and oceans

Learning goal

Talk about the wider world using continents and oceans.

How can a world map help us name land and ocean?

I can name a continent and an ocean and point to them on a world map.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write.

Teacher card

Staff only | BUILD_SU1_W05

Continents and oceans

Outcome: Talk about the wider world using continents and oceans.

Assessment: F: matching or labelling response and short retrieval check.

Teaching: Use a globe and large map to locate the UK, continents and major oceans. Match labels together; independently identify a chosen continent and ocean using a reduced choice bank if needed.

Preparation

Use the original SVG labelled simplified teaching map, not to scale. It shows seven continents and five oceans, without country borders. The UK is marked by a small dot and callout in Europe; it is a country, not a continent. Use Australia as the continent name in this seven-continent convention. The Pacific appears at both edges of the flat map and is one ocean. Check a globe if available; the flat map distorts shapes and sizes.

40 minute route

Opening 0; Arrival 4; Starter 3; I do 6; We do 7; Check 2; Independent 12; Review 3; Exit 3.

Access and feedback

Give one instruction at a time. Offer pointing, speech, drawing, typing or writing. Use a reduced choice set if needed. Keep communication support and fade content prompts before the individual check.

Offer a two-card first choice, private pointing or an adult reading labels. No speed race or public score is needed.

Retain one individually identified continent and one ocean, plus the short exit check. Record whether the learner used a reduced word bank, reading support or a location prompt.

Worked model

  1. Question: Which continent contains the UK, and which ocean lies to its west?
  2. Evidence: The UK dot is in Europe. The Atlantic Ocean label is to the left, west of Europe.
  3. Explain: Europe is the continent. The Atlantic is the ocean to the west of the UK.
  4. Check: Point to both labels. A globe shows that Earth is round; this map is simplified.

Arrival answers

Supported

  1. Any land region.
  2. Any ocean water area.
  3. Europe is beside the UK dot.
  4. Atlantic Ocean.

Standard

  1. A round model of Earth.
  2. A continent.
  3. Europe.
  4. Arctic, Atlantic, Indian, Pacific or Southern Ocean.

Stretch

  1. Africa.
  2. Atlantic Ocean.
  3. The map cuts through one Pacific Ocean; the edges join around Earth.
  4. No. It is simplified and not to scale.

Shared activity answers

  1. The continent containing the UK. Europe. The UK dot and callout point to Europe.
  2. The continent south of Europe, with the Atlantic to its west. Africa. Africa is below Europe on this north-up map.
  3. The ocean between the Americas and Europe/Africa. Atlantic Ocean. The Atlantic label sits between those land regions.
  4. The ocean shown at both the left and right edges. Pacific Ocean. Both edge labels name the same Pacific Ocean.

Check answer

No. Both labels name the same Pacific Ocean. The flat map cuts through the Pacific; there are five named oceans in this lesson.
