Opening

GROW · Humanities · Spring 2 · Week 4

Worldviews and responses to change

Learning goal

Compare how different communities respond to change with an RE link.

How do climate and hazards affect communities?

I can locate climate zones and hazards, explain a cause and effect, compare community responses and present findings using maps and data.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

Word and evidence bank

A Zones and events

Broad temperature zones relate to latitude. Near the equator is generally warmer; high latitudes are generally colder. Local climate also depends on altitude and other factors. Earthquakes relate mainly to plate boundaries, not climate zones.

Authored map explanation

Limit: Broad bands simplify climate; they are not a detailed climate classification.

B Human influence

Burning fossil fuels adds carbon dioxide. Greenhouse gases slow heat loss to space. Human activity is the main cause of recent global warming.

NASA Causes of climate change

Limit: One local hot or cold day does not establish the global trend.

C Two responses

Reducing fossil-fuel use tackles a cause of warming. Shade, water access and cooler buildings can reduce harm during heat. These responses do different jobs.

Authored examples based on climate mechanisms

Limit: Their suitability depends on the place and the needs of people there.

D Community accounts

Fictional account 1: a faith group explains caring for creation as its reason for planting shade trees. Fictional account 2: a non-religious residents’ group gives human wellbeing as its reason for a cool-room project.

Two authored teaching accounts with explicitly stated values

Limit: These are not reports of real organisations and do not represent every religious or non-religious person.

Teacher brief

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Worldviews and responses to change / GROW_S2_W04

Outcome: Compare how different communities respond to change with an RE link.

Evidence to retain: F: comparison response, questioning and two exit questions.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Use evidence to explain problems and feasible responses. Avoid asking pupils to carry responsibility for solving global climate change.

Access and regulation

Chunk sources and tasks; pre-teach vocabulary with images. Offer sentence stems, organisers, oral rehearsal and typing. Start with one modelled example; extend through a second source, comparison or supported judgement. Check the learner can begin within two minutes, then fade prompts.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Compare two carefully selected community accounts, including stated beliefs or values. Build a shared comparison grid; independently explain one similarity or difference without generalising to everyone in a community.

Look for

F: comparison response, questioning and two exit questions.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
