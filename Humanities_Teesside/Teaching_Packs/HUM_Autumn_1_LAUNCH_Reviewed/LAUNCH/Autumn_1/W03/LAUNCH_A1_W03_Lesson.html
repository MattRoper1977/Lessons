Evaluating a digital archive source

Learning goal

Research and evaluate a digital archive source.

How can evidence explain migration and identity?

I can use chronology, evaluate provenance and usefulness, explain causes and consequences and reach a supported conclusion.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

A 11 May 1948 telegram

The Acting Governor of Jamaica told the Colonial Secretary that men had booked passages hoping for employment in Britain.

TNA HO 213/714; official message, 1948

Limit: A hope is not a guarantee. This does not establish everyone’s reasons or later experience.

B 1947 to 1948 report

Eric Walrond reported varied experiences: work for some men, but unemployment also described in the north-west.

TNA CO 876/88; report, 1947–48

Limit: Selected accounts do not measure the experiences of all migrants.

C 8 August 1972 Cabinet record

British ministers discussed Idi Amin’s order that Asian British-passport holders leave Uganda within three months.

TNA CAB 128/50; British Cabinet minutes, 1972

Limit: This records the ministers’ discussion, not the voices of all the people affected.

D 15 March 1973 support letter

The Uganda Resettlement Board asked voluntary organisations to support resettled families through visits.

TNA T 353/63; letter, 1973

Limit: A request records an intended response, not proof that every visit happened.

Teacher card

Evaluating a digital archive source / LAUNCH_A1_W03

Outcome: Research and evaluate a digital archive source.

Evidence to retain: F: annotated source and supported evaluation.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Authored paraphrases of real records. Reasons and experiences differed. Migration is not new to Britain; these cases are selected examples.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.
