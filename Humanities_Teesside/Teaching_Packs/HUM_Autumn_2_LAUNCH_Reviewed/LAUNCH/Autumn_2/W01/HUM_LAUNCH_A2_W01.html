Humanities · LAUNCH · Autumn 2 · Week 1

Map symbols and grid references

Learning goal

I can use a map key and grid references to locate features.

Today I can

  • I can identify features using the key.
  • I can give a four-figure grid reference and direction.
  • I can explain what a reference shows and what it leaves out.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write.

You may pass. Your answer can be given privately.

You do · choose one route

15 min · 22–37

Use the practice map to complete one route.

I · S · C

Independent

Use the invented settlement map and its key.

1. Identify Sch, PO and FB.

2. Give the four-figure reference for the school, post office and footbridge.

3. Describe the hall's direction from the library.

4. Explain why 1221 alone cannot tell a reader whether you mean the post office or the health centre.

5. Explain one thing this map cannot tell us about the settlement's population.

Frame if useful: 'The left line is ____. The bottom line is ____. The reference is ____.'

You may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

Review and improve: Trace one pair of grid lines. Check easting digits come first; amend one answer or strengthen its reason.

I · S · C

Supported

Use the map and key with an adult, one feature at a time.

1. Identify PO: post office / park. Identify FB: footbridge / library.

2. Find the school. Read the left line: 10 / 20. Read the bottom line: 20 / 10. Put easting first, then northing.

3. Find the post office. Choose its square: 1221 / 2112. Trace the two lines to check.

4. Find the footbridge. Choose its square: 1122 / 2211. Trace the two lines to check.

5. Describe the hall's direction from the library: east / west.

6. Does 1221 mean the whole square or one exact dot? Use the post office and health centre as your clue.

Frame: 'Easting ____ then northing ____ gives ____.'

You may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

Review and improve: Check one reference by pointing to the left and bottom lines. Correct its order or show why it is right.

I · S · C

Choice

Choose your way to show the answer: direct a pointer, arrange digit cards, speak or sign, or make a short record.

1. Identify Sch, PO and FB using the key. Choose from school / post office / footbridge.

2. Match all three features to their four-figure references: 1020 / 1221 / 1122.

3. Choose one match. Show the left line and the bottom line that support it.

4. Choose the hall's direction from the library: east / west.

5. Choose the claim that fits 1221:

A: It names a whole square with more than one feature.

B: It names only the post office's exact dot.

Give one map clue for your choice.

You may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

Review and improve: Check one match with the actual grid lines. Change it or show its deciding clue.

I · S · C

Aspire

Use the map, key and tenth divisions.

1. Identify Sch, PO and FB. Give each feature's four-figure reference.

2. Give the six-figure references for the post office and health centre. Count tenths across and up from each square's left and bottom lines.

3. Compare their four-figure and six-figure references. Explain the gain in precision.

4. Describe the hall's direction from the library.

5. Evaluate this claim: 'This map proves the settlement is becoming more urban.' State what further evidence would be needed.

Frame if useful: 'The map shows ____, but it cannot establish ____ because ____.'

You may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. You may pass.

Review and improve: Check all three easting digits come before all three northing digits. Make one correction or add a precise limitation.

TA Brief

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Current stage

1. Prepare the complete invented 1 km grid, key and exact feature dots. Keep answer coordinates off the pupil map. Do not use a real person's or school's name.

2. Keep exam command words and explain the task briefly. Offer point, speak, sign, symbols, draw or write; any pupil may pass. No cold calling.

3. Use one Arrival tier, one Exit tier and one You do route. Keep adult answers hidden until the pupil responds. Separate reading access from content prompts.

4. In We do, every pupil decides privately before the shared trace. Check left/bottom grid lines, easting-first order and the distinction between a square and a feature dot.

5. Keep review within the final two minutes of You do. Record exact support and one map clue; upload the evidence to the evidence app the same day, labelled HUM-LAUNCH-A2-W01, linked only to a relevant agreed EHCP outcome.

Shared preparation, access and safety

For EVERY task, pupils may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. Read prompts aloud and retain geographical command words. Allow thinking time and a private response. No cold calling. Any pupil may pass. An adult may record the pupil's own gesture, directed placement or exact words. A pass needs no book entry. No board copying or typing is needed.

Use one Arrival tier and one Exit tier. Each Arrival tier has four short questions and each Exit tier three brief checks. Keep Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until pupils respond. Pupils complete one You do route; the four separate route pages are alternatives.

Prepare the complete invented practice map and its key from the specification below. No external map, hidden asset or prior lesson is needed. All locations and measurements are practice data. This is an indoor map-skills lesson; no journey or fieldwork is required.

Adult-only EHCP note: Outcome opportunities are cognition and learning (sequence eastings then northings and check a reference), communication and interaction (justify a map answer), SEMH (request help or use a planned pause), and sensory or physical access (use an enlarged grid or direct another person to a point). Observe only the pupil's relevant agreed target. Regulation strategy: mask all but the current square, take an agreed short eyes-away pause, then rejoin at 'find the left grid line'.

Same-day evidence — HUM-LAUNCH-A2-W01: The assigned adult records the pupil's exact response, response mode and actual prompt used, and uploads one chosen book item, optional photo or brief observation to the evidence app the same day, labelled HUM-LAUNCH-A2-W01. Include a relevant agreed outcome observation where appropriate. A photo alone does not establish understanding.

I do · method

Identify, locate, check

Step 1 — Identify

The key says B means library.

Step 2 — Read eastings first

Find the library's square.

Its left grid line is 10.

Step 3 — Read northings second

The bottom grid line of that square is 21.

Put them together: 1021.

Step 4 — Check precision

1021 identifies the whole square, not just the library dot.

For a six-figure reference, split each side into ten equal parts.

The library dot is five tenths across from 10: easting 105.

It is five tenths up from 21: northing 215.

So its six-figure reference is 105215: all three easting digits, then all three northing digits.

Each tenth is 100 m on this 1 km practice grid. A six-figure reference locates a 100 m grid square, more precisely than a four-figure reference; it is not an infinitely exact coordinate.
