Opening

BUILD · RE & World Views · Autumn 1 · Week 3

A story of belonging

Learning goal

Listen to a story of belonging and identify one example of belonging.

What makes something special, and how do we show respect?

I can say why something is special, name a special book or place, and show one respectful action.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Editable Word packPrintable pupil resources PDF

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

A The visit

Fictional story: Sam visits a gurdwara with his friend Jas. A gurdwara is a Sikh place of worship. They follow the host’s guidance about covering their heads and removing shoes.

Authored story based on Sikh practice

Limit: One story cannot show every gurdwara or every Sikh family.

B The welcome

A volunteer smiles and says hello. She shows Sam where to sit. Nobody asks what Sam believes. Everyone is welcome.

Authored story based on Sikh practice

Limit: One story cannot show every gurdwara or every Sikh family.

C The shared meal

The visitors share langar, a free community meal. Many people sit on the floor together as a sign of equality. Chairs or other arrangements can support people who need them.

Authored story based on Sikh practice

Limit: One story cannot show every gurdwara or every Sikh family.

D Joining in

Sam chooses to help carry cups after the meal. Jas thanks him. The welcome and shared meal helped him feel included; helping is a choice, not a condition of belonging.

Authored story based on Sikh practice

Limit: Belonging can feel different for different people.

Teacher brief

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

A story of belonging / BUILD_RE_A1_W03

Outcome: Listen to a story of belonging and identify one example of belonging.

Evidence to retain: F: sequenced story and one sign of belonging.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Pause frequently; check literal understanding before meaning; avoid asking students to discuss their own family/community if uncomfortable.

Access and regulation

Chunked story; visuals throughout; repeated key sentence; sequencing instead of writing; verbal/scribed route; processing time.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: 'What helps someone feel they belong?' picture choices. I Do: read/view a short accessible faith or worldview story of belonging. We Do: identify who belongs, where and what shows it. You Do: sequence 3–4 story images or complete 'They belong because...'. Exit ticket: one sign of belonging.

Look for

F: sequenced story and one sign of belonging.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
