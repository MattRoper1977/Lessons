Respecting special objects

Learning goal

Show one respectful way to handle or respond to a special religious object.

What makes something special, and how do we show respect?

I can say why something is special, name a special book or place, and show one respectful action.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Editable Word packPrintable pupil resources PDF

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

A Our handling rules

Clean hands. Wait for the adult. Use two hands. Keep the object over the table. You may choose to look and not touch.

Authored classroom rules

Limit: Rules for a classroom replica are not the same as the rules believers follow.

B A holy book

Many Muslims wash before touching the Qur’an and keep it in a high, clean place. In class we use a photo or a protected copy and do not put it on the floor.

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Respecting special objects / BUILD_RE_A1_W04

Outcome: Show one respectful way to handle or respond to a special religious object.

Evidence to retain: F: three respectful actions in order and one reason.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

State rules clearly before handling; model each step; use replicas/photos where appropriate; praise specific respectful behaviours; low-arousal correction.

Access and regulation

Option to observe rather than handle; visual sequence; symbols; verbal answer; no authentic sacred object required; reduced choices.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

2. Treating people and things with care

3. So that fingers do not touch the handwritten scroll.

4. No. Looking carefully is a respectful choice.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Hold the replica with two hands over the table — Respectful. Two hands and a table keep it safe.

2. Grab the object before the adult is ready — Needs changing. Waiting is part of our handling rules.

3. Choose to look closely and not touch — Respectful. Looking carefully is a respectful choice.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: respectful / not respectful image sort. I Do: model how to look at, pass or observe a special object respectfully. We Do: practise with replicas, protected resources or photographs. You Do: choose three respectful actions and put them in order. Exit ticket: one respectful action and why it matters.

Look for

F: three respectful actions in order and one reason.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
