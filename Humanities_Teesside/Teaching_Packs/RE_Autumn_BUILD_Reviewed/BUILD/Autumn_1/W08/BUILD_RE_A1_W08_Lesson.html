BUILD · RE & World Views · Autumn 1 · Week 8

Special things, respect and belonging

Learning goal

Share or describe a 'special to me' object respectfully.

Today I can…

describe an object card and why it matters · choose an accurate earlier RE example

40 minutes · take a pause when you need one.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss. You can work with fictional source cards.

Re-entry · 60-second recap

The previous lesson practised describing a special object with one reason. Meaning can come from a person, memory, belief or place. Respect means treating people and things with care. Give a speaker time; eye contact is optional. Read or listen to this recap, then point to one word you recognise.

Teacher card

Special things, respect and belonging

Outcome: Share or describe a 'special to me' object respectfully.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3 minutes. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Print the glue-in strips, the evidence and shared cards, the required visual or data sheet and the route card. Exits go in books.

Keep the answers with staff. Use fictional source profiles and object cards; no personal objects, beliefs or experiences are required.

Access and regulation

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Collect four picture-choice answers and the new key idea in books. Keep the RAG private. Record normal access support separately.

UAS: staff must match evidence to the centre-selected unit and its exact criteria; this lesson makes no automatic award claim.

Private RAG rule

Green: describes an object card with a relevant reason and makes accurate choices about earlier ideas. Amber: gives an accurate reason but needs a source prompt or one earlier idea revisited. Red: needs direct teaching to connect an object with meaning and earlier vocabulary. A reader, scribe or pointing response does not lower the RAG; record access support separately.

What the check feeds into

Use the private RAG and exact access support to choose the next lesson’s opening example and Supported, Standard or Stretch route. Give one feedback code and one next step. Do not read scores aloud.

Planned adaptations

Broad areaEEF five-a-day strategy and adaptation
Cognition and learningExplicit instruction; scaffolding: Show the picture choices and one short question. Model matching the key ring to a memory, then use four picture-choice items. Fade pointing cues after a successful choice.
Communication and interactionScaffolding; flexible grouping: Pre-teach the key words with the source pictures. Offer silent planning, partner rehearsal, pointing, typing or exact scribing before an individual response.
SEMHCognitive and metacognitive strategies; flexible grouping: Use the predictable nine-stage routine, private feedback and an agreed pause. Keep fictional examples, allow a pass on public speaking, and rejoin at one named step.
Sensory and physicalUsing technology; scaffolding: Offer the normal enlarged print, read-aloud, typing, pointing or adapted grip. Use the manual model controls and reduce motion; assess the RE idea rather than handwriting.
EAL (EAL is not SEN)Explicit instruction; scaffolding: Pre-teach the lesson vocabulary with an image and example, allow first-language planning, and distinguish language access from conceptual understanding.

Shared activity reasoning

Seaside key ring — A memory — It reminds Lee of a day out.

Prayer beads — A belief — They help some people pray or reflect.

A medal from a relative — A person — It links to someone who matters.

Hinge and diagnoses

Lee keeps a key ring from a seaside day out. Why might it be special?

B. It reminds Lee of a meaningful day with an aunt. The key ring links to Lee’s memory of a person and place.

A: Confuses meaning with price.

C: Confuses special with sacred.

D: Treats personal meanings as identical.

Parallel hinge after a teaching move

Prayer beads matter to a person because they help the person reflect. What makes this a useful explanation?

A. It gives a reason why the object matters. It connects the object to a meaning.

Private RAG rule

Green: describes an object card with a relevant reason and makes accurate choices about earlier ideas. Amber: gives an accurate reason but needs a source prompt or one earlier idea revisited. Red: needs direct teaching to connect an object with meaning and earlier vocabulary. A reader, scribe or pointing response does not lower the RAG; record access support separately.
