Evidence cards

Read, point or listen. Keep the source label with any detail you use.

A Japan 11 March 2011

A magnitude 9.1 earthquake occurred off north-east Honshu, Japan. Sudden movement on the subduction boundary displaced the seafloor and produced a tsunami.

Source: USGS event record, official20110311054624120_30

Limit: Magnitude measures the earthquake; it is not a direct count of people affected.

B Effects and responses

Shaking and coastal flooding damaged places and disrupted people’s lives. Earthquake-resistant design addresses shaking; warnings, evacuation routes and higher ground address tsunami exposure.

Source: Authored explanation of the case and risk-management principles

Limit: A sea wall or warning cannot eliminate all risk. Do not judge people without considering access and warning time.

C Heat and unequal risk

On 19 July 2022 the Met Office recorded 40.3°C at Coningsby, 40.2°C at St James’s Park and 39.6°C at Topcliffe. Outdoor work increases exposure; difficulty accessing cool spaces can increase vulnerability. Shade and accessible cool spaces can reduce harm.

Source: Met Office July 2022 report, station table; risk explanation authored for teaching

Limit: This explains risk factors, not a medical assessment of an individual.

D Reading a hazard feed

A USGS event row can include UTC time, place, magnitude and depth in kilometres. A short feed is a dated snapshot. It cannot predict the next earthquake.

Source: USGS earthquake feed documentation

Limit: Small samples, reporting thresholds and later revisions affect patterns.
