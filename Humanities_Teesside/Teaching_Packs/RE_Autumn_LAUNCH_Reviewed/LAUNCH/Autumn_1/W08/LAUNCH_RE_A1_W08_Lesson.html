Evidence, belief and choices

Learning goal

Use evidence from the half-term to explain how beliefs and worldviews can influence identity, values or choices.

Today I can…

explain a belief-to-choice link with relevant evidence · use an accurate earlier example and improve a response

40 minutes · take a pause when you need one.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss. You can work with fictional source cards.

Choice/MCQ (C)

Choose the letters or match the alternatives in your book. Record C beside the work.

  1. Choose the belief-to-choice link in the care-worker source: A valuing dignity leads to caring work; B age decides every choice.
  2. Choose the difference: A both reject belief in God; B Christianity includes belief in God, while Humanism does not.
  3. Choose authority: A a trusted guide; B a high price. Choose guidance from conscience or a sacred text: right and wrong / handwriting.
  4. Choose the shared value: A avoiding neighbours; B peace and welcome. Choose the source clue: meeting neighbours and resolving conflict / having identical beliefs.
  5. Choose the evidence: A the person’s age; B choosing care work. Choose what the explanation adds: how care work puts dignity into action / an unrelated fact.
  6. Read both remembrance sources. Match source to action: A prayer; B silence. Choose the shared value: peace / wealth. Match each belief: God’s care / living on through memories.

Which detail is most relevant to explaining how Daniel’s belief influences his work?

B. He chose care work because he believes everyone deserves dignity. It explicitly links Daniel’s belief in dignity to his job choice.

A: Selects a detail without linking it to the question.

C: Gives background without explaining this influence.

Individual check answers and source map

  1. Earlier learning: Identity and choice. He chose care work because he believes everyone deserves dignity; the work lets him act on this belief. Source: Autumn 1 Week 1.
  2. Earlier learning: Two worldviews. For example, Christians believe in one God as Trinity; Humanists do not believe in a god and regard this as our only life. Their beliefs about God differ. Source: Autumn 1 Week 2.
  3. Earlier learning: Authority. A trusted source of guidance. Amira may use the Qur’an to guide honesty; a person may consult conscience about right and wrong. Source: Autumn 1 Week 3.
  4. Earlier learning: Shared values. Peace, welcome or dialogue. Mediation helps neighbours resolve conflict; the open day helps neighbours meet. Source: Autumn 1 Week 4.
  5. Earlier learning: Explaining. “For example, he chose care work” is evidence; the last sentence links that choice to the value of dignity and explains how. Source: Autumn 1 Week 5.
  6. Earlier learning: Extended response. Both remember people and value peace. The Christian source prays and believes people are in God’s care; the Humanist source does not pray and values memories and preventing war. They share remembrance and hope for peace while their beliefs and practices differ. Source: Autumn 1 Week 6.

Exit guidance

The Christian source chose care work because everyone deserves dignity; this choice is evidence of belief influencing action.
