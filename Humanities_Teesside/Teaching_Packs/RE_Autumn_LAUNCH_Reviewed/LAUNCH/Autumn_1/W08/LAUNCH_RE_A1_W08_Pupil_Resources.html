Route card · answers go in your book

Independent (I)

Give brief answers for 1 to 5. For 6, use both sources in a short paragraph.

Supported (S)

Read one prompt at a time. Use the word bank. For 6, dictate or complete point, evidence, explain.

An adult may read or scribe your exact response. They should not choose the answer.

Choice/MCQ (C)

Choose the letters or match the alternatives in your book. Record C beside the work.

  1. Choose the belief-to-choice link in the care-worker source: A valuing dignity leads to caring work; B age decides every choice.
  2. Choose the difference: A both reject belief in God; B Christianity includes belief in God, while Humanism does not.
  3. Choose authority: A a trusted guide; B a high price. Choose guidance from conscience or a sacred text: right and wrong / handwriting.
  4. Choose the shared value: A avoiding neighbours; B peace and welcome. Choose the source clue: meeting neighbours and resolving conflict / having identical beliefs.
  5. Choose the evidence: A the person’s age; B choosing care work. Choose what the explanation adds: how care work puts dignity into action / an unrelated fact.
  6. Read both remembrance sources. Match source to action: A prayer; B silence. Choose the shared value: peace / wealth. Match each belief: God’s care / living on through memories.

Stretch

Answer all six. In 6 compare reasons as well as actions and qualify claims as these fictional sources.

  1. Identity and choice. Daniel believes everyone deserves dignity. Explain how this influences his choice of work.
  2. Two worldviews. Give one key belief in Christianity and one in Humanism, then identify a difference.
  3. Authority. What is authority in RE? Explain how a sacred text or conscience could guide a choice.
  4. Shared values. Name a shared value shown by the church mediation group and the mosque open day. Give a source detail.
  5. Explaining. In “Daniel values dignity. For example, he chose care work. This lets him care for others”, identify the evidence and explain what the final sentence adds.
  6. Extended response. Compare the Christian and Humanist remembrance responses. Use a point, a detail from each source and an explanation of a similarity and a difference.

Sources for the comparison

A Christian response: Fictional source: “At our service we pray for those who died and for peace. I believe they are in God’s care, which gives me hope.”

A Humanist response: Fictional source: “I don’t pray, but I stand in silence. People live on in what they did and in our memories. Remembering should make us work to prevent war.”

Read one response back (?); improve one detail and mark //. Show your exit privately.

Evidence, belief and choices

Learning goal: Use evidence from the half-term to explain how beliefs and worldviews can influence identity, values or choices.

Words to use

WordMeaning
evidenceAn example or fact that supports the point
relevantDirectly connected to the question
authorityA source people trust to guide what they believe or do
next stepOne thing to improve next time

How I know

It directly answers the question being asked.

No. Use your agreed access route.

Model steps

  1. Read the question.
  2. Select a useful source detail.
  3. Explain what it shows.
  4. Check the link.

Point: ___. For example, ___. This shows ___ because ___.

Evidence cards

A The enquiry question

“Explain how beliefs and worldviews can influence identity, values or choices.”

Authored enquiry task

Limit: Use evidence from the source cards.

B Selecting evidence

Relevant evidence answers this question. Ask: does it show a belief leading to a value, a choice or a sense of identity?

Authored classroom resource

Limit: Interesting facts are not always relevant.

C Evidence bank

Daniel and care work · Priya and volunteering · Amira and the Qur’an · the mosque open day · the two remembrance sources.

Authored classroom resource

Limit: Choose the evidence you can explain.

D Routes

Written: three sentences or more. Structured: complete the point, evidence, explain frame. Supported: answer aloud with a scribe or recorder.

Authored classroom resource

Limit: Use your normal way of working.

E1 Source Daniel

Fictional profile: Daniel is 24, Christian and a care worker. He says, “I believe every person is made in the image of God, so everyone deserves dignity.” He chose care work over a better-paid job.

Authored profile

Limit: This is one person's view. Others who share the worldview may describe it differently.

E2 Source Priya

Fictional profile: Priya is 26 and a Humanist. She says, “This is the one life we have, so I want mine to be useful.” She volunteers as a mentor and gives blood.

Authored profile

Limit: This is one person's view. Others who share the worldview may describe it differently.
