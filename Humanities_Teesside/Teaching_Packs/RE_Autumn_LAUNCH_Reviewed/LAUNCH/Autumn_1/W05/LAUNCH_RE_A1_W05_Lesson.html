Opening

LAUNCH · RE & World Views · Autumn 1 · Week 5

A structured “explain” response

Learning goal

Write or give a structured explanation using a point, evidence/example and explanation.

How do beliefs and worldviews shape identity, values and choices?

I can explain an influence, compare two worldviews, identify a source of authority and give a structured explanation.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

A structured “explain” response / LAUNCH_RE_A1_W05

Outcome: Write or give a structured explanation using a point, evidence/example and explanation.

Evidence to retain: F: one explain answer using point, evidence/example and explanation.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Chunk one sentence at a time; model aloud; adult checks point before evidence; do not turn this into GCSE-style extended writing unless appropriate.

Access and regulation

Sentence stems; colour-coded model; oral-first option; scribe/typing; reduced response target; MCQ evidence bank.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Humanists are nice and do good things. — Humanists try to live good lives without religion. For example, Priya gives blood. This shows her belief that this one life should be useful. It now has a point, an example and an explanation.

2. The Day of Peace is about peace. — The Day of Peace brings people together around a shared value. For example, a mosque open day lets neighbours meet. Therefore understanding grows and conflict is less likely. “Therefore” links the evidence to the point.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions on key Autumn 1 vocabulary. Starter: which answer explains rather than lists? I Do: model a short PEEL-style RE explanation. We Do: improve a weak response together using colour coding. You Do: answer one accessible explain question from Weeks 1-4 using a scaffold. Exit ticket: identify the evidence/example in a model answer.

Look for

F: one explain answer using point, evidence/example and explanation.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
