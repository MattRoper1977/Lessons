A structured “compare” response

Learning goal

Produce a structured comparison using evidence from two worldviews.

How do practices express what people believe?

I can explain the purpose of a practice, compare two worldviews and give a structured comparison using evidence.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

4. No. A comparison links them.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Muslims go to Makkah. Catholics go to Lourdes. — Both Hajj and Lourdes are journeys to a sacred place. However, Hajj is a duty for Muslims who are able, whereas visiting Lourdes is a personal choice. It now has a similarity and a difference.

2. Prayer and reflection are different. — Both can create time to consider what matters. In these examples, Muslim and Christian prayer addresses God; the non-religious reflection does not. Name the particular examples instead of claiming every kind of prayer is identical.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: spot the difference between ‘describe’ and ‘compare’. I Do: model a structured compare paragraph with 'both / however / because’. We Do: improve a weak comparison together. You Do: answer one Autumn 2 comparison question using a scaffold or oral recorded response. Exit ticket: highlight the comparison word used.

Look for

F: one comparison answer using both / however / because.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
