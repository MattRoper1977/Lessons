LAUNCH · RE & World Views · Autumn 2 · Week 3

Rites of passage and community

Learning goal

Describe a rite of passage and explain how it can connect a person to a community.

How do practices express what people believe?

I can explain the purpose of a practice, compare two worldviews and give a structured comparison using evidence.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Editable Word packPrintable pupil resources PDF

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Word and evidence bank

A Bar and Bat Mitzvah

Bar or Bat Mitzvah marks becoming responsible for the commandments in Jewish life. Common ages are 13 for boys and 12 or 13 for girls, depending on the community. A synagogue celebration may include reading Torah; practices vary.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Jewish communities mark this in different ways.

B Confirmation

Confirmation is an expression of Christian commitment in churches that practise it. In the Church of England it includes affirming baptismal promises and a bishop laying on hands. Other Christian traditions differ.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Christian churches differ. Some do not practise confirmation.

C A Humanist naming ceremony

Some non-religious families hold a naming ceremony to welcome a child. Family and friends make promises of support. There is no religious content.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Many non-religious families hold no ceremony.

What happens? What does it mean? How does it show belonging?

Rites of passage and community / LAUNCH_RE_A2_W03

Outcome: Describe a rite of passage and explain how it can connect a person to a community.

Evidence to retain: F: “What happens? What does it mean? How does it show belonging?” task.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Keep discussion third-person; model respectful comparison; prompt meaning after identifying what happens.

Access and regulation

Visual life-stage sequence; simplified case studies; matching before writing; sentence starters; avoid asking about students’ own family ceremonies.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: order life events from birth to adulthood. I Do: explain ‘rite of passage’ using one religious example. We Do: compare two ways communities mark an important life stage. You Do: complete a ‘What happens? What does it mean? How does it show belonging?’ task. Exit ticket: define rite of passage.

Look for

F: “What happens? What does it mean? How does it show belonging?” task.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
