LAUNCH · RE & World Views · Autumn 2 · Week 4

Moral teachings across worldviews

Learning goal

Apply a moral teaching or principle from two worldviews to an everyday dilemma.

How do practices express what people believe?

I can explain the purpose of a practice, compare two worldviews and give a structured comparison using evidence.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Teacher card

Moral teachings across worldviews / LAUNCH_RE_A2_W04

Outcome: Apply a moral teaching or principle from two worldviews to an everyday dilemma.

Evidence to retain: F: one everyday dilemma with two possible responses explained.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Separate description from endorsement; model that people within the same worldview may respond differently; support reasoning with prompts.

Access and regulation

Concrete scenarios; 2-option choices; read-aloud; visual consequence prompts; oral/scribed responses; extension through justification.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

Arrival answers

1. A ceremony that marks a new stage of life.

2. To do with right and wrong

3. Both: the Golden Rule and Humanist empathy.

4. No. Description is separate from endorsement.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Hand the wallet in, as Sam would want if it were his — Fits the Golden Rule. Treat others as you would want to be treated.

2. Keep it, because nobody saw — Does not fit. Neither principle depends on being seen.

3. Hand it in, because the owner may be worse off than Sam — Fits consequences and empathy. It weighs the effect on the owner.

Exit responses

Which principle asks you to imagine being the other person? Both: the Golden Rule and Humanist empathy.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: quick dilemma vote with no ‘correct’ answer revealed. I Do: model how a teaching/principle can guide a choice. We Do: apply two worldview principles to the same scenario. You Do: select one everyday dilemma and explain two possible responses. Exit ticket: which principle influenced each response?

Look for

F: one everyday dilemma with two possible responses explained.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
