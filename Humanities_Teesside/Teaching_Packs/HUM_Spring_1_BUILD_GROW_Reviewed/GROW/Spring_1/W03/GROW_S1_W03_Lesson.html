GROW · Humanities · Spring 1 · Week 3

Causes and consequences of protest

Learning goal

Explain causes and consequences of a protest movement.

How do people bring about change?

I can order protest events, use evidence, explain cause and consequence and judge why a change mattered.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

Word and evidence bank

A Before December 1955

Rosa Parks had years of civil-rights organising experience, including work with the NAACP. Montgomery buses enforced racial segregation.

US National Park Service, Rosa Parks

Limit: This later biography selects events; it is not a recording made at the time.

B 1 December 1955

Parks was arrested after refusing to give up her seat to a white man on a Montgomery bus. Her action helped trigger a sustained community boycott.

US National Park Service, Rosa Parks

Limit: One person’s action should be connected to wider organisation and community participation.

C 1956 change

The boycott and legal action challenged segregation. A Supreme Court ruling and pressure on bus revenues contributed to the desegregation of Montgomery buses.

US National Park Service, Rosa Parks

Limit: Bus desegregation did not end all racism or inequality.

D Later work

Parks moved to Detroit in 1957 and continued civil-rights work. In 1987 the Rosa and Raymond Parks Institute was established to support young people.

US National Park Service, Rosa Parks

Limit: Later recognition is evidence of legacy, but not a measure of everyone’s views.

Teacher brief

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Causes and consequences of protest / GROW_S1_W03

Outcome: Explain causes and consequences of a protest movement.

Evidence to retain: F: causal explanation and exit check.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

No segregation simulation, forced role-play or repetition of racist abuse. Explain the discriminatory system clearly and recognise collective action.

Access and regulation

Chunk sources and tasks; pre-teach vocabulary with images. Offer sentence stems, organisers, oral rehearsal and typing. Start with one modelled example; extend through a second source, comparison or supported judgement. Check the learner can begin within two minutes, then fade prompts.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Arrival answers

1. No. The source describes years of civil-rights work.

2. An action expressing opposition

3. It enforced racial segregation and unequal treatment.

4. Community action and legal decisions also mattered.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Enforced bus segregation — Cause. This was the unjust system being challenged.

2. Boycott and legal action — Action. People organised and challenged the rules.

3. Desegregation of buses — Consequence. The 1956 change addressed the bus system.

Exit responses

Why was the bus system challenged? It enforced racial segregation and unequal treatment.

Why avoid saying Parks alone ended segregation? Community action and legal decisions also mattered.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Build and discuss cause/event/consequence chains; independently explain a link using because/therefore and evidence.

Look for

F: causal explanation and exit check.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
