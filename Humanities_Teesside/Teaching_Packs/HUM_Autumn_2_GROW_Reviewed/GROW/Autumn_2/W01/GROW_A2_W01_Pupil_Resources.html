Arrival choices

You can begin without previous learning. Word help: continent means a large land region.

Start with 1 and 2. Try 3 and 4 when ready. Reading and exact scribing are available.

1 What does continent mean?

Support: Use the reminder strip or talk it through with an adult.

2 Which meaning fits continent?

Support: Choose: A large land region OR A large body of salt water.

3 Which of the three countries lies beside the Pacific?

Support: Use the model evidence anchor B or read one relevant card together.

4 What changes when a map shows a much larger area?

Support: Give a reason when ready.

Stretch: use a precise example or explain which detail supports your answer.

Evidence cards

Read, point or listen. Keep the source label with any detail you use.

A United Kingdom

The UK lies in Europe, beside the Atlantic Ocean. Teesside is in north-east England. A map can show different scales, from a neighbourhood to the world.

Source: Natural Earth world map and labelled place coordinates

Limit: This small-scale map cannot show a safe street route.

B India and Japan

India and Japan are in Asia. India borders the Indian Ocean; Japan is an island country beside the Pacific Ocean.

Source: Natural Earth world map

Limit: A country is not a single culture or belief.

C A community link

Fictional case: a Middlesbrough community group shares festival greetings with a partner group in Mumbai. The link can involve people and communication without a physical journey.

Source: Authored classroom case

Limit: This example does not describe every person or place.

D Celebrations across places

Diwali and Hanukkah are observed by communities in different countries. Locate examples using the map; a festival is not confined to one country.

Source: Hindu American Foundation and Chabad accounts

Limit: Do not assign a religion to everyone living in a country.

Shared investigation

1. Zoom from Teesside to the world.

2. Locate the UK, India, Japan and their neighbouring oceans.

Work with a partner or adult, or use your own table. One person suggests; the other checks the evidence. Swap after one turn. Passing is allowed.

Use the evidence cards and any visual sheet. Follow the two shared steps above, then record one decision.
