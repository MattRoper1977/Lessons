Teaching assessment and support

Arrival and retrieval

Offer a low-demand start linked to prior learning. BUILD: match two pictures with an optional reason. GROW: retrieve one fact using a word bank, then explain. LAUNCH: interpret a short source with optional prompts, then question its limits.

Model and practise together

State the outcome and explicitly teach two to four key words. Think aloud through one example. Rotate paired sorting, source detective work, map annotation, timeline building, discussion and shared data interpretation.

Independent application

Set an individual task that produces the evidence in the weekly plan. Offer a choice of response mode and staged prompts. Keep the Humanities concept and level of thinking intact when reducing reading or writing.

Review and record

Revisit vocabulary and use a short exit check. Record what the learner did independently and the support used. Use this evidence to decide whether to revisit, consolidate or extend.

F means formative assessment; S means summative assessment. Keep individual evidence within group outcomes. UAS means unit Award Scheme: match evidence to the exact selected unit and its requirements before submission. The plans do not identify approved unit codes.

Prepare sensitive content in advance, use age-appropriate material and avoid distressing imagery. Offer a predictable regulation break and return-to-learning routine. For Holocaust education, use factual context and carefully curated testimony; avoid simulations or victim/perpetrator role-play. Respect religious and non-religious worldviews, family structures and identities. Do not require personal disclosure.

Weekly plans

BUILD · Autumn 1 · My family my community and special people

Key vocabulary: family, special, belong, community, local, service, past, present, change, diversity, respect, identity

Resources: Supported Coming to England extracts; local and community photographs; picture/symbol cards; paired old/new objects; simple maps; mini-whiteboards.

Access and challenge: Use one-step instructions, symbols and a model first. Offer talk, point, draw, type or write. Reduce the number of choices; extend by asking for a reason or a second example. Adults prompt the first response then fade support.

Enquiry: Who helps us belong to a community?

Success criteria: I can identify important people and places, describe a change, and explain one way I belong.

Week 1 · Talk about family and people special to me.

Model an inclusive people map. Together match people to caring actions; independently add a chosen person using symbols or a sentence. Allow a fictional example without personal disclosure.

Evidence: F: people map and one reason the person matters.

Week 2 · Identify people who help in the community.

Match helper cards to places/jobs. Solve “Who could help?” scenarios together; choose and explain one independently.

Evidence: F: scenario choice and one recorded response.

Week 3 · Recognise places in my local community.

Use a photo hunt and simple map. Match places to purposes; mark two or three familiar locations.

Evidence: F: labelled or symbol-supported map.

Week 4 · Sort then and now photographs.

Model a paired comparison; sort 2–4 images, identify one change and one similarity, then add a caption. Pair-match before writing.

Evidence: F: sorted images and a change/similarity response.

Week 5 · Show respect for similarities and differences linked to Black History Month.

Use a carefully chosen person/source and a shared evidence discussion. Independently connect one contribution to respect or belonging; finish with two exit questions.

Evidence: F: source-linked response and exit check.

Week 6 · Contribute to a class community map.

Co-construct a photo/symbol map. Each learner adds a person or place and explains its importance; use a simplified map and clear role.

Evidence: F: individual contribution plus one independent location/pattern question.

Week 7 · Share a group team or community I belong to.

Choose picture prompts; rehearse with a trusted adult. Create a symbol/drawing and short explanation; choose live, recorded or supported delivery.

Evidence: S: belonging outcome against unit criteria; retain best evidence.

Arrange picture cards together; independently retell the sequence orally or with captions using respectful vocabulary.

Evidence: F: ordered sequence and retelling.

Read a short scenario; sort fair/unfair or caring/not caring choices. Record one action showing fairness or care, linked to Human Rights Day.

Evidence: F: justified choice and action.

Week 7 · Make a festivals display and gather UAS evidence.

Select and explain work from across the unit; build an annotated display and review what each example shows.

Evidence: S: half-term review; relevant source skill check; retain UAS evidence.

BUILD · Spring 1 · Then and now how things change

Enquiry: How can we find out what has changed?

Success criteria: I can order events, compare then and now, ask a question and use a picture or object to support my answer.

Order familiar event cards; model a placement and time cue. Make a personal or fictional weekly timeline with short captions.

Evidence: F: correctly ordered sequence and recorded check.

Handle/view paired objects; sort then/now and identify a change and something unchanged. Limit to 2–4 sources; pair-match before writing.

Evidence: F: comparison and caption.

Week 3 · Order pictures on a simple timeline.

Build a floor timeline together; independently place 2–4 pictures and explain one placement using before/after.

Evidence: F: timeline and recorded explanation.

Week 4 · Talk about how my local area has changed.

Compare paired local photographs; annotate one change and one continuity. Record a caption or spoken response.

Evidence: F: annotated comparison.

Week 5 · Ask a question about the past.

Use an old object/photo as a mystery source. Generate who/what/where/when/why questions; choose one and suggest a source to investigate.

Evidence: F: enquiry question and source suggestion.

Week 6 · Record then and now with photos and captions.

Select paired evidence and create a short then/now record. Explain one change and one continuity using speech, symbols or captions.

Evidence: S: outcome against unit criteria; retain best evidence.

Week 6 · Record local features on a class map.

Evidence: S: class map contribution plus individual evidence against unit criteria.

BUILD · Summer 2 · Looking after our planet

Explore selected story images and sort helpful/unhelpful actions. Model a reason; independently choose one action and explain how it helps using a picture, speech or caption.

LAUNCH · Spring 1 · Conflict the Holocaust and remembrance

Key vocabulary: conflict, persecution, Holocaust, testimony, remembrance, evidence, significance, cause, consequence, impact

Resources: Age-appropriate Holocaust education resources; carefully selected The Diary of a Young Girl extracts; IWM sources; curated testimony; glossaries; writing frames.

Access and challenge: Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. For sensitive material, explain content beforehand, offer a short regulated break and avoid graphic imagery, simulation or role-play.

Enquiry: How should we understand conflict and remembrance?

Success criteria: I can explain causes and consequences, evaluate a curated source in context and create an accurate respectful response supported by evidence.

Week 1 · Explain the causes of a twentieth century conflict.

Model a causal explanation with a short source set; guide sorting of factors; independently explain supported links.

Evidence: F: book task, questioning and two exit questions.

Week 2 · Study the Holocaust sensitively linked to Holocaust Memorial Day.

Establish factual context using curated sources. Examine respectful remembrance examples; create a brief evidence-based memorial response and explain a choice.

Evidence: F: accurate contextual response and exit check; monitor regulation.

Week 3 · Evaluate survivor testimony from a curated online archive.

Model source interrogation; annotate what a short testimony reveals and its limitations; write an evidence-based evaluation. No simulation or role-play.

Evidence: F: source annotations, evidence selection and evaluation.

Week 4 · Explain the significance of remembrance.

Examine age-appropriate remembrance sources; identify why people remember and create a respectful evidence-based reflection.

Evidence: F: significance response and exit check.

Week 5 · Create a respectful digital memorial.

Review examples and agree criteria; select accurate facts/quotations, create a short digital memorial and explain a design choice.

Evidence: F: factual accuracy, respectful representation and explanation.

Week 6 · Complete a structured evaluation of causes and consequences.

Organise causal evidence from the unit; develop a structured evaluation with supported links and a conclusion.

Evidence: S: half-term outcome against unit criteria; retain best evidence.

LAUNCH · Spring 2 · Natural hazards and risk

Key vocabulary: hazard, risk, tectonic, vulnerability, management, data, table, graph, pattern, conclusion

Resources: Hazard case studies; teacher-selected live hazard-tracking sites; data sets; prepared tables; spreadsheet or graph paper; glossary; enquiry frames.

Access and challenge: Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers.

Enquiry: How can data help us understand hazard risk?

Success criteria: I can explain hazard causes, effects and management, analyse data, evaluate risk and produce an evidence-based case-study report.

Week 1 · Explain causes of tectonic hazards.

Locate a hazard; build a cause/effects/responses diagram using evidence; interpret one graph/map and make a case-study judgement.

Evidence: F: explanation, map/data interpretation and two exit questions.

Week 2 · Track live hazards online and interpret data.

Record the date/time of a teacher-selected data snapshot; enter a small set in a table, graph it and identify a pattern with a short conclusion.

Evidence: F: data accuracy, interpretation and exit check.

Week 3 · Explain effects and management of a hazard.

Locate a hazard and use case-study evidence to map causes, effects and responses. Compare management choices; independently judge which response addresses a stated risk and why.

Evidence: F: supported judgement, map/graph interpretation and two exit questions.

Week 4 · Investigate extreme-weather hazards and risk.

Interpret a weather-hazard map and case-study evidence. Distinguish the event from exposure and vulnerability; independently explain effects and evaluate a management response.

Evidence: F: risk explanation and two exit questions.

Week 5 · Analyse hazard data in spreadsheets and produce a digital report.

Model a table and suitable graph using a reduced data set. Enter or check data, label the graph and identify a pattern. Independently write a short conclusion and note a limitation.

Evidence: F: data accuracy, graph, interpretation and two exit questions.

Week 6 · Complete a hazard case-study report.

Combine location, causes, effects, risk and management evidence into a structured report. Select a useful map/graph, justify a judgement and evaluate a source or data limitation.

Evidence: S: case-study outcome against unit criteria; retain best evidence.

LAUNCH · Summer 1 · Resources climate and sustainability

Key vocabulary: resource, sustainability, climate change, mitigation, adaptation, cause, effect, response, evidence, provenance, usefulness, decision, criteria

Resources: Climate and sustainability data; selected No One Is Too Small extracts by Thunberg; online data sets; curated sources; highlighters; prepared tables; spreadsheet or graph paper; planning frame; presentation device; glossary.

Access and challenge: Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. Use labelled examples and a reduced data set before the full task.

Enquiry: How can we make a justified decision about sustainability?

Success criteria: I can interpret resource and climate evidence, evaluate responses, compare options against criteria and justify a practical decision.

Week 1 · Explain resource use and climate change using online data.

Check source provenance, date and purpose. Annotate a small data set and identify a pattern; independently explain a link between resource use and climate change using evidence and a limitation.

Evidence: F: source annotation, evidence selection and questioning.

Week 2 · Evaluate responses to climate change.

Distinguish causes, impacts and responses using maps/data. Compare mitigation and adaptation options in a decision grid; independently justify a response with evidence.

Evidence: F: evaluation, targeted questioning and two exit questions.

Week 3 · Make a reasoned geographical decision.

Compare two or three options using environmental, social and practical criteria. Discuss trade-offs; independently choose an option and justify it with evidence, noting a possible disadvantage.

Evidence: F: reasoned decision and two exit questions.

Week 4 · Create a digital sustainability campaign linked to Earth Day.

Analyse how a short campaign uses claims and evidence. Identify an audience and feasible action; independently create a digital message with accurate supporting evidence and explain its intended effect.

Evidence: F: campaign accuracy, evidence and two exit questions.

Week 5 · Link learning to local action with Community Project links.

Use local evidence to identify a manageable action. Agree roles and indicators of success; independently explain a contribution and how its impact could be checked.

Evidence: F: action proposal, targeted questioning and two exit questions.

Week 6 · Complete a sustainability decision report.

Score options against explicit criteria. Use unit evidence to write a reasoned report explaining the preferred option, trade-offs, limitations and a realistic next action.

Evidence: S: decision report against unit success criteria; retain evidence.

LAUNCH · Summer 2 · Local heritage fieldwork and my place

Key vocabulary: heritage, locality, change, continuity, source, evidence, provenance, usefulness, interpretation, fieldwork, enquiry, data, GIS, layer, pattern, scale

Resources: Local archives and heritage sources; OS and historical maps; digital GIS; curated source packs; highlighters; fieldwork records; planning frame; multimedia device; glossary; enquiry/writing frame.

Access and challenge: Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. For GIS, begin with a labelled example and reduced data. Use fieldwork roles and offer rehearsal plus live, recorded or supported presentation.

Enquiry: How can history and geography explain change in my place?

Success criteria: I can evaluate local sources, collect fieldwork data, map change, connect history and geography and present a justified conclusion in a multimedia project.

Week 1 · Investigate local heritage and change using digital archives.

Model provenance, content and purpose. Compare an archive item with a modern source; independently annotate evidence and judge its usefulness for a local enquiry.

Evidence: F: source annotations, evidence selection and questioning.

Week 2 · Conduct geographical fieldwork in the locality.

Agree a focused question, route and roles; model the method. Collect observations or tallies safely and accurately. Record enough location detail to connect findings to later mapping.

Evidence: F: method, safe participation and accurate data recording.

Week 3 · Use GIS to map how the locality has changed.

Model one digital-map layer. Compare historical and modern evidence, locate features and describe a pattern; independently capture and annotate a map with a scale and evidence labels.

Evidence: F: map-skills check and independent location/pattern question.

Week 4 · Connect history and geography to my place with preparation for adulthood links.

Compare maps, photos and fieldwork findings. Explain change and continuity and how this affects use of a local place; independently add a supported link to the enquiry.

Evidence: F: connected explanation and two exit questions.

Week 5 · Build a multimedia my-place project.

Storyboard a focused enquiry using selected archive, map and fieldwork evidence. Create labelled multimedia sections and a supported conclusion; review accuracy and evidence quality.

Evidence: F: project draft, targeted questioning and two exit questions.

Week 6 · Present the local-heritage project.

Rehearse the enquiry question, key evidence and conclusion. Present live, recorded or with support; answer an evidence question and use self/peer feedback to identify a final improvement.

Evidence: F: speaking checklist, questioning and brief reflection.

Week 7 · Complete the project and bank UAS evidence.

Make the final improvement and complete a relevant map/source check independently. Review against criteria, label individual evidence and reflect on enquiry method, change and continuity.

Evidence: S: half-term review, relevant map/source checks and UAS evidence.

Evaluation is completed after teaching. Select resources appropriate to the learner and retain individual evidence.
