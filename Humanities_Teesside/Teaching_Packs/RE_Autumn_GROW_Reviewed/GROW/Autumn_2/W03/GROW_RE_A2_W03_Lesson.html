GROW · RE & World Views · Autumn 2 · Week 3

Religious practice and subject vocabulary

Learning goal

Use accurate RE vocabulary to describe one religious practice.

What does it mean to belong to a community of belief?

I can describe belonging, explain a feature of a place of worship, use RE vocabulary and give a reasoned viewpoint.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Teacher card

Religious practice and subject vocabulary / GROW_RE_A2_W03

Outcome: Use accurate RE vocabulary to describe one religious practice.

Evidence to retain: F: description of one practice using at least three key terms.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Pre-teach terms; model one sentence at a time; praise accurate vocabulary rather than quantity; rephrase questions where needed.

Access and regulation

Three-word minimum vocabulary target; word bank; cloze version; verbal rehearsal; scribe/typing; image prompts.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

Arrival answers

1. For example, the mihrab shows the direction of Makkah for prayer.

2. Something people do as part of their beliefs

3. A set of actions done in a fixed way with meaning.

4. No. Prayer can be aloud or silent.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Muslims do praying stuff a lot. — Muslims offer salah, a ritual prayer, five times a day. “Salah”, “ritual” and “five times a day” make it precise.

2. Christians eat bread at church. — Christians share bread and wine in Holy Communion, an act of worship. Naming the practice and its purpose improves it.

Exit responses

Define the word “ritual”. A set of actions done in a fixed way with meaning.

Is all prayer spoken aloud? No. Prayer can be aloud or silent.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval/misconception questions. Starter: match vocabulary to images. I Do: model a concise description of a practice using subject terms. We Do: improve a vague description by replacing everyday words with RE vocabulary. You Do: describe one practice using at least three key terms. Exit ticket: define one key term.

Look for

F: description of one practice using at least three key terms.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
