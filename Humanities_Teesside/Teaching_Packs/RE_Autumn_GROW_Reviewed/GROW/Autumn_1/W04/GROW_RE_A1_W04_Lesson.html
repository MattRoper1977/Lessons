GROW · RE & World Views · Autumn 1 · Week 4

Hanukkah and the theme of light

Learning goal

Describe how light is used at Hanukkah and explain one meaning it can represent.

How do beliefs and festivals show what matters to people?

I can name different worldviews, explain a festival symbol, compare two festivals and explain a shared value.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Arrival answers

1. Good overcoming evil, hope or welcome.

2. A Jewish festival of lights that lasts eight nights

3. Remembering the rededication of the Temple, faith or hope.

4. Eight nights.

Shared activity reasoning

1. The Temple is taken over and misused — 1. The story starts with the loss of the Temple.

2. The Maccabees win the Temple back — 2. The victory comes next.

3. The lamp is lit and the oil lasts eight days — 3. The rededication follows the victory.

4. Families light the menorah for eight nights — 4. This is how the story is remembered today.

Give one meaning of light at Hanukkah. Remembering the rededication of the Temple, faith or hope.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions including one Diwali comparison. Starter: menorah image — what might the candles communicate? I Do: tell the Hanukkah story in accessible chunks and introduce the menorah. We Do: sequence story/symbol cards and connect light to remembrance, faith and hope. You Do: complete a short ‘What happens? What does it mean?’ task. Exit ticket: one meaning of light at Hanukkah.

Look for

F: “What happens? What does it mean?” response.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
