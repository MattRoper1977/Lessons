LAUNCH · Humanities

Can this record prove it?

Autumn 1 · Week 3 · 40 minutes

Learning intention

I can trace a digital source and judge what it can support.

Success criteria

  • Identify the record, date, holder and version I used.
  • Separate a recorded detail from an inference or unanswered question.
  • Explain a useful claim, a limitation and a suitable next check.

Use Next to begin. Ask for word help or your usual support when needed.

Arrival · 2 min

A job is not a reason

What does an occupation tell us?

A record gives someone's job as carpenter.

That tells us the recorded occupation. It does not, by itself, explain why that person travelled.

Which claim is directly supported?

Starter · 3 min

One document, several versions

Which version are you using?

An original record can be photographed, described in a catalogue and typed out later.

A clear digital page is easier to read. Clear presentation does not prove every entry is correct.

I do · 5 min

Follow the reference

Read the record identity before the claim.

The reference helps us find the same record again.

Our extract keeps three factual fields. It leaves other columns out. A short extract cannot stand for the whole passenger list.

I do · 4 min

Useful for a question

Make the judgement specific.

Weak: “It is reliable because an archive has it.”

Stronger: “The occupation field helps answer what job was recorded. It cannot tell us his later work. I would check other records for that.”

  1. Name the question.
  2. Point to a detail.
  3. State what is missing.
  4. Choose a relevant next check.
We do · 5 min

Choose the right next check

What would answer the gap?

We want to check whether a typed age matches the handwritten entry.

Choose a source that lets us compare the same field. A general history of the ship will not make that comparison.

Which next check best tests a possible typing error?

Independent · 12 min

A new digital source

Evaluate project card C for a new question.

Question: How useful is this project for finding a named passenger's recorded details?

Use C to state one strength, one limitation and one next check. Identify which version it offers.

This time, build your own evaluation.

LAUNCH Weekly - Autumn!C48

  • Research a digital archive source; evaluate it.

Exact match to the operative Autumn 1 Week 3 outcome. Research is a guided investigation of named digital archive records through supplied, credited excerpts. The complete enquiry works offline; optional direct archive links allow further investigation, without personal family research.

Prior: Week 2 considered causes and consequences of migration. Retrieve the difference between a person's stated occupation and their reason for moving.

Next: Week 4 builds a twentieth-century timeline. Carry forward the rule that a document's date and the date of a later digital version are different.

Preparation

  • Print A/B/C and claim cards or open the self-contained lesson.
  • Provide paper or a source/claim/limit response grid.
  • Keep direct source links available for optional staff-led extension, not a dependency.

Care and safety

  • No family migration or archive-search disclosure is required.
  • Use respectful historical enquiry; one named passenger does not represent a whole community.

Teacher response guidance

  • A/B identity: BT 26/1237; document date 21 June 1948; this lesson uses selected reformatted factual fields from the published transcript.
  • Practice claims: age 22 = recorded; skills may help work = inference; feelings and every passenger's occupation = not answered by the extract.
  • Best typing-error check: compare the same entry with an image; retain uncertainty if still illegible.
  • Example independent: Goldsmiths' searchable transcription helps locate a named entry efficiently. It may reproduce a reading error from a damaged document. I would compare the relevant entry with an original image before relying on an uncertain detail. Its recreated card is a modern presentation, not an original landing card.
  • Stretch: the recreated card is derived from the same passenger data, so not independent corroboration. A dated employment record or a carefully evaluated interview may help investigate later life.
  • Exit: source and reference/version should travel with the claim. A different layout does not create independent evidence.

Evidence of learning

Check independent evaluation against the actual question: does the pupil trace C, name its version, explain a relevant feature and choose a check that addresses a real limitation? This is formative practice, without invented marks or GCSE grades.

Access and independence

Read short source fields exactly, enlarge them and offer oral, written, pointed or scribed responses. Keep the reasoning demand while reducing copying. No reading-age claim is inferred from LAUNCH membership.

Lundy Loop

Learners can request a clearer source version or another explanation. The teacher checks their research trails, states the most useful next teaching focus and explains what will be revisited. No pupil is asked to share family records.

Arrival · A job is not a reason

Retrieve Week 2 causation. Keep the distinction between evidence and motive; do not ask about pupils' own families. This is practice, not the formal Week 7 assessment.

Starter · One document, several versions

Ask whether changing the font changes the date of the original. Expected no. A catalogue describes a record; it is not a complete copy. Explain that this lesson provides an adapted extract from a published transcript, not the original sheet.

I do · Follow the reference

Model: 'For the question about recorded occupation, B is useful: the occupation field supplies a specific entry. I would check the image if accuracy mattered because a transcription can contain errors.' Do not call the archive the person who wrote the list. Catalogue 21 June and disembarkation commemoration 22 June describe different things; this task needs the document date only.

We do · Sort the claims

Answers: 1 recorded; 2 inference; 3 not answered. Clarify that 'not answered' does not mean the opposite is true. Ask which other kind of source might speak to feelings: a letter, interview or memoir, evaluated in its own context.

I do · Useful for a question

Do not score a whole source simply high/low. Distinguish accuracy of a stated field from whether it answers the enquiry. A later job record addresses later employment; an original image checks transcription. These are different checks.

We do · Choose the right next check

Accept a second independent transcription as helpful corroboration, while noting it may copy the first. The most direct check here is the image, not an unrelated account. No paid account or live image access is required to make this decision.

Independent · A new digital source

Keep C available. Fade the scaffold before pupils explain why the limitation matters. A good answer sees searchable access as useful but does not treat re-created cards as independent evidence corroborating the list.

Exit · Leave a research trail

Collect a three-part research trail. Choose whether next lesson needs more practice with document dates, inference or version-checking. Tell pupils which issue their responses identified.

Misconceptions

An official archive guarantees every entry is accurate. Custody helps traceability; recording and copying errors remain possible. Ask what evidence checks the particular claim.

A job field explains a person's motive. Occupation is not a reason-for-travel field. Find a source suited to motives.

Digital version date changes the historical document date. Separate the old record, its catalogue description and later reproduction.

Two layouts of the same data are independent evidence. Trace both back to their shared origin before claiming corroboration.

Word help

archive: A collection of records kept as evidence of the past.

catalogue: A description that helps people find a record.

transcription: A typed version of the words in a source.

inference: An explanation suggested by evidence, which needs checking.

corroborate: Check a claim against another source.
