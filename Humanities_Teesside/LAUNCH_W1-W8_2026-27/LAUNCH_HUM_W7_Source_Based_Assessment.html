3 min
Arrival · retrieve

Retrieving what the criteria ask

Four criteria: point stated, evidence quoted with origin, evidence explained, judgement reached.

Explaining means saying what the evidence shows, not repeating it in other words.

Arrival decision: Take the assessment paper and the criteria strip. Everyone works from the same two.

Keep the strip visible during the timed section. Marking criteria withheld until afterwards teach memory, not assessment.

Starter · create the need

A marked answer with four errors in it

One answer, marked as though it met every criterion.

Marker note: “All four met. Full marks.”

Your job: the note is wrong. Four criteria are unmet, and each one is visible on the page.

You are not marking a person. The answer is written for this lesson and it is nobody work, which is why we can be direct about it.

4 min
I Do · model

Reading an answer against the criteria

The diagram lists the four criteria down the left and, on the right, the commonest way each one is missed.

Modelled: criterion two. I find a quotation and look for its origin. There is none. Error found. Smallest repair: eight words naming who wrote it and when. The paragraph does not need rewriting.

Smallest repair is the least you could add to meet the criterion, without rewriting what is already there.

Model the repair as well as the error. Learners who only learn to spot errors mark harshly and improve nothing.

3 min
We Do · spot the error

Spot the error: four of them are planted

Paragraph 1: which criterion does it miss, and what is the smallest repair?

The quotation in paragraph 2: what is missing before it can count?

The final paragraph: is that a judgement, or a summary?

Spot first: spot the error and mark it on your copy with the criterion number, then correct the line in the margin. Everyone commits at the same time, then we hold up the copies together and compare.

We may not all find the same four. When we compare, we ask which criterion the marked line fails. A mark with a criterion behind it stands.

Take the criterion from one learner and the repair from another; splitting them doubles the number of learners who speak.

3 min
I Do 2 · connect

What each error costs

The picture separates errors that cost a phrase from errors that cost a paragraph.

An answer with three cheap errors is usually closer to the standard than one with a single expensive one.

Ranked together: the missing origin is cheap. The missing judgement is expensive, because nothing in the answer can be reused to build it.

Push for cost, not count. It is the reasoning that transfers to a learner own work.

4 min
Exit · Audience and Influence

Audience receives; influence changes something

Read one of your marks to a partner. They say back which criterion you meant.

Did they name a different criterion? Change the wording of your mark. That change is your influence.

Close by naming one learner whose mark was rewritten after a partner misread it. That is the influence record.
