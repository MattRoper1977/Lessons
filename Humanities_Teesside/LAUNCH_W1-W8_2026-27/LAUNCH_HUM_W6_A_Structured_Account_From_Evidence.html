A structured account from evidence

No learner is asked about their own heritage, nationality or family history. All evidence is from the pack. Nothing said here leaves this room.

You can work from the frame alone. You can dictate and the words stay yours. You can sort cards instead of writing. Each is a full route.

Structured account is writing where each paragraph makes one point and shows the evidence for it.

Arrival · retrieve

Retrieving the shape of a paragraph

A paragraph in an account makes one point, not three.

Six extracts and three paragraphs means two extracts each, and the sort is the plan.

Arrival decision: Take the six extracts and the three point cards. Everyone works from the same nine.

Have every extract read before sorting begins; the first extract read tends to capture the first point card.

3 min
Starter · create the need

Two paragraphs, same evidence

Two openings using the same extract.

Version A: “One source says the numbers rose sharply.”

Version B: “Employment in the town rose because of the works, and the wage returns show it.”

Version B opens with the point and treats the source as support. Version A opens with the source and leaves the point unstated.

Do not sort a card for the class. The sort is the plan and the plan is assessed.

4 min
I Do · model

Point, evidence, link

The diagram shows one paragraph. The point is stated first so a reader knows what the evidence is for.

Modelled: point, employment rose because of the works. Evidence, the wage return of that year, quoted with its date, and one sentence saying it records a rise in the same quarter the works opened. Link, so the growth the question asks about begins here rather than a decade later.

Link sentence is the last sentence, which ties the paragraph back to the question.

Read the modelled paragraph twice, once with and once without the link. Hearing the omission is faster than being told about it.

3 min
We Do · sort and match

Sort the extracts. Match each to its point.

Extract 1: which point does it support, and what in it says so?

Extract 4: does it fit two points? Which one does it serve better?

Extract 6: what would you have to add to use it at all?

Sort first: sort all six extracts under the three point cards, then match each to the sentence it would support. Everyone commits at the same time, and then we hold up the first match together.

Two tables will sort extract four differently. Both stand if both say what work it does under that point. We ask for the work, not the placement.

Take the match from a learner who has not spoken today, and scribe the reason exactly.

I Do 2 · connect

Writing from the sort

The picture shows the sort becoming the account. Three piles, three paragraphs, in the order you chose.

Writing from a sort is faster than writing and then reorganising, which is why the sort is assessed as part of the account.

Decided together: point two opens, because its evidence is the hardest to argue with and the reader meets it first.

Ask for the reason the opening pile was chosen; deliberate order is the difference between a plan and a shuffle.

4 min
Exit · Audience and Influence

Audience receives; influence changes something

Read one paragraph to a partner. They say back the point it argued.

Did they name a different point? Rewrite the first sentence. That change is your influence.

Close by naming one learner whose opening sentence changed after a partner misheard the point. That is the influence record.
