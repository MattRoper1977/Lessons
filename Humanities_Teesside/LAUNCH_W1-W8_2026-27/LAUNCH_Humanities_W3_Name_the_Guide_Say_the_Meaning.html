Lesson overview
SPACEVOICEAUDIENCEINFLUENCE

LAUNCH Humanities · Week 3 of 8 · Humanities slot · Religious Education and Ethics.

Objective: I can name where a source of authority comes from, and say one meaning believers take from it, using the approved source cards.

Success evidence: a record sheet with a card letter, a source type, a meaning sentence, and a sure or not sure mark.

You are never asked what you believe. You are never asked about home, family or religion at home. Every card is school-approved and supplied.

40 minutes. Offline.

Resources, all in the LAUNCH Humanities W3 folder in the Humanities cupboard: one laminated set of approved source Cards A to F per table, the printed record sheets, and the word bank sheet. Card A and Card C are the two you model, and their answers are given to the class. Card B is the We Do card. Card D is the shared We Do 2 card and is worked on the board only. Cards E and F are the independent cards; do not show them before Independent. Keep record sheets and word banks face down until Independent. Nothing here is online and nothing else is needed.

Starter · create the need

Starter · create the need

SPACEVOICEAUDIENCEINFLUENCE

Two pupils read the same approved card. They gave different answers.

Answer one: it is right because the sacred text says so.

Answer two: it is right because our community has always done it this way.

Both answers name a guide. Authority means a guide people follow. The two guides are different. Each needs a name.

  • Notice: find the words that show where it came from.
  • Choose: pick one of the four source types on the board.
  • Explain: say what it means in one short sentence.
Write the four source types on the board now: T sacred text, L religious leader, C community tradition, R reason and conscience. Leave them up all lesson.

I Do · model

I Do · model

SPACEVOICEAUDIENCEINFLUENCE

Three words

  • Source: something you can point at.
  • Authority: a guide people follow.
  • Meaning: what believers take from the source.

The four source types

  • T sacred text: words from a book a religion treats as holy. Card A prints one line.
  • L religious leader: a person believers listen to. A card shows a leader giving advice.
  • C community tradition: what a group has done for a long time. Card C shows a yearly shared meal.
  • R reason and conscience: a person works out what is right. A card shows a believer who chose to help.

Watch me work on Card A. I never use my own life.

Card A prints one line from a sacred text. The card names the book. The source type is T.

The line is about welcoming a stranger. Many believers read it as a rule to be kind.

Card A does not say who wrote the line. I do not guess. I tick not sure.

Now I join two jobs. One is where it comes from. One is what it means.

Card C shows a yearly shared meal. The group has done it a long time. The source type is C.

Believers may take this meaning: we eat together to remember.

We Do 2 · rank the responses

We Do 2 · lab

SPACEVOICEAUDIENCEINFLUENCE

One card now. We work Card D together on the board.

Name the source type first. Then write one meaning sentence.

Choose a row. You can change row at any time. A row is a choice, not a label.

  • ◆ Name the source type of Card D. Use the word bank.
  • ▲ Name the source type, then write one meaning sentence.
  • ★ Name the source type, write one meaning, then a second meaning and one way they differ.
Card D stays on the board; nobody has a copy. Read it aloud twice. Then let each row write.
8 min
Independent · evidence

Independent · evidence

SPACEVOICEAUDIENCEINFLUENCE

This is doing time. You write.

Take a record sheet, Card E and Card F. These cards are new. Do both if you can. One card done fully is a real result.

For each card write three things.

  • The card letter and the source type.
  • One sentence saying what it means to believers.
  • Sure or not sure. If not sure, say what is missing.

Work at your own pace. You may use the word bank. An adult can read a card aloud, or write your words down.

Read any card aloud whenever a pupil asks. This is not a reading test. Scribe on request and read the words back.

Exit · Audience and Influence

SPACEVOICEAUDIENCEINFLUENCE

You say one thing. An adult writes it in your words.

Say one card letter and its source type. Or point at your sheet. Pointing counts.

The adult reads your words back exactly. You say yes, or you correct it.

Then you choose one next action. The adult writes it on your sheet.

  • Read my line to the class.
  • Put my card on the source wall.
  • Ask for the word bank next time.

TA Brief

Use the least prompt needed. Keep adult preparation and exact-word scribing separate from pupil authorship. Guidance is hidden from the learner surface by default.
