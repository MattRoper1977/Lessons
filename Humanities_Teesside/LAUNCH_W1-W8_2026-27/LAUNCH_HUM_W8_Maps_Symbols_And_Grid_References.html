Arrival · retrieve

Retrieving how the grid works

Read along the bottom first, then up the side. Along the corridor, then up the stairs.

On this sheet one grid square is one kilometre across, so a tenth of a square is a hundred metres on the ground. That is what the sixth figure is worth.

Arrival decision: Take the extract and the key. Everyone works from the same sheet.

Insist the key is consulted for each symbol. Confident guessing is the failure mode here and it looks identical to fluency.

3 min
Starter · create the need

Two references for the same feature

Two learners give a reference for the same circled symbol.

Reference A: four figures, naming the square.

Reference B: six figures, naming a point inside it.

Both are correct. They are different resolutions, and which you need depends on what somebody has to find. At this scale, four figures put a person inside a square a kilometre across; six figures put them within about a hundred metres.

Take no reference aloud yet. Resolution is the judgement this lesson assesses.

4 min
I Do · model

Reading a six-figure reference

The diagram divides one grid square into tenths both ways. The four-figure reference names the square; the extra digit on each pair names the tenth.

Modelled: the symbol sits in square 3421. Across, it is six tenths into the square, so 342 becomes 3426. Up, five tenths, so 21 becomes 215. Reference: 342 215.

Symbol is an agreed mark on a map that stands for one kind of feature.

Model the reversal deliberately. A learner who has seen it go badly wrong is far less likely to do it under pressure.

3 min
We Do · label and annotate

Label the symbols. Annotate with references.

Symbol 1: what does the key say it is?

Symbol 3: four figures or six, and why?

Symbol 5: what does this symbol not tell you about the feature?

Label first: label all five symbols on your extract from the key, then annotate each with its grid reference. Everyone commits at the same time, and then we hold up the first reference together.

Two people may give different six-figure references for the same symbol, and neither has to be wrong; a feature covers ground, and a hundred metres either way can still be inside it. We check by landing on it, not by comparing digits.

Have the class land on each reference. A reference nobody tests is an assertion.

3 min
I Do 2 · connect

What a symbol does not tell you

The picture separates what a symbol carries from what it leaves out. The left box is the claim the map makes.

Saying what a source does not show is the same skill you used on written sources, applied to a map.

Annotated together: symbol four is a place of worship. What it does not tell us is whether it is in use, or how large the building is.

Connect this explicitly to the source work of the previous weeks; learners rarely transfer it to maps unless told to.

Exit · Audience and Influence

Audience receives; influence changes something

Read one reference to a partner without the label. They say what they landed on.

Did they land elsewhere? Recheck along before up and change your figures. That change is your influence.

Close by naming one learner whose reference was corrected by a partner landing elsewhere. That is the influence record.
