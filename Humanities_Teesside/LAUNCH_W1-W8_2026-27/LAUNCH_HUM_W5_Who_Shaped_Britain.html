Arrival · retrieve

Retrieving how judgement works

A ranking without a stated criterion is a preference.

Reach, endurance and whether it opened a door for others are three usable criteria.

Arrival decision: Take four contribution cards from the set. Everyone works from the same four.

Insist all four are read first. A ranking begun after one card is an anchoring effect, not a judgement.

3 min
Starter · create the need

Two rankings, one set of cards

Two learners rank the same four cards.

Ranking A puts the card with the widest documented reach first.

Ranking B puts the card whose change lasted longest first.

Neither is wrong. They used different criteria, and each order follows from the criterion that produced it.

Take no ranking aloud at this point; the first spoken order sets the room.

4 min
I Do · model

Criterion, then order

The diagram shows the same four cards under two criteria. The orders differ, and neither is a mistake.

Modelled: criterion, documented reach. I read each card for how many people the contribution touched, not for how well known it is. Card C names a change applied across a whole service, so C leads. That order came out of the criterion, not out of my preference.

Reach is how many people a change actually touched, as documented.

Model the swap. It converts the lesson from ranking people to reasoning about criteria, which is the assessable skill.

3 min
We Do · commit and reveal

Commit to your ranking. Reveal together.

Card A and B: which leads under reach, and what in the card says so?

Card C: does endurance move it up or down?

Card D: which criterion is kindest to it, and is that a reason to choose it?

Commit first: write your criterion and your order on a whiteboard and turn it over. Everyone commits at the same time; nobody reveals until every board is down. Then we hold up together.

We will not agree, and that is the design. On the reveal we ask what criterion produced each order. An order defended by its criterion stands, whoever else disagrees.

Wait for every board. Take the defence from a learner who has not spoken today, and scribe the criterion exactly.

3 min
I Do 2 · connect

Defending the criterion, not the name

Two challenges arrive at a ranking. One says the order is wrong. One says the criterion was too narrow.

Refusing the second challenge is the commonest weakness at this level, and it is visible in a single sentence.

Modelled reply: under reach my order stands. Under a criterion that counted whether a door was opened for others, card D would rise, and I would not be able to keep it fourth.

Reward the concession. Learners who never concede are not being rigorous, they are being defensive, and the distinction is worth naming.

4 min
Exit · Audience and Influence

Audience receives; influence changes something

Read your criterion and concession to a partner. They say back what your criterion counted.

Did they name something you did not mean to count? Change one phrase. That change is your influence.

Close by naming one learner whose criterion was rephrased after a partner misread it. That is the influence record.
