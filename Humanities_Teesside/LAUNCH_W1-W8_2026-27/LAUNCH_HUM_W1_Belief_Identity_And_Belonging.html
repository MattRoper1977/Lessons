Arrival Task

Arrival Task – Retrieving three words adults live by

No learner is asked what they believe, what their family believes, or whether they belong to any faith or none. Every reading is made from the card on the table, and no learner speaks for a group. A learner may take every route without saying what they hold true. Nothing said here leaves this room.
You can work from the frame alone. You can dictate and the words stay yours. You can show your reading by pointing to a line on the card. Each is a full route, and none asks about your own belief.

In adult life the three show up in small choices: what to eat, when to rest, what to wear, who to turn to.

Arrival decision: Take four case cards from the set. Everyone works from the same four.

Insist all four are read before any reading is offered. Do not invite pupils to add themselves as a fifth card; the cards are the only material, and a pupil who mentions their own practice is thanked and returned to the card.

Starter

Starter: One habit, two readings

An adult on a card keeps one day each week free of paid work.

Reading A says this is belief: the adult holds that the day is set apart.

Reading B says this is belonging: the adult does what the community they are part of does together.

Neither is wrong on its own. Each reading follows from a different line of evidence, and the card decides which line is there.

Take no reading aloud at this point; the first spoken reading sets the room. Keep the example adult unnamed and the faith unstated, so that no pupil hears their own practice being judged.

I Do: Line on the card, then the word

The diagram shows one card read three ways. Each arrow starts from a different line on the card, and none of the arrows is a guess.

Modelled: Card C. The adult works nights in a hospital and eats no meat. The card gives the reason as: it is right to do so. That line points to belief, because it tells me what the adult holds to be true. I did not guess from the habit; I read the reason on the card.

Identity is the answer a person gives to the question of who they are, in their own words and choices.
Model reading the reason on the card rather than the habit. It keeps the lesson on evidence and off any assumption about what a practice must mean for the people who keep it.

We Do

We Do: Commit to your reading. Reveal together.

Card A: the adult wears a head covering at work. Which line on the card says why, and which word does that line point to?

Card B: the adult gives one evening a week to a food bank. Belief, identity or belonging, and which line proves it?

Card D: the adult keeps no faith and marks a family day every year. Which word does the card support, and is none of the three an honest answer?

Commit first: write the card letter, your word and the line you are pointing to on a whiteboard and turn it over. Everyone commits at the same time; nobody reveals until every board is down. Then we hold up together.

Evidence check

We will not all agree, and that is the design. On the reveal we ask which line on the card produced each reading. A reading defended by a line on the card stands, whoever else disagrees.

Wait for every board. Take the defence from a learner who has not spoken today, and scribe the line they point to exactly. No pupil is asked to add their own case or to say whether a card matches their life.

I Do

I Do: When one choice carries all three

One adult choice on a card can be read three ways at once. The adult who fasts for one month each year may hold it true. They may see it as part of who they are. They may end each day of it with other people.

The commonest weakness at this level is to stop at the first word that fits. It is visible in a single sentence.

Modelled reply: under the reason line, card A shows belief. Under the line about who the adult eats with after sunset, it also shows belonging, and I could not keep it as belief alone.

Reward the added word whenever a line supports it. Do not let the room settle on any faith being about one word only; every faith and every non-religious view can carry all three, and the cards are written so that each does.

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket — Audience receives; influence changes something

Read your card, your line and your word to a partner. They say back which line on the card you pointed to.

Did they point to a different line than the one you meant? Change one phrase. That change is your influence.

Close by naming one learner whose line was rephrased after a partner found a different one. That is the influence record. Do not close by asking who in the room shares a habit on a card.
