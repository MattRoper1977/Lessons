W9Aut2·W2 · Use digital mapping/GIS to describe a placeGIS Layers · Reading Place
What can GIS reveal about a place—and what does it hide?		I can select and combine GIS layers at two scales to produce and qualify an evidence-based place description.Place description; Two mapped details; Layer/scale limitationThe chosen pattern becomes W10’s urbanisation question; the missing layer becomes the next evidence request.
W10Aut2·W3 · Explain settlement & urbanisationSettlement & Urbanisation · Why Here?
Why do settlements grow, and who experiences the effects differently?		I can explain settlement growth through a connected causal chain, weigh a counter-factor and reach a qualified judgement.Linked explanation; Quantified change; Trade-off judgementThe selected consequence becomes W11’s fieldwork variable or desk-based enquiry.
W11Aut2·W4 · Local-area fieldwork: collect dataLocal Fieldwork · Test the Claim
How can we collect safe, fair and repeatable local fieldwork evidence?		I can design and use a repeatable sampling method, identify bias and apply ethical and safety controls.Replicable method; Labelled observations; Safety/bias evaluationThe class agrees which raw observations are legitimate enough to enter W12; missing values stay missing.
W12Aut2·W5 · Record fieldwork in a spreadsheet; graph itFieldwork Data Lab · Pattern & Anomaly
Which representation makes the fieldwork pattern clearest without distorting it?		I can check and present fieldwork data in a suitable graph, then interpret pattern, anomaly and reliability.Checked data table; Suitable labelled graph; Pattern + anomaly + limitThe strongest local measure and its uncertainty become W13’s matched comparison rule.
W13Aut2·W6 · Compare a contrasting place (online resources)Contrasting Places · Same Measure, Different Meaning
How far are two contrasting places different—and what evidence might challenge that claim?		I can compare two places using matched evidence and scale, explain similarities and differences, and qualify the comparison.Matched comparison; Contextual explanation; Counter-example/limitThe best-supported comparison and limitation enter W14’s frozen evidence pack.
W14Aut2·W7 · Fieldwork enquiry write-up (December)Fieldwork Enquiry Verdict ★
What does our evidence support about place—and what stops us claiming more?		I can independently construct and evaluate a fieldwork enquiry using question, method, data, analysis, conclusion and limitations.Learner-owned verdict; Selected data/graph; Evaluation + improvementA named teacher receives the post-assessment condition recommendation and reports what will change, or why it cannot, before the next assessed enquiry.
Non-negotiable boundaries
  • Personal/family location, migration history and travel routes are never required.
  • Constructed/adapted sources are labelled beside the material and on print pages.
  • Live fieldwork requires centre approval, risk assessment, supervision and an approved route; the desk-based route has equal curriculum status.
  • Access can enlarge, chunk, read, transcribe exact words or change response mode. It cannot supply the geographical judgement.
  • Interactive panels rehearse reasoning; clicks and screen states are not assessment evidence.
  • Exact UAS unit/version/outcomes remain centre-controlled; developmental bridge language is not a GCSE grade.
