Stage 2 of 8 · 5 minutes

Read the source

SECONDARY TEACHING SUMMARY: The National Archives and UK Parliament.

A. In 1948, Empire Windrush brought passengers from the Caribbean to Britain.

B. In 1963, Bristol campaigners opposed a bus company's racial discrimination.

C. Paul Stephenson and other organisers helped lead that campaign.

D. The 1965 Race Relations Act prohibited racial discrimination in some public places.

Stage 3 of 8 · 5 minutes

Watch a model

  1. Section A: How did people challenge unfair treatment in Britain?
  2. Section B: How can values shape belonging? Use a stated example, not a guess about someone.

Point to the detail that supports the answer.

Stage 4 of 8 · 4 minutes

Choose

Which response respects the evidence?

Choose first. Give a reason before checking.

Stage 5 of 8 · 3 minutes

Check

Reveal the deciding clue

B

A reasoned example does not erase variation within groups.

Keep your first choice. If you change it, say what changed your mind.

Stage 6 of 8 · 12 minutes

Your evidence

Beliefs and values

Independently compare two sources and separate evidence from inference.

CLASSROOM SOURCE SUMMARIES — paraphrases or explicitly labelled fictional applications; not quotations. Use within the one 40-minute Humanities/RE lesson.

A1. In a Christian Christmas source, Jesus’ birth is described as a gift from God.

B1. In a humanist source, meaning can be developed through this life and concern for others.

F1. Fictional claim to evaluate: “Two volunteers help at the same project, so their beliefs must be identical.”

Your tasks

  1. Write a short historical account answering the question with two source details.
  2. Evaluate the history card: one use for this question and one limitation.
  3. Name both sources, give one accurate idea from each, and evaluate F1. Finish with one way a value might influence a voluntary action.

Writing stays in this open page only. It is not saved or sent. Copy what you want to keep before leaving.

Stage 7 of 8 · 5 minutes

Improve

Check your answer against the source. Show the detail you used.

Add evidence, improve a link, or mark a claim you cannot support.

Keep your first attempt visible.

Stage 8 of 8 · 3 minutes

Reflect

Keep both enquiry responses and one next step.

Teacher guidance

Preparation, access and misconceptions

Print the matching pupil sheets. Provide pencils and coloured pencils; pointing or adult scribing is available.

Think, point, speak, draw or write. You may pass or ask for a quiet start. An adult may scribe your own words.

Watch for: Split the 40 minutes across both outcomes. This is curriculum evidence, not an external qualification result.

Use the matching pupil sheets for any map, table or writing frame named in these tasks.

Teacher key · task answers
  1. Credit an accurate claim, two facts and reasoned links about campaigns or legal change.
  2. Useful for key events and dates; limited for participants’ voices, every cause and the full impact. Link the judgement to the historical question.
  3. A1 identifies a religious source; B1 a non-religious one. The same action does not establish identical belief. Credit accurate source identification, supported comparison and a clearly signalled inference; do not demand personal-faith disclosure.
