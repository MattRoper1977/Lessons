Hinge and diagnoses

Ruth prays at a remembrance service; Dan stands in silence. Which value can they share?

A. Only Ruth can show respect because prayer is religious.

B. Only Dan can hope for peace because silence is non-religious.

C. Both can show respect and hope for peace in different ways.

D. They must agree on every belief to share a value.

Reveal: C. Both can show respect and hope for peace in different ways. Ruth prays and Dan stands in silence.

A: Treats religious participation as the only form of respect.

B: Treats a non-religious response as the only hope for peace.

D: Confuses one shared value with identical beliefs.

Parallel hinge after a teaching move

A church group reads names; a Humanist group keeps silence. What can both actions show?

A. Respect for people remembered.

B. Identical beliefs about religion.

C. That all people remember in the same way.

D. That only a public action shows respect.

Reveal: A. Respect for people remembered. Both actions can show respect without assuming identical beliefs.

B: Shared values do not require identical beliefs.

C: The two actions differ.

D: Quiet reflection can also show respect.

Misconception: Only religious people take part in remembrance. Correction: People with religious and non-religious worldviews can remember.

Misconception: Sharing a value means sharing every belief. Correction: People can value respect for different reasons.

Shared values and what we remember

Learning goal: Explain one shared value connected to remembrance and respect.

Words to use

WordMeaning
worldviewThe way a person sees life and what matters.
valueSomething a person thinks matters in how to live.
DiwaliA festival of lights celebrated by many Hindus, Sikhs and Jains.
HanukkahA Jewish festival of lights lasting eight nights.
incarnationThe Christian belief that God became human in Jesus.
remembranceTaking time to remember people and events.
shared valueSomething people of different worldviews agree is important.
respectShowing care for people and their different ways of remembering.

How I know

  • Ruth prays for peace at a church service. Dan reflects quietly at a town memorial.
  • People can share respect, gratitude or hope for peace without sharing every belief.
  • Earlier work: identify a worldview, link belief to a value, explain festival symbols and compare respectfully.

Model steps

  1. Read the source card.
  2. Name the action.
  3. Choose the value it shows.
  4. Explain the clue without assuming everyone agrees.

Source examples are fictional. The remembrance summary uses the Royal British Legion; Humanism background uses Humanists UK.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Evidence cards

A Remembrance in the UK

On 11 November and Remembrance Sunday many people keep two minutes of silence and wear a poppy to remember people who died in wars.

Authored summary; see Royal British Legion, The poppy

Limit: Wearing a poppy is a personal choice. People hold different views.

B A religious response

Fictional profile: Ruth is Christian. At her church service people pray for peace and read out names from the village memorial.

Authored profile

Limit: This is one person's view. Others who share the worldview may describe it differently.

C A non-religious response

Fictional profile: Dan is a Humanist. He stands in silence at the town memorial and thinks about how to prevent war.

Authored profile

Limit: This is one person's view. Others who share the worldview may describe it differently.

D Shared values

Ruth and Dan believe different things. Both show respect, gratitude and a hope for peace.

Authored classroom resource

Limit: Sharing values does not make two worldviews the same.

Re-entry · 60-second recap

The previous lesson linked remembrance with shared values. People may remember through different actions, such as prayer or silence. A source detail can show respect, gratitude or hope for peace. Sharing a value does not mean sharing every belief. Read or listen, then point to one action or value you recognise.

What you need for today: your book, a writing method that works for you and the fictional source cards. Begin with Arrival; no missed work is needed first.

Private RAG rule

Green: names a shared value with a relevant source clue and accurately uses earlier RE examples. Amber: value is accurate but needs a source prompt or one earlier idea revisited. Red: needs direct teaching to connect value with source and earlier vocabulary. A scribe or reader does not lower the RAG; record the access support separately.
