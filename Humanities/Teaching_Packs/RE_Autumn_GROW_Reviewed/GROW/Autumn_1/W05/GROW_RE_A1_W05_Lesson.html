Christmas and Christian belief

Learning goal

Explain how one Christmas tradition links to a Christian belief.

How do beliefs and festivals show what matters to people?

I can name different worldviews, explain a festival symbol, compare two festivals and explain a shared value.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Editable Word packPrintable pupil resources PDF

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Word and evidence bank

A The belief

Christians believe Jesus is the Son of God. The incarnation means God became human in Jesus. Christmas celebrates his birth.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Christians explain the incarnation in different ways.

B The Nativity

Matthew and Luke tell different parts of the Christian Nativity accounts. Luke describes a manger and shepherds. Matthew describes wise men visiting Jesus. Many Christmas retellings combine these accounts.

Authored summary of the Gospel accounts

Limit: The two Gospels include different details.

C Religious traditions

Nativity plays, carol services, Advent candles and Midnight Mass all point to the birth of Jesus. Many Christians call Jesus the light of the world.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Belief and practice vary between individuals and communities.

D Cultural traditions

Trees, crackers, Father Christmas and turkey are enjoyed by many people of all worldviews. They are part of Christmas culture, not Christian belief.

Authored classroom resource

Limit: Some traditions have both religious and cultural meanings for different people.

Teacher card

Christmas and Christian belief / GROW_RE_A1_W05

Outcome: Explain how one Christmas tradition links to a Christian belief.

Evidence to retain: F: one tradition linked to a belief using “This links to… because…”.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Keep cultural and religious Christmas distinct; model ‘for Christians...'; check understanding of belief before asking for explanation.

Access and regulation

Visual matching; reduced reading; one tradition minimum; sentence stems; option to speak/record response; extension through a second example.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

Look for

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
