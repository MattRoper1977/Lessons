GROW · RE & World Views · Autumn 1 · Week 7

Remembrance and shared values

Learning goal

Explain one shared value connected to remembrance and respect.

How do beliefs and festivals show what matters to people?

I can name different worldviews, explain a festival symbol, compare two festivals and explain a shared value.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Editable Word packPrintable pupil resources PDF

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

A Remembrance in the UK

On 11 November and Remembrance Sunday many people keep two minutes of silence and wear a poppy to remember people who died in wars.

Authored summary; see Royal British Legion, The poppy

Limit: Wearing a poppy is a personal choice. People hold different views.

Authored profile

Limit: This is one person's view. Others who share the worldview may describe it differently.

C A non-religious response

Fictional profile: Dan is a Humanist. He stands in silence at the town memorial and thinks about how to prevent war.

Authored profile

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Remembrance and shared values / GROW_RE_A1_W07

Outcome: Explain one shared value connected to remembrance and respect.

Evidence to retain: F: reflection naming one shared value and why it matters.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Use calm, non-devotional framing; monitor emotional response; allow neutral reflection; reinforce respectful differences in how people remember.

Access and regulation

No personal bereavement disclosure; third-person examples; word/symbol choice; audio or scribed response; quiet alternative available.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: image prompt — why do people remember? I Do: explore remembrance through accessible religious and non-religious examples. We Do: identify shared values such as respect, gratitude, peace and community. You Do: create a remembrance/shared-values reflection using words, symbols or a short explanation. Exit ticket: name one shared value and why it matters.

Look for

F: reflection naming one shared value and why it matters.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
