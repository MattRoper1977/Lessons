Use: Big question: do beliefs make us who we are?

Autumn 2 · Week 4Title

Learning goal: I can give a respectful viewpoint with a reason on whether beliefs make us who we are, support it with an example, and weigh other identity factors.

In this lesson

  • State a clear viewpoint backed by a reason ('because').
  • Support ideas with an example, such as a helping belief leading to sharing food.
  • Weigh other factors (family, friends, place) and reply respectfully.

Link a viewpoint to a reason, add an example, and weigh other factors respectfully.

Beliefs can guide daily values and choices. In the left drawing, a helping belief leads a person to share warm food across the table.

Family, friends and experiences also shape identity. Two people with the same belief can still make different everyday choices.

Discuss the idea with a reason rather than teasing anyone. Always respect privacy, offer a right to pass, and avoid assuming what classmates believe.

Worked model: Beliefs partly shape who we are because they guide values like sharing food, while family and friends shape choices too.

Watch out: Never assume everyone with the same belief acts identically, and never ask a classmate to share personal beliefs unless they choose to.

Box 5

Independent task

What you need to know
  • An 'it depends' evaluation balances belief guidance against family, friends and community.
  • A source limit states what one example or drawing cannot prove about everyone.
Worked example: Beliefs guide values like generosity, therefore shaping actions such as sharing food. Yet two people with the same belief have different families and friends, so identity depends on several factors. One drawing cannot show every person's life.
Key words: viewpoint · evidence · depends · therefore · comparison · limit · respect
  1. 1. Weigh two sides of 'do beliefs make us who we are?' using 'because ... therefore ...'.
  2. 2. Explain why two people sharing the same belief might still act differently ('it depends').
  3. 3. Add one source limit explaining what the shared-food picture cannot prove on its own.
Sentence starter: On one hand, beliefs shape choices because ___, therefore ___. On the other hand, it depends on ___; one picture limit is ___.
Model answer: Beliefs shape choices because values guide generosity, therefore leading to acts like sharing food. However, identity depends on family, friends and experiences too, so people with the same belief still differ. Limit: one drawing shows one moment, not everyone's whole identity.
