Evidence cards

Read, point or listen. Keep the source label with any detail you use.

A Two priorities

Fictional case: Aaliyah puts family first and leaves a club early for a family meal. Ben puts commitment first and stays to the end. Neither is wrong.

Source: Authored classroom case

Limit: This example does not describe every person, family or community.

B A respectful response

Ben says, “See you next week. Hope the meal is good.” He respects a value he does not share.

Source: Authored classroom case

Limit: This example does not describe every person, family or community.

C A disrespectful response

A team-mate says, “You’re letting us down for a dinner?” This mocks Aaliyah’s value.

Source: Authored classroom case

Limit: This example does not describe every person, family or community.

D Respect is not agreement

You can respect a person’s right to their values without agreeing. Respect has limits: it never means accepting harm.

Source: Authored classroom rules

Limit: Hard cases need a trusted adult, not a slogan.

2. Write a respectful response that still disagrees.
