Understanding the Holocaust respectfully

Learning goal

Study the Holocaust sensitively linked to Holocaust Memorial Day.

How should we understand conflict and remembrance?

I can explain causes and consequences, evaluate a curated source in context and create an accurate respectful response supported by evidence.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

P Factors before September 1939

Nazi expansion brought Austria and the Sudetenland under German control in 1938; German forces occupied the Czech lands in 1939. Repeated expansion and the later invasion of Poland help explain the widening conflict.

USHMM Introduction to the Holocaust, territorial expansion context

Limit: These selected factors do not exhaust the diplomatic, economic and ideological causes.

A Europe in 1939

Nazi Germany’s expansion and invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939 led Britain and France to declare war on 3 September. Longer-term conditions and leaders’ choices help explain the conflict.

USHMM historical context

Limit: A sequence of dates alone is not a complete causal explanation.

B The Holocaust

The Holocaust was the systematic persecution and murder of six million European Jews by Nazi Germany and its allies and collaborators. Persecution developed from 1933; the genocide took place during the Second World War. Nazi policies also persecuted and murdered other groups.

United States Holocaust Memorial Museum

Limit: Do not collapse different groups’ histories into identical experiences or treat genocide as inevitable.

C Gerda Haas remembers Ansbach

In her later oral history, Gerda Schild Haas recalls that religion mattered in her family. She describes Jewish affiliation appearing on forms and in community life in Ansbach before persecution.

US Holocaust Memorial Museum oral history; Gerda Haas, prewar community life in Ansbach

Limit: This is a later remembered account of her experience. Broad generalisations in the full interview should not be treated as a survey of every Jewish family.

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Understanding the Holocaust respectfully / LAUNCH_S1_W02

Outcome: Study the Holocaust sensitively linked to Holocaust Memorial Day.

Evidence to retain: F: accurate contextual response and exit check; monitor regulation.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Explain the content before starting. Offer a planned break and a factual written route. Never simulate persecution. Teacher previews any external archive page or video. The supplied four-word excerpt and paraphrase provide a non-graphic written route. The external page also contains family-loss context; preview it before projecting. Do not invent a recording date if the item page does not state one.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers. For sensitive material, explain content beforehand, offer a short regulated break and avoid graphic imagery, simulation or role-play.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

3. Nazi Germany, its allies and collaborators murdered six million European Jews.

4. No. It refers to the Nazi-led genocide of European Jews; other victim groups also require accurate recognition.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Antisemitism was central — Supported. The historical source explains the targeting of Jews.

2. All experiences were identical — Not established. People and places experienced different events.

3. Lives before persecution matter — Respectful context. People should be represented as individuals with lives and identities.
