Opening

BUILD · RE & World Views · Autumn 2 · Week 4

Taking part respectfully

Learning goal

Take part respectfully in a classroom celebration activity.

How do people use light and stories to show what matters?

I can explain a festival symbol, compare two celebrations, retell a story in order and give a reason for my view.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

A Learning, not worship

In RE we learn about celebrations. Making a pattern or a card in class is learning. It is not worship, and nobody is asked to share a belief.

Authored classroom rules

Limit: Pupils and families hold different views. Anyone can choose an equivalent task.

B Our activity

Choose one: a light-themed card, a rangoli-style pattern, a paper decoration or a symbol drawing. We use battery lights only. No real flame.

Authored classroom rules

Limit: A classroom activity cannot show a whole festival.

C Rangoli-style patterns

Rangoli patterns are often bright and symmetrical. Many families make them by the door to welcome guests at Diwali.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Not every family makes rangoli.

D Taking part with respect

Use the symbols carefully. Speak kindly about other people’s celebrations. Ask if you are unsure what something means.

Authored classroom rules

Limit: Respect can be shown in many ways.

Teacher brief

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Taking part respectfully / BUILD_RE_A2_W04

Outcome: Take part respectfully in a classroom celebration activity.

Evidence to retain: F: artwork with one explained meaning; observed respectful participation.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Explicitly frame as educational and non-devotional; model materials use; support regulation during practical activity; keep symbolism accurate.

Access and regulation

Choice of practical activity; no requirement to share; no real flame; drawing/colouring rather than writing; opt-out equivalent task.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Arrival answers

1. They light rows of lamps.

2. A way of marking a special time

3. No. It is learning about a celebration.

4. Battery lights only. No real flame.

Shared activity reasoning

1. Ask what a symbol means before using it — Respectful. Asking shows care for what the symbol means.

2. Laugh at someone’s pattern — Needs changing. Kind words keep the activity safe for everyone.

3. Use a battery light, not a flame — Respectful. It follows our safety rule.

Exit responses

Is making a rangoli-style pattern in class worship? No. It is learning about a celebration.

Which kind of light do we use in the classroom? Battery lights only. No real flame.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: respectful / not respectful actions. I Do: explain that RE learns about celebrations and the activity is not worship. We Do: model expected behaviour for a simple non-devotional activity. You Do: create a light-themed card, pattern, decoration or symbolic artwork and explain one meaning. Exit ticket: one way to take part respectfully.

Look for

F: artwork with one explained meaning; observed respectful participation.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
