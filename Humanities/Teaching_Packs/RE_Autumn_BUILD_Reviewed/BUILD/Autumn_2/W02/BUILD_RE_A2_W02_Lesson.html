Comparing light in celebrations

Learning goal

Identify one similarity between how light is used in two celebrations.

How do people use light and stories to show what matters?

I can explain a festival symbol, compare two celebrations, retell a story in order and give a reason for my view.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Word and evidence bank

A Diwali

Many Hindus, Sikhs and Jains celebrate Diwali. Families light diya lamps. For many the light stands for good overcoming evil.

Authored summary; see Hindu American Foundation, Diwali

Limit: Families celebrate in different ways.

B Hanukkah

Hanukkah is a Jewish festival. It lasts eight nights. Each night one more light is lit on the hanukkiah. It recalls the Temple being rededicated and oil that lasted eight days.

Authored summary; see Chabad, What is Hanukkah

Limit: Jewish families celebrate in different ways.

C Both

Both festivals use light. Both bring families together. Both are linked to stories that matter to the people who celebrate.

Authored classroom resource

Limit: Sharing a feature does not make two festivals the same.

D Comparing fairly

We compare to understand, not to decide which is better. Start with one fact for each celebration.

Authored classroom rules

Limit: A comparison of two festivals leaves out many others.

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Comparing light in celebrations / BUILD_RE_A2_W02

Outcome: Identify one similarity between how light is used in two celebrations.

Evidence to retain: F: sorted cards and one 'Both...' sentence.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Model one accurate fact for each celebration first; avoid ranking beliefs; prompt from concrete images before abstract meaning.

Access and regulation

Compare only two celebrations; picture sorting; one-sentence target; oral response accepted; colour coding.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions. Starter: identify two celebrations from images. I Do: model a simple comparison, e.g. 'Both use light...'. We Do: sort picture cards into celebration A / celebration B / both. You Do: complete one 'Both...' sentence or visual comparison. Exit ticket: one similarity.

Look for

F: sorted cards and one 'Both...' sentence.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
