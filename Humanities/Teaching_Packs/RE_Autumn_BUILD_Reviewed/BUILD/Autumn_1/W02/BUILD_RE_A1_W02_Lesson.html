Special books and places

Learning goal

Recognise one special book or place linked to a faith.

What makes something special, and how do we show respect?

I can say why something is special, name a special book or place, and show one respectful action.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief.

Editable Word packPrintable pupil resources PDF

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Word and evidence bank

A Bible and church

The Bible is the holy book of Christians. Many Christians meet to worship in a church. Some read the Bible at home as well.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Christians worship and read in many different ways.

B Qur’an and mosque

The Qur’an is the holy book of Muslims. Many Muslims pray together in a mosque. Many keep the Qur’an in a clean, high place to show respect.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Muslim practice varies between families and communities.

C Torah and synagogue

The Torah is sacred to Jewish people. In a synagogue the Torah scroll is kept in a special cupboard called the Ark. Readers often use a pointer instead of touching the words.

Authored RE summary

Limit: Jewish practice varies between communities.

D Book or place?

A sacred book is read or listened to. A sacred place is somewhere people go. Both can be special to believers.

Authored classroom resource

Limit: A book or place is not the whole religion. It is one part of it.

Teacher brief

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Special books and places / BUILD_RE_A1_W02

Outcome: Recognise one special book or place linked to a faith.

Evidence to retain: F: one matched book or place with a simple reason.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Use respectful handling language; distinguish book/place from the religion itself; adult prompts one match at a time; reinforce 'special to some believers'.

Access and regulation

Limit to 2–3 examples; image/word matching; reduced text; verbal answer accepted; enlarged images; supported reading.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

This is learning about religion and worldviews, not worship. Nobody is asked to share a personal belief, family detail or loss.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Arrival: 4 retrieval questions from Week 1. Starter: photo reveal — book or place? I Do: introduce a small set of sacred books/places, e.g. Bible/church, Qur’an/mosque, Torah/synagogue. We Do: match books and places to faith traditions. You Do: choose or match one book/place and explain simply why it is special. Exit ticket: name one special book or place.

Look for

F: one matched book or place with a simple reason.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
