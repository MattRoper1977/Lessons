Use: Comparing belief and celebration

Autumn 2 · Week 5Title

Learning goal: I can compare Account D (Diwali) and Account H (Hanukkah) carefully, identifying shared light, distinct meanings, and source limits.

In this lesson

  • Read Account D (Diwali) alongside Account H (Hanukkah).
  • Spot one shared feature (light) and two different stories and meanings.
  • State the source limit: households vary and two summaries cannot speak for everyone.

Step 2 of 5: Account H · Hanukkah

In Account H, Hanukkah lights (hanukkiah) recall the Temple rededication and the oil tradition.

Say it: In Account H, Hanukkah lights recall the ___ and the ___ tradition.

tradition = practice from history and storieshanukkiah = lights used in Hanukkah celebration

Step 3 of 5: Shared light, distinct stories

Both accounts use light in celebration, yet a shared feature never proves their belief stories identical.

Say it: Both D and H use ___, whereas Account D focuses on ___ and Account H recalls ___.

belief = faiths or meaning in a tradition

Step 4 of 5: Source limit

Keep the source limit: households vary, so two short summaries cover some or many homes, never all.

Say it: Because households vary, two short summaries cover ___ or ___, never all.

source limit = two summaries cannot represent every household

Step 5 of 5: Put it together

  • Similarity: both Account D and Account H use light in celebration.
  • Difference: Account D focuses on good and knowledge, whereas Account H recalls the Temple and oil.
  • Source limit: households vary; two short summaries cannot cover everyone.

Watch out: check the source boundary: use many or some, never all. Personal beliefs stay private, and you can pass or speak quietly with a trusted adult in the room.
