Analysing hazard data for a report

Learning goal

Analyse hazard data in spreadsheets and produce a digital report.

How can data help us understand hazard risk?

I can explain hazard causes, effects and management, analyse data, evaluate risk and produce an evidence-based case-study report.

40 minutes, with flexible pauses. Point, speak, draw, type or write. A fictional example is welcome.

A magnitude 9.1 earthquake occurred off north-east Honshu, Japan. Sudden movement on the subduction boundary displaced the seafloor and produced a tsunami.

USGS event record, official20110311054624120_30

Limit: Magnitude measures the earthquake; it is not a direct count of people affected.

B Effects and responses

Shaking and coastal flooding damaged places and disrupted people’s lives. Earthquake-resistant design addresses shaking; warnings, evacuation routes and higher ground address tsunami exposure.

Authored explanation of the case and risk-management principles

Limit: A sea wall or warning cannot eliminate all risk. Do not judge people without considering access and warning time.

Teacher notes and answers

Teacher card

Analysing hazard data for a report / LAUNCH_S2_W05

Outcome: Analyse hazard data in spreadsheets and produce a digital report.

Evidence to retain: F: data accuracy, graph, interpretation and two exit questions.

Forty minute route

Arrival 4 min; starter 3; model 6; shared task 7; check 2; independent 12; review 3; exit 3. Pause time is flexible.

Preparation

Choose one independent route. Print the evidence and shared cards, required visual or data sheet, route worksheet and exit. The organiser is one separate A4 page. Keep answers with staff.

Use non-graphic maps and data. A bigger magnitude does not automatically mean greater harm; exposure and vulnerability matter.

Access and regulation

Pre-teach terminology and model evidence use. Chunk sources and data; provide an initial frame, then reduce it. Maintain normal access arrangements. Extend by weighing limitations, alternative explanations and evidence quality. Adults support access and evidence selection without supplying answers.

Offer a predictable start, private response and agreed pause. No cold calling or forced personal disclosure. Rejoin at one clearly named next step. Keep teen-appropriate examples and the same learning goal across response modes.

Use sensitive content warnings where relevant. Do not ask for family details or personal experiences.

Assessment

Use questioning and the exit response to decide the next step. Record the learner’s individual contribution and the access support used.

UAS: no approved unit code is supplied. Match the learner’s evidence to the centre-selected unit and exact criteria before submission. This pack makes no automatic award claim.

Teaching and evidence record

Supplied scheme of work teaching sequence: Model a table and suitable graph using a reduced data set. Enter or check data, label the graph and identify a pattern. Independently write a short conclusion and note a limitation.

Look for

F: data accuracy, graph, interpretation and two exit questions.

Accept accurate equivalents, including speech or exact scribing. Check the concept and use of evidence rather than length or handwriting. Record access support separately from content prompting.

BUILD: a concrete example with a reason or demonstrated action. GROW: explain or compare with a relevant detail. LAUNCH: connect evidence and reasoning; use a limitation when the outcome requires it. Keep RE responses non-GCSE.
