Humanities · BUILD · Autumn 2 · Week 1

A festival of light

Learning goal

I can show how a lamp can mean more than light.

Today I can

  • I can name a festival.
  • I can link a lamp to a meaning.
  • I can show respect: care for what matters to people.

You can point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write.

You may pass. You do not need to tell us about your life.

TA Brief

No cold calling; nobody is asked to read difficult text aloud.

Current stage

1. Read cards A–C aloud and set out the unlit lamp outline, not a live flame. Do not ask for personal belief, family details or loss.

2. Invite every pupil to choose privately before a reveal. Offer point, speak, sign, symbols, draw or write; any pupil may pass. No cold calling.

3. Use one Arrival tier, one Exit tier and one You do route. Hide adult answers; note whether a clue, frame or choice was used.

4. In We do, keep every pupil active with their own choice signal. For the display label, check the pupil links the lamp to its meaning; avoid turning 'many' into 'all'.

5. Give one private next step, allow the final two-minute check, and upload the pupil's own response to the evidence app the same day, labelled HUM-BUILD-A2-W01. Record only relevant agreed EHCP outcomes.

Shared preparation, access and safety

Read each pupil prompt aloud; show one short task at a time. For EVERY task, pupils may point, speak, sign, use symbols, draw or write. Invite everyone, allow quiet thinking time and accept a private response. No cold calling. Any pupil may pass. An adult may record the pupil's own gesture, choice or exact words. A pass needs no book entry. No board copying or typing is needed.

Choose one Arrival tier and one Exit tier, not all three. Read the four Arrival questions and three Exit checks in that tier; responses can be brief. Keep all Answer (adult) lines out of pupil view until pupils have responded. Choose one You do route; its separate page is an alternative, not an extra task.

This lesson studies how people celebrate. Do not ask about a pupil's belief, family, home practice or loss. No one practises worship. Use drawings or unlit objects only: no flames, heat, scent, food tasting or loud sounds. Do not infer a person's faith from where they live or what an object looks like.

Prepare the four source cards below, two sorting labels (Fits the card / Not shown), and a plain lamp outline. The outline is a small open bowl with a short wick at one edge; no flame. A square card labelled Ball is the contrast option. A diagram or adult description is enough; no unseen photograph or video is needed.

Adult-only EHCP note: Outcome opportunities are communication and interaction (show a choice or reason), cognition and learning (link an object to a meaning), SEMH (use an agreed pause and return), and sensory or physical access (choose an accessible response mode). Observe only the pupil's relevant agreed target. Regulation strategy: show 'first one card, then a quiet pause'; after the pause, point to the single next card.

Same-day evidence — HUM-BUILD-A2-W01: The assigned adult records the pupil's exact response, response mode and actual prompt used, and uploads one chosen book item, optional photo or brief observation to the evidence app the same day, labelled HUM-BUILD-A2-W01. Link an observation to the relevant agreed outcome where appropriate. A photo alone does not establish understanding.

Lesson cards

Card A — Diwali

Diwali is a festival. Many Hindus use small lamps. A lamp can be called a diya. The light can stand for good winning over bad. It can also stand for learning, like a new idea that helps us see. People may mark Diwali in more than one way.

Card B — A drawing

An unlit small lamp: a bowl with a wick. The drawing shows a lamp. It does not show who owns it or what they believe.

Card C — Care

Ask before you touch an object. Listen when a person speaks. You can learn without joining in a prayer.

Card D — One made-up display plan

First, clear a space. Next, place the unlit lamps. Last, add the labels. This is a plan for a class display, not a rule for how people celebrate.

I do · method

Step 1 — Look

I can see a small lamp.

Step 2 — Read

The card says many Hindus use lamps at Diwali.

Step 3 — Link

The light can stand for good winning over bad.

A lamp gives light. The light is a symbol: it stands for an idea.

My short label:

'Diwali. Many Hindus use lamps. The light can stand for good winning over bad.'

Check the claim:

I say 'many'. I do not say 'all'.

Must-know words — four

festival — a special time when people mark what matters to them

lamp — a thing made to give light

symbol — a thing that stands for an idea

respect — care for people and what matters to them

Show one link: lamp → light; light → an idea.

In your book: One link from the model, with words or pictures.

Lesson cards

Card A — Diwali

Diwali is a festival. Many Hindus use small lamps. A lamp can be called a diya. The light can stand for good winning over bad. It can also stand for learning, like a new idea that helps us see. People may mark Diwali in more than one way.

Card B — A drawing

An unlit small lamp: a bowl with a wick. The drawing shows a lamp. It does not show who owns it or what they believe.

Card C — Care

Ask before you touch an object. Listen when a person speaks. You can learn without joining in a prayer.
