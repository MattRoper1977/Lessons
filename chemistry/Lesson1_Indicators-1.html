Red in acid
Blue in alkali

Comes as paper or liquid. The oldest indicator — used for over 700 years! 🧠 Memory aid: "Litmus = Literally just Red or Blue"

🧪 Phenolphthalein

Colourless in acid
Pink in alkali

🧠 Memory aid: "Phenolphthalein = Phantom in acid (invisible!), Pink in alkali." Used in titrations because the colour snaps from nothing to bright pink — impossible to miss.

Red in acid
Yellow in alkali

🧠 Memory aid: "Methyl orange = Mars bar Red in acid, Yellow sunshine in alkali." 🚦 Traffic-light colours make it easy to remember.

I Do: Universal Indicator (2.30)

Key Idea: Universal indicator is a mixture of dyes that gives a range of colours to show the approximate pH of a solution.

🌈 What Makes It Special?

Litmus, phenolphthalein and methyl orange each only tell you acid or alkali (two colours).

Universal indicator gives a range of colours – from red through yellow, green, blue to purple – so you can estimate the pH (how acidic or alkaline).

📏 What Is pH?

pH is a scale from 0 to 14 that measures how acidic or alkaline a solution is. You will learn the full scale next lesson – for now, just know:

• Low pH (0–6) = acidic  |  pH 7 = neutral  |  High pH (8–14) = alkaline

⚠️ Limitation

Universal indicator only gives an approximate pH – the colours can be tricky to read between values. For precise measurements, a pH meter is better.
