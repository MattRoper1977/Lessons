H₂

Hydrogen

Test: hold a burning splint near the gas.

Result: a "squeaky pop".

The pop is the tiny explosion of H₂ + O₂ → H₂O.

O₂

Oxygen

Test: place a glowing splint into the gas.

Result: the splint relights.

Oxygen supports combustion — it makes things burn.

CO₂

Carbon Dioxide

Test: bubble through limewater (calcium hydroxide solution).

Result: turns milky / cloudy.

CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → insoluble CaCO₃ (the white cloudiness).

💡 Key Distinction: Burning splint = flame still lit (for H₂). Glowing splint = blown out so only the red ember remains (for O₂). Students often confuse these!

    Exit Ticket

    No notes. From memory only. Your tier question = one observation + one conclusion. Then everyone answers the safety/method check below.
    ✅ Method check (everyone): Name one safety precaution a scientist takes when testing gases.

    1) How do you test for hydrogen?

    Hold a burning splint near the gas. It makes a squeaky pop.

    2) What does CO₂ do to limewater?

    Turns it milky/cloudy.

    3) A glowing splint relights — name the gas.

    Oxygen.

    1) A glowing splint relights. Name the gas and explain why.

    Oxygen. It relights because oxygen supports combustion.

    2) Describe how you would prove a gas is carbon dioxide.

    Bubble it through limewater — the limewater turns milky/cloudy.

    3) Why is the splint test for hydrogen different from the splint test for oxygen?

    Hydrogen needs a burning splint (gives squeaky pop as H₂ ignites). Oxygen needs a glowing splint (relights it because O₂ supports combustion).

    1) Explain the chemistry behind why CO₂ turns limewater milky.

    CO₂ reacts with calcium hydroxide solution: CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O. The calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) is insoluble and forms a white precipitate, making the solution appear milky.

    2) Why does a burning splint go OUT in CO₂ but a glowing splint RELIGHTS in O₂?

    CO₂ does not support combustion (it can smother flames), whereas O₂ supports combustion strongly, so a glowing ember bursts back into flame.

    3) Write the balanced equation for the squeaky pop and name the product.

    2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. The product is water.

    Chemistry Behind the Tests (Higher)

    • H₂: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (explosive combustion → pop)

    • O₂: supports combustion → relights glowing material

    • CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O (CaCO₃ is insoluble → milky)

    • CO₂ does not support combustion → a burning splint goes OUT (useful contrast with O₂)

    • Litmus-pair chemistry (NH₃, Cl₂) → Lesson 4b

    🎯 Command words: DESCRIBE = say what happens · EXPLAIN = say WHY (use "because") · COMPARE = similarities AND differences · PLAN/METHOD = numbered steps.
