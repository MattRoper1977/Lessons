Before We Start: Flame Tests in 60 Seconds

New to this? Here is everything you need for today — fast. (It is all on Side A of your bridge sheet if you want it on paper.)

Five metal ions — five colours

A flame test identifies the metal ion in a compound by the colour it turns a flame.

1. Clean a nichrome wire loop in concentrated HCl, then heat it until it shows no colour.
2. Dip the clean loop in the sample, hold it in a blue Bunsen flame, and read the colour.
3. Match the colour to the ion. Clean between every test — sodium’s yellow masks everything.
Whiteboards — 20 seconds: which ion is crimson red? Which is blue-green? Then we go straight into today’s colours.

📋 The Method (worth marks on its own)

1. Clean a nichrome wire loop in concentrated HCl.

2. Heat it in the flame until it shows no colour (clean).

3. Dip the clean loop into the sample.

4. Hold it in a blue Bunsen flame and observe the colour.

5. Compare to the known colours and identify the ion.

🎯 Say this in the exam: “Dip a clean nichrome loop in conc. HCl, heat until no colour, dip in sample, hold in a blue flame, observe the colour.” That sentence is worth 4 marks.

🌟 Higher: electrons absorb flame energy, jump to a higher level (“excited”), then fall back — emitting light of a colour unique to that element.

Don’t write: calcium = orange · sodium = gold

Write: calcium = ORANGE-RED · sodium = YELLOW · lithium = pure CRIMSON

Lithium and calcium are the trap — pure crimson vs brick orange-red. Use the exact words.

Talk to your partner: which two colours are easiest to mix up — and what is the exact word for each? Be ready to match them next.

    Scaffolding Sheet – Higher (Grades 2–4)

    Why each ion has its own colour

    • Electrons absorb flame energy → jump to a higher level (“excited”).

    • Falling back, they emit light of a specific wavelength → a colour unique to that element.

    • Na⁺ emits so strongly that traces mask every other colour — hence rigorous cleaning.

    Command words

    DESCRIBE = say what happens · EXPLAIN = say WHY (use “because”) · COMPARE = similarities AND differences · METHOD = numbered steps.

    5-mark frame (three bottles)

    Per bottle: clean wire → TEST → COLOUR → ION. Then explain cleaning: name sodium and masking for the last 2 marks.
