PROGRESS SCHOOLS I Do

I Do: How to Answer a 6-Mark Identification Question

Watch the thinking: I test the cation and the anion separately, give evidence for each, then combine. Every claim needs a “because”.

6 marks · Describe & Explain

Q: An unknown white compound dissolves in water. Tests: flame = yellow; acidified silver nitrate = white precipitate.

(a) Identify the cation and explain [2]   (b) Identify the anion and explain [2]   (c) Name the compound and write the ionic equation [2]

(a) ✓ Cation = Na⁺ (sodium). ✓ Because a yellow flame is characteristic of sodium ions.
(b) ✓ Anion = Cl⁻ (chloride). ✓ Because a white precipitate with acidified silver nitrate indicates chloride (the precipitate is AgCl).
(c) ✓ Compound = sodium chloride (NaCl). ✓ Ionic equation: Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).

🎯 Pattern: Flame → cation. Reagent test → anion. Combine → compound. Each identification needs evidence (“because the flame was yellow”), never just the answer.

Talk to your partner: in part (a), what is the evidence that wins the second mark? Be ready to build an answer yourselves next.
PROGRESS SCHOOLS 🧭 Observe

Observe: The Identification Decision Tree

The systematic method examiners reward. Predict each branch out loud, then tap to reveal it. This is exactly the order to follow for any \u201cidentify the unknown\u201d question.
🧪
Step 1 — Dissolve
Dissolve the unknown in water to make a solution.
🔥
Step 2 — Flame test → the CATION
Yellow = Na⁺ · Lilac = K⁺ · Crimson = Li⁺ · Orange-red = Ca²⁺ · Blue-green = Cu²⁺.
🫧
Step 3 — Add dilute HCl
Fizzes? Gas turns limewater milky → CO₃²⁻ (carbonate). No fizz → carry on.
⬜
Step 4 — Acidified silver nitrate (HNO₃)
Precipitate colour gives the halide: white = Cl⁻ · cream = Br⁻ · yellow = I⁻.
⬜
Step 5 — Acidified barium chloride (HCl)
Still nothing? A white precipitate here → SO₄²⁻ (sulfate).
🎯
Step 6 — Combine
Cation + anion = the compound. e.g. Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl.
Ready check: cover the screen — can you say the method in order? Dissolve → flame (cation) → HCl (fizz?) → silver nitrate → barium chloride → combine.
